The Postbag: 0-3 against the wall, $1.50M a point, and a streak that went from seven to nine

A pattern at Montreal nobody printed, the per-point rate at New York, and Anaheim's December streak becoming January's.

Thursday, 12 January 2006 · edited by Tunde Oyelaran

Three clubs with patterns the standings have been printing for weeks, and a payroll that keeps generating mail. A club that cannot beat one opponent at all. A club that is two different teams depending on which city it wakes up in. And the most penalized man in thirty clubs.

We have played New Jersey three times this season and not beaten them once. At what point is that a pattern and not bad luck? We are trying to get into this thing and they don't even lead the division.

Philippe Deslauriers, Verdun

Montreal Canadiens is 0-3 against New Jersey Devils and the Devils are not going to lose to you just because you want them to. New Jersey has allowed 86 goals in 40 games, the fewest in the Eastern Conference. Martin Brodeur95 has a 1.85 goals-against average and a .907 save percentage across 34 starts. The Devils sit at 24-9-3 with 59 points and a 15-4 road record, so they are winning games in your building. You are 13-16-8 with 44 points and a minus-8 goal differential. Three meetings against the best defensive club in the conference. That is not bad luck.

We are a different team on the road and nobody around here will say why. Is it the travel, is it the building, is it something else? The record prints it and the local paper doesn't.

Earlene Whitcomb, Nashville

Nashville Predators is 17-4 at home and 8-12 on the road. Nashville's payroll is $51.08M and it buys 55 points, which the per-point column puts at $0.93M, second-best in the league behind Pittsburgh Penguins at $0.91M. The club is getting value on the ledger while losing games it should win away from home. You are right to ask why. The record prints the 17-4 and the 8-12 and leaves the cause to the coaches.

Nine straight losses. Nine. Is anybody at this club going to say something, or do we all just sit here and watch? I remember last year we were in the playoffs.

Delphine Okonkwo, Anaheim

The streak was seven when I wrote about this in late December. It is nine now. Mighty Ducks of Anaheim is 11-18-5 with 43 points, 93 goals for and 108 against. In the last 10 games the Ducks have scored 21 and allowed 33. They sit 10th in the Western Conference, 4 points behind the 8th seed at 47, with 40 games left. Jean-Sebastien Giguere95 has a 2.30 goals-against average and a .879 save percentage in 33 starts, a 95 from the Bureau's Book. Sergei Fedorov88 has 58 points in 42 games at a 113 pace. The goaltending and the leading scorer are not the problem.

Tie Domi72, is he helping this team or is he just a man who sits down a lot? I do not ask because I dislike him. I ask because we are losing and he is in the box more than anyone.

Delphine Okonkwo, Anaheim

Tie Domi has 126 penalty minutes in 42 games, the most in the league. You asked in November and the answer has not changed: the number is what the number is. Mighty Ducks of Anaheim has allowed 108 goals, and Domi's 126 minutes put his team short-handed for portions of every game he plays. Whether he earns those minutes back with something the box score does not show is a question about lines and matchups. What the record prints is 126.

We have played Toronto three times this season and not beaten them once. At what point is that a pattern and not bad luck? We beat everybody else.

Aaron Lindqvist, Yonkers

New York Rangers is 0-3 against Toronto Maple Leafs with 7 goals for and 15 against. Toronto is 29-8-2 with 68 points, 157 goals for and 90 against, the league's best by every column in the standings. The Maple Leafs are 16-3 on the road, which means the losses came in your building as well as theirs. You face them tomorrow at home. The fourth meeting tells you whether three games against the league's best team is a sample or a pattern you carry all season.

We pay more than anybody and I want to know what it bought. Ninety-three million dollars and we are second in the division.

Aaron Lindqvist, Yonkers

New York Rangers carries the largest payroll in the league at $92.95M. It buys 62 points in 45 games at $1.50M per point, and you are first in the Atlantic Division. The club turns $3.99M in profit, draws 14,626 a night and sells a seat at $82. Toronto Maple Leafs pays $89.94M for 68 points at $1.32M per point. St. Louis Blues pays $69.76M for 57 points at $1.22M. The division lead is real and the per-point rate is where the argument lives.
