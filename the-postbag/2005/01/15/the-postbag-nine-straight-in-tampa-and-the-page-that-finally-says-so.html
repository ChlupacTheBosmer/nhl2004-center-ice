The Postbag: nine straight in Tampa, and the page that finally says so

One reader asks what Tampa Bay has to do. The record has the answer, and six other things he printed while he had it open.

Tunde OyelaranLetters editor · The PostbagSaturday, 15 January 2005
ShareCopy link

The Postbag

The week has a story and nobody wrote it. Tampa Bay Lightning has won nine straight, and the letter about it only made it to this page after a reader noticed the page had not. So here it is, with the record open.

Isadora Fontaine of Brandon writes: "We have won 9 in a row and I have not read a word about it anywhere. What does this club have to do?"

What they have to do is outrun Toronto, and Toronto is 33-8-1 with 73 points. Your club is 25-11-3, 61 points in 43 games, 168 for and 141 against, and four of the wins have come in overtime. Over the last ten games it is 45 for and 26 against. That is a real streak, Isadora, and the man carrying it is Vincent Lecavalier84, 37 goals and 50 assists, 87 points in 43 games, with nobody up the road talking about him either.

The pattern in New York. Rosalind Fairweather of Hicksville writes: "We have played PIT 4 times and not beaten them once. At what point is that a pattern and not bad luck?"

At four. The record shows New York Islanders 0-3-1 against Pittsburgh Penguins, three of the meetings in regulation and the fourth beyond it. Pittsburgh is 12-21-5, 41 points in 44 games. You are 20-19-2, 46 points in 43 games. Four meetings, four losses, all of them to a club that is 12-21-5. That is a performance.

A man who sits down. Aaron Lindqvist of Yonkers writes: "Is Matthew Barnaby80 helping this team or is he just a man who sits down a lot?"

The record carries Matthew Barnaby through 44 games with 59 penalty minutes, the most in the league. That is all the record has on him, and I will not build a case from what it does not hold. But a man whose line is 44 games and the league lead in penalty minutes earns the suspicion he is not doing more.

In Ottawa, the words can wait. Genevieve Charbonneau of Nepean writes: "7 straight losses. Is anybody at this club going to say something, or do we all just watch?"

Nobody has said anything on the record, and I would not push them to. The record is Ottawa Senators 15-21-4, 40 points in 43 games, and the number that explains the silence is 165 goals against. Patrick Lalime86 is 13-19 in 35 starts at 3.68. A club that has given up 165 in 43 games has nothing that survives being said out loud. Seven straight losses is what is left to say.

The road, and the excuse. Ottilie Brandvold of Dearborn writes: "We are a different team on the road and nobody will say why. Is it the travel, or is it an excuse?"

The record is Detroit Red Wings 17-6 at home and 6-15 away, and that is the whole of it. The one building figure the Bureau publishes belongs to Edmonton, not to you, so the record holds the split and nothing else. Anyone naming a cause is guessing.

The building that costs. Callum Fotheringay of Edmonton writes: "Does the state of this building actually cost us anything on the ice, or is that something people say?"

The Bureau's Index carries Edmonton Oilers's building at 3.65 off every man's overall, the worst figure in the league. Edmonton is 19-14-3, 59 points in 45 games, third in the West, playing in the arena the Bureau taxes hardest. So yes, it costs you. They are paying it and winning anyway.
