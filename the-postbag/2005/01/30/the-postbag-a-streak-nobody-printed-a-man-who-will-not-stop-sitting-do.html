The Postbag: a streak nobody printed, a man who will not stop sitting down, and two clubs chasing the same ghost

Six letters, and the record answers every one

Sunday, 30 January 2005 · edited by Tunde Oyelaran

The Postbag is the page where a reader says the thing nobody at his club will say, and the answer comes back with the record open. Six letters: a streak in Atlanta, a slide in Columbus, two clubs chasing the same ghost, a man who will not stop sitting down, and a building that costs its own team more than any other building in the league.

We have won 5 in a row and I have not read a word about it anywhere. What does this club have to do?

Royce Vandermeer, Atlanta

The Atlanta Thrashers are on a 5-game win streak, tied with Carolina Hurricanes for the longest active run in the Southeast. The streak is real, and you are right that this page has not printed it. Consider it printed. Atlanta is 17-25-2 through 50 games with 48 points. Six of those 17 wins came in overtime. They have scored 184 and allowed 197, a gap of 13 goals. They sit 7 points behind New York Islanders for the 8th seed in the East. Five wins is a good week. It is not enough to close 7 points on its own.

4 straight losses. Is anybody at this club going to say something, or do we all just watch?

Marguerite Oyelowo, Columbus

Columbus Blue Jackets is 17-24-3 with 51 points in 51 games and 160 against. They hold the 8th seed in the West, 2 points ahead of Phoenix Coyotes and San Jose Sharks at 49. In their last 10, they are 3-5-2 with 19 scored and 24 allowed. A 4-game skid in November is noise. In late January, with 31 games left and two clubs 2 points behind you, it is arithmetic. You want somebody to say something. I cannot make them. What I can tell you is where the record puts you tonight, and it puts you 2 points from the edge.

We have played OTT 4 times and not beaten them once. At what point is that a pattern and not bad luck?

Philippe Deslauriers, Verdun

Montreal Canadiens is 0-4 against Ottawa Senators across 4 meetings. In 13 division games, Montreal is 1-8-1 with 3 overtime wins, and their road record is 6-20. Stefaano Bullard told The Tunnel's Marcus Bell this week that the club's travel budget is "zero. Not low. Zero," and that it costs the team "minus six on every attribute" on the road. One series against one club is not proof of that. But a team going 6-20 on the road who cannot beat one opponent four times is past bad luck.

Matthew Barnaby80, is he helping this team, or is he just a man who sits down a lot?

Aaron Lindqvist, Yonkers

Matthew Barnaby carries 79 penalty minutes in 50 games for the New York Rangers, the most in the league. The Rangers are 22-21-5, 61 points through 54 games, 6th in the East. You ask if he is helping. The record gives you the minutes he spent sitting and the standings. What it does not give you is what happened in the minutes he played, and that is the only part of your question that carries an answer.

We have played PIT 5 times and not beaten them once. At what point is that a pattern and not bad luck?

Rosalind Fairweather, Hicksville

New York Islanders is 0-5 against Pittsburgh Penguins across 5 meetings. In their 12 division games, the Islanders are 4-5-2 with 1 overtime win. Pittsburgh is 7-2-0 in 11 division games with 2 overtime wins. The Islanders hold the 8th seed in the East at 55 points; Pittsburgh sits 13th at 51. Five meetings went every way Pittsburgh wanted and none the Islanders wanted. That is a pattern. The season is not finished.

Does the state of this building actually cost us anything on the ice, or is that something people say?

Callum Fotheringay, Edmonton

The Edmonton Oilers carry a minus 3.65 on every player's overall grade from their facilities. That is the worst in the league. Edmonton is 22-14-5, 67 points through 50 games, 4th in the West. The number applies to every man on the roster, every night the club plays in its own building. Whether 3.65 per man translates into standings points is a question the number raises and the league cannot answer. No team plays twice in a better building and reports the difference.
