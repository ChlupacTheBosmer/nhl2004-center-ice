Letters to the editor

The Postbag: 119 minutes, no goals, and two clubs that cannot beat the same opponent

Six letters on the week's patterns, including a man the Kings dress who has 2 points and the league's worst disciplinary record.

Monday, 7 March 2005 · edited by Tunde Oyelaran

Six letters this week. A heavy, a payroll, a skid, a 4th-liner nobody can justify, two 0-5 series, and a four-game run the league walked past again.

We are a different team on the road and nobody will say why. Is it the travel, or is it an excuse?

Ottilie Brandvold, Dearborn

Detroit Red Wings is 23-12 at home and 12-19 away. Eleven more wins on one side of the schedule than the other, across only 4 more home games. You can point at the travel, or you can point at the 6 men on the injured list, more than any other club, including Tomas Holmstrom68 who is out until 2005-05-03. When a roster is that thin, the road is where it shows.

We pay more than anybody and I would like somebody to tell me what for.

Ottilie Brandvold, Dearborn

$80.89M for 75 points. The cost per point is $1.08M. Vancouver Canucks spends $35.16M and earns 90 points, $0.39M each. Detroit sits 7th in the West with 16 games left, but the gap down to Phoenix Coyotes is only 6 points, and Phoenix Coyotes has 2 fewer games played. The seat costs $81.

4 straight losses. Is anybody at this club going to say something, or do we all just watch?

Verna Kaltenbrunner, Kirkwood

St. Louis Blues is 20-31-5, 63 points in 65 games, 236 against. The last 10 reads LLLLWWWOTLWW from newest backward, so the four losses are right now. The club sits 6 points behind Phoenix Coyotes in the West standings with 17 games left. Nobody says anything because the arithmetic says it is already over.

Ian Laperriere75 -- is he helping this team, or is he just a man who sits down a lot?

Marcus Delacroix, Torrance

119 penalty minutes in 66 games. Most in the league. Ian Laperriere has 1 goal, 1 assist, 2 points, and 686 minutes played, 10.4 a night. He earns $0.90M. His toughness reads 78 and his aggression 78 on the Book. Last season: 52 penalty minutes, 3 points, 68 games. The penalty total doubled. The point total halved. His own teammate Sean Avery83 is second in the league at 118, up from 101 the last time I answered a letter about him. Two players on the same roster, one for being the most penalized, one for being the second-most-penalized. The 4th line is a 4th line, and the question you asked is whether the 119 minutes buy anything the 2 points do not. The record cannot answer that.

We have won 4 in a row and I have not read a word about it anywhere. What does this club have to do?

Callum Fotheringay, Edmonton

Edmonton Oilers is on W4. The club has 12 overtime wins, the most in the league, and 86 points in 66 games. Goal differential is negative 1: 225 for, 226 against. The 4 wins matter because they came after the LLL in the last 10, and the team is still 5th in the West, 17 points clear of Phoenix Coyotes. Paul Farkas told The Tunnel's Marcus Bell on 2005-02-26, during that losing stretch, "We're in a playoff spot. I'm not going to sit here and pretend four losses feels good, because it doesn't." The league did not print it because a -1 differential and 12 overtime wins do not make a headline. They make a coin-flip club.

We have played PIT 5 times and not beaten them once. At what point is that a pattern and not bad luck?

Rosalind Fairweather, Hicksville

Five meetings, five defeats, 10 goals for against 21. The scores: 1-5 at Pittsburgh Penguins, 6-7 in overtime at Pittsburgh, 1-4 at Pittsburgh, 2-3 at home, 0-2 at home. Only the 6-7 game was within one goal in regulation. Three were decided by 3 or more. You have been outscored 11-2 in the four regulation losses. That is a pattern.

We have played EDM 5 times and not beaten them once. At what point is that a pattern and not bad luck?

Solveig Aakerlund, Bloomington

Minnesota Wild is 0-3-2 against Edmonton Oilers across five meetings, 15 scored, 20 allowed. Every single one of those five games was decided by a single goal. That is not the same problem New York has. Minnesota has been inside every one of these and cannot close. Five one-goal losses against one club is a pattern, but it is the pattern of a team that gets to the third period and has no answer, not a team that gets shelled.
