The Postbag: six straight down, five one-goal losses, and $1.00M a point in Dearborn

A losing streak, a season series that went one way twice over, a man in the box, and the league's most expensive point

Monday, 14 March 2005 · edited by Tunde Oyelaran

Five readers wrote this week about patterns they can feel from their seats and the page has not named: a club losing six in a row, a man with the most penalty minutes in the league, two season series that have gone one direction, and the most expensive point on the market.

Six straight losses. Is anybody at this club going to say something, or do we all just watch?

Marcus Delacroix, Torrance

Marcus, Los Angeles Kings sits at 21-32-9, 67 points in 70 games, 236 goals against. The streak has put them 7 points behind Phoenix Coyotes for the last playoff spot in the West, and they have 12 games left against PHX's 13. They are mathematically alive. Their ceiling is 91 points if they win every remaining game. What the last 10 say about that ceiling: 25 for, 28 against, three wins, seven losses counting overtime, and the most recent result an overtime loss. The record can't answer whether anyone at the club is talking, but the arithmetic is not yet finished.

Sean Avery71 is he helping this team, or is he just a man who sits down a lot?

Marcus Delacroix, Torrance

Marcus, I printed this man at 101 penalty minutes on 21 February. He is at 134 now. That is 33 more in three weeks, the most in the league, and he has played every game. Seventy for a club that has won 21. The record does not have a line for helping. It has penalty minutes, and 134 is the number.

We have played EDM 5 times and not beaten them once. At what point is that a pattern and not bad luck?

Solveig Aakerlund, Bloomington

Solveig, I pulled the goals. Minnesota Wild scored 15 across those five games against Edmonton Oilers and gave up 20. Three regulation losses, two in overtime. Every single one of the five was decided by a single goal. The season series table carries all five as one-goal games. Your club went three a night and the one that mattered on the board was always theirs. The series is over. No sixth game will be played, and whatever the answer to your question is, the record won't produce new evidence.

We pay more than anybody and I would like somebody to tell me what for.

Ottilie Brandvold, Dearborn

Ottilie, the payroll is $80.89M, the largest in the league. Detroit has 81 points in 70 games. That is $1.00M per point. Here is what the rest of the league pays for a point. Vancouver Canucks spends $35.16M for 92 points, $0.38M each. Edmonton Oilers spends $36.81M for 90 points, $0.41M each. Toronto Maple Leafs spends $60.04M for 114 points, $0.53M each. Minnesota Wild spends $37.91M for 61 points, $0.62M each. Detroit's cost per point is the highest in this league. The team sits 7th in the West with 81 points and the ticket is $81. What the money bought beyond the men on the ice, the record won't say. What it does say: 81 points on the biggest bill in the league.

We have played PIT 5 times and not beaten them once. At what point is that a pattern and not bad luck?

Rosalind Fairweather, Hicksville

Rosalind, New York Islanders is 0-4-1 against Pittsburgh Penguins across five meetings. Four regulation losses, one in overtime, no wins. One club in the Pacific went 2-0 against them this season: Los Angeles Kings won 5-4 at home on 14 December and 3-2 on the road on 25 February. New York Islanders sits 9th in the East at 70 points, 2 behind Atlanta Thrashers for the eighth spot; Pittsburgh Penguins is 11th at 70 as well. Neither is in. Five chances produced nothing, and the closest any of them came was an overtime loss.
