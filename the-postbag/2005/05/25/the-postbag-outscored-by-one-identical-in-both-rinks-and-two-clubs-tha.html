Letters to the editor

The Postbag: outscored by one, identical in both rinks, and two clubs that never beat the same opponent

Five letters from clubs that finished in April. The record has been sitting here with the answers while everyone watched the Cup Final.

Wednesday, 25 May 2005 · edited by Tunde Oyelaran

Tomorrow Dallas Stars visits Toronto for Game 4 of the Cup Final, up 2 games to 1. The letters below arrived from clubs whose seasons ended in April. The record has the answers.

We have won 4 in a row and I have not read a word about it anywhere. What does this club have to do?

Rafael Villalobos, Tempe

Phoenix Coyotes closed the regular season 40-31-9, 93 points, the 8th seed in the West. Your four-game run was real, but it landed after the seeding locked. The number nobody printed is the goal differential: 241 scored, 242 allowed. A club outscored by one goal across 82 games drew Colorado Avalanche in the first round. Colorado Avalanche had 112 points, 310 goals for, and 246 against. Phoenix Coyotes won that series 4 games to 3, then pushed Dallas Stars to six games in the second round. The Coyotes never had a positive goal differential all season and beat the top seed in the conference anyway.

11 straight losses. Is anybody at this club going to say something, or do we all just watch?

Yolanda Espinosa, Sunrise

The longest losing streak in the league: 11 straight to close the season at 25-36-16, 76 points. The number worth putting in front of your club is the split. Florida Panthers went 15-26 at home and 15-26 on the road. Identical. The 11-game skid ran the full length of a club that could not win at any rink. They scored 236 and allowed 255 across 82 games. The season was already gone before the streak started.

We are a different team on the road and nobody will say why. Is it the travel, or is it an excuse?

Kiyoshi Yamagata, Burnaby

The record cannot answer the why. It can show the size of the gap. Vancouver Canucks went 28-13 at home and 18-23 on the road. A 10-game swing in wins between the two halves of the schedule. The home record was the 2nd-best in the West, behind only Dallas Stars's 30-11. The road record sat alongside Detroit Red Wings and Minnesota Wild, three clubs with fewer points. Vancouver finished with 99 points and the 4th seed, then lost to Edmonton Oilers in Round 1, 4 games to 2. Burnaby sits on the far edge of the Pacific, three time zones from every Eastern division rink. The sheet records results, not causes. But 28-13 against 18-23 is not a small gap.

We have played TOR 5 times and not beaten them once. At what point is that a pattern and not bad luck?

Halina Sobczyk, Cheektowaga

Five games, five regulation losses, no overtime. Buffalo Sabres went 0-5-0 against Toronto Maple Leafs this season. The season series carries no per-game goals for this matchup, but the division record puts it in context. BUF finished 4 regulation wins and 2 overtime wins against 11 regulation losses and 3 overtime losses in 20 Northeast games: 6 wins, 14 losses, 63 for, 92 against. Five losses to the division leader were the deepest cut, but the whole division table says the same thing. Six wins in 20 games is not a club that beats anyone, and the 0-5 against Toronto Maple Leafs is the pattern the rest of the division was already showing.

We have played PIT 5 times and not beaten them once. At what point is that a pattern and not bad luck?

Rosalind Fairweather, Hicksville

New York Islanders went 0-4-1 against Pittsburgh Penguins: 4 regulation losses, 1 in overtime, 5 meetings, 0 wins. The shape matches Halina's question from the night before, and the answer is the same. From Pittsburgh Penguins's side, the Penguins went 4-0-1 against the Islanders over the same 5 games. PIT's division record was 18 wins and 8 losses across 26 games, 78 goals for, 60 against, the best in the Atlantic. New York Islanders finished 84 points, the 8th seed in the East, and lost to Toronto Maple Leafs in Round 1. Five games against one opponent is a sample. Eighteen wins in the division against 60 goals allowed is a club that could have beaten anyone, and Pittsburgh Penguins was that club.
