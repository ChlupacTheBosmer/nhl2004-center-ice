Letters to the editor

The Postbag: six letters from clubs that went home, and the final table that answers them

The regular season finished a month ago. The readers have not.

Wednesday, 11 May 2005 · edited by Tunde Oyelaran

The regular season ended a month ago and the record has had time to settle. Every letter in this edition comes from a reader whose club is out, and every question in them is one I have printed in some form since March. I said I would answer them with the final table. Two of these correspondents are wrong.

We pay more than anybody and I would like somebody to tell me what for. I sat through four playoff games and we never scored more than two goals in any of them. First round again, and the seat is not getting cheaper.

Ottilie Brandvold, Dearborn

Detroit Red Wings spent $80.89M on this roster, the largest payroll in the league. The only club near it, New York Rangers at $78.14M, won 84 points. Detroit won 96 in 82 games, which is a good season on its own, and then drew Dallas Stars and lost four straight: 4-1, 3-2 in overtime, 1-2, 1-3. Five goals in four nights.

Five clubs that spent under $46.00M finished above the Wings. New Jersey Devils spent $45.15M for 114 points, Vancouver Canucks $35.16M for 99, Edmonton Oilers $36.81M for 98, Boston Bruins $43.97M for 97, and Mighty Ducks of Anaheim $41.01M for 97. Your seat at $81 is the second-priciest in the league; only Toronto's $105 is higher. Spending more is not the problem by itself. Toronto spent $60.04M and won 133 points. Detroit spent $80.89M and went out in the first round.

Eleven straight losses to finish, and nobody at this club will stand in front of a camera and say the word. Our man gave an interview last week and talked about the gate, the travel budget and the rooms. I would like one of them to talk about the hockey.

Yolanda Espinosa, Sunrise

Florida Panthers went 25-36-16 for 76 points, gave up 255 goals, and closed the year on the 11 straight losses the streak column carries. The arithmetic had the Panthers out before the streak ended: the eighth line in the East came to 84 points and Florida's ceiling was 76. Inside the division they went 0-3-2 against Washington Capitals and 0-3-1 against Tampa Bay Lightning, with 54 goals for and 70 against in 20 games. A club that cannot beat the two best teams in its own division and cannot beat anybody in April is not unlucky. It is 76 points.

Boris Van Ryswyk told The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk on 2005-05-04, "I'm not going to stand here and tell you I sleep great about losing 11 in a row. I don't. But I'm not the coach." He is right that he does not dress a line on Tuesday. He also said the roster money comes before the room money, and that order has kept the gate at 14,971 a night and the payroll at $51.07M, the 12th-largest in the league. I have no number that says the rooms caused 11 losses. Neither does he.

We have played Toronto five times and not beaten them once. At what point does that become a pattern and stop being bad luck?

Halina Sobczyk, Cheektowaga

Five meetings, five losses, no overtime point, nothing. Buffalo Sabres went 4-11-3 inside the Northeast with 63 goals for and 92 against in 20 games, and 0-5-0 against the Toronto Maple Leafs Maple Leafs inside that. The record holds no game before October 2004, so those five are the whole of your club's history against Toronto. In the same division, over the same 20 games, Toronto scored 90 and gave up 46. That is a 49-point gap in the standings, not a bounce.

We have played Pittsburgh five times and not beaten them once. Same question I sent you about the other club: when does that stop being bad luck?

Rosalind Fairweather, Hicksville

Five meetings, 0-4-1. Pittsburgh Penguins lists it from the other side as 4-0-0 with one overtime win. What I would tell you is that the club you could not beat is not the club that put you out. New York Islanders met Toronto in the first round, won Game 4 at home 3-1, and lost the series in five. Pittsburgh then took Toronto to seven games and lost the last one at Toronto, 3-2, on 2005-05-04. Two desks, home-ice-network and penalty-box, called Pittsburgh to win that night and both settled wrong. This page took Toronto. The page's ledger on settled calls this year is 2 and 3, so I am not throwing stones, but there it is.

Does the state of this building actually cost us anything on the ice, or is that something people say?

Rosalind Fairweather, Hicksville

It is something the Bureau prints. The Islanders carry -3.64 on every man's overall from their facilities, the worst figure in the league, and I have printed it before and it has not moved. What it bought was 84 points, tied with three other clubs at the eighth line in the East, and a first-round exit.

What it did not stop is Rick DiPietro89. The first pick of the 2000 draft went 32-28 in 63 games with a 2.77 goals against and a .871 save percentage, and the Development Index has him up four on his base, at 84. A club can carry the worst room in the league and still get a goaltender playing above his card. The -3.64 does its work on the other nineteen men.

We won four in a row and I did not read a word about it anywhere. What does this club have to do to get printed?

Rafael Villalobos, Tempe

Phoenix Coyotes won 40 games and finished with 93 points, 241 goals for and 242 against. Forty wins is the third-best total in the West, behind Colorado's 43 and the 42s in Dallas and Nashville, and it still finished eighth and out, four points under the line. The reason is in the overtime column: 2 overtime wins all season, the fewest in the conference and the fewest in the league. The Coyotes won inside 60 minutes and had nothing to fall back on, while the clubs around them banked 8, 9 and 10 extra points after it. Then they took Colorado to seven games and won it, and lost to Dallas in six.
