The Postbag: what −3.64 a man, eleven losses, and $0.84M a point have in common

Six readers from six clubs that finished their seasons weeks ago, and the record that has been answering them since October

Tuesday, 17 May 2005 · edited by Tunde Oyelaran

Six letters this week, six clubs that finished their seasons over six weeks ago and are still asking what cost them. The standings have been printing answers since the regular season ended on 2005-04-06.

Eleven straight losses. Is anybody at this club going to say something, or do we all just watch?

Yolanda Espinosa, Sunrise

Florida Panthers finished 25-36-16 with 76 points in 82 games and 255 against, 14th in the East. Atlanta Thrashers took the eighth seed with 84. The losing streak reached eleven and never broke.

Boris Van Ryswyk, who dresses the roster and the rooms, told The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk on 2005-05-04: "I'm not going to stand here and tell you I sleep great about losing 11 in a row. I don't." He was talking about the rooms that sit behind those losses: the practice rooms, the workout room, the equipment room. "I know where they sit. And I'm not going to pretend I haven't looked at them."

Florida Panthers made $13.64M in profit, drew 14,971 a night at $79 a seat, and carries a payroll of $51.07M. Van Ryswyk says the player money comes first because it keeps the gate up, and the gate feeds the rooms. Somebody is saying something, Yolanda. He is just not saying what you want to hear.

We pay more than anybody and I would like somebody to tell me what for.

Ottilie Brandvold, Dearborn

Detroit Red Wings carries the league's largest payroll at $80.89M and finished with 96 points, seventh in the West. That is $0.84M per point, behind only New York Rangers at $0.93M. The seat costs $81.

Detroit Red Wings was swept 4-0 by Dallas Stars in Round 1. Dallas spends $67.99M, finished with 109 points, and pays $0.62M per point. You paid more per point than the club that swept you, and you lost every game in the series.

Does the state of this building actually cost us anything on the ice, or is that something people say?

Rosalind Fairweather, Hicksville

It costs you. The Bureau's buildings table has New York Islanders at −3.64 on every man's overall from its facilities, the worst number in the league. Every player in that sweater reads 3.64 below where his base would sit in a different building.

New York Islanders went 30-38-4 with 84 points, earned the eighth seed in the East, and Toronto Maple Leafs beat them 4-1 in the first round. Payroll $52.47M, profit $12.46M, attendance 12,943 on $79 seats. The attendance is the lowest in the Atlantic.

We are a different team on the road and nobody will say why. Is it the travel, or is it an excuse?

Kiyoshi Yamagata, Burnaby

Vancouver Canucks went 28-13 at home and 18-23 on the road. Same roster, a 15-game swing. The lowest payroll in the league at $35.16M, and the best per-point at $0.36M. You do a great job spending little.

The sheet notes the travel room on the buildings table. You are not making it up. Vancouver Canucks finished 38-29-7 with 99 points, fourth in the West, and lost the first round to Edmonton Oilers 4-2. You won Game 3 at Edmonton Oilers 5-4 in overtime and Game 5 at home 5-4 in overtime. Both clubs were trading in both buildings. The road drag is real, but you also gave up the first two at home to start the series.

Sean Avery77 — is he helping this team, or is he just a man who sits down a lot?

Marcus Delacroix, Torrance

Sean Avery took 150 penalty minutes in 82 games this season, the most in the league.

Los Angeles Kings finished 25-37-11 with 79 points, 11th in the West, payroll $52.43M. The club scored 251 and gave up 273. Three men are on the injured list past today: Adam Deadmarsh87 (knee, back 2005-06-04), Lubomir Visnovsky80 (concussion, back 2005-05-21), Ian Laperriere74 (knee, back 2005-05-18). Avery is not hurt. What that is worth to a team that outscored nobody, you can decide.

We have played TOR 5 times and not beaten them once. At what point is that a pattern and not bad luck?

Halina Sobczyk, Cheektowaga

Five division meetings against Toronto Maple Leafs, five regulation losses, no overtime. Buffalo Sabres went 0-5 across the whole year against one club.

Buffalo Sabres finished 27-34-12 with 84 points, the same total as New York Islanders, which took the eighth seed. Buffalo Sabres has 27 wins. New York Islanders has 30. That gap is why you watched the playoffs from home. Payroll $44.16M, attendance 16,080 at $76 a seat, profit $24.30M. The building fills and the money works. The club cannot win a single game against one opponent.
