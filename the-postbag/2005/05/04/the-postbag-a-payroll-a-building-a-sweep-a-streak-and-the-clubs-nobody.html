Letters to the editor

The Postbag: a payroll, a building, a sweep, a streak, and the clubs nobody writes about anymore

Six readers from clubs the bracket already left behind, and the record open in front of me.

Wednesday, 4 May 2005 · edited by Tunde Oyelaran

Six letters from six clubs the playoff bracket doesn't mention anymore. A man who sits down, a payroll that bought a first-round exit, a division sweep, a road team, an eleven-game collapse, and the worst building in the league.

I keep watching Sean and I can't tell. He's out there for twenty minutes, he puts somebody down, the ref sends him off, and then we're shorthanded. Is he helping this club, or is he just a man who sits down a lot?

Marcus Delacroix, Torrance

Sean Avery69 carries 150 penalty minutes in 82 games, the most in the league. He appears on no scoring line the Bureau publishes. Los Angeles Kings finished 25-37-11 with 79 points, 11th in the West, mathematically out before April. He leads the league in minutes spent sitting, on a club that leads the conference in nothing. The record can describe that pattern. It can't tell you why he played every night.

We pay more than anybody in this league. I would like somebody to tell me what for.

Ottilie Brandvold, Dearborn

Detroit Red Wings carries $80.89M in payroll, the largest in the league. It produced 96 points, which works out to $0.84M per point. The New York Rangers pays $78.14M for 84 points, $0.93M a point, the worst exchange rate the league saw. So Dearborn pays the most in absolute dollars but not the most per point of product. That is small consolation to a woman who watched her club lose 4-0 to Dallas Stars in the first round, 1-2, 2-3 in overtime, 1-2, 1-3. The Wings scored 5 goals in 4 games. Marty Turco97 stopped the rest. The $80.89M bought a first-round exit.

We have played Toronto five times and not beaten them once. At what point is that a pattern and not bad luck?

Halina Sobczyk, Cheektowaga

Buffalo Sabres went 0-5-0 against Toronto Maple Leafs this season. Toronto Maple Leafs went 59-13-5, 133 points, the most in the league. In division play, Toronto Maple Leafs outscored the Northeast 90-46. You used the word pattern; I'll use the word arithmetic. A 27-win club met a 59-win club five times and lost five times. Buffalo Sabres finished 11th in the East at 84 points, 13 games back of the last playoff spot.

We are a different team on the road and nobody will say why. Is it the travel, or is it an excuse?

Kiyoshi Yamagata, Burnaby

Vancouver Canucks went 28-13 at home and 18-23 on the road, a swing of 10 games. The travel is real; every road trip from Burnaby costs a night in someone else's time zone. But the sheet adds something to that answer. Vancouver Canucks carries $35.16M in payroll, the smallest in the league, and produced 99 points, $0.36M per point, the best exchange rate the league saw. The roster is thin because the payroll was thin. Vancouver Canucks lost 4-2 to Edmonton Oilers in the first round, three of those four losses on the road. It's the travel, and the travel is the cost of a building at the edge of the map, carried by the cheapest roster in the league.

Eleven straight losses. Is anybody at this club going to say something, or do we all just watch?

Yolanda Espinosa, Sunrise

Florida Panthers lost 11 in a row to close the season, finishing 25-36-16 with 76 points. The 16 overtime losses are the league's highest, which means 16 games came down to one goal and Florida Panthers was on the wrong side of every single one. At home they went 15-26. Away they also went 15-26. The Development Index has one man on that roster trending up: Vaclav Nedorost80 at +6 on the form term, overall 78. The payroll is $51.07M at $0.67M per point. The streak was the last 11 games of a season that was already finished. The club ended 14th in the East, mathematically out.

Does the state of this building actually cost us anything on the ice, or is that something people say?

Rosalind Fairweather, Hicksville

The Bureau's facilities reading gives New York Islanders a penalty of −3.64 on every man's overall grade, the worst in the league. That means every New York Islanders player walks into his own building and plays as if the Book had cut his grade by 3.64. The Islanders made the playoffs as the 8th seed and Toronto Maple Leafs beat them 4-1 in the first round. Rick DiPietro86 is the one man on that roster the Index has climbing, +4 on the form term, overall 84. The cost is visible in the standings: 84 points, a first-round exit, and 12,943 seats filled at $79 a night that a better building might have filled at more.
