The Postbag: the men who hung up the skates, and the payroll that stayed still

Two games in, the mail is still about the summer: 38 retirements, the largest payroll in the league, a building that costs every man on the roster, and a road team that does not exist yet.

Thursday, 13 October 2005 · edited by Tunde Oyelaran

The league's own record of the offseason runs to 96 arrivals, 78 departures and 38 retirements, and two games into the season the bag is still full of it. Here is the summer in six of the letters it produced.

I have backed this team through everything, and what I cannot stand is the excuses. We are a different team on the road, plain as that, and nobody in the building will tell you why. Travel, they say. Fine, everybody travels. Which is it, and why won't anybody say it out loud?

Halina Sobczyk, Cheektowaga

Buffalo has played one game. The standings have them 1-0 at home and 0-0 on the road, which means there is no away team yet, good or bad, to argue about. The road part of this season starts tonight, at Chicago Blackhawks. The one game that has been played was a 5-3 win with Daniel Briere80 and Joel Bouchard77 a goal and two assists apiece and Owen Nolan86 scoring twice.

What the summer bought, in dollars: the Bureau's business page has the payroll up $2.51M, to $57.39M, and the arrivals list is one 20-year-old goaltender rated 79 out of the draft and a 20-year-old left wing at 66. On the travel question, one coach in your division said it out loud this week. "Travel is next," Montreal's Stefaan Bullard told The Tunnel's Marcus Bell. "Right now we're taking that penalty every road trip."

We pay the most money in this league. I sit in that seat, I pay for that seat, and I would like one person with a clipboard to tell me what it is for. Not next April. Now.

Aaron Lindqvist, Yonkers

The clipboard says $92.95M, the largest payroll in the league, after adding $4.78M this summer. The return so far is 4 points in 2 games, which the business page prices at $23.24M a point. The seat that bothers you costs $82, 13,831 of them came per game last time out for the money side of the ledger, and the club cleared $21.41M in profit, so the club is not the one losing money here.

Two games is two games, and one of them had Alexei Kovalev86 with 3 points and Pavel Bure84 and Petr Nedved84 two goals each. The biggest move of the summer was a trade: Jamie Lundmark81 went to Toronto for a 31-year-old defenseman. Toronto's payroll came down $5.50M over the same summer, so the gap closed some. Toronto Maple Leafs plays at the Garden on 2005-10-15 and you can ask him then.

People back home talk about our building like it is a member of the roster. Does the state of that place actually cost us something on the ice, or is that a thing supporters say to feel better?

Delphine Okonkwo, Anaheim

It costs, and it is measured. The Bureau's facilities index puts Mighty Ducks of Anaheim at -3.65 on every man's overall, the worst number on any club in the league, which is a worse answer than the one you were hoping for.

Behind that number: the payroll went up $2.81M to $52.84M, the best defenseman left for Nashville, and the replacements on the blue line are a 20-year-old rated 69 and a 19-year-old at 59 out of the draft. The ice so far says 1-1-0 with 4 goals for and 4 against, carried by Erik Cole77, Vaclav Prospal77 and Niclas Havelid75 at 3 points each. Detroit Red Wings is in the building tonight at 0-1-0, which is about the kindest matchup a bad building could ask for this week.

He was 40 years old and we still felt safe. Then he stopped, just like that, and now we have lost two and I am sitting in an empty house wondering whether a retirement can cost you a season this early.

Greta Lindgren, Ottawa

It can show up this early. Top of the retirement list sits Ed Belfour: 40 years old, 91 overall, the highest-rated man to leave the pool this summer, and he left it at Ottawa. The record gives the Senators two seasons of him and then nothing, and it gives them 2 goals for and 7 against in 2 games, a minus-5 that is the worst in the East.

The business page shows Ottawa's payroll at $58.72M before the summer and $58.72M after, no change, with one arrival, a 29-year-old defenseman rated 73 from Calgary. Whatever Belfour cost, the money did not go looking for him. A second 40-year-old goaltender came out the same summer, Dominik Hasek at 84 in Detroit, and that club at least raised its payroll $3.91M while saying goodbye.

Everything old is gone and the payroll went down and I am not sure I have been talked out of liking it. Then overtime happens. Tell me the summer was worth it.

Etienne Charbonneau, Montreal

The cut is real: $54.89M, down $2.15M, and outside Toronto, whose payroll fell $5.50M, Montreal is the only club in the league that spent less money this summer than last. What came of it, on the record so far, is 0-0-1 and 2 goals for and 3 against, and this from their coach after the game: "I'd rather be 0-0-1 with a point than 0-1-0 with none. But you don't sit here and smile about a loss," Bullard told The Tunnel's Marcus Bell.

The rebuilt part has one name on it that the Bureau will back you up on. The Development Index has Mike Komisarek81 at +10, one of the biggest form jumps published this edition, a 23-year-old defenseman reading 82 overall. One game, no points, and a 23-year-old on the Index is not yet a season. Philadelphia Flyers, at 1-0-0, is in the building on 2005-10-15.

The number that used to mean something on the first line does not get mentioned anymore, and the kid in the middle of the ice wears a look like he knows he is supposed to replace it. Tell me something about these young men besides that we miss the old one.

Marcus Whitfield, Coral Springs

The old one went out the top of the list: Joe Nieuwendyk, 39 years old, 81 overall, retired. What the replacement chart has produced so far is 0-0-1, 3 goals for and 4 against, and a business page reading of $57.33M a point, which is the most expensive point in the league.

The young men, plainly: the Development Index has Vaclav Nedorost83 at +12, the largest jump this edition publishes, his overall up to 83 at 23 years old, and he has done it across one game in which he did not score. Stephen Weiss75 has 2 points at 22. The payroll rose $3.44M to $57.33M and 15,207 people came at $81 a seat to watch all of this begin, and the first real test of whether the +12 is a player or a rumor comes at Minnesota Wild on 2005-10-15.

What changed: cut the run-up "You want an answer and you deserve one, so here it is"; removed decorative bold on $92.95M and -3.65; folded the restating closer in the Anaheim reply ("That is the answer to your question...") into the sentence above it; dropped "Here is the part that will interest you more than any excuse" and led with the payroll figure instead; softened "the summer's real statement was a trade" to "the biggest move"; turned "The young men, since you asked for them plainly." from a fragment into a lead-in. Every figure, name, token, quote and date is untouched, and the letters are verbatim.

Still there on purpose: the records lists in the third paragraph of a few replies run three names deep, but those are box-score facts, not rhetorical triads, so they stay.
