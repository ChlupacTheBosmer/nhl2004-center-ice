The Postbag: zero road games, $23.24M a point, and the tax on every sweater

Three letters. Two ask questions the season has not reached. One asks about a number that has been sitting in the Bureau's index since summer.

Thursday, 13 October 2005 · edited by Tunde Oyelaran

Three letters this week, and the record answers them unevenly. Two of them ask questions the season has not reached yet. The third asks about a number the Bureau has been publishing since summer, and the answer there is clean. I print them in the order they arrived.

We are a different team on the road and nobody will say why. Is it the travel, or is it an excuse?

Halina Sobczyk, Cheektowaga

Buffalo Sabres has played 1 game this season. It was a 5-3 win at home. The standings read 1-0 at home and 0-0 away. You are describing a split that has not started. The road column will carry its first entry after tonight's game at Chicago Blackhawks, and until then the record has nothing to give you.

Daniel Briere80 had 3 points in that game. Owen Nolan86 scored 2 goals. The payroll sits at $57.39M. What you felt in that building is real, and the travel penalty is a figure the Bureau measures for every club. But the away sample is empty. The question you are asking lives on the road, and the road has not arrived yet.

We pay more than anybody and I would like somebody to tell me what for.

Aaron Lindqvist, Yonkers

The New York Rangers payroll is $92.95M, the largest in the league. After 2 games, the Contract Ledger puts the cost at $23.24M per point on 4 points collected. That number moves every night, and right now it does not put you first in the cost column either: St. Louis Blues has paid $34.88M per point on a $69.76M payroll, and San Jose Sharks sits at $33.12M per point on $66.24M. Per point depends on points collected as much as payroll spent. You are winning your games, and that matters to the denominator.

Six goals for, 4 against, 2nd in the Eastern conference table. Alexei Kovalev90 leads the club with 3 points. Pavel Bure88 and Petr Nedved88 each have 2 goals. Revenue came in at $31.23M, attendance averaged 13,831 per game, the seat costs $82, and the bottom line shows $21.41M in profit. You asked what the money bought. Two wins, a positive goal differential, and the league's largest profit. The payroll is a decision about the product on the ice. The ticket is a different decision. The profit is where those choices meet, and right now both point the same direction.

Does the state of this building actually cost us anything on the ice, or is that something people say?

Delphine Okonkwo, Anaheim

The Bureau's index puts the Mighty Ducks of Anaheim facility penalty at −3.65 on every man's overall, the worst in the league. It is a published figure, revised every edition, and it sits under the grade on every Mighty Ducks of Anaheim player's record tonight. The payroll carries $52.84M, and the per-point cost on 2 points in 2 games reads $26.42M.

Erik Cole77 carries a 79 overall. Vaclav Prospal77 is at 79. Niclas Havelid77 is at 77.

Mighty Ducks of Anaheim has not played a home game yet. The standings show 0-0 at home and 1-1 on the road: a win at Dallas Stars and a loss at San Jose Sharks. The first look at what the building does, or fails to do, for your club arrives tonight against Detroit Red Wings.

Stefaan Bullard of Montreal Canadiens told The Tunnel's Marcus Bell this week about building his rooms first: "I built that room first because I needed it first." He manages a different club, but the arithmetic he describes is the same one sitting under every shift an Anaheim player takes. It moves the baseline every man starts from, and −3.65 is the largest tax the Bureau has on the books.
