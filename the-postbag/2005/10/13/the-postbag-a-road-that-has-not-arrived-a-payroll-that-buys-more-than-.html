The Postbag: a road that has not arrived, a payroll that buys more than people think, and the number in the walls

Three letters about what a club buys, and one man in Montreal who has been counting the cost.

Thursday, 13 October 2005 · edited by Tunde Oyelaran

This edition is about what a club buys and what it does not. A Sabres supporter asks about the road, a Rangers man about the payroll, an Anaheim woman about the building. Stefaan Bullard of Montreal Canadiens told The Tunnel this week about rooms, and his answer runs through all three.

We are a different team on the road and nobody will say why. Is it the travel, or is it an excuse?

Halina Sobczyk, Cheektowaga

The split you are describing does not exist because the road has not come. Buffalo Sabres is 1-0 at home and 0-0 away. One game played in the building, none played outside it. The first road game is tonight, at Chicago Blackhawks.

Home: 5 goals for, 3 against, a win. Daniel Briere82 has 3 points in 1 game at 21.0 minutes a night. Joel Bouchard79 has 3 points in 1 game at 29.0 minutes. Owen Nolan88 has 2 goals in 1 game. The attendance is 16,447, the seat costs $78, the payroll $57.39M. All of that reads well because you watched one game against one opponent in a building you know and the result went your way.

Stefaan Bullard of Montreal Canadiens told The Tunnel's Marcus Bell yesterday about the penalty from the other side. "Travel is next. It's next because we spend half the year on a plane, and right now we're taking that penalty every road trip. Every single one." Montreal Canadiens sits 0-0-1, outscored 3-2, with a payroll of $54.89M. Bullard says the travel room is what he will build, but not yet and not this season. The Buffalo Sabres road record is 0 games long. Tonight is the first read.

We pay more than anybody and I would like somebody to tell me what for.

Aaron Lindqvist, Yonkers

The payroll is $92.95M, the largest in the league, and the New York Rangers record does not match the panic in that sentence. Revenue is $31.23M, profit $21.41M. The seat costs $82 and 13,831 people come every night. On the ice: 4 points in 2 games, 6 goals for, 4 against, on a 2-game win streak. The cost per point is $23.24M.

That number puts the New York Rangers payroll behind only Vancouver Canucks ($23.18M a point) and Toronto Maple Leafs ($22.49M) among clubs that have recorded points. St. Louis Blues pays $34.88M a point. San Jose Sharks pays $33.12M. Carolina Hurricanes pays $31.89M. Boston Bruins pays $30.16M. You are the most expensive club in hockey in dollars spent and the third-cheapest in terms of what each point costs.

Alexei Kovalev84 has 3 points in 2 games at 25.5 minutes a night. Pavel Bure82 has 2 goals. Petr Nedved82 has 2 goals. The offense is there. The one name on the Bureau's Development Index fallers is Mike Wilson62, a defenseman, at -4 form with a base of 72. The question is whether the back end holds in March.

Does the state of this building actually cost us anything on the ice, or is that something people say?

Delphine Okonkwo, Anaheim

The League Scouting Bureau's buildings table has the number at -3.65 on every man's overall from the facilities. Every player wearing the Mighty Ducks of Anaheim sweater carries a deduction of 3.65 on his grade because of what the building gives him and what it does not. It is not something people say; it is the worst facility penalty on the Bureau's page.

The payroll is $52.84M. The per-point cost is $26.42M. The attendance figure on the record is 0 and the seat costs $81. Revenue runs $30.00M and the bottom line clears $21.06M. The building is not costing the club money. It is costing it players.

Erik Cole73 carries an overall of 79. Vaclav Prospal73 carries 79. Niclas Havelid73 carries 77. Those are the grades that print after the penalty is applied. Each man's base sits higher. What you see in the Book is the deduction already subtracted.

Bullard of Montreal Canadiens told The Tunnel about the same arithmetic from the other end. "I lost games in February. I lost games where we were the team that got run over in the third period. And the workout room, that's where you build the strength, the checking, the endurance. You don't get that in October. You get it in January when your legs are gone and you've got 4 minutes left."

You are in October. Mighty Ducks of Anaheim sits 1-1-0, 4 goals for, 4 against, and tonight they host Detroit Red Wings. The number is on the Bureau's page and it has not moved. When February arrives, watch the third periods and the legs.
