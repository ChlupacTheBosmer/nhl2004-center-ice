Letters to the editor

The Postbag: what the building costs, what the payroll buys, and the road record that does not exist

Six letters arrive eight days into the season. The record has answers for some and not for others.

Wednesday, 12 October 2005 · edited by Tunde Oyelaran

Eight days of games and the letters are already in. A woman in Anaheim asks whether the building costs them on the ice. In Yonkers, a man wants to know what the biggest payroll in the league bought this week. A supporter in Cheektowaga says her club plays two different sports in two rinks. Three more arrive from clubs that lost on opening night, scored nothing, or found a goaltender who looked like a different man.

Does the state of this building cost us anything on the ice, or is that just something people say?

Delphine Okonkwo, Anaheim

The Bureau puts it at −3.65 on every man's overall, the worst in the league. Each Mighty Ducks of Anaheim player starts the night 3.65 grades below what his Book says he is, and that gap does not close when the puck drops. Mighty Ducks of Anaheim has played 2 games and split them: 4 goals for, 4 against. The payroll is $52.84M, the 19th-largest in the league. The penalty compounds with every rotation call the coach makes.

We pay more than anybody and I would like somebody to tell me what for.

Aaron Lindqvist, Yonkers

$92.95M, the largest payroll in the league. New York Rangers has 4 points in 2 games, the best mark in the Eastern Conference, and the cost per point sits at $23.24M. The seat is $82, attendance is 13,831, revenue is $31.23M, profit is $21.41M. Alexei Kovalev84 has 3 points in 2 games at a 123 pace. Pavel Bure82 has 2 goals. Petr Nedved82 has 2 goals. The figure that will be quoted if this stalls is the cost per point, and it starts at $23.24M. I would not build an argument against the payroll until that number is the only one on the page.

We are a different team on the road and nobody will say why. Is it the travel, or is it an excuse?

Halina Sobczyk, Cheektowaga

Buffalo Sabres has played 1 game this season. It was at home, and they won 5-3. The record reads 1-0 at home and 0-0 away, which means there is no road game yet. Daniel Briere80 had 1 goal and 2 assists. Owen Nolan86 scored twice. The payroll is $57.39M. The seat is $78, the cheapest in the Eastern Conference. Attendance is 16,447. The Bureau's buildings index does not carry a travel penalty for Buffalo Sabres this edition. Buffalo Sabres plays Montreal Canadiens tonight, the first road game on the schedule. After 90 minutes there, you will have a road number. Before that, 1-0 and 0-0 is not a split. It is one game.

We did not score a single time in front of our own building. I watched the whole game and nobody looked like they could put one in. Is this the start of something, or was it just one night?

Tom Vrana, Coraopolis

It is one night. Pittsburgh Penguins played 1 game and lost 0-4. The payroll is $48.92M, 13th in the league. Shane Willis69 sits on the faller side of the Development Index, down 4 to 69 from a base of 78. Attendance is 15,262 at $81 a seat. Pittsburgh Penguins hosts New York Rangers tonight. A 0-4 loss is one night. It is also the first page, and that is what a fan reads at midnight.

We spend less than every other club and we still lose. I keep hearing that the revenue is fine, that the books balance, that the club makes money. That is good for the owner. I am asking about the team on the ice.

Lena Kowalski, Saskatoon

Edmonton Oilers pays $42.61M, the second-lowest in the league. Revenue is $31.26M, profit $23.04M. The club makes money. The team lost 3-4 in 1 game. Ryan Smyth80 had 1 goal and 1 assist. Ales Hemsky75 is +9 on the Development Index, up to 81 from a base of 75, and he scored in that game. Jani Rita75 is +8, up to 81 from 76. The payroll table says Edmonton Oilers will never outspend New York Rangers. The Index says some of these young men are rising. Your front office is betting that the rise is steep enough. The record will tell you whether that bet is right, and it will take more than 1 game.

We lost 1-2 on opening night, fine, that happens. But I sat there watching Pascal Leclaire91 all game and something felt different. He was everywhere. Is he ready to be our starter, or did he have one good night?

Marcus Webb, Grove City

The Bureau's Development Index has Pascal Leclaire at +9, a base of 83 and an overall reading of 91. He is 22 years old. Columbus Blue Jackets lost 1-2 in 1 game. The payroll is $62.03M, the revenue $31.26M, the seat $83, the highest ticket price in the league alongside Pittsburgh Penguins and Ottawa Senators. Columbus Blue Jackets is home against Pittsburgh Penguins tonight. A +9 after 1 game is noise. A +9 on a 22-year-old goaltender who read 83 in the Book is the kind of jump the Bureau flags. Watch four weeks before you name him your starter.
