The Postbag: a quiet heater in Boston, a five-game skid, and the payroll that only wins one column

Five letters. A club nobody has written about that has won four straight, a Kings fan who wants a word, and the Yonkers man who asks twice because once was not enough.

Thursday, 27 October 2005 · edited by Tunde Oyelaran

A Bos man wants to know what his club has to do to get a line of print. A Kings supporter has watched five straight losses and wants someone at the club to say something. A Ducks woman asks whether her building costs games on the ice. And the Yonkers correspondent is back twice in one edition, once about the road and once about the payroll.

We have won four in a row and I have not read a word about it anywhere. What does this club have to do to get noticed?

Eamon Prendergast, Somerville

Eamon, Boston Bruins are 4-1-1 with 11 points in 7 games, 24 for, 18 against, four straight wins, third in the Eastern Conference. Glen Murray81 leads the league with 15 points in 7 games on a 176 pace. Joe Thornton86 has 9 goals in those 7 games and 13 points. You have two of the league's top four scorers on your roster.

One reason nobody is writing: your club has played 1 home game and 6 on the road. The schedule has kept you on the bus this month, 4-2 away with a single home date, and road teams don't get ink. Andrew Raycroft80 is 3-2 with a 2.39 GAA and hasn't had the building to fill.

You've also done it without Sergei Samsonov82, who is out with a neck injury through November 9. And you don't have a Stanley Cup on the wall, which is the simplest explanation for the silence.

Five straight losses. Is anybody at this club going to say something, or do we all just watch?

Marcus Delacroix, Torrance

Los Angeles Kings are 1-3-2 with 6 points in 7 games, 15 goals scored against 19 allowed. Roman Cechmanek86 has a 2.20 GAA and a 0.871 save percentage, so the goaltending is not the problem. The scoring is. 15 goals in 7 games is the third-worst output in the league.

Mike Cammalleri81 is the riser on the Bureau's Development Index, up 10 with an 87 overall, but he carries 4 points in 7 games. He's doing what he can at 23 on a 79 base. The help hasn't arrived. Yannick Tremblay73 has been out at Atlanta with a leg since October, and the rest of the forward group isn't covering for him.

Does the state of this building actually cost us anything on the ice, or is that something people say?

Delphine Okonkwo, Anaheim

It costs. Mighty Ducks of Anaheim carries -3.76 on every man's overall from its facilities, the worst penalty in the league, home and away. Jean-Sebastien Giguere97 has a 2.14 GAA on a 0.869 save percentage with a 93 overall in the Book, and he's playing under it every night.

Tampa Bay's Michael Burt told The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk after his club's game on October 24 that "1-2 away tells you the -4 is still too big." A road penalty of -4 on one set of games does visible damage. Anaheim's -3.76 applies to all of them.

We are a different team on the road and nobody will say why. Is it the travel, or is it an excuse?

Aaron Lindqvist, Yonkers

Aaron, New York Rangers are 5-1 at home and 1-3 away. Toronto Maple Leafs beat you 6-2 in your building on October 15 and 2-0 at theirs on October 26. Toronto is the only club that has beaten you at home. The other two road losses came against different opponents. Strip out the Toronto series and you are 5-0 at home and 1-1 on the road.

That's not the travel. That's a club with the Stanley Cup that has outscored you 8-2 in two games, and it started by finding you out in your own building.

We pay more than anybody and I would like somebody to tell me what for.

Aaron Lindqvist, Yonkers

Aaron, New York Rangers pay $92.95M, the league's largest payroll, and sit at $6.64M per point on 14 points in 10 games. Toronto Maple Leafs pay $89.94M and sit at $6.42M per point on 14 points in 9 games. You pay more and you get the same points.

ClubPayrollGPPoints$M per PointSeat

The seat is the one column where you're ahead: $82 to $112. The club is solvent, revenue $37.74M and profit $19.99M. Whether the $92.95M is buying what it should is a different question from whether the club loses money, and the per-point gap to Toronto, a club with two Cups and a $34.62M profit, answers it.
