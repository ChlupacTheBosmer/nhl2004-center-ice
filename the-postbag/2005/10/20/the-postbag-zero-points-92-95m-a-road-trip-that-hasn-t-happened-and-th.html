Letters to the editor

The Postbag: zero points, $92.95M, a road trip that hasn't happened, and the streak nobody printed

Five letters. A club with no points, a payroll nobody can justify yet, a team that has not left its building, and the Atlantic division nobody is writing about.

Thursday, 20 October 2005 · edited by Tunde Oyelaran

Five letters this week, and three of them are about clubs the rest of the press has stopped watching: one with zero points, one with the largest payroll in the league and a three-game skid, and one that has not played a single road game yet. The fourth asks why nobody printed a winning streak. The fifth asks what a bad building costs on the ice.

We have won 3 in a row and I have not read a word about it anywhere. What does this club have to do? We're 2-1-0 and I keep checking the wire services and there's nothing. Not a paragraph. We beat Philadelphia, we beat New Jersey, and then the article I read was about Toronto's power play. I don't hate Toronto. I just want to know what we need to do.

Rosalind Fairweather, Hicksville

You are 2-1-0 with 1 overtime win, 6 points in 4 games, 15 goals for and 12 against. You sit tied for third in the Eastern Conference with Pittsburgh Penguins, one point behind Carolina Hurricanes and four behind Toronto Maple Leafs. Your three-game winning streak is the longest active run in the Atlantic Division, and the reason nobody is writing about it is that Pittsburgh is on the same streak, the same point total, and Pittsburgh has Mario Lemieux85 on the television.

Alexei Yashin83 has 5 points (3 goals, 2 assists) in 4 games at 24.5 minutes a night. Rick DiPietro86 sits 9 above his base on the Bureau's Development Index, an overall of 88. Your only man on the injured list is Stephane Robidas69, a shoulder designated by the league, out until 2005-10-31. You host Carolina Hurricanes on 2005-10-22. Carolina is 3-1-1 with 7 points and a two-game winning run. Win that and the wire services will find you.

We are 2-2 at home and I want to know what happens when we go away. We haven't played a road game yet this season, so I'm asking the question before the answer arrives. Is it the travel? Is it the building? Or is it the excuse we'll make after the first loss in somebody else's rink?

Priya Ramanathan, Campbell

You have not played a road game. Your record reads 2-2 at home, 0-0 away. Your first trip is at Edmonton Oilers on 2005-10-24, four days from now. Edmonton is 0-2-2 with 2 points and a four-game losing streak, so the road test arrives at the softest venue on your schedule. The Bureau has not published a travel figure for your building that I can print. What I can tell you is that Montreal's general manager, Stefaan Bullard, told The Tunnel's Marcus Bell this month: "Travel is next. It's next because we spend half the year on a plane, and right now we're taking that penalty every road trip. Every single one. I know what it costs us." He was talking about his own club's facilities plan, not yours. But the league's facilities penalty is a real mechanism, and your first road game will tell you whether yours bites.

Five straight losses. Zero points. I have been a Coyotes supporter since they arrived in this city and I have never watched us start a season this flat. Nobody in the building says anything. The coaches do the press conference, they say "we'll get it," and then we lose another one. Is this normal? Is this just what a young club looks like in October, or has something actually broken?

Rafael Villalobos, Tempe

You are 0-5-0. Zero points. 9 goals for, 16 against. The n/a in the Bureau's cost-per-point column is arithmetic: you cannot divide $55.41M by zero. Your previous playoff run was the second round, so this is a club that was competitive in April and cannot find a single point in October. The Development Index has Billy Tibbetts68 at −4, an overall of 60 against a base of 72.

Your next game is at Nashville Predators on 2005-10-22. Nashville is 1-1-0 with 2 overtime wins, 6 points, and a 2-1 record on the road. They are not a fortress away from home. You have not lost to them this season because you have not played them.

We pay more than anybody. Ninety-two million. And we lose three in a row and the best I get from this paper is "rebuilding." A rebuild is what happens when you have no money. We have all the money. My neighbor drives to work in a car older than me and I pay him to come to the building with me. What is the $92.95M for if the product is a three-game losing streak and $82 seats?

Aaron Lindqvist, Yonkers

The $92.95M buys 6 points in 5 games, which is $15.49M per point, the second-worst figure in the league after Ottawa Senators at $29.36M (Ottawa has only 2 points in 3 games, so the divisor is small). Your seat is $82 and 14,195 people come. The revenue line reads $32.53M per game, which means the building pays for itself even at your payroll, and you clear $19.31M in profit. The $92.95M buys a building that clears $19.31M a season. That part is working. The product on the ice is what you are not getting, and you are right to be annoyed about it.

Petr Nedved84 has 6 points in 5 games. Brian Leetch84 has 5 points in 5 games at 37 years old, playing 34.8 minutes a night. You are on a three-game losing streak with Dale Purinton68 out through 2005-11-17 with a back injury. The next game is home against Tampa Bay Lightning on 2005-10-21. Tampa Bay is 1-0-0 with 2 overtime wins, 6 points, a three-game winning streak, and 13 goals for against 6 against in three games. They are the best team in the league at goal differential right now. That is the test your payroll has to clear, and it arrives on a Sunday afternoon.
