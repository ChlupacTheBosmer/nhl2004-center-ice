The Postbag: five questions the record has already answered

Round 1 is over. Five readers from clubs that went home are still asking. The standings have been printing the answers since October.

Thursday, 21 April 2005 · edited by Tunde Oyelaran

The regular season is finished and Round 1 of the playoffs is done. Five readers write about what it meant. The record has the answers.

We pay more than anybody and I would like somebody to tell me what for.

Ottilie Brandvold, Dearborn

The Detroit Red Wings payroll is $80.89M, the largest in the league. They finished with 96 points, which comes to $0.84M a point. They were the 7 seed in the West and got swept 4-0 by Dallas Stars. DET had gone 2-5-1 against DAL across 8 regular-season games, outscored 21-29. The sweep added 4 more losses. The playoff scores were 1-4, 2-3 in overtime, 1-2 and 1-3. Brett Hull and Kirk Maltby81 had 4 points each across four games. Dominik Hasek went 0-3 with a 2.44 GAA and a .862 save percentage. Steve Yzerman is out with a groin until June 26. Ottilie, the money is real. It hasn't bought a series win.

We are a different team on the road and nobody will say why. Is it the travel, or is it an excuse?

Kiyoshi Yamagata, Burnaby

Vancouver Canucks won 28 at home and 18 on the road. The gap is real, but it didn't follow them into the series. Against Edmonton Oilers, VAN lost Game 1 and Game 2 at home, won Game 3 in Edmonton, lost Game 4 there, won Game 5 at home, and lost Game 6 on the road. One and two at home, one and two away. The road split was a regular-season pattern, not a principle. VAN finished 38-29-7 with 99 points and lost the series to the 5 seed. Markus Naslund89 had 13 points in six games and Todd Bertuzzi91 had 11, but Dan Cloutier88 went 2-3 with a 2.97 GAA and a .837 save percentage. The travel isn't the whole answer. The goaltending was part of it.

11 straight losses. Is anybody at this club going to say something, or do we all just watch?

Yolanda Espinosa, Sunrise

Florida Panthers finished 25-36-16 with 76 points and 255 goals against, last in the Southeast. The payroll is $51.07M, which works out to $0.67M a point. Boris Van Ryswyk told The Tunnel's Marcus Bell on April 7: "We've had rough patches, but eleven, that's, that's something else." He didn't point at the coach. "That's not the coach's fault. That's not the players' fault. That's me." The streak is over because the season is. Nobody watched eleven games and said nothing, until the man running the club said it himself.

We have played TOR 5 times and not beaten them once. At what point is that a pattern and not bad luck?

Halina Sobczyk, Cheektowaga

Buffalo Sabres went 0-5-0 against Toronto Maple Leafs. Five meetings, five regulation losses, no overtime points. The answer is now. TOR finished 59-13-5 with 133 points, the most in the league, scoring 381 and allowing 190. BUF finished 27-34-12 with 84 points and missed the playoffs. When one club sweeps another across five meetings, the record calls it a pattern. There is no entry for bad luck in the standings.

We have won 4 in a row and I have not read a word about it anywhere. What does this club have to do?

Rafael Villalobos, Tempe

Phoenix Coyotes won its final four to finish 40-31-9 with 93 points, 241 for and 242 against. The differential was negative, the seed was 8th in the West, and Rafael is right that nobody printed it. The streak carried them seven games past Colorado Avalanche, which had 112 points and 310 goals, the most in the league. Sean Burke84 went 4-2 with a 2.44 GAA and a .909 save percentage. Mike Johnson83 had 9 goals and 2 assists in 7 games. What this club had to do, Rafael, was keep winning and wait for the schedule to turn. Four in March became a first-round upset in April.
