Letters to the editor

The Postbag: the streak nobody printed, two zeroes, and the road team

Five readers wrote about patterns the regular season built and the playoffs opened with.

Tuesday, 12 April 2005 · edited by Tunde Oyelaran

Five letters. Four clubs met the same opponent five times and could not get a win, and one club won four in a row while the press stayed home. The regular season closed on 2005-04-06 and Round 1 opened two days later. Every pattern these readers described showed up on a playoff scoresheet this week.

We have won 4 in a row and I have not read a word about it anywhere. What does this club have to do to get mentioned?

Rafael Villalobos, Tempe

You finished 40-31-9 with 93 points, the eighth seed in the West. Your goals sat at 241 for and 242 against, one goal from the wrong side of the ledger all season, and nobody called you a fluke because 40 wins do not look like one. Then Phoenix Coyotes went to Colorado Avalanche on 2005-04-08 and won Game 1 of the West's top-seeded pairing 2-1. Sean Burke88 stopped 37 of 39 shots across two games for a 1.86 GAA. Game 2 went to overtime and Colorado took it 3-2 on 2005-04-10. The series is 1-1 and Game 3 is tonight in your building. You asked what this club has to do. You just beat the first seed on the road in a playoff game. That is what mentioning looks like.

We have played PIT 5 times and not beaten them once. At what point is that a pattern and not bad luck?

Rosalind Fairweather, Hicksville

Five meetings is the pattern. Pittsburgh Penguins won four in regulation and one in overtime against you. You went 30-38-4 for 84 points and Atlanta Thrashers tied you at 84 with 31 wins to your 30, which is how the eighth East seed was decided. You scored 59 and allowed 60 in 20 division games. The one opponent in your own division you could not solve, you solved for zero across five nights. That is not bad luck at 82 games. It is a bad matchup that ran its course.

We have played TOR 5 times and not beaten them once. At what point is that a pattern and not bad luck?

Halina Sobczyk, Cheektowaga

Buffalo Sabres went 0-5 against Toronto Maple Leafs, every one in regulation, no overtime to even hope in. Toronto scored 381 and allowed 190 across 82 games, the widest differential in the league. You scored 63 and allowed 92 in 20 division games, the worst goal differential in the Northeast at minus 29. Same question you asked last year, different season, same result. Five meetings. Zero wins.

We pay more than anybody and I would like somebody to tell me what for.

Ottilie Brandvold, Dearborn

$80.89M bought 96 points, which is $0.84M per point, the highest payroll in the league and the ninth-best efficiency on the per-point line. Your seat cost $81 and 15,651 of them filled. Detroit Red Wings finished 35-31-6 and drew second-seeded Dallas Stars in Round 1. Dallas won 4-1 at Joe Louis on 2005-04-09 and 3-2 in overtime on 2005-04-11. Brett Hull77 has 1 goal and 2 assists. Kirk Maltby75 has 2 goals and 1 assist. Steve Yzerman79 has 1 assist. Dominik Hasek86 allowed 4 on 31 shots in Game 1. Curtis Joseph84 came in for Game 2 and allowed 3 on 26. The payroll bought a building, a roster and a seventh seed. The playoffs have not yet found a return on the first two.

We are a different team on the road and nobody will say why. Is it the travel, or is it an excuse?

Kiyoshi Yamagata, Burnaby

28-13 at home. 18-23 away. Vancouver Canucks finished fourth in the West with 99 points and drew fifth-seeded Edmonton Oilers. Game 1 was in your building and Edmonton Oilers won 4-3 in overtime on 2005-04-09. Game 2 was in your building and Edmonton won 3-2 on 2005-04-11. You are 0-2 in the series and Game 3 is at Edmonton, where your season record was 18-23. The travel question belongs to the club. The result belongs to the road, and it is about to decide whether the season keeps going there.

---
