Letters to the editor

The Postbag: six questions from the regular season, six answers arriving in April

The patterns that kept readers writing through February are now being tested where the record keeps score.

Tuesday, 12 April 2005 · edited by Tunde Oyelaran

Six letters this edition. Every one asks something the regular season left unanswered. The playoffs started four days ago and the answers are coming through in a different currency now.

We won 4 in a row at the end and I have not read a word about it anywhere, in any paper, on any site. What does this club have to do to get noticed? The building was shaking and the league acted like we don't exist. Rafael Villalobos, Tempe

Your four became five. Phoenix Coyotes closed the regular season 40-31-9 with 93 points, the eighth seed in the West, and won Game 1 in Colorado Avalanche, 2-1. Sean Burke88 is at .926 across two playoff starts. The series is tied at 1, and Game 3 is at home tonight.

The record shows what the streak bought. Phoenix Coyotes finished with 241 goals scored and 242 against, even across 82 games. Those five wins are the only reason 93 points got them into the bracket instead of watching Game 1 on television.

We pay more than anybody in this league and I would like somebody to tell me what for. $80.89 million and I am watching a team that cannot get a bounce. My seat costs $81 and the man beside me is a lawyer who says we could have bought this team twice over in the years we have been paying this payroll and still not made it past the second round. Is he right? Ottilie Brandvold, Dearborn

The lawyer is close. Detroit Red Wings carries the league's largest payroll at $80.89M and finished with 96 points, $0.84M per point. New York Rangers is $78.14M for 84 points, $0.93M per point, the worst value in the league. You are ahead of them by the price of a meal, and that is not a lead anybody should be proud of.

What it bought this spring: a 0-2 deficit to Dallas Stars, Game 3 on your own ice tomorrow. Dallas Stars won 4-1 and 3-2 in overtime. Mike Modano88 has 3 goals in 2 games against you. Kirk Maltby75 and Brett Hull77 have 3 points each, and behind Dominik Hasek86 at 2.34 GAA sits Curtis Joseph84 at 4.00 with an .818 save percentage. The regular season against Dallas Stars was 2-3-1, 19 goals for, 24 against.

I have been coming to this building for 20 years and I can tell you, we are a different team when we get on a plane. Nobody in the press box will say why. Is it the travel, or is it an excuse? Four time zones, the late starts. Kiyoshi Yamagata, Burnaby

It was not the travel this time. Vancouver Canucks is 28-13 at home and 18-23 away, and you have been right about that split all season. In these playoffs the split reversed itself: both losses to Edmonton Oilers came in your building. Edmonton Oilers won 4-3 in overtime on 2005-04-09 and 3-2 on 2005-04-11, both at home. Markus Naslund89 has 4 points in 2 games. Dan Cloutier85 is at 3.00 GAA with an .842 save percentage. Game 3 is in Edmonton tomorrow, where your record this season was 18-23. The road problem is still there and the home problem just arrived.

Eleven straight losses. Is anybody at this club going to say something to us, or do we all just sit in our seats and watch? We still fill the building. What are we filling it for? Yolanda Espinosa, Sunrise

Florida Panthers finished 25-36-16 with 76 points. You are right about the gate: 14971 per game at $79 a seat, $13.64M in profit. The 11 straight losses are the longest active streak in the league.

Boris Van Ryswyk spoke to The Tunnel's Marcus Bell on 2005-04-07 and did say something, and he did not dodge the building. "What I can do is give him a room to work in. A practice surface. And right now, I can't." On the profit people point at: "The money is there, yes. I've said that before, the gate is good here, the fans show up. They pay up." And then: "A payroll of fifty-one million doesn't move on its own."

The payroll is $51.07M, the 12th highest in the league, and the streak is the worst in the league. Van Ryswyk says the building is the thing he cannot fix right now.

We carry the worst room in the league on every man's back and now we are down 0-2 to Toronto in the playoffs. At what point does the building cost us a series? Rosalind Fairweather, Hicksville

The Bureau's facilities data puts New York Islanders at minus 3.64 on every man's overall from its facilities, the worst figure in the league. Toronto Maple Leafs carried 59 wins, 133 points, and 381 goals scored in the regular season. The series between these clubs finished 4-1-1 to Toronto Maple Leafs, 18-11 on goals. In these playoffs, Roberto Luongo94 is at 1.01 GAA with a .971 save percentage in 2 starts. Your side has scored 2 goals in 2 games. Game 3 is in your building tonight.

The minus 3.64 is real and it is the worst in the league. Against a club that won 31 of 41 on the road during the regular season, the building is not the gap between 2-0 and 2-1. It is a tax on a roster that could not win 3 of 6 meetings in October through March.

Five times against Toronto, five times nothing. I have watched this team since the 80s and I have never been 0-5 against a single club in one season. What do I say to my son when he asks why we can't beat them? Halina Sobczyk, Cheektowaga

You went 0-5-0 against Toronto Maple Leafs in the division. Across all 20 division games, Buffalo Sabres scored 63 goals and gave up 92, finishing 4-11-3, the worst record in the Northeast. Martin Biron88 went 27-34 at 3.37 GAA. You are out. The 0-5 against Toronto Maple Leafs is part of a division record where every club in the Northeast went at least 3-1 against them. Toronto Maple Leafs was not the only problem.
