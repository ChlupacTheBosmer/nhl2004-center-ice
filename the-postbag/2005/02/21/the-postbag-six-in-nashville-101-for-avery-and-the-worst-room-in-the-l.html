The Postbag: six in Nashville, 101 for Avery, and the worst room in the league

Five readers, five patterns the rest of the league walked past

Monday, 21 February 2005 · edited by Tunde Oyelaran

The streak grew to six since we last printed it. The league's most penalized player, a club that plays two different seasons depending on where it wakes up, a building that costs its roster points, and a general manager who says the operation is sound while his club loses five straight.

We have won 6 in a row and I have not read a word about it anywhere. What does this club have to do?

Earlene Whitcomb, Nashville

When this page printed the four-game run, you would not have known it was the start of anything. It is now 6 straight wins. Nashville Predators is 34-15-5, 81 points in 58 games, first in the West at 81 to Dallas Stars's 80 in 60, with 2 more games to play than Dallas. 207 goals for, 170 against. The 6 wins sit on top of a season that carried them to first in the Central and 4 division games still to play. You do not have to do anything. You are first.

Sean Avery77 -- is he helping this team, or is he just a man who sits down a lot?

Marcus Delacroix, Torrance

The sheet gives 101 penalty minutes in 59 games, the most in the league. Los Angeles Kings is 17-28-6, 56 points, 11th in the West, 5 back of the 8th seed. Sean Avery is on a club that has not reached 20 wins with 23 games left. Whether 101 minutes in the box helps or hurts a team in that position is a question the PIM column does not answer.

We are a different team on the road and nobody will say why. Is it the travel, or is it an excuse?

Delphine Okonkwo, Anaheim

20-10 at home and 11-19 away. 9 more wins on their own ice than anywhere else. Mighty Ducks of Anaheim is 24-18-11 with 73 points, sitting 6th in the West, inside the playoff line. Every Pacific club travels, and none swing like this. Sergei Fedorov86 has 61 goals and 89 points on the season, the same man in both buildings. The sheet does not carry a reason for the split. The Bureau's facilities table is the nearest thing to one that the Bureau publishes, and I will look at it before the next edition.

Does the state of this building actually cost us anything on the ice, or is that something people say?

Rosalind Fairweather, Hicksville

It costs you. The facilities rating on the Bureau's table is -3.65 on every man's overall. That is the worst in the league. Every player on New York Islanders is playing with 3.65 fewer points of grade than he would carry in an average room. The club is 24-28-3, 59 points, 11th in the East, on a four-game losing streak. You cannot fix a building by March. You can fix it before October.

5 straight losses. Is anybody at this club going to say something, or do we all just watch?

Stanislaw Kubicki, Berwyn

The general manager said something on 2005-02-16. Jason Hughes told The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk that the operation is sound, that the profit is what buys the slow things, and that the locker room was the right call because morale is where it is. The sheet gives Chicago Blackhawks $39.27M in payroll against 51 points, $0.77M per point. Chicago carries a $24.65M profit. The 18-30-9 record, the five-game losing streak, the 14th place in the West: none of that comes from the payroll line. Hughes admitted the equipment room is at zero. He said they would get to it when the numbers say they can. The numbers say 51 points in 60 games, and they are still saying it on 2005-02-21.
