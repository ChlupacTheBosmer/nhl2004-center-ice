Letters to the editor

The Postbag: a positive differential, an unnoticed streak, five coin flips, a bad building, and a road team nobody can explain

Five readers ask about clubs the national pages skip, and the record gives every one of them a straight answer.

Sunday, 6 February 2005 · edited by Tunde Oyelaran

Five letters, all from clubs the league's coverage walks past. A fan at the bottom of the Southeast wants to know if his team is as bad as the record says. A man in Raleigh wants someone to notice what his team has done. A woman in Bloomington has a division rival she cannot beat. A man in Edmonton wants to know if his building costs him goals. A Rangers supporter cannot explain why his team is two teams.

Six straight losses and I still cannot find a single person at this club who will stand up and tell us what they see from the inside. Everyone smiles at the press conference and nobody says a word. Are we really this bad, or is there something going on that the papers will not print?

Yolanda Espinosa, Sunrise

Yolanda, the numbers underneath the record tell a different story than the points column. Florida Panthers sit 17-27-7 with 45 points in 53 games. They have scored 161 and allowed 167. That is 6 goals across 53 games. You are being outscored by about one-tenth of a goal a night and you sit 45 points. David Aebischer87 has been 16-5 with a 2.48 goals-against and a .904 save % in 22 starts. Those are top-six goaltender numbers from a 22-year-old. The problem is 22 starts out of 53. The team in front of him is good enough to be in every game and bad enough to lose the close ones. Six straight losses over 53 games is nothing to build a story on. The whole line is the story, and the whole line says this club is closer to .500 than it looks.

We have won six in a row and I cannot find a single national story about it. Not on the wire, not on the cable show, nothing. We are sitting right behind the best team in the East and nobody seems to care. What does this club have to do to get a paragraph?

Wendell Ashby, Raleigh

Wendell, Carolina Hurricanes are 19-14-9 with 69 points in 53 games, second in the Eastern Conference behind Toronto Maple Leafs. They have 11 overtime wins. That is the most of any club in the league. They are 19-9 at home. Kevin Weekes84 is 26-16 with a 2.99 and a .858. None of that is small. You have been building a playoff team without anyone paying attention, on a payroll of $55.96M, which is the 9th-highest in this league, and you are getting 69 points. That is $0.81M per point. The streak is the first week people noticed because the standings made it too big to walk past. But look at what is behind the streak: 11 overtime wins, the best in the league. You did not stumble into that. You built it, game by game, in front of nobody.

Five meetings, five losses. Not one win. At what point do I stop telling myself it is bad luck and start believing they simply cannot beat this club?

Solveig Aakerlund, Bloomington

Solveig, Minnesota Wild are 0-3-2 against Edmonton Oilers over 5 meetings. They scored 15 goals and allowed 20. Every single game was decided by one goal. Five games, five one-goal games. You are not being outclassed. You are getting to the third period within a goal five times and breaking the wrong way five times, and that is a coin-flip series that went five against you. Two games remain this season. The coin flips can land the other way. I would not call it helplessness.

Our building is a joke and I know that. But does the state of it actually cost us wins on the ice, or is that something fans say to feel better about the team they root for?

Callum Fotheringay, Edmonton

Callum, the League Scouting Bureau's buildings table has Edmonton Oilers at -3.65 on every player's overall grade from its facilities. That is the worst modifier in the league. Every man in that building plays below his Book grade because of it. When the Bureau grades Mike Comrie83 at 78, he gets less than 78 out there. Raffi Torres73, base 74, gets less than 74. Tommy Salo85, 82, gets less than 82. It costs you, and the record says exactly how much. But look at what you did anyway. You are 22-14-6 with 70 points, third in the Western Conference, on a payroll of $36.81M. That is the fourth-cheapest roster in the league producing the third-best record. $0.53M per point. The building costs you goals, but you are getting more from the money than almost anyone else, and you are third in the West doing it.

We are one team at home and a completely different team on the road. Nobody will tell me why. Is it the travel or is it that we do not want to work when we are not in front of our own building?

Aaron Lindqvist, Yonkers

Aaron, New York Rangers are 19-10 at home and 10-16 away. That is the split, and it is not small. You pay $77.49M, the second-highest payroll in this league, and you sit 6th in the East with 63 points. You score 205 goals, fourth-best in the conference. You allow 201. The record does not carry the travel room on the page I have in front of me today, so I cannot tell you whether it is the travel. What I can tell you is that the split is real, the roster is good enough to be better than 6th, and I am not going to invent a reason I do not have.
