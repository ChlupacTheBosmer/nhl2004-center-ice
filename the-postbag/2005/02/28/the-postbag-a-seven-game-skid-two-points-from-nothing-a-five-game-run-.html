Letters to the editor

The Postbag: a seven-game skid two points from nothing, a five-game run nobody printed, and the road club worse than Detroit

Five letters about streaks February walked past. One answer surprises even me.

Monday, 28 February 2005 · edited by Tunde Oyelaran

Five readers sent me patterns this fortnight. A seven-game losing streak, a five-game winning streak, two clubs that never beat the same opponent, and a road record everybody in Dearborn complains about. The road answer is not about Dearborn.

Seven straight. Seven. I have sat in Section 214 for years and I have watched the Sabres be bad before, but this is different because we are not out yet. Are we out, or are we just bad? Nobody in this building will tell us.

Halina Sobczyk, Cheektowaga

You are not out. Buffalo sits 2 points behind New York Islanders, which holds the eighth and final playoff seat in the East. The gap is small because the eighth-place club is not good: the Islanders have 64 points in 62 games. Your Sabres have 62 in 62. That means one win closes it to a point and two wins take the seat.

What will not close it is the goal prevention. Buffalo Sabres has allowed 231 goals in 62 games, which is 3.7 a night. The last ten have been ugly: 18 scored, 27 allowed, seven of them losses in regulation and one in overtime. Martin Biron84 has faced the whole of that workload at 3.52 and 0.840, which is a goaltender holding a leaky ship together.

We have won 5 in a row. Five games and I have not read a single word about it in any column, on any site, from any broadcast. What does this club have to do, lose 10 straight, before somebody writes our name?

Rafael Villalobos, Tempe

You are right that nobody wrote it, and you are right to be angry. The Phoenix Coyotes Coyotes sit in the eighth playoff spot in the West with 64 points in 62 games. Five straight wins got them here. Nobody wrote about it because they look wrong from the outside: 184 scored, 198 allowed. A goal differential of minus 14, and the only top-8 club in either conference with that kind of deficit. But Sean Burke82 has 25 wins at 2.86, and the 2 overtime wins in those 29 total victories are the difference between a team in the race and a team out of it. Los Angeles Kings is 2 points behind you. If the Coyotes hold this, nobody will have an excuse for ignoring them come April.

We have played Edmonton 5 times and not beaten them once. Not once in five games. At what point is that a pattern and not bad luck? I watch this team fight and I cannot understand why they cannot close one.

Solveig Aakerlund, Bloomington

Five games, five one-goal games. The Minnesota Wild Wild have scored 15 and allowed 20 across those five meetings, which means three of the five were decided by a single goal on the other end. The record says you lost 3 in regulation and 2 in overtime. Two of those overtime losses came after the clock was already dead.

Five almosts in a row is not a coin flip. Edmonton Oilers has 12 overtime wins on the season, more than any other club. They are comfortable in the one-goal moment and your roster has not learned to win the kind of game that decides whether you go home or go deeper. You have 1 meeting left and 16 wins in 62 games, so the pattern will not cost you a playoff spot. It will cost you the next time you need to close against somebody who matters.

Five games against Pittsburgh and we have not won one. Not one. I know they are rebuilding and I know we are fighting for eighth but that should mean nothing when the puck drops. What is it about this club?

Rosalind Fairweather, Hicksville

Pittsburgh beat you 4 times in regulation and once in overtime. Five meetings, five losses, zero points against your division rival. The Pittsburgh Penguins Penguins have 17 wins in 63 games and 61 points, which is 3 fewer than your 64. They are a bad club. You could not beat them.

The question is why a 25-28-4 team goes 0-5 against a 17-28-9 team. I do not have the answer, and the 5 meetings on the record do not show me the reason. You sit in eighth in the East. Those are two points you will not get back.

We are a different team on the road and nobody in this league will say why. Is it the travel? Is it the building? Or is it an excuse we tell ourselves in Dearborn because the home building makes us feel like something we are not? I want to know if somebody at the league has an answer.

Ottilie Brandvold, Dearborn

I do have an answer and it will not make you feel better, because it means the club with the worse road problem is not you. Detroit wins 66.7 percent of its home games and 40.0 percent of its away games. That gap is 26.7 percentage points. Anaheim wins 67.7 percent at home and 36.4 percent on the road. That gap is 31.4 percentage points. The Mighty Ducks of Anaheim Ducks travel farther than you do and they come apart wider than you do, and nobody calls them a road team. Nobody says the travel room explains Anaheim.

Detroit sits 9 points ahead of the eighth-place club in the West. Your road record is a weakness, but it is not killing your season. You are 73 points with 7 wins and 7 overtime wins. You are in the race. Anaheim is 78 and in the race.
