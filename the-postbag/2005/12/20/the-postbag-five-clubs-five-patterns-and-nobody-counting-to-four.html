The Postbag: five clubs, five patterns, and nobody counting to four

The standings printed them weeks ago. Five readers caught on.

Tuesday, 20 December 2005 · edited by Tunde Oyelaran

Five clubs, five patterns the standings have been printing for weeks. A Buffalo woman who has watched her team lose three times to Boston. A Washington reader at seven straight losses. A Detroit supporter who has noticed his team is a different club on the road. A New York man watching his team go winless against Toronto. And a woman in Anaheim who thinks nobody outside her building knows her team has won six in a row.

We have played BOS 3 times and not beaten them once. At what point is that a pattern and not bad luck?

Halina Sobczyk, Cheektowaga

Three meetings, zero wins. That is a pattern. Buffalo Sabres sits at 30 points in 30 games; Boston Bruins has 42 in 31. Boston went 3-0-0 against Buffalo in the Northeast and Buffalo went 0-3-0 in return, the worst single-opponent mark in that division. Across all division games, Boston is 38 goals for and 29 against. Buffalo is 20 for and 24 against. The goals tell you the same thing the record does: three is not luck. Buffalo has 12 division games left in all, and one more meeting with Boston among them.

We have played TOR 3 times and not beaten them once. At what point is that a pattern and not bad luck?

Aaron Lindqvist, Yonkers

New York Rangers 0-3 against Toronto Maple Leafs over 3 meetings. Toronto has 51 points in 33 games, the best record in the East. New York has 45 in 34. The Rangers trail by 6 points with a game more played. Toronto is 11-2 on the road; the Rangers are 8-7 away. No next meeting between these two appears on the schedule yet. Toronto has beaten every club it has faced three times this season and lost once, while the Rangers have beaten every Atlantic opponent they have faced. Three is enough for a pattern. It is not enough for a verdict. Toronto is simply the better club at this point in the season.

7 straight losses. Is anybody at this club going to say something, or do we all just watch?

Lorraine Buckminster, Silver Spring

Washington Capitals is 7-18-5 with 23 points in 32 games, the fewest in the East. 74 goals for, 90 against. Olaf Kolzig82 is 7-17 with a 2.37 goals-against and a 0.875 save percentage. The team is 5-15 on the road. Fifty games remain. You do not have to watch in silence, and you do not need anyone at the club to say something for you. 90 goals against in 32 games is the whole answer. The seven-game streak is where that number shows up first. Until the goals-against drops, the wins will not arrive no matter what gets said.

We are a different team on the road and nobody will say why. Is it the travel, or is it an excuse?

Ottilie Brandvold, Dearborn

Detroit Red Wings is 11-6 at home and 5-9 on the road. 74 goals for, 70 against overall. The +4 goal differential says the goals are nearly even, and the difference lives entirely in wins. Manny Legace91 has a 2.03 goals-against and a 0.896 save percentage, which means the goaltending is not the road problem. Phil Housley79 is out with a wrist injury through 2005-12-28, which removes a defenseman from the blue line during the next week's games. Travel carries a cost; the Bureau does not grade it separately from the players who carry it. Six fewer wins away than at home is the price. It is real and it is the same price every road team pays, only larger for some clubs than others.

We have won 6 in a row and I have not read a word about it anywhere. What does this club have to do?

Delphine Okonkwo, Anaheim

You are right that nobody printed it. Mighty Ducks of Anaheim is on a 6-game winning streak, 10-13-1 with 37 points in 32 games, 5th in the West. Eight overtime wins this season is the most in the league. Columbus Blue Jackets holds 8th with 34 points and 52 games left. The wire prints records above .500, not a 6-game streak on a 10-13 club. Your streak is real. Your record is what it is. Six more wins and you will read about it everywhere.

---

Remaining patterns:

  • "That is a pattern." as a standalone confirmation after restating the facts (§2, but it directly answers the reader's question, so I kept it).
  • "It is not enough for a verdict." is a clipped negative tail (§1), but it carries a distinct point (pattern ≠ final judgment), so it stands.
  • The opening paragraph is a five-item list (§6 at paragraph scale), but it functions as a table of contents and the voice demands it.
