The Postbag: six in overtime nobody printed, 1-13 in your own building, and a man who sits down more than anyone

Three days. A woman in Anaheim has written four times about the same club. A man in Chicago is watching his team lose at home for the thirteenth time.

Sunday, 11 December 2005 · edited by Tunde Oyelaran

Six letters. A woman in Anaheim has written four times about the same club since October and is owed more than she has gotten. A man in Chicago is watching his team go 1-13 in its own building. Two clubs have met an opponent three times this season and cannot beat them. The figures follow.

We have won 3 in a row and I have not read a word about it anywhere. What does this club have to do?

Delphine Okonkwo, Anaheim

Mighty Ducks of Anaheim is on a 3-game winning streak. 9-13-1 in 29 games, 31 points, 62 for and 68 against. 7th in the West, tied on points with Detroit Red Wings in 6th, who have played one fewer game. The heater is real.

Jean-Sebastien Giguere93 carries a 2.12 GAA, a 0.889 save percentage and a 97 in the Book, the best overall grade among goaltenders on the sheet. That holds the ship. But Anaheim has 6 overtime wins in 29 games, tied with Nashville Predators for the most in the league. A club that goes to overtime 7 times and wins 6 of them is a club that cannot close regulation. 62 goals for, 68 against. They are outscored and they have 31 points because they keep finding the second intermission and winning it. The standings call that a heater. The desk sees the same pattern in a different colour.

We have played DET 3 times and not beaten them once. At what point is that a pattern and not bad luck?

Delphine Okonkwo, Anaheim

Three meetings, zero wins. That is a pattern. Detroit has taken six points off you and you have taken zero off them. Detroit sits at 12-12-1 with 31 points in 28 games, the same total you have, in the same 6th-and-7th neighborhood you occupy. They are a middle-of-the-conference club that has your number three times over. Three meetings is a small sample and you know that, but three meetings with no regulation win and no overtime win is a clean sweep. You have not played them since that third one, and the next date is not on what the desk can see.

Tie Domi70 -- is he helping this team, or is he just a man who sits down a lot?

Delphine Okonkwo, Anaheim

Tie Domi has 86 penalty minutes in 29 games, the most in the league. The next closest man on the Bureau's scoring-and-discipline page is well behind him. That is 3.0 penalty minutes a game, a number that means Domi is in the box every other night or close to it. Whether he is helping is a question about what he does when he is not serving, and the scoring table does not put his name near any line that would make that answer obvious. He is the most penal man in the league. That is what the record says, and that is the number you asked for.

4 straight losses. Is anybody at this club going to say something, or do we all just watch?

Stanislaw Kubicki, Berwyn

Chicago Blackhawks is 6-14-6 with 20 points in 27 games, on a 4-game losing streak, 55 goals for, 76 against. The 4-game skid is tonight's problem. The 1-13 at home is the season's problem.

One home win in 14 games. Chicago plays better on the road: 6-7 away, 1-13 at home. A 19-game swing between buildings is the largest in the league by a wide margin. The next-worst home record belongs to San Jose Sharks at 3-11, and San Jose at least splits close to even on the road at 8-5. Chicago's payroll is $48.74M for $2.44M a point. Pittsburgh Penguins pays $48.92M, almost the same number, and gets $1.44M a point because Pittsburgh has 34 points to Chicago's 20. Nobody from Chicago has spoken on The Tunnel in the last fortnight. I cannot tell you if they will.

We are a different team on the road and nobody will say why. Is it the travel, or is it an excuse?

Ottilie Brandvold, Dearborn

Detroit Red Wings is 10-5 at home and 5-8 away. A 9-game swing between your building and everyone else's. That is not small. St. Louis Blues is the league's mirror image: 6-5 at home and 12-4 on the road, a 7-game swing running the other way, and the Blues carry the best road record in the league.

You asked whether it is the travel or an excuse. The Bureau publishes a facilities grade for every club. The worst in the league belongs to Mighty Ducks of Anaheim at -3.77 per man, and Detroit's is not that. The Bureau does not measure travel distance or time zones or the number of road trips in a month. The 10-5 and the 5-8 are on the standings and the why is not. You are not in trouble: $63.16M in payroll, $60.11M in revenue, a $31.28M profit, 31 points and a seat in the Western playoff arithmetic. The road is costing you something you cannot see on any page I have.

We have played PIT 3 times and not beaten them once. At what point is that a pattern and not bad luck?

Isadora Fontaine, Brandon

Tampa Bay Lightning is 0-3 against Pittsburgh Penguins, outscored 9 to 13 across 3 meetings. Two of those three were one-goal games, and you lost both in overtime. That is a sweep, but a sweep decided by a single goal in two of the three. One more point and you are 1-2-0 in the series instead of 0-3.

Pittsburgh Penguins is 9-8-4 with 34 points, 5th in the East, and they have a 6-7 road record that says they are better at home than you might think from these results. The next meeting is on 2006-01-17. You will want to take something off them. Two of the three you did not take them to the second intermission.
