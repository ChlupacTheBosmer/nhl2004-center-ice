The Postbag: two sevens, the worst ice in hockey, and a 0-3 that was never luck

Two seven-game streaks in opposite directions, the worst home record in the league, and a road team that keeps beating you because the road is where it lives

Thursday, 29 December 2005 · edited by Tunde Oyelaran

Two clubs carry seven-game streaks in opposite directions and neither has reached the wire. A Devils man wants to know what Newark has to do to get a word in print. A Sharks woman wants to know if anyone at San Jose is going to say anything. The record answers both before the page reaches the rest of your mail.

Seven straight losses. Is anybody at this club going to say something, or do we all just watch?

Priya Ramanathan, Campbell

San Jose Sharks sits at 11-16-7 with 31 points through 35 games. The seven-game skid is the longest active losing streak in the league. It sits on a 3-14 home record, worst in the league, against 9-9 on the road. They are a .500 team when they leave the building and the worst team in hockey when they play inside it.

San Jose carries a $66.24M payroll, 5th largest in the league, and produces 31 points. The Bureau's business table prints $2.14M a point, second-worst in the league behind Washington at $2.19M. The Sharks have outspent 25 clubs and sit 12th in the Western Conference, 8 points back of the 8th-place line. Evgeni Nabokov83 has a 2.78 GAA and a .861 save percentage in 29 games. The 8th-place seat is held by Anaheim, whose payroll is $54.64M and who play in a worse building.

We have won 7 in a row and I have not read a word about it anywhere. What does this club have to do?

Dominic Castellano, Kearny

Nothing. You are 20-6-3 with 51 points through 33 games, second in the Eastern Conference. The 71 goals you allow are the fewest in the league. On the road you are 13-3. Martin Brodeur95 is 22-6 with a 1.76 GAA and a .907 save percentage, the lowest GAA of any goaltender in the league.

Your payroll is $58.20M, 13th in the league. Your cost per point is $1.14M, second only to Nashville at $1.02M. You buy a point at close to half the rate of Toronto ($1.52M) or the Rangers ($1.94M), and the league's press boxes reward spending. That is the word nobody prints about New Jersey Devils: efficiency.

We have played BOS 3 times and not beaten them once. At what point is that a pattern and not bad luck?

Halina Sobczyk, Cheektowaga

Three meetings, 0 regulation wins, 0 overtime wins. Buffalo Sabres is 0-3 against Boston Bruins this season.

Boston is 13-4 on the road and 8-10 at home. They play better away from Boston. Every time you host them, you face the version that wins nearly 80 percent of its road games. When you travel to their building, you face a team that drops more than it wins in front of its own crowd, yet still has more depth than yours. The 0-3 is not bad luck. You drew the road team, and the road team is the one that beats you.

Tie Domi74 -- is he helping this team, or is he just a man who sits down a lot?

Delphine Okonkwo, Anaheim

We printed 62 penalty minutes on him in late November. He carries 120 now, through 35 games, the most in the league. The number has nearly doubled in five weeks. The Bureau's Book gives him an overall grade and the penalty minutes; beyond those two lines, the record does not price a draw or a shift. 120 in 35 games is the number the page can give you, and the number is the answer.

We are a different team on the road and nobody will say why. Is it the travel, or is it an excuse?

Ottilie Brandvold, Dearborn

13-7 at home and 5-10 on the road. Twenty games in your building, fifteen outside it. Detroit Red Wings sits 10th in the Western Conference at 37 points, 2 behind Anaheim in 8th. The home record would put you in the top half of the conference. The road record puts you in the bottom five. You have lost 2 in a row and your last 10 read LLWWLWLLLL with 23 goals for and 27 against.

The split holds across both stretches and enough games to be a pattern. You are a top-half team at the Joan and you are not one when you leave. The Bureau's buildings table carries a travel figure for Detroit, and if the desk prints that table I will take the question again.
