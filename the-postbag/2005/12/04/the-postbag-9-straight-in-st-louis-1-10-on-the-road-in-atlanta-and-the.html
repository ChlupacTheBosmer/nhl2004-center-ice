Letters to the editor

The Postbag: 9 straight in St. Louis, 1-10 on the road in Atlanta, and the payroll that finally has an answer

Six letters. A heater nobody printed, a road that collapsed, two clubs stuck on the same opponent three times running, and the biggest cheque in the league buying first place.

Sunday, 4 December 2005 · edited by Tunde Oyelaran

Remaining patterns after edit: None significant. Two tells removed (§2 one-line closer, §6 triad) from the Aaron reply; one §2 closer cut from the Ottilie reply.

---

This edition prints six letters from clubs the national page has passed over: a club on the league's longest active streak, a club that cannot win away from its own building, two clubs that have played the same opponent three times and lost every one, and the biggest payroll in the league, which finally has a line to show for it. The record has been sitting here with all of it.

We have won 9 in a row and I have not read a word about it anywhere. What does this club have to do, win 12 games on the road? Do we have to go undefeated in January before somebody notices?

Verna Kaltenbrunner, Kirkwood

You do not have to go undefeated in January. You are already at 16-4-3 and 37 points, first in the Western Conference, and the 9 straight wins are the longest active streak in the league. Nobody printed it because the national page has been watching the clubs above you in payroll, not the ones buying points cheaply. Your per-point cost is $1.89M, third-best in the league. The road split is what should stop a writer: 12-3 away and 5-4 at home. You are better in somebody else's building, which is the kind of number that does not survive contact with a highlight reel. Keith Tkachuk84 is carrying 33 points in 24 games at 21.2 minutes a night. Brandon Reid73 has 31 in the same number of games on 18.7 minutes a night, a 7th-round pick from 2000 putting up the kind of line the Bureau would have called a fantasy before October.

4 straight losses. Is anybody at this club going to say something, or do we all just watch? You don't have to tell me we're young. I know the roster. I want to know why we're this bad in somebody else's building.

Royce Vandermeer, Atlanta

The answer is in the split. Atlanta Thrashers is 6-6 at home and 1-10 away. The 4-game skid sits inside the 1-10. You are not a bad team; you are a team that plays 23 games in 2 different buildings and wins exactly one of the 10 it plays in the other one. 49 goals for in 23 games, 65 against. Ilya Kovalchuk91 is 15 goals and 26 points in 23 games, but the team around him is 1-10 when it leaves the building. You next play at Boston on 2005-12-05. That will be the 11th road game.

We are a different team on the road and nobody will say why. Is it the travel, or is it an excuse? 9 and 4 at home, 4 and 7 away. That's not a slump, that's a personality.

Rosalind Fairweather, Hicksville

It is a pattern. The New York Islanders road record is 4-7 against 9-4 at home, and the swing is the same size as the one in Atlanta, only yours has the offense to cover it at home. 76 goals in 24 games is the kind of number that travels, but it does not. You are 3 games over .500 overall, 29 points, 6th in the Eastern Conference. Alexei Yashin83 has 33 points in 24 games. He does not stop scoring on the road; the team does.

We have played CGY 3 times and not beaten them once. At what point is that a pattern and not bad luck?

Ottilie Brandvold, Dearborn

Three meetings, three losses, and all three games decided by one goal. Detroit Red Wings has scored 5 and allowed 8 against Calgary across those 3 games. The season series table shows it: 3 one-goal games out of 3. That is not bad luck in the ordinary sense. You have had every chance and closed on none of them. The next meeting is 2006-01-25.

We have played PIT 3 times and not beaten them once. Same question, different city. Are we just a team that cannot beat Pittsburgh?

Isadora Fontaine, Brandon

You have played Pittsburgh Penguins 3 times and gone 0-3, including 2 overtime losses. 13 goals for, 9 against. 2 of the 3 games were decided by one goal. The record calls it 1 win, 0 regulation wins, 0 losses, and 2 overtime losses in the format, which means every game went past regulation or Pittsburgh won by more than a goal. Your next meeting is 2006-01-17. You have 5 overtime losses on the season, tied for the league lead. The pattern is not Pittsburgh. The pattern is that you lose close games everywhere and they happen to have found you three times.

We pay more than anybody and I would like somebody to tell me what for. I am not asking for sympathy. I am asking what we bought.

Aaron Lindqvist, Yonkers

You bought first place in the Eastern Conference. 40 points in 27 games, which is the most in the league. The payroll is $92.95M and the per-point cost is $2.32M, which is mid-range: Dallas pays $1.61M a point, Nashville pays $1.51M, Pittsburgh pays $1.58M. You are not getting a bargain, but you are getting points. The profit line reads $10.16M on $48.32M of revenue. The building seats 14699 at $82 a seat. Mike Dunham84 has 13 wins and a 1.92 GAA in 21 games. The last letter you wrote from here was about a team with zero points and the same payroll. The answer changed because the record changed.
