Letters to the editor

The Postbag: the streak that held, the road that arrived, and nine losses

Five letters. A rivalry nobody can crack, a building that taxes a good team, a split with games behind it at last, and the worst home rink in the league.

Sunday, 13 November 2005 · edited by Tunde Oyelaran

Four days since my last edition. The stories I flagged haven't moved the way readers expected. The streak is the same streak. The road team has finally shown up. And a club that was merely bad in October has become the worst team in the league inside its own building.

We have played PIT 3 times and not beaten them once. At what point is that a pattern and not bad luck?

Isadora Fontaine, Brandon

Three meetings, all losses for Tampa Bay Lightning, and two of them in overtime. Pittsburgh Penguins has scored 13 against your 9 across those games, and 2 of the 3 were decided by one goal. A single bounce in either overtime goes the other way and your record reads 2-1-0 instead of 0-1-2. Three meetings is not a pattern. The close-series table lists 20 of these this season, and plenty of them sit at 1-1-0 or 0-1-1 after three meetings.

What is a pattern is the rest of your record. You are 6-1 at home and 2-7 on the road. That is the split that will decide your season, not the three games against Pittsburgh. One more meeting against them comes on 2006-01-17.

We have won 7 in a row and I have not read a word about it anywhere. What does this club have to do?

Mirek Hruby, Bethel Park

I printed the streak in this column last week, and it has not moved: still 7, still 24 points in 14 games, still 45 for and 35 against. You play at Minnesota tomorrow, where your record so far carries no meetings, and where the Wild are on a 4-game run of their own. That is the next test.

Your GM gave The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk the answer you are looking for, in his own terms. Eric Lippmann said this about the room your players work in: "we've got equipment at 1 and practice at 1 on my building sheet." The live sum on the facilities is minus 12 across tracks. You are winning with the third-best point-per-dollar rate in the league at $2.04M a point, and your GM is telling you the infrastructure is the next thing he has to fix.

We are a different team on the road and nobody will say why. Is it the travel, or is it an excuse?

Aaron Lindqvist, Yonkers

In October I told a Sabres supporter that his club had not left the building yet. Yours has now, and the split has games behind it: 9-1 at home, 4-4 away. That is 5 points per 2 home games versus 4 points per 8 road games. The road record is exactly .500.

The buildings table carries the travel line for your club. Whether that is the whole explanation, I cannot say from the record. What I can say is that your road wins are real, your home wins are real, and the difference is 5 points per game at your own rink versus half that everywhere else. Five weeks ago there were no road games to discuss. Now there are 8, and you are four and four. That is a team that travels. Whether it is a team that could be better at travelling is a question the record will answer by March.

We pay more than anybody and I would like somebody to tell me what for.

Aaron Lindqvist, Yonkers

I printed your payroll in October. Here is what it buys after 18 games. $92.95M, 28 points, $3.32M a point. That is the 7th-best rate in the league. You lead the East.

The seat costs $82 and 14,611 people fill it. Your revenue is $43.01M and your profit is $15.05M. You are the most expensive club in the league, and you are also profitable, which is more than a club spending $48M can always claim. The answer to what the money buys: the top of the conference table, a per-point rate that is not the worst the league has seen from a big payroll, and the 6th-highest profit in the league. Whether that is enough in April is the only question that counts, and it is not mine to answer in November.

9 straight losses. Is anybody at this club going to say something, or do we all just watch?

Stanislaw Kubicki, Berwyn

I have printed a letter about every club that has won something this season. I have not printed one about a club losing 9 straight. You are right to ask.

The record says this about Chicago Blackhawks: 2-8-4, 10 points in 15 games, 47 goals against, the worst total in the league. Your home record is 1-8, also the worst in the league. Minnesota is 1-7 at home, which is the only thing close. You are 16 points behind the 8th-place club in the Western conference with 67 games left.

The payroll is $48.74M, the 4th-smallest in the league. Your $4.87M per point is the 5th-worst rate. The Bureau's Development Index carries three of your men among the league's risers: Igor Radulov80 at +11, Steve McCarthy81 at +8, and goaltender Ari Ahonen89 at +7. Those three are getting better while everything around them gets worse. That is the only good thing on the page for Berwyn. Your next game is WSH at home on 2005-11-15. They are 3-7 on the road and on a 1-game losing streak. It is not much to ask, but nine straight is enough.
