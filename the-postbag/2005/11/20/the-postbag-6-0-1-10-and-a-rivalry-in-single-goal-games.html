The Postbag: 6-0, 1-10, and a rivalry in single-goal games

Five letters from clubs the wire has left alone: a heater nobody prints, a team that folds on the road, the worst streak in the league, the worst building, and a rival in inches.

Sunday, 20 November 2005 · edited by Tunde Oyelaran

Five letters from clubs in the league that the morning headlines forgot. A heater in Nashville nobody will name, a road team that can't win on the road in Montreal, an 11-game collapse in Chicago, a building that subtracts from every man in Anaheim, and two goals across three meetings between Tampa Bay and Pittsburgh.

We have won 5 in a row and I have not read a word about it anywhere. What does this club have to do?

Earlene Whitcomb, Nashville

Nothing. You already are doing it. Nashville Predators is 9-3-2 with 28 points in 18 games, 2nd in the West, one point behind Dallas Stars. The 5-game run is the longest current streak in the conference. Their 6-0 home record is the only unblemished one in the league. David Legwand89 has 28 points in 18 games, a pace of 128 over 82, third in the league behind Markus Naslund91 and Todd Bertuzzi93. Tomas Vokoun91 is 9-3 with a 2.29 GAA and a .887 save percentage. The payroll is $51.38M, which returns $1.84M per point, 8th best of 30 clubs. The wire chases clubs with better name recognition. NSH is 8 points ahead of 8th-place Colorado Avalanche in the West with 64 games left.

We are a different team on the road and nobody will say why. Is it the travel, or is it an excuse?

Philippe Deslauriers, Verdun

Montreal Canadiens is 6-6 at home and 1-5 away. The home half is middle-of-the-division. The road half is one win in six. The Buildings table carries a travel room that feeds every man's overall, and you are right to look at it before you look at excuses. They face Pittsburgh Penguins on the road tonight, the 3rd seed in the East at 8-4-0. Richard Zednik81 has 23 points in 18 games, and Jose Theodore87 carries a 2.34 GAA and a .890 save percentage, both good numbers. The problem is that 12 of those 18 games are over, and 6 of them produced one win. A point in Pittsburgh tonight makes it 2-5 on the road, which is still not enough, but it stops the bleeding.

11 straight losses. Is anybody at this club going to say something, or do we all just watch?

Stanislaw Kubicki, Berwyn

The GM said something. Chicago Blackhawks GM Jason Hughes told The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk on November 16: "We're getting outscored and we're losing enough to make the point over and over. The first period especially, at home, 1 and 8 in our own building." He called the roster "short" and said he would not trade bodies off it. CHI is 1-10 at home, the worst home mark in the league. The road is 2-4, better in raw terms, but that is only 6 games. The 11-game losing streak is the longest active run in the league. 10 points in 17 games sits last in the West, 10 behind 8th-place Colorado Avalanche. The payroll at $48.74M costs $4.87M per point, the worst rate of any club. Deron Quint75, out with a groin since between October 20 and October 22, returns November 24. That is 4 more games before the back end gets a body back.

Does the state of this building actually cost us anything on the ice, or is that something people say?

Delphine Okonkwo, Anaheim

It is on the Bureau's Index. Mighty Ducks of Anaheim carries −3.77 on every man's overall from facilities, the worst penalty of any club in the league. The Development Index applies it before form is calculated. Jean-Sebastien Giguere97 is a 91 in the Book, a 91 with the penalty built into the number the engine works from. ANA is 5-10-1 with 17 points in 19 games, 4-8 on the road against 4-3 at home. At $54.64M payroll, that is $3.21M per point. Sergei Fedorov90 is 35, has 19 points in 19 games, and his overall is the Book's number with the −3.77 subtracted. The number sits inside the model that produces the standings, which means you cannot isolate it in one season of 17 points against a 30-club league where the roster, the coaching and the schedule all move independently. But it is the worst in the league, and you are paying $3.21M per point for the privilege.

We have played PIT 3 times and not beaten them once. At what point is that a pattern and not bad luck?

Isadora Fontaine, Brandon

Three games. Pittsburgh Penguins scored 13, Tampa Bay Lightning scored 9. Two of the three finished as one-goal games. Pittsburgh has 2 overtime wins and 1 regulation win; you have zero of any kind. The next meeting is January 17. Tampa Bay Lightning sits 6th in the East at 23 points, 2 above the 8th-place mark. Pittsburgh Penguins is 3rd at 26. Two goals across three games in a season where you have 54 total for and 47 against means this rival is eating a specific slice of your output. Three games is not a pattern you can bank on. Two of them decided by one goal is closer than the record looks. You have 1 left, and it is in Pittsburgh.
