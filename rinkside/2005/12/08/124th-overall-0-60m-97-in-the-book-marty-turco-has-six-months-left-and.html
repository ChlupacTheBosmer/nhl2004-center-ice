124th Overall, $0.60M, 97 in the Book: Marty Turco Has Six Months Left and No One Knows What He's Worth

The best goaltender in hockey played Jr. B in Ontario, won two NCAA titles, and earns entry-level money. His contract ends in June.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideThursday, 8 December 2005
ShareCopy link

Marty Turco97 sits alone at the top of the Bureau's Book among goaltenders. The overall grade reads 97, two above his base, a +2 on the Development Index. He makes $0.60M. That contract has zero years left. In June, someone will write a number that has no precedent in goaltending, or Turco will stay where he is and the Dallas Stars front office will never face the question. The rest is how a kid from Sault Ste. Marie, Ontario, who no junior team in his province wanted, ended up here.

Turco grew up in the S.S. Marie Legion program and went undrafted by the Ontario Hockey League. No major junior club took him. He signed with the Cambridge Winterhawks Jr. B in 1992, played two seasons in the Ontario Hockey Association, and accepted an NCAA scholarship to the University of Michigan. He won two NCAA championships there. The Dallas Stars Stars drafted him 124th overall in the fifth round of 1994. After graduating he played in the International Hockey League for Kalamazoo and was named IHL Rookie of the Year in 1999.

The Stars made him their starter after the 2001-02 season. Ed Belfour left for Toronto. Turco finished 2002-03 with a 1.72 GAA, a modern league record. Last spring he won 14 of 21 playoff games against Colorado. Nobody wrote a check for him.

This season carries 23 games, 15 wins, a 1.84 GAA, a 0.905 save percentage, and one shutout. His grades read GSL 99, GSH 98, INTE 98, AGIL 96, breakaway 96, endurance 96. His toughness sits at 50. His consistency sits at 51. He is not a big man at 5'11" and 183 pounds, and the Book knows it. What the Book also knows is that none of that matters when the glove is in the right place 38 times a night out of 42.

The $1.26M problem

Jean-Sebastien Giguere97 also reads 97 in the Book this week. He has 10 wins in 21 games, a 2.13 GAA, a 0.887 save percentage. The Bureau rates the two of them the same goaltender on any given night. Giguere has five years left on his deal. His cost per win, on the numbers the desk ran, comes to $1.26M a win. Turco costs $0.04M a win. Thirty times the money for fewer wins and a higher GAA.

Martin Brodeur93 has 15 wins in 21 games, a 2.00 GAA, a 95 overall, and three years remaining. His cost per win lands at $0.45M. Eleven times what Dallas Stars pays for the man with the better GAA and the better save percentage.

That $0.04M a win is the number holding up a payroll. Dallas Stars has 40 standings points in 29 games, third in this league. Their payroll runs $58.02M and their cost per point sits at $1.45M, second-cheapest in the sport behind Nashville Predators at $1.42M. Strip out the goaltending line and the math shifts. On a $58.02M budget, $0.60M looks small. But it is a structural advantage that disappears the moment Turco walks or signs a new deal.

What the room looks like without that discount

Mike Modano88 is 88, the best skater in the building. Jason Bacashihua91 is 91 in goal at 23, a +10 on the Index, and carries $0.90M with one year left. Jere Lehtinen86 and Brenden Morrow86 sit at 86, Jason Arnott85 at 85. The roster is built. The coaching is good enough to sit at 16-7-4. What is not built is the goaltending from next year forward.

Turco's morale reads 94. He has no injuries on record. He is 30 years old. His potential in the Book is 94. The Bureau thinks he is essentially done growing. The +2 on the Index is a hot streak, not a ceiling. He is done growing, and the number is coming in June.

What does a 97-grade goaltender command? The sheet does not carry a contract with that grade already in place. The Roberto Luongo deal runs $1.60M with one year left, and his overall sits at 94. Turco is three grades higher and eight years older. The market for a man who is finished growing and producing at a league-best level has no floor because no one has ever paid for that exact thing before.

The road to Sault Ste. Marie

No one at the Bureau's office, no one in the Dallas front office that picked him 124th in 1994, saw this. The encyclopaedia record is blunt: undrafted by the OHL. A man who plays Jr. B at 17 in Ontario and ends up the highest-rated goaltender alive at 30 has taken a road that has no map. Two NCAA titles at Michigan. A year in the IHL that earned him a trophy nobody outside Kalamazoo remembers. Nine seasons in the NHL before the Book gave him 97.

The Dallas Stars Stars have been here before, in reverse. They let Ed Belfour go in 2002 because Turco was cheaper and good enough. That worked for three years. Now the same arithmetic faces them in the other direction. A 30-year-old with no injury history, a +2 Index, a morale of 94, and six months of control will not take $0.60M again. The question is whether anyone else can afford him.

The last save he makes on this deal will happen sometime in April. Until then he is the cheapest $0.04M a win in hockey, and the rest of the league is paying eleven times that for a worse version of the same thing.
