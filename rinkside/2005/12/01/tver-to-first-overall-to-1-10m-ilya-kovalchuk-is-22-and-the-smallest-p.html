Tver to First Overall to $1.10M: Ilya Kovalchuk Is 22 and the Smallest Payroll in Hockey

The first Russian ever drafted first overall wore Valeri Kharlamov's number, won the Lady Byng on a team that missed the playoffs, and now leads the league's cheapest payroll in scoring while his deal runs out in June.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideThursday, 1 December 2005
ShareCopy link

The number 17 is for Valeri Kharlamov. A Soviet superstar in the 1970s, a man Ilya Kovalchuk91 had never seen play a live game, born two years before Kovalchuk himself arrived in Tver in April 1983. Kovalchuk put on 17 the first time he dressed for Atlanta Thrashers and has worn it ever since, four seasons after a club in this league made him the first Russian any team had taken with the first overall pick.

He is 22. He makes $1.10M. His contract ends this season and Atlanta holds nothing on him after June. The Thrashers' payroll sits at $40.34M, the smallest of the 30 clubs. Their 16 standings points in 23 games are second from the bottom. He has 25 points in 22 games, 15 of them goals, and a pace of 93 that places him 23rd in the scoring race. The player closest to him on the score sheet, Dany Heatley90, makes the same $1.10M and has 20 points.

The Bureau's Book has him at 90 overall today, base grade 89 plus five on the Development Index. Speed 99. Shot power 99. Hero 99. Agility 97, acceleration 98, endurance 95. Fight 50. Injury 51. The man who won the Lady Byng and the man who finishes a play at the far post share a body, and the Book gives that body a potential of 84, the Bureau's word for the likelihood of further development rather than a ceiling.

A boy from Tver who chose a dead man's number

Kovalchuk grew up in Tver, about a train ride northwest of Moscow, playing for Spartak Moscow's youth squads. He moved to their Vysshaya Liga club for two seasons before entering this league. That was the whole of his hockey life before the draft. The background sheet lists no top-flight Russian season, no major-junior year in the Canadian west, no college line. He was 18 when Atlanta took him first overall in 2001, a kid out of the Vysshaya Liga who had never once worn the sweater of a club in his own country's top division.

That is not a road the scouts here take often. The Bureau and its peers build their first-round boards out of major junior, the American college circuit, and the handful of European top flights. A teenager from a second-tier Russian club is a leap of faith for a general manager holding the first pick. Kovalchuk repaid the leap with the number on his back, 17, for Kharlamov. He had never seen the man play. He chose him anyway.

The shoulder, the goals, and the trophy nobody expected

His rookie season here ended with a shoulder. The background sheet says the injury cost him 17 games and still he scored 29 goals and 51 points in what was left. The next year brought 38 goals and 67 points. In 2003-04, at 21, he scored 41 goals, shared the Maurice "Rocket" Richard Trophy, played his first All-Star Game, and won the Lady Byng.

A player with 99 shot power and 99 speed who takes the league's sportsmanship trophy is a contradiction the numbers do not explain and do not need to. The Fight grade is 50. The overall is 90. The trophy sits in a building in Atlanta where the last playoff appearance was a year the record shows no playoff games played.

He also carried a shoulder on the injury list last March, onset somewhere between March 25 and March 30, return March 30. A single game. Nothing like the 17 games the first one cost him.

The room he is in

Atlanta Thrashers is 6-14-2. The last 10 games read LLLWWLLLWOTL and the current losing streak is four. Today they lost 1-3 at home to Washington Capitals. In 23 games they have scored 49 goals and allowed 65.

Patrik Stefan79, the center between Kovalchuk and Heatley on the top line, has 5 points in 17 games. He is out with an ankle, return date December 3. Behind Stefan, the roster carries no center above 67 overall. The best remaining player on the roster by the Book is Byron Dafoe84, a goaltender at 83 overall with a 2.76 goals-against average and a 0.864 save percentage in 18 starts.

Atlanta's payroll of $40.34M is $2.27M below the next-cheapest club. The five cheapest payrolls in the league look like this:

Atlanta is paying the least and getting the least back, which is the only way a club gets to $40.34M without the roster depth to cover for a center on the shelf and a goaltender at 0.864.

What 99 and 99 are worth in June

His salary is $1.10M. His contract has zero years left beyond this season. That means in June he will carry no obligation to Atlanta, and Atlanta will carry whatever money it can find against whatever the rest of the league decides a 22-year-old with 99 speed and 99 shot power is worth.

The sheet has the comparison the desk can print. David Legwand89 is 25, has 32 points in 22 games, and makes $8.37M with one year left. Peter Forsberg89 is 32, has 29 points in 24 games, and makes $9.90M with one year left. Both of those men carry less speed than Kovalchuk, less shot power, and less youth, and they are on contracts several multiples of his. Kovalchuk's $1.10M is what an entry-level deal from 2001 looks like when the player has not been re-signed.

The desk does not say what he will earn next season. Nobody can yet. But the numbers the sheet carries are enough: 25 points, 22 games, 22 years old, 90 overall, $1.10M, zero years left, and $40.34M of payroll around him that has produced 16 standings points and four consecutive losses.

The form and the path

The Bureau's Development Index lists Kovalchuk among its risers, up five on the form term, the fourth-smallest gain among the 20 players on that list. His base grade is 89. His potential is 84. The Bureau calls that a likelihood of further development. At 22, the sentence reads like a door left open. At 27 it would read like a door closed. The body is responding now, and the 25 points in 22 games are the proof the Index is asking for.

He scored his 15 goals in 22 games on a line that has lost its center. The next game is four days away. The Kharlamov number will be on the ice. The payroll behind it will still read $40.34M.
