What the Book Never Said: Vaclav Nedorost, +12 on the Index, and the $1.00M Center Who Leads Florida

The Panthers traded him for a defenseman and a second-round pick. Two years later he's the biggest riser in the league and their leading scorer.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideThursday, 15 December 2005
ShareCopy link

Vaclav Nedorost83 is +12 on the Bureau's Development Index. No other player in the league has climbed that far above his base grade this season. He earns $1.00M, his contract expires in June, and he leads the Florida Panthers Panthers in scoring with 24 points in 30 games.

Florida's payroll is $57.33M. The Panthers have 27 standings points, a cost of $2.12M per point, the 5th-worst in the league. Their leading scorer costs $1.00M.

From Ceske Budejovice to Hershey to nowhere

Nedorost came from HC České Budějovice, drafted 14th overall by Colorado in 2000. He played for the Hershey Bears in 2001-02 and logged 25 NHL games that season: two goals, two assists. The next year he played 42 games for the Avalanche. Two points in 25, then whatever you got in 42, was not the arithmetic that keeps a first-round pick in the building.

On July 19, 2003, Colorado traded him to Florida along with Eric Messier for Peter Worrell and a second-round selection. A middle defenseman and a pick for a first-rounder who hadn't stuck.

His first year in Florida: 25 points in 69 games, 13.4 minutes a night. A depth center, cheap and replaceable.

Then came a leg injury sometime between March 9 and March 11, 2005, and he missed the final stretch of the season, back on April 24. Whatever that window did to his summer, whatever it did to his body between April and October, this year he looks different.

The numbers this year

Nedorost has 7 goals and 17 assists in 30 games. He plays 22.5 minutes a night, nearly double last year's role. His 24 points lead the Panthers. The next closest teammate is Viktor Kozlov80 with 21, then Kristian Huselius at 19, then Olli Jokinen84, the club's highest-paid center at overall 85, with 17.

The 17 assists matter more than the 7 goals suggest. His passing grade is 96, his speed 99, his acceleration 93. He makes everyone else finish. He has the puck in his stick and finds the man with more room, and at 22.5 minutes a night that pattern repeats.

The fight grade is 50, the stubbornness 61, the hero metric 60. The Book says a man with 99 speed and a 50 fight grade survives because people cannot catch him, and this year they have not.

The biggest gap in the league

The Bureau's Development Index measures the gap between what a player's base grade says he is and what he is showing right now. Nedorost's base is 75. His current overall is 84. That +12 is the widest gap in the league this season.

Gaborik has a bigger overall, but he started at 91. Nedorost started at 75 and is producing 24 points on a 75-base body. Cammalleri's potential is 97. Nedorost's is 94, and unlike Cammalleri, who has 5 points in 9 games, he has the full season to prove it.

The room around him

Florida has 27 standings points. They sit 12th in the league. Their last ten games produced 6 wins and 4 losses, and their last three in a row were regulation losses: 3-4 to New York Islanders, 3-4 to Philadelphia, 2-4 to Edmonton. The offense dries up around him.

David Aebischer86 started 23 games in goal at a 2.43 GAA and a .875 save percentage. He was traded to the Panthers at some point before the record opened. Jay Bouwmeester82, 22, is their top defenseman at overall 83. The payroll is spread thin across a roster that has 27 points on 30 games played. Nedorost has 24 of them.

The Panthers' revenue is $49.07M against a $57.33M payroll. They profit $20.52M anyway. The roster is cheap for what the club takes in, and $2.12M per standings point makes a front office nervous about every line item. The cheapest line item is the one they are about to lose.

The contract year

Nedorost's deal pays him $1.00M and has 0 years left. Come June he is a free agent. His morale is 100. He is 23 years old, 6'0, 190 pounds, and he has 99 speed.

What is that worth in a league where the average first-round pick signs for three or four million and the top centers clear seven? The Bureau's Book says he is an 84 overall with potential 94. The Index says the Book is underselling him by another 12 points right now. If the form holds, if the Index converges with the base at some point, the player who becomes a free agent in June is not the one Florida traded a defenseman and a second-round pick for.

Colorado got Peter Worrell. He was 25 when he arrived in Denver, and the trade happened almost two and a half years ago. No one in Colorado is asking about this one anymore. In Sunrise, they are looking at a man who plays 22.5 minutes, leads their team in points, earns one million dollars, and will ask for what the Index says he is worth.

The leg is fine and the speed is the same, but 17 assists is not something this body has done before.
