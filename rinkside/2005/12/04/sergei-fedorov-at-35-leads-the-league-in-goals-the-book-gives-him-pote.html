Sergei Fedorov at 35 Leads the League in Goals. The Book Gives Him Potential 61.

He defected from the Soviet Union at 20. Thirty-five years old, 74th overall in 1989, he has 21 goals and no one has more.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideSunday, 4 December 2005
ShareCopy link

Sergei Fedorov90 has 21 goals in 25 games. That is the most in this league. Jarome Iginla92 sits one behind at 20. Keith Tkachuk88 and David Legwand88 have 18 each. Fedorov is 35 years old. His overall is 86. His potential is 61, which is the Book's way of saying the man will not grow from here.

He doesn't need to.

Fedorov was born on December 13, 1969, in Pskov, Russia. The background sheet carries what happened next: in August 1989 a man named Gave met Fedorov and teammate Vladimir Konstantinov and slipped each of them a Red Wings media guide with a letter hidden inside. By July of 1990 Fedorov had decided to defect. The Soviet national team was in Portland for the Goodwill Games. On July 22 he walked away.

Detroit had drafted him 74th overall in the third round, a pick nobody remembers making because it turned into Sergei Fedorov. He won the Hart in 1993-94, the season Wayne Gretzky watched from a nearby bench and said afterward that he had never seen a player dominate the game the way Sergei did. Gretzky told anyone who would listen that Fedorov was the best player in the game. He won the Selke in 1996 after scoring 39 goals and 107 points while playing the other end too. He scored five goals in one game against Washington on December 26, 1996, all of Detroit's in a 5-4 overtime win. The Wings won the Cup in 1997 and 1998.

In the summer of 1998 he signed an offer sheet with Carolina worth up to $38 million, including bonuses. Detroit matched. That was the money story of the decade.

The Book has its own record of Fedorov, and it starts in season 0. That first one, Fedorov scored 76 goals and 55 assists for 131 points in 82 games and won the Lester B. Pearson Award. Last season he had 71 goals, 40 assists, 111 points. This season he is at 21 in 25, which paces to 69 goals over 82.

Three seasons on record. 76 goals, 71 goals, a 69-goal pace. He has never dropped below 69 in any season the record carries. The Book's potential grade of 61 says the chance of further development is low. He has been scoring at this level for so long that further development is beside the point. He needs to stay.

The grades and the man

Fedorov's speed grade is 91. His passing is 90. His puck handling is 90. His deking is 93. His shot power is 88. His fight grade is 50, and his injury resistance is 50, and his shot blocking is 52. He does not block shots. He does not fight. He skates past you, he passes around you, he shoots over you, and at 35 he has more goals than anyone in the league.

His morale is 94. The Development Index has him below his base, which sits at 94; the overall reads 86 today. He has been here before. In the fall of 1997 he held out over a contract, sat home, and came back to win another Cup. Men at 35 with morale of 94 and a fight grade of 50 are not men who need the Index to validate them. They just need to keep showing up in October.

What Anaheim gets

Mighty Ducks of Anaheim runs a $54.64M payroll, sits with 25 standings points, and costs $2.19M per point. They have their Vezina winner from last season in Jean-Sebastien Giguere97, who carries a 93 overall and is giving them a 2.21 goals-against average this year. Around Fedorov: Vyacheslav Kozlov78 at 29 points in 24 games, Vaclav Prospal77 on the shelf until 2005-12-17 with an ankle, and a room that over their last ten games scored 24 and allowed 28.

Fedorov makes $6.30M with 3 years left. In a room where the per-point cost is $2.19M, a man producing 29 points through 25 games is the cheapest thing in the building. What matters more is that the 21 goals at the top of the column belong to a man the grading system finished with a long time ago.

The room he plays in

Anaheim's best center is 35 years old. Their best right wing is 33. Their best left wing is on the shelf until December 17 with an ankle. Kozlov has 7 goals and 22 assists. That is a different kind of player, a set-up man whose speed grade and puck grade are nowhere near Fedorov's. The Ducks built a room around a 35-year-old center with a potential of 61 and a goaltender whose name is already on the Vezina. It is a short window and a narrow one.

Fedorov will not grow from here. The Book is right about that. He scored 76 goals the first season this league wrote down. He scored 71 the second. He is at a 69-goal pace now, 35 years old, 200 pounds, a shot grade of 88 that still beats the league's defencemen and a speed grade of 91 that makes the 50 in fight and the 50 in injury look like a joke the Book makes about a man who decided in July 1990 to walk out of a hotel in Portland and never go back. He is not growing. He is arriving, every October, into the same body he has had since he was 20, and the body still does what it did then.

Twenty-one goals in 25 games. The most in this league. Potential 61 and morale 94 and a contract with 3 years left. The Book said what it said. Sergei Fedorov is still scoring.
