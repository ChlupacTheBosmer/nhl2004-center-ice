The Cheapest 82 Points in Hockey: Mike Komisarek at $1.10M, +10 on the Index, and 6 Months From a Decision

The 23-year-old defenseman from West Islip played 30.4 minutes a night last year, scored 82 points, and is on a contract that ends in June. Montreal has 36 standings points and no answer for what comes next.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideThursday, 22 December 2005
ShareCopy link

Mike Komisarek81 was 14 years old when he played the Quebec International Pee-Wee Hockey Tournament with the New York Islanders' minor ice hockey team. He grew up on Long Island, played two years of Varsity hockey at St. Anthony's High School, then went to the New England Jr. Coyotes under Gary Dineen, then to USA Hockey's National Team Development Program, then to the University of Michigan, where he was a First Team Division I All-American at 20. The Montreal Canadiens Canadiens took him seventh overall in 2001. He signed on July 24, 2002, at 20 years old.

He has been in Montreal for three seasons now, and his contract expires this June.

Three points in 24 games as a 20-year-old. Eighty-two points the following year at 21. Now 29 in 33 games at 22, with 62 penalty minutes, more than he had in the whole of last season.

The Bureau's Development Index has him at +10 today, which means his overall of 83 sits ten points above the Book's base grade of 77. He is 23 years old, his potential is 92, and the 9 points between them are closing.

What the grades say about the man on the ice

Komisarek is 6 feet 3 inches and 225 pounds with a speed grade of 95, a shot power of 93, and acceleration at 91. Those numbers describe a defenseman who can skate with anyone, fire a puck from the point, and close the gap on a forward who has gotten behind the defense. His checking is 89, his agility 87, his aggression 84. He is, by the Book's arithmetic, a physical force who moves like a winger.

The bottom of his card tells you what he hasn't figured out yet. Fighting is 51. Puck-issuing backhand is 52. Read of the defensive break is 55. He is still learning to protect himself, still finding where to put the puck under pressure, still waiting on the ability to read a rush before it arrives. These are the numbers of a man who has been thrown into 32.1 minutes a night on a team with 36 standings points and asked to carry the load.

He has 62 penalty minutes in 33 games this season, 1.88 per game, which puts him 17th in the league. He has never had a fight on the record. The Book gives him aggression of 84 and fighting of 51, and those two numbers together describe a man who finishes his checks but does not drop the gloves, and who pays for it in penalties because he cannot scare anyone into steering clear.

The room around him

Montreal Canadiens finished last season with 21 wins, 46 losses, 66 points, 238 goals for, 295 goals against. They have 36 standings points this year and a last-ten record that reads OTLWOTLLLWOTLWLW. Their payroll is $54.89M and they are paying $1.52M per standings point.

Komisarek's salary is $1.10M. That is less than one-fifth of what Montreal spends per standings point. He plays more minutes than anyone on the roster. He is the team's highest-rated defenseman by 6 points (his overall of 83 against Pascal Trepanier64's 77, if you look at the best under-23s table where Komisarek sits at 83). His teammates by overall grade are Jose Theodore87 at 89, Dwayne Roloson82 at 84, and Richard Zednik81 at 83. Nobody else on the team is above 83.

He is the best player on a team that has not won two games in a row since October.

The contract that does not say anything yet

$1.10M, zero years left. That is what the Bureau's Contract Ledger carries for him today. He is on the list of players with one year remaining and the contract-year table sorts by overall grade, which means at 83 he is the 14th-best player in the league heading into unrestricted free agency in July. The man above him on that list is Patrick Marleau81 at $7.57M. The man below him is Jeff Friesen88 at $7.08M.

Komisarek is in the middle of them at $1.10M. He is 23. He scored 82 points at 21. He is +10 on the Index, which puts him in the top three risers in the league alongside Vaclav Nedorost80 at +12 and Marian Gaborik99 at +11.

What West Islip looks like from Montreal

Komisarek told The Tunnel's Marcus Bell nothing this fortnight, and there is no quote from him on the record. He sits in his room in Montreal, a kid from Suffolk County who played Varsity hockey at a Catholic high school and then two seasons in Ann Arbor for Red Berenson, and he has 33 games left in the only contract he has ever signed in this league.

The Book has his potential at 92. His speed is 95, his shot power is 93. His read of the defensive break is 55, but he is 23 years old, he plays 32.1 minutes a night, and he has never been injured on the record.

The contract ends in June. The Montreal Canadiens Canadiens have 36 standings points and a payroll of $54.89M. Somewhere in the next 168 days, before what the calendar has as the rookie draft, someone in Montreal's front office is going to have to sit down and decide what 82-point hockey costs when the man giving it to you is still figuring out where to put the puck on the backhand side and still cannot read the rush.
