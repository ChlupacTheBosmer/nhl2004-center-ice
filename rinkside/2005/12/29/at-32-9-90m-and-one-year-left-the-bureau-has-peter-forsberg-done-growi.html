At 32, $9.90M, and One Year Left: The Bureau Has Peter Forsberg Done Growing

He came to Quebec as a teenager in the Lindros trade. The question that summer is whether any club pays a man with Potential 61 franchise money again.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideThursday, 29 December 2005
ShareCopy link

Peter Forsberg89 is 32 years old, plays left wing for the Colorado Avalanche at 22.6 minutes a night, and earns $9.90M. His contract expires in June. The Bureau's Book gives him an overall of 95 today, which is two points below his base grade of 97. His potential reads 61.

Potential in the Book measures how likely further development is. At 61, the Bureau has stopped waiting for more from him. He peaked last season at 129 points. He is scoring at a 93 pace now.

Twenty-three goals in 36 games. Forty-two penalty minutes, up from 32 last year, the most he has carried in any full season on the record. His ice time climbed from 21.4 minutes to 22.6. Morale 100.

Born ten days apart, now $5.50M apart

Forsberg and Markus Näslund91 were born in Örnsköldsvik, Sweden, ten days apart in July 1973. They played on the same regional all-star team at the under-16 TV-pucken championship in 1988. They attended the same high school. At 18 they both worked summers at the same electrical company: Näslund's mother worked there, Forsberg's father worked there, and both boys wired houses on the same crew.

Now Näslund leads the league with 56 points in 36 games, paces at 128 per 82, and earns $4.40M on a deal that also expires in June. Forsberg has 41 points, paces at 93, and earns $9.90M. They are the same age. Both are one year from free agency. The difference in their salaries is $5.50M, and the Bureau gives Näslund a base grade of 89 to Forsberg's 97.

Two boys who wired houses on the same crew, one making more than twice the other.

The trade that made him a property

Forsberg was drafted 6th overall by Philadelphia in 1991, the first European selected that year. The Hockey News had him at 25th in its preview. The Philadelphia media called it a reach. The Flyers' general manager Russ Farwell and chief European scout Inge Hammarström said time would prove them right.

It did not. Forsberg went to Quebec in the Lindros deal along with four other players, two first-round picks, and $15M. The background sheet calls it "one of the most one-sided deals in sports history." Forsberg was 18 and still in Sweden when it happened. He finished the 1992-93 season with Modo, won the Guldpucken and the Guldhjälmen, and arrived in North America for the Nordiques the following fall.

He has never worn another NHL club's sweater.

Four contracts, $29.45M, and a room that makes $1.62M per point

Colorado carries a $66.38M payroll against 41 standings points. That is $1.62M per point. Four of the club's highest-paid players have one year left on their deals:

What the Index has at minus one

The Bureau's Development Index has Forsberg at minus one today. His base is 97, his current overall reads 95, the one-point gap is all the movement the Index finds. He is drifting, one point at a time, in the direction the potential grade already pointed.

At his earliest season on record: 101 points, 19.8 minutes a night, 37 penalty minutes. Last season: 129 points, 21.4 minutes, 32 penalty minutes. This year at 32, halfway through: 93 pace, 22.6 minutes, 42 penalty minutes. The minutes went up, the production went down, the penalty minutes went up. A player pushing to hold the number.

He is doing it with his three weakest grades reading 50, 54, and 59. The grades that carry him are his hands and his vision: Deking 99, HERO 99, Passing 99, Puck Control 98, Accuracy 95, Speed 95. Those grades do not decay as fast as the ones that ask a man to hit a defenseman along the wall.

What June asks

At 33, with potential 61, Forsberg enters a market where the clubs that will pay $9.00M-plus for a forward are the same clubs with payroll advantages. New York Rangers already carries $92.95M for 48 points. Colorado Avalanche itself would have to build around Sakic, Oates, and Foote while paying a 33-year-old franchise money again.

The other option is what happened to everyone else: a shorter deal at a lower number. He has been happy at 100 morale. He plays 22.6 minutes a night. The room turned toward him last season when he carried it to 129 points, and it is asking him to do it again this year while the calendar runs out.

He came over as a teenager in a deal that the rest of the league still calls one-sided. The question that summer is whether anyone at 33 pays the same money a 32-year-old makes.

His friend from the same high school in Örnsköldsvik has the same question on a smaller contract, and leads the league while waiting for the answer.
