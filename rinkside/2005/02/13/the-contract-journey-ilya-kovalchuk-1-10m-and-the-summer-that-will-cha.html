The Contract Journey: Ilya Kovalchuk, $1.10M, and the Summer That Will Change Atlanta

He's 21, the cheapest point in hockey, and his team has never made the playoffs. June will force them to pick.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideSunday, 13 February 2005
ShareCopy link

At $11,702 a point, Ilya Kovalchuk90 is the cheapest goal-scorer in the league. The man at the other end of the table, Mario Lemieux88 in Pittsburgh, costs $122,209 a point. Kovalchuk is 21 years old, from Tver, Russia, and he will be a free agent in five months.

A first-overall pick on an entry-level salary, producing at a 143 pace, on a club with 54 standings points and no playoff series win in its history. The Atlanta Thrashers Thrashers will have to decide this summer whether they can afford the kind of money that number demands, and whether the team they build around it can ever give him what the number says he deserves.

The boy from Tver

Kovalchuk played youth hockey for Spartak Moscow, then two seasons on Spartak's senior team in the Vysshaya Liga before the 2001 draft made him the first Russian selected first overall in NHL history. He wears 17 for Valeri Kharlamov, the Soviet winger who won eight world titles in the 1970s. A kid in Tver picks a number because a man on a television screen made him feel something, and then twenty-two years later he is scoring 44 goals in 54 games on a team that sits twentieth in the league.

His rookie year in 2001-02 ended with a shoulder that put him out 17 games. He came back and scored 29 anyway. His second year: 38 goals, 67 points. His third: 72 goals, a share of the Richard Trophy with Jarome Iginla94, an All-Star Game. Now his fourth season is 54 games in and the pace says 143 points, which would top his 134 from last year.

What the Book sees

The Bureau has him at 91 today. The Development Index adds 5 to his base of 89, which means his form is up from where the Book's long-term grade sits. At 21, with a potential rating of 86, the Bureau thinks there is more in the tank but not a lot more. He is close to what he will be.

He is a body built for scoring. Speed 99. Shot power 99. Acceleration 99. Agility 98. Deke 95. Heroic 95. These are finished grades. His game is finding him a way to score from every possible angle.

The weaknesses are the ones you can build around or the ones you cannot. Fighting 50, faceoffs 51, injury resistance 52. He does not hit you. He does not win puck battles in the corners. He goes out and he scores, and the team has to be organized enough that he is not asked to do anything else. Atlanta has not been that team.

The gap

Dany Heatley88 has 87 points. Kovalchuk has 94. Between them they account for 181 of the club's offense. The third forward has 58. The fourth has 43. The center has 42 and is on the injured list with a foot injury that keeps him out until 2005-03-14.

Two generational wingers, a center who cannot stay healthy, a roster where the fifth-best scorer this season has 43 points on the year. The payroll is $36.79M, the second-smallest in the league. The revenue is $52.02M. They sell 14,005 tickets at $78 a night to watch a team that is 19 and 26 and 2.

The contract

$1.10M. One year left. Morale 95. He likes it there. The question is whether liking it is enough when June arrives and 22-year-olds with 72-goal seasons go shopping.

Atlanta's payroll would need to roughly double if they wanted to sign Kovalchuk at the money his production commands and still fill a roster around him. At $36.79M, they are already near the floor of the league. If they commit $7M, $8M, $9M to one man, the rest of the room gets thinner. If they do not commit, they lose him.

This is the contract journey the league has seen before, always from the outside: a kid comes in cheap, develops into something the team cannot reproduce, and the franchise discovers that its economics were never built to hold him. The difference here is that Kovalchuk is the cheapest elite scorer in the league on a per-point basis, and he is 21. The next cheapest forward in that class is Heatley at $12,643, also from Atlanta, also at $1.10M with one year left. They are the two cheapest point-producers in the league, and they are teammates, and their team has 54 points.

What the wins look like

Atlanta Thrashers has won three straight. They beat Ottawa 3-2 on the road, New Jersey 2-1 on the road, Montreal 4-3 on the road. Three road wins, three low-scoring games, a stretch that looks like a team figuring out how to win without its star carrying every shift. They scored 30 goals in those eight wins over the last ten games, which is not dominant but is enough when the goaltending holds.

Kovalchuk has 44 goals in 54 games. He is on pace for 67, which is a step back from last year's 72. His assists are up (50 through 54 games, pace of 76, against 62 over 82 last year). The game is changing a little. He is finding the second man more often. Whether that is growth or whether it is the team needing him to distribute because nobody else can finish, the sheet does not say.

$1.10M. One year. A team that has never been to a playoff series. A kid from Tver who wears 17 for a man he watched on television when he was small enough to fit in a doorframe. Atlanta's room may not be big enough for what he has become. June is 134 days away. The money will be the money. The question is whether he will let them find out.
