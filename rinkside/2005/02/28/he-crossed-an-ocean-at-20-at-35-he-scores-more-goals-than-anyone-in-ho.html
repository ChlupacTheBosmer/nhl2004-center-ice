He Crossed an Ocean at 20. At 35, He Scores More Goals Than Anyone in Hockey.

Sergei Fedorov has 65 goals in 64 games for Anaheim, a 122-point pace, a speed grade of 98, and 4 years left on $6.30M. The Development Index has him going backward.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideMonday, 28 February 2005
ShareCopy link

Sergei Fedorov90 has 65 goals in 64 games for Mighty Ducks of Anaheim. No one else in the league has more. He is 35 years old, he plays 23.0 minutes a night, and the Development Index has him one grade below the Book's base of 94. His potential sits at 61, which is the Bureau's way of saying the climb is over. The goals keep going up anyway.

He scored 76 goals last year in 82 games and won the Pearson Award, the one his peers vote for. This year he has 65 in 64, which is the same rate wearing a different jersey. Speed grade 98, shot power 95, deke 94. At 35, on a club that has never made the playoffs, Sergei Fedorov is the most prolific goal scorer in hockey while the league's grading system has decided he is finished.

The road that brought him here

Fedorov was born in Pskov, Russia, in December 1969. Detroit drafted him 74th overall in 1989, a pick nobody outside the Wings' war room would have staked a career on. He was 19 when a Red Wings employee slipped a media guide to him in Moscow with a letter hidden inside. He defected in July 1990 at the Goodwill Games in Portland, stepping off a Soviet national team plane into a country that did not know his name yet.

By 1993-94, Wayne Gretzky said he had never seen a player dominate a game the way Fedorov did. That year Fedorov won the Hart Trophy. He added a Selke in 1996, the year he scored 39 goals and 107 points while playing the best defensive center in hockey. He scored all five of Detroit's goals in a single game on December 26, 1996, and led the Wings' playoff scoring that spring as they won the Stanley Cup. He did it again in 1998.

A man who was, by the assessment of the greatest player alive, the best player in the game, is 35 and scoring at a 122-point pace for the Anaheim Ducks, a franchise that has never made the postseason. The sheet starts here, but the road before the sheet is what makes these numbers strange.

What the Book says, and what the goals say

The Book has Fedorov's base at 94. The Index has him at minus 1, which puts his overall at 90 today. His potential grade is 61. Read together, those three numbers say what the Bureau believes: the peak is behind him, the remaining seasons are maintenance.

Markus Naslund89 has 63 in 63 for Vancouver Canucks, a player three years younger with a grade of 89. Jarome Iginla94 has 57 in 58 at 27 with a grade of 91. Fedorov is older than both, graded between them, and ahead of both.

His 30 assists in 64 games are the other half. Last year he had 55 assists in 82. This year the playmaking has thinned and the shooting has held. The goals have become a larger share of his production than they were last season, a shift that reads like a man whose legs have started to tell him to arrive earlier and less often. The 23.0 minutes a night suggest the coach has not gotten that message. His fighting grade is 50 and his injury resistance is 51, and the sheet carries no injury for him this season.

The contract and the club

Mighty Ducks of Anaheim has 78 points this season and has never made the postseason in its history. They spend $41.01M, fifth from the bottom in the league. They make $28.52M in profit, the kind of margin that keeps a building open but does not buy a second line. Their last ten games read LOTLWWWWLLWW, five wins in ten, 25 goals for and 24 against, a team grinding.

Fedorov is on $6.30M with 4 years left. At 35, that is the Ducks committing $25.20M to a man whose potential grade is 61. His morale is 94. His best teammate is Jean-Sebastien Giguere97 at 97, the goaltender who carried his own career through the worst stretches and now shares a room with a 35-year-old center who scores 65 goals. After Giguere the next name on the roster is Vaclav Prospal80 at 80. The room around him is thin.

The age and the grade

There are players over 33 in this league who still carry their clubs. Mario Lemieux88 is 39 and scoring at a 137 pace. Jaromir Jagr84 is 33 and second in the league. But neither of them is 35 and leading the league in goals on the fifth-cheapest payroll in hockey.

The Development Index measures form against base. Fedorov at minus 1 means his current play sits below what the Bureau expected from a 94-rated player. That is a fair reading of a man whose assists have dropped. It is a harder reading when the goals are the most in hockey, the speed grade is 98, and the body has not missed a game.

The Bureau looked at a 35-year-old with a 94 base and said: there is no upward room. Four more years on $6.30M, in Anaheim, far from Pskov, far from Detroit, far from Portland, in a league that drafted him 74th and never quite knew what it had found.

He crossed an ocean as a teenager, won everything the league gives, and is now scoring more goals than anyone alive at 23.0 minutes a night for a club with 78 points and no playoff berth. The potential grade is 61. The goals are the most in hockey. Both numbers are on the sheet.
