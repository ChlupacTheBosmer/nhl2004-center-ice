The Man Who Went Back: Sandis Ozolinsh, 33 Minutes a Night, and the Room That Kept Him

Traded away in 2002 for the defenceman he now skates beside. At 32, he leads all blueliners in assists, and [[team:CAR]] has won six straight.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideSunday, 6 February 2005
ShareCopy link

Sandis Ozolinsh84 took the ice in Carolina Hurricanes's home sweater on the night of January 29, Toronto Maple Leafs sitting across from him with 85 points, and played 33.0 minutes. The club had won six in a row. The room was full. Nobody traded him away this time.

He is 32, he has 53 assists in 53 games, and he is the most-used defenceman on a club that has never won a playoff series. His contract reads $5.10M with 3 years left. His morale is 95. The overall the Bureau carries for him is 82, and the Book grades the rest: ENDU 99, HERO 91, SHPW 89, SPEE 89, DEKG 87, AGIL 85. The bottom reads FACE 50, FIGH 50, INJU 50. He cannot win a draw, cannot fight, cannot take a hit, and he plays 33 minutes a night anyway.

The road that brought him back

Ozolinsh was born in Riga, Latvia, in August 1972. The Bureau took him 30th overall in the 1991 draft, a first-round pick. He went to the Kansas City Blades of the IHL and won a Turner Cup in 1992. He reached San Jose Sharks the next season, played 37 games, lost most of the year to a knee, and then scored 64 points as a sophomore, 26 goals among them, which led all defencemen in the league.

In October 1995 he went to Colorado Avalanche for Owen Nolan. Fifty points in 66 games, the Stanley Cup in 1996, a career-high 68 points in 1996-97. Third in the Norris voting that year. Six years in Denver. Then June 2000: Colorado Avalanche sent him to Carolina for Nolan Pratt72 and draft picks. Carolina gave him a five-year deal worth more than $25M. He played one and a half seasons there. The team did not make the playoffs.

In January 2002, Carolina traded him to Florida Panthers for Bret Hedican, a forward, a defenceman, and a draft pick. The man they got back, Hedican, is still on the blue line in Raleigh. He has 9 goals and 27 assists this season. He plays 1037 minutes. Ozolinsh plays 1751.

The record opened on December 1, 2004. Ozolinsh was on Carolina Hurricanes's roster that day, and every day since. The transactions do not say how he came back, only that he is here, 32, playing 33 minutes a night on a team that needed him back.

The 99 and the 50s

The Bureau's Book gives Ozolinsh an endurance grade of 99. He has not missed a game. 53 of 53, every night, 1751 minutes. The speed is 89, the shot power is 89, the deke is 87. Those are the tools of the 22-year-old who scored 68 points for Colorado and finished third in the Norris race.

What the Book also says: he is 75 inches, 205 pounds, with an INJU of 50. When a winger runs at him at the boards, the grade says he comes off second. FIGH 50 means he will not drop them. FACE 50 means he is not winning a draw in his own zone. He has 6 penalty minutes in 53 games, which has nothing to do with care; 33 minutes a night does not leave time for the box.

The potential the Bureau carries for him is 61. At 32, there is no next level to climb toward. The development that was promised in 1991, in Riga, at the Blades' rink, is done. What is left is the endurance and the speed and the shot, and the 33 minutes a night that the coach takes from him because there is no one else who can play that many without stopping.

The room on the other side of the glass

Carolina Hurricanes is 19-14-9, 69 points in 53 games. They scored 4 at Tampa Bay Lightning on January 30, beat Toronto Maple Leafs 6-2 the night before, have a six-game win streak, and play in front of 16435 people at $80 a seat. Their payroll is $55.96M, their cost per point $0.81M. They have 0 Cups. They have never won a playoff series.

The man and the money

Ozolinsh is not in the contract-year class. Three years left, $5.10M, a man who turns 35 before the last season ends. He signed the five-year, $25M deal in 2000 when Carolina was trying to build something around him. It did not work. They traded him, they traded the man they got for him, and then, somehow, they had both of them again.

The last season was the floor: 21 games, 1 goal, 14 assists. Something happened in those 61 days. This season there are no gaps, no 21-game stretches. 53 games, 53 assists, 33 minutes a night, 6 penalty minutes, a speed grade that reads 89 and a shot that reads 89 and an endurance that reads 99. The numbers say what they always said about this man: he will play 33 minutes and he will find your winger with a tape pass before your winger sees it coming, but he will not fight you, he will not face off, and he will not take the hit.

At 32, he came back to the club that let him go, and the club kept him. The man with the 99 endurance plays every game because the 50 in FIGH and the 50 in INJU mean he already knows what happens when he stops, so he does not stop.
