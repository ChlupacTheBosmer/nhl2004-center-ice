Mario Lemieux Is 39, the Highest-Paid Player in Hockey, and the Record Keeps No Trophy for Him

The first pick of 1984 has five years left on the biggest contract in the league, a passing grade of 96, and 82 points in 49 games.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideSaturday, 19 February 2005
ShareCopy link

Mario Lemieux88 is 39 years old, the highest-paid player in the league at $9.90M, and he has five years left on the contract.

The Pittsburgh Penguins center has 82 points in 49 games this season, a 137 pace, at 20.1 minutes a night. The Bureau's Book has him at 85. His passing grade is 96.

Twenty-one years after Pittsburgh called his name first overall, the league's record keeps no trophy for him.

The sweater he would not wear

The 1984 draft was held in Montreal, his own city. Lemieux and his agent had deadlocked with the Penguins over a contract, and when Pittsburgh took him first overall he did not shake general manager Eddie Johnston's hand and he did not put on the sweater. He said afterward that Pittsburgh did not want him badly enough.

Johnston signed him anyway. Two years at $600,000 plus a $150,000 signing bonus.

The Civic Arena carried the draft on a broadcast that day. More than 3,000 people in Pittsburgh watched it. A typical Penguins game at the time drew fewer than 7,000.

What the hands still do

He is 76 inches, 225 pounds, shoots right, born in Montreal on 1965-10-05.

Last season, at 38, he played all 82 games: 74 goals and 76 assists, 150 points, 26 penalty minutes, 21.8 minutes a game. That point total was the sixth-best in the league, one spot behind Glen Murray83 and ahead of Eric Daze87.

This season the games have come more slowly. He has 49 of them, 39 goals and 43 assists for 82 points, 8 penalty minutes, 20.1 minutes a night. He sits 30th in the scoring table with 33 games left to play.

The grades read the way a 39-year-old's should read if he spent his life on the puck. Passing 96. Deking 95. Puck handling 92. Speed 88. Shot power 87. The fighting grade is 50, strength on the puck 52, toughness 58. He missed a few days in January with a rib, sometime between Jan 19 and Jan 22, and was back on the 23rd.

The room around him

The best man beside him is Martin Straka85, 82 on the Book, with Johan Hedberg87 at the same number in goal. Behind Hedberg sit Sebastien Caron79 at 76 and Jean-Sebastien Aubin77 at 74. Up front there is Aleksey Morozov79 at 75, and Eric Meloche77 at 74, who last season won the Calder with 88 points in 82 games and has not scored at that rate since.

Pittsburgh has 56 points. The payroll is $44.95M, $0.80M for every point in the table, and the club turned a $13.40M profit on $51.77M of revenue. Attendance runs 14,062 a night at $79 a ticket. The prestige figure is 354, there is no Cup in the building, and last spring the club was not in the playoffs at all.

The last ten games read OTLLWOTLLLOTLWWL, 21 goals for and 27 against.

The money and the years

Lemieux signed a deal worth $9.90M a season, and it runs five years. No one in the league is paid more. Joe Sakic90 is at $9.40M and Chris Pronger86 at $9.50M, and neither has the years left that Lemieux does.

The club he carried out of the deadlock in 1984 was bankrupt nine years before he arrived and drawing fewer than 7,000 a night when he did. Pittsburgh now draws 14,062 and clears $13.40M a year. He was with the club before the record opened on 2004-12-01, and nothing has moved him out of it.

The passing grade read 96 when he was young and it reads 96 at 39, on the biggest check in the sport, with five years still to run on a contract that asks him to be the player he has always been.
