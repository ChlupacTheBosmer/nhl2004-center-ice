The 154 Pace: Bill Guerin Is 34, the League's Fastest Scorer, and 87 on the Book

Fifth pick in 1989, Stanley Cup winner in 1995, two trades and a hometown call behind him. At 34, on $8.80M in Dallas, he is scoring faster than anyone in hockey and the Bureau still hasn't caught up.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideSaturday, 12 February 2005
ShareCopy link

Bill Guerin was 18 years old when the New Jersey Devils took him fifth overall in the 1994 draft, three years before the Cup in June 1995 and ten before the phone call that sent him to Edmonton. He has been 34 all season. On the night that counts, which is tonight, he has scored 52 goals in 56 games and done it at a 154 pace, the highest rate in this league. Jagr is at 152. Naslund is at 152. Guerin is at 154.

The Bureau's Book has him at 87. The Development Index places him one below his base grade. Neither of those numbers explains what 52 goals and a 154 pace look like on a man entering his sixteenth season.

The pace nobody built for

Dallas Stars pays $66.88M, second in the league behind Detroit. Guerin's $8.80M is the third-largest salary on that roster, behind Mike Modano86 and Marty Turco95 combined, and his 105 points on the season are the second-highest total behind Jagr's 106. But pace is not the same as volume, and at 154 he clears Jagr by two full points per 82. His prior career-best pace, set in 2003-04 on 93 points in 58 games, was 131. He has added 23 points of pace in a single year at 34.

He has played for four clubs in the record's span of memory. Before that: eleven seasons in the East, the Cup at 24, a 41-goal year in Boston that got him the five-year contract in Dallas. He has always been good. He has never been this good.

What the Book says and what the goals say

The Book's per-attribute grades on Guerin tell you who he is with blunt accuracy. Shot power: 92. Speed: 90. Endurance: 88. Checking: 87. Hero: 92. Faceoffs: 51. Fighting: 52. A man with a 92 shot and a 90 speed and a 51 faceoff win is a goal scorer and has never pretended to be anything else. The Bureau sees that. They rate it at 87 overall, below players like Naslund (91) and Kovalchuk (92) who do not have his shot power or his endurance.

The Development Index has him at negative one. That means the Index sees his current play as one grade below what his base should produce. In a season where he is the fastest-scoring player in hockey. The Index is a form measure, not a results measure. It tracks trajectory. And right now it says Guerin is settling. The 154 says otherwise.

His morale sits at 92. He told nobody anything this fortnight. He has not needed to.

The room and the money

Marty Turco at 94 overall, Mike Modano at 88 and 89 points on the year, Jason Arnott83 at 85, Brenden Morrow84 at 84. This is a club with four players the Bureau rates at 84 or above in the forward group and a goaltender at 94. Dallas has 74 points through 56 games, a .634 point percentage, and sits behind only Toronto, New Jersey, and Edmonton in the league standings. They won the Presidents' Trophy last season. This year they are chasing the same thing.

Guerin arrived in Dallas as a free agent in the summer of 2002, after 41 goals in Boston earned him the five-year deal. The contract has two years left beyond this one. He is 34. His speed grade is 90. His endurance is 88. These are not the grades of a man slowing down. The Bureau's Development Index disagrees, and maybe it will be right in March. But March has not come yet, and tonight 52 goals is 52 goals.

The road that made him

He grew up in Worcester, Massachusetts. Drafted fifth overall in 1989 by New Jersey. Won the Cup in 1995 at 24. In 1997-98 the Devils traded him to Edmonton for Jason Arnott and Bryan Muir. He spent parts of four seasons in Alberta. In November 2001 he went home, to Boston. One season and a half later the hometown experiment ended and the five-year deal brought him to Texas.

The man who drafted him is not on the record. The man who traded him to Edmonton is not on the record. What is on the record is the speed grade: 90 today, the same number it was when he was 22, and the shot power: 92, which the Bureau says is the same grade as the one that put the puck in the net nine times in ten playoff games last spring.

A 154 pace is what happens when a man with a 92 shot and a 90 speed finds a room that puts him in position 20.5 minutes a night and lets him shoot. Dallas does that. Modano draws the center ice attention. Arnott wins the faceoffs (Guerin's 51 means he is not winning his own). Guerin shoots, scores, and leaves the rest to men who do it better.

What it costs

$8.80M. Three years left. At 34, with two seasons guaranteed beyond this one, Dallas is paying for a man the Index believes is falling and the goals believe is climbing. The per-point cost of the entire Dallas payroll is $0.90M per league point, the fourth-cheapest in the league. If Guerin finishes at his current pace, his personal cost per point lands at roughly $0.84M, below the club average.

He is not the best player on this team by the Book. That is Turco at 94. He is not the best by points; that is Jagr's 106, two more than Guerin's 105 across one more game. But pace is the number that says who is scoring fastest tonight, in February. And the number is 154, and the name attached to it belongs to a man who was drafted before half these players were born, who won a Cup in his fifth season, who played in Edmonton when nobody wanted to play in Edmonton, who went home to Boston because home was where his father watched, and who now skates 20.5 minutes a night in a building where 16,073 people pay eighty dollars to watch him score at the highest rate in hockey.

The Index says one. The pace says 154. The 52 goals are in the column tonight. The rest is March.
