At 33, We Finally Know Who He Is: Geoff Sanderson, 101 Points, and a Grade of 79

The 36th pick of 1990 is the captain of a 19-win club, sixth in the league in scoring, and the Book hasn't moved.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideSaturday, 26 February 2005
ShareCopy link

Geoff Sanderson85 is 33 years old, 55 goals in 61 games, and the best player on the Columbus Blue Jackets. He has held that title since the club drafted nobody who could take it from him. The Book has him at 79.

Sixth in the league in scoring. A pace of 136 points over 82 games. Twenty-two minutes a night, the captain's letter on his sweater, and a payroll line that reads $6.34M with two years still to run. The Columbus Blue Jackets sit at 19-28-6, sixty points. The man carrying them is the 36th player selected in the 1990 entry draft, a second-round pick out of Hay River, Northwest Territories, who played his first NHL shift for the Hartford Whalers as a teenager.

The Development Index has him at +1. The Book's base has not moved in any direction that matters. At 79, the Bureau says Sanderson is a good player, a solid top-line left winger, someone you want on your roster. At 101 points through 61 games, the scoreboard says something else. The two views sit in the same seat, and nobody at Columbus has figured out which one to believe.

The Road from Hay River

Sanderson was 18 when Hartford took him 36th overall in 1990. The Whalers had not yet decided to leave Connecticut. He played 64 games in his first full season, 1991-92, and then scored more than 30 goals every year for five seasons except the lockout year of 1994-95. The Whalers missed the playoffs every one of those years. So did the Carolina Hurricanes after the move.

In the middle of 1997-98, Carolina traded him to Vancouver Canucks. A separate deal sent him to Buffalo. The Sabres reached the 1999 Stanley Cup Final that season; Sanderson played third-line minutes and watched Dallas win Game 6 on the Brett Hull77 goal that everyone still argues about. He never scored a playoff goal that spring.

The expansion draft in 2000 put him back in Columbus. He scored 30-plus goals in 2000-01 and 2002-03. At the deadline in 2003-04, Columbus sent him back to Vancouver. He scored one goal in the first round against Calgary Flames and the Canucks went home. Then, in the summer, he came back. The record does not carry the transaction. He was simply there again when the season started, wearing the C.

The gap between Sanderson and the next-highest forward is 31 points. Todd Marchant, the center he plays beside most nights, carries a grade of 78 and 70 points. Sanderson has 101 on a team that scored 180 goals in 61 games. Morale on the Bureau's scale has him at 87, not 99.

The Number the Book Refuses to Move

Sanderson put up 115 points in 64 games last season, mostly with Vancouver. That was a 147 pace. He is running a 136 pace this year, slightly slower, with a different jersey. The Book says 79 today. The potential grade reads 59, which the Bureau defines as likelihood of further development, not a ceiling. A 59 at 33 means the Bureau expects this to be as good as it gets.

He has $6.34M left for two more years. The Columbus Blue Jackets payroll sits at $49.21M and the club pays $0.82M per point across the roster. Sanderson's individual salary, divided by his production, makes him one of the cheaper large contracts in hockey relative to what it buys. Columbus gets 101 points from a man on $6.34M. The league's average club pays $0.74M per point. The Blue Jackets pay $0.82M per point as a team, above that average, but the man who puts up the most volume costs them well under it.

At the end of this contract he will be 35. Columbus hasn't reached the point of deciding whether to re-sign the man who carried them through this season at the next number, or let the 79 in the Book do the arguing for them.

The Captain's Letter

Sanderson wears the C. He has worn it since the beginning of the season. The Bureau's morale figure for him is 87. The strongest grades on his card are speed at 87, accuracy at 86, heroics at 86, puck handling at 86. His weakest are fighting at 50, injury resistance at 50, and a speed-under-pressure grade of 52. He is fast, he is accurate, he makes a play in a tight window, and he will not drop the gloves to protect you.

A 79 overall with 86s and 87s scattered across it. His parts are better than his whole. He will not fight and will not absorb a hit, and those are the things that move a Book grade from the 70s into the 80s. But he scores 55 goals at 33 for a club with 19 wins.

The Index says +1. The potential says 59. Two more years on the contract. 101 points in 61 games and nobody else on the roster has 70. At 33, on a 19-win club, from Hay River, Northwest Territories, he puts up 55 goals and wears the C and nobody has written him out yet.
