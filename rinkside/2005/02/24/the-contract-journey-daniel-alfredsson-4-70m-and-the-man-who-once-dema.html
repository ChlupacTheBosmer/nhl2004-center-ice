The Contract Journey: Daniel Alfredsson, $4.70M, and the Man Who Once Demanded a Trade Over Less

In 1997 he told Ottawa he would leave before he accepted a million dollars less than his teammate. Eight years later he's the most productive player on a 60-point club, and the contract runs out in June.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideThursday, 24 February 2005
ShareCopy link

On August 24, 1997, Daniel Alfredsson90 told the Ottawa Senators he wanted out. The club had offered him a five-year deal worth $1 million less annually than what Alexei Yashin was getting. He was 24 years old, a year removed from the Calder Memorial Trophy, and he had decided that a million was a line he would not cross. The Senators kept him. The captaincy came to him. The rest of the decade passed in Toronto series losses and a hip injury that cost him 24 games in 1998-99.

Now he is 32, playing in Ottawa still, and the arithmetic has a different shape. The Ottawa Senators have 60 points in 63 games. Alfredsson has 89 of them. The Book has him at 90, the same grade that sits on Patrick Lalime90, the same grade that sits on Martin Brodeur91. His contract pays him $4.70M and expires this June.

The man who once thought a million was an insult is producing at a 116 pace for a club that will watch March from home.

The road from 133rd

The Bureau's scoring table lists his draft as 1994, 133rd overall, fifth round. He came from Gothenburg, Sweden, 71 inches and 198 pounds, a right shot who did not look like the player Ottawa would build a franchise around. He arrived at his first NHL training camp in 1995 and the next spring he was in the All-Star Game. Two All-Star Games in his first three seasons.

What followed was not the clean arc the Calder suggested. The contract fight in 1997 set the tone. Then the injury in 1998-99: 11 goals in 58 games. Then the years of Yashin's return, the captaincy, and the Toronto Maple Leafs in the spring. Four-game sweep in 2001. Seven games in 2002. Seven games again in 2004, when Alfredsson managed 1 goal and 3 points.

In 2001-02 he scored 37 goals and 71 points in 78 games. For three years that was the best his name had ever looked on a stat sheet.

This season he is at 47 goals in 63 games. The pace is 116. His old personal best would be a 75 pace over 82. He has cleared it by 41 points per 82 games.

What the Book says

The overall is 90, and the Development Index has him flat. His base grade on the Book is 92, which means his current form reads two below what the Bureau considers his true level. In a season where he is outscoring his base grade, the Index says nothing. That is the Bureau's way of saying it always knew he was here. The Index only moves when someone surprises us.

His strongest grades tell you how he scores. DEKG is 96, which is the highest grade on his card and among the best in the league. PASS is 95. SHPW is 94, SPEE is 94. FIGH is 50, SPBI is 52. What works for him is putting a pass through a gap you cannot see yet, and shifting weight in a direction that makes your hip commit before his does.

Potential is 66. He is 32, and the 90 is his ceiling. There is no climb left.

The room without him

Ottawa Senators have 60 points. Their last ten games read WLOTLWWLOTLLLW, four wins against four losses and two overtime defeats. They scored 26 in that stretch and gave up 26. They are a team hovering at .500 hockey in a league where .500 means nothing.

Marian Hossa87 is out with a back until March 29. Todd White85 is out with a hand, same date. The room is thinner now than it was in January. Hossa is the second-best forward on the roster at 87 on the Book, and White is third at 83. When they come back, Martin Havlat88 (88), Alfredsson (90), and Hossa (87) will line up against second-pairing defensemen for a while, which is where the Senators find their best hockey.

But Alfredsson is carrying the weight right now. The Index has him flat, which is the Bureau's way of saying this is not a hot streak. He is on the scoring table at 116 in 63 games, 21.7 minutes a night, playing every situation for a club that is $42.80M a year and 21 wins.

What June will ask

The contract says $4.70M. That is what Ottawa pays the player with the 96 deception grade, the 116 pace, the 47 goals, and the 89 points. For comparison: Todd Bertuzzi93 of Vancouver Canucks, also 30, also a captain, scored at a 140 pace through 61 games, and the contract-year class lists him at $2.60M. Ilya Kovalchuk90, 21 years old, 98 points, is on $1.10M. These are the numbers a general manager looks at in June and then goes quiet for a while.

Alfredsson is 32 and his potential reads 66. He will not get better. The question is how long 116 lasts. The man who set his own floor at a million over Yashin in 1997 is now the cheapest 90-rated player in the league by any reasonable measure, and the only question that belongs to June is how long a club with 60 points can afford to find that out.

The Senators will not make the playoffs. The Senators will not make the playoffs next year either, probably. $4.70M against 60 team points forces neither a rebuild nor a contender. What it leaves open is how much this man is worth to the people who watch him in a building that seats 14,866 and sells tickets at $80.

He is 32, playing at a 116 pace with a 96 deception grade, on a club with 60 points whose contract for him runs out in June. The man who once thought a million was too little is asking nothing at all.
