At 24, Edmonton Owes Him: Mike Comrie, 119 Points, $1.00M, and the Series He Carried Into the Building

The hometown kid who was despised for holding out 30 games is the man leading the 5th seed over the 4th, on the cheapest salary on the roster and one year from free agency.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideWednesday, 13 April 2005
ShareCopy link

The building shook on the second goal. Not the kind of shake that comes from a lead in a first-round series against a higher seed, but the kind that comes from a sound a city has been waiting all year to make: the noise that says we were wrong, and we are glad you stayed. Mike Comrie81 banked it off the far post at 24.0 minutes of ice time and a city that had made its feelings known about him at the arena, on talk radio, in the parking lots of Jasper Place High School, started to come back around.

He is 24. 119 points in 82 games on a $1.00M salary, one year left on the contract. 5 assists in 3 playoff games against Vancouver Canucks, the 4th seed, and Edmonton Oilers, the 5th, who lead the series 2-1. The man who scored 42 goals and 133 points last season, the one the Oilers won the Hart with, Ryan Smyth82, sits in a room with a shoulder that will not clear before April 17, watching Comrie do this on a fifth-round pick from 1999, the 91st overall, made by a team that was already his.

The arc is not complicated. A boy from Edmonton goes to the University of Michigan, comes back to junior with Kootenay, leaves the WHL as an overage player, and signs with the team that drafted him. Instant local hero, the kid from the Whitemud arena who made the Pee-Wee tournament in Quebec. Then a holdout, 30-some games into 2003-04, a contract dispute that turned a city against its own boy. He came back and scored 133 points anyway. This season he scored 119 in 82, all of them, with a hero grade of 96 that the Book carries like a dare: the number the Bureau puts on the man who plays for the building, who finds a way when the room needs one.

What the Book Said About Him

The potential grade on the Book for Comrie is 76. His overall today is 83. He is 7 points above the ceiling they set for him at 18, when they took him in the fourth round thinking he might be a bottom-six center who could put up a few in a strong league. The Development Index has him at base today, neither climbing nor falling. The Book's attributes tell a different story than the draft position ever did: Speed 92, Deke 92, Endurance 88. A man fast enough to beat you clean and durable enough to play 82 nights and still log 22.1 minutes in a playoff game.

His Fight Grade is 50. His Injury Resistance is 63. The Bureau reads him as someone who will not protect himself, who takes a hit in the corner for an extra half-step of ice and comes back for the next shift because the hero number says he should.

The Holdout

The 2003-04 season started without him. Thirty-some games. The city that had made a hero out of a hometown kid watching his Oilers from the stands at Jasper Place turned on him with the particular cruelty of people who feel betrayed by one of their own. He signed, played, and put up 133 points in 75 games. Then the playoffs came, and he put up 16 in 12, and Edmonton lost in the second round to the club that would win the Cup.

That summer the contract ran out. What followed is on the sheet now: a $1.00M deal, one year. At 24, in the prime of a center's body, with 119 points on the board and a hero number of 96, he is making what a third-liner makes. The Development Index doesn't show him climbing. The overall is 83 and has been 83. The Book is not calling him up. The salary says the Oilers do not believe the Book will.

Without Smyth

Ryan Smyth is the 2003-04 Hart. He is 84 on the Book, a shoulder, out until April 17. Tommy Salo85 is 87 in goal and has been carrying this team all spring the way a 33-year-old carries a team when the young ones are supposed to be the story. The room is not deep: Mike York79 at 81, Brad Isbister78 at 80, Radek Dvorak78 at 80. Behind them, the development pipeline has Ales Hemsky73 at 75 and Raffi Torres73 at 75. The Oilers' payroll is $36.81M, the second lowest in the league. Their $0.38M per point is the best in hockey.

Vancouver is not a better team on paper. Todd Bertuzzi93 is 95 and has 128 points. Markus Naslund89 is 91 and has 138. They are the two best players in this series, and they cost $2.60M and roughly $5.00M. Their payroll is $35.16M, the lowest in the league, and their profit is $39.13M, the highest. They have the cheapest talent in the league and they are down 2-1 to a team whose best player makes $1.00M.

In the regular season, Vancouver went 4-1-2 against Edmonton. They knew how to play them. The playoffs found out differently.

The Series

Game 1: 4-3 in overtime on the road. Comrie had the assist that set it up. Game 2: 3-2 on the road again, another point for him. Game 3 came back to Edmonton and the Oilers lost 5-4. Comrie had a third point. Five assists, no goals, 5 points in 3 games. He is not the one putting the puck in. He is the one putting it on a teammate's stick. The hero number 96 is not about what he scores. It is about what happens when the puck finds him on the half-wall and a building full of people who once booed him gets to their feet.

Rick DiPietro84 went 0-3 against Toronto in the other first round. Comrie has not lost two in a row. He has not lost at home. His team is 2-0 at home and 2-1 overall, and the next game is here, in the building where he learned to play, where the Whitemud kids still show up on the cold Saturday mornings hoping to see a game.

The contract expires in the fall. Free agency closes on July 28, 106 days from today. He will be 25 by then. The Book says 83. His ceiling was 76. He has beaten it and will beat it again. The question is whether anyone in this league will pay him what the Book has made him look like. The question is whether the city that owed him an apology has enough money in the payroll to make one that sounds like a number.

He has one year left. The playoffs are the longest conversation the city and he have been having since the holdout. The city is learning to say his name again. He is learning to not look up when the boo comes. Neither one is working, and both of them are getting better.

The Oilers lead the series 2-1. Comrie has 5 assists. He has $1.00M in the bank. The building is his again. He is 24 and the season is not over.
