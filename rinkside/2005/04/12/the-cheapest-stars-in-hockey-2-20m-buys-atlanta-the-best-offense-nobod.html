The Cheapest Stars in Hockey: $2.20M Buys Atlanta the Best Offense Nobody Can Touch

Ilya Kovalchuk has 138 points. Dany Heatley has 131. They cost $2.20M combined on the lowest payroll in the league. Both have one year left. The club that built around them has 107 days before the market moves.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideTuesday, 12 April 2005
ShareCopy link

The Atlanta Thrashers Thrashers have the lowest payroll in this league. $36.79M across every roster spot, every contract, every entry-level deal and every veteran minimum. They made $25.99M in profit this season, sold 14,430 seats at $78 a ticket, and finished with 84 points. They did not make the playoffs.

Two of those men cost $1.10M each. Their combined salary, computed from the Contract Ledger, is $2.20M.

Ilya Kovalchuk91 is 21. He was the first overall pick of 2001. He scored 63 goals and handed out 75 assists in 79 games this season, 138 points, 143 pace over 82. His overall is 90, the Development Index has him at +5, and the Bureau's potential sits at 85. He is in his final year on a deal that pays him $1.10M. Free agency closes 107 days from today.

Dany Heatley90 is 24. He was the second overall pick of 2000. He scored 65 goals and 66 assists in 79 games, 131 points, 136 pace. His overall is 88, his morale is 87, and he costs the same $1.10M. He also has one year left.

What $2.20M buys

No other two players on this sheet produce 138 points and 131 points for that combined number. The Toronto Maple Leafs Maple Leafs pay $60.04M for their entire roster and have one Cup. Atlanta Thrashers pays $36.79M for the whole book and has neither a Cup nor a playoff berth, but they have two of the top four scorers in the league for less than what a single mid-tier defenseman earns elsewhere.

The per-point number for the club as a whole is $0.44M. That is the second-best ratio in the league, behind New Jersey Devils at $0.40M: two players on entry-level deals carrying the offensive production of a team that cannot spend.

What happens next

The trade deadline came and went 24 days ago. Both men are still here. The hot-stove desk logged a prediction on 2005-03-19 that both would still be on the active roster after the deadline. They are. The hockey-gazette desk made the same call and settled it as a hit.

Now there are 107 days. Free agency does not close until 2005-07-28, and the rookie draft is 57 days away. Nothing on the calendar has a firm deadline for either player except the close of free agency itself, which is the point where the option becomes an obligation.

What will Kovalchuk cost? The sheet does not say. What the sheet does say: he is 21, he scored 138 points in a 30-club league, the Bureau's Book has him at 90 with a potential of 85 (which means the Bureau believes the mountain is ahead, not behind), and his next contract will be his first real one. He came to North America at 19 as the number-one pick in 2001. The Bureau's Contract Ledger has him at $1.10M. That number will change.

Heatley is three years older and four picks lower on the all-time draft list. He came from the second spot in 2000, which was the same draft class that saw the first pick go to New York Rangers and become Rick DiPietro88. Heatley is not the franchise anymore. Kovalchuk is. But Heatley scored 65 goals and 131 points for $1.10M, and at 24 he is entering the years where a man should be at his peak.

The arithmetic

Atlanta Thrashers revenue is $70.29M. Subtract $36.79M in payroll and you have $33.50M before expenses, which is how you get to a $25.99M profit. The gap between revenue and payroll is $33.50M, and $2.20M of that gap sits in two men's pockets. Double their combined cost to any reasonable franchise figure and the profit line moves. The club goes from the most profitable in the league by payroll ratio to somewhere in the middle.

That is not a disaster. It is not the same thing as what happens to Detroit Red Wings at $80.89M or New York Rangers at $78.14M with no playoff berth and no profit line. But it changes the room. It changes the decisions the general manager makes in July when the draft is over and the phone starts ringing.

The room they built

Atlanta Thrashers finished 84 points this season. They did not make the playoffs. Their last playoff run was zero. They have never won a Cup. Their prestige points sit at 408, near the bottom of the league.

They found a 19-year-old in Russia who became the first pick in 2001. They found a 19-year-old in the United States who became the second pick in 2000. They put them together on the same wing and let them learn. Four years later Kovalchuk scores 138 points and Heatley scores 131. The team around them has no depth. Byron Dafoe84 in net has a 3.41 GAA and a .849 save percentage. The defense has no one with a Book grade above 82.

That is the shape of the roster. Two men worth $1.10M each carrying the league's best offense on the lowest payroll in the sport, and a building full of bodies that cost too much for what they do.

Heatley has been here since his draft. Kovalchuk arrived in 2001 at 19. Neither man has ever been traded. Neither man has been injured on the record. Both of them are 107 days from a decision that will change the arithmetic of this club.

The Bureau's Book has Kovalchuk at 90 with a +5 on the Development Index and a potential of 85. It has Heatley at 88 with no movement. The one is still climbing. The other one has arrived.

At $1.10M each, they are the cheapest production in the league. In 107 days, the market will tell Atlanta Thrashers what they cost.
