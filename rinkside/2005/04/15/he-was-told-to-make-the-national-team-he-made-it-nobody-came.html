He Was Told to Make the National Team. He Made It. Nobody Came.

Johan Hedberg played in Baton Rouge so he could play in Pittsburgh. At 31, back with the club that once traded him away, he has a 2.00 GAA in the series and a 2-1 lead over the second seed.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideFriday, 15 April 2005
ShareCopy link

Philadelphia drafted him 218th in 1994. Eighth round. They told him, across the ocean, that if he made the Swedish national team they would send for him. He made it. Nobody came.

Johan Hedberg86 stayed in Sweden for five more seasons at Leksands, posting a 2.79 GAA in 116 games, and then left. Not for a European first division or a tryout in Toronto. For the East Coast Hockey League. For Baton Rouge.

That was 1997. He was 24 years old and he played in the lowest professional league in North America because the eighth round had not prepared a pathway, and nobody was coming to find him.

The airport trade and the Moose helmet

He played for Baton Rouge, then Detroit in the IHL, then Manitoba. The Flyers traded his rights to San Jose in 1998. The Sharks had goaltenders younger than him, all of them ranked ahead, and after one season at their AHL affiliate in Kentucky he left for Manitoba again. The career arc skips this part. He is going sideways.

Eddie Johnston was Pittsburgh's assistant general manager. He noticed. In March 2001, Randy Carlyle, the Moose's coach, pulled Hedberg aside at Winnipeg International Airport and told him he had been traded to Pittsburgh. He was told to report directly. He had no time to change equipment.

He played his first game in Pittsburgh wearing the helmet he had brought from Manitoba, the one with the large moose painted across it. The crowd saw it. They started calling him Moose. They cheered after his saves by yelling "Mooooose" through the building. People in the upper deck thought it was a boo.

That spring he beat Olaf Kölzig. Then he beat Dominik Hašek. Pittsburgh reached the Eastern Conference Final with a man who had been playing in Baton Rouge four years earlier.

In 2002 he set career highs: 25 wins, 66 games. A club record. He went to the Olympics in Salt Lake City as Tommy Salo84's backup and started one game for Sweden, a win over Germany.

Then Pittsburgh traded him to Vancouver for a second-round pick in 2004. He posted a 2.51 GAA in 21 games there, a career best by rate. And then, somehow, he was back.

What the Book says and what the pad says

The Bureau has Hedberg at 88 today. His base grade is 85. The Development Index has him two above, which means he is playing better than his standing. His endurance grade is 94, the highest on his card. His toughness is 50. His fighting is 51.

Toughness 50 next to endurance 94. A reader who has never seen him play would guess toughness is what makes a goaltender. It wasn't. He was never the hardest man on the ice. He was the one who was still there the next season.

This season he started 63 games for Pittsburgh. He won 30, lost 33, posted 3 shutouts. His goals against average is 2.77, his save percentage .867. The Pittsburgh payroll is $44.95M. His salary is $5.65M with four years left, and the building is full every night: 13,990 through the gate at $79 a ticket.

Back at the club that traded him

Pittsburgh Penguins entered these playoffs as the 7th seed, 85 points, last spring's run zero wins. They opened against New Jersey Devils, 114 points, the 2 seed. Martin Brodeur95 in net, 46 wins on the season. In the regular season these two clubs split their eight meetings, 4-4, with five of them decided by one goal.

The first three games of this series have done what the regular season said they would do. Game 1 in Newark: Pittsburgh wins 3-2. Game 2 in Newark: New Jersey wins 3-1. Game 3 in Pittsburgh: Hedberg holds them to one, a 2-1 win on a night where the only goal that mattered came with the crowd on its feet.

Hedberg is 2-1 in this series with a 2.00 GAA and a .905 save percentage. Mario Lemieux85 has four assists in three games, the old man still setting up the kids. Eric Meloche75, the kid they won the Calder on last spring, has three goals and four points. The scoring is distributed, the goaltending is not, and the goaltending is what matters.

Game 4 is tonight at Mellon. Pittsburgh has the lead. One more win takes them to the second round, and the 7th seed will have knocked out the 2nd.

The man who kept showing up

There is a version of this story where the Flyers call. A training camp invitation arrives at Leksand in the winter of 1995 or '96, and the rest is ordinary. A backup job somewhere by '99, a starting job by 2002, no nickname, no airport, no moose painted on a helmet.

That version did not happen. What happened is this: a 21-year-old in Leksand was told to earn a job by accomplishing a thing, he accomplished it, and the people who set the test moved on to the next prospect. He had no contract, no agent, no way to force a phone call.

So he went where hockey was being played. Baton Rouge. Detroit. Manitoba. Kentucky. Back to Manitoba. Fourth on the depth chart at San Jose. The eighth-round pick from a country nobody scouted then. He was 24 in the ECHL and 25 in the IHL and 27 when Pittsburgh said come here, right now, tonight, no time to change your mask.

He is 31. Four years left on a $5.65M contract, a 94 endurance grade, a 2-1 lead over the second seed. The Development Index has him up two from his base. Morale 92. Not hot, not cold, just in one direction for a long time without a promise that anyone was watching.

Game 4 is tonight. The building will be full. When he makes a save, someone in the upper deck will yell "Mooooose" and the person next to them will think it is a boo.
