23 Wins, 26 Wins, and the Only Number That Matters: Pascal Leclaire Is Cheaper Than He Should Be

Columbus scored 222 goals. Their goaltender won 23 games and posted a 2.65 GAA. He makes $1.10M. He has one year left, and the Blue Jackets are about to find out what a franchise goaltender costs.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideWednesday, 11 May 2005
ShareCopy link

Pascal Leclaire87 is 22 years old. He has an 88 overall, a 94 potential, a +5 on the Development Index, and a contract that pays him $1.10M with one year remaining. Columbus Blue Jackets won 26 games. Leclaire was the goaltender in 49 of them, and his 23 wins are 88.5% of the team total.

That number is not a compliment. It is a diagnosis. Columbus scored 222 goals in 82 games, 2.71 per night. Their top scorer, Geoff Sanderson80, made $6.34M and had 121 points. Their second-highest paid forward, Todd Marchant80, made $4.88M and had 89. The goaltender who kept the games close enough to watch had his name printed in the box score at $1.10M.

From 24 games to 49

Leclaire played 24 games in 2003-04, going 11-10-1 with one shutout, allowing 72 goals across 1,393 minutes.

This year, 49 games. Twenty-three wins, 25 losses, one shutout, a 2.65 GAA, and a save percentage of 0.872. He played 60.5 minutes a night when he played. He did not miss a game. No injuries on record.

Marc Denis85 played 30 games. He is 86 overall, with a $1.00M salary. Leclaire's 88 overall is higher than Denis's 86, and his form reads +5 on the Index. The jump from 24 games to 49 is not a springboard. It is a season. Leclaire was the starting goaltender from October to March, and he played 60.5 minutes a night.

What 0.872 means

The league's goalies with 50 or more games finished with save percentages between 0.841 and 0.889. Leclaire's 0.872 is the same mark that Evgeni Nabokov83 posted in 69 games for San Jose Sharks. On a team that scored 222 goals and allowed 235, that number kept the building from emptying.

Jean-Sebastien Giguere97 of Mighty Ducks of Anaheim is the league's highest-rated goaltender at 97 overall, with a 2.44 GAA and a 0.877 save percentage. Martin Brodeur95 is 91 overall at 2.42 GAA. Leclaire is not at their level. His 88 overall sits in the second tier. But his potential is 94, and the Development Index has him playing five points above his base grade of 83.

The kid from Repentigny

Pascal Leclaire was born in 1982 in Repentigny, Quebec. He played in the 1996 Quebec International Pee-Wee Hockey Tournament with the Rive-Nord Elites. He played junior hockey with the Halifax Mooseheads and the Montreal Rocket between 1998 and 2002.

He was the first goaltender selected in the 2001 NHL entry draft, chosen eighth overall by Columbus Blue Jackets. He has been a Blue Jacket since the day the club drafted him. No trades. No waivers. A man who arrived at eight and stayed.

He is 73 inches tall and 185 pounds. For a goaltender, that is not large. His grades say what that means. His agility is 95. His glove hand, 95. His speed, 91. His lateral push, 92. The columns that govern his movement are elite. His positioning, 51, and his pad stack, 51, are not. He does not control the crease with his frame. He beats you from the outside, with angles and reflexes and the kind of movement that makes a lean goaltender look twice his size in a rebound scramble. When the angles are clean and the screen is set, the puck gets in.

What a franchise goaltender costs

The Business table has Columbus Blue Jackets at a $49.21M payroll. Revenue: $71.04M. Profit: $14.42M. Attendance: 13,958 per game. Tickets at $81. The money is there to fix the scoring. The question the Blue Jackets face this summer is whether they believe a 22-year-old with a 94 potential is worth a franchise goaltender's money, or whatever the market says a goaltender with 0.872 and 23 wins on a 26-win team should make.

Rick DiPietro86, at 23, 84 overall, was the first pick of the 2000 draft. He is also on a one-year contract, and he is also about to find out what the market pays for a young goaltender with potential. The difference is that DiPietro plays for New York Islanders, and Leclaire plays for Columbus Blue Jackets, and Columbus won 26 games last year.

The rookie draft is 28 days away. Free agency closes on July 28. Leclaire's contract has one year left. If he is still in Columbus in September, the price will be higher than $1.10M. If he is not, it will be because somebody in a warmer market decided that 0.872 on a 222-goal team is worth the raise that Columbus would not give.

He is 22. He has not been hurt. He has played every game he was asked to play for two seasons. The 94 potential is the Bureau's projection, not a ceiling, but it is a high number, and it is sitting in a locker room that produced 81 points in a season. If Columbus wins a round next spring, Leclaire's contract will be the only number that matters in the room.
