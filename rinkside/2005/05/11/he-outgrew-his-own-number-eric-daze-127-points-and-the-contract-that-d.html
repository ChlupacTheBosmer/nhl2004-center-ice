He Outgrew His Own Number: Eric Daze, 127 Points, and the Contract That Doesn't Fit

The Blackhawks' left wing scored at a 127-point pace for a 78-point team. He is 29. He has one year left. And the Bureau's Book said he would never be this good.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideWednesday, 11 May 2005
ShareCopy link

Chicago Blackhawks finished with 78 points. Eric Daze87 had 127. Those numbers do not belong in the same sentence. They belong to the same season.

He makes $3.00M and has one year left on his contract. Morale is 96. He is not going anywhere this season, and next season he is a free agent, and the number the Bureau printed for his potential, back when he was 19, was 78. He plays at 87.

From Beauport to the ninth round of memory

Dazé grew up in Montreal. He played midget hockey with Laval in the Quebec AAA Midget Hockey League. The Hull Olympiques picked him fourth round, 44th overall, in the 1991 QMJHL entry draft. He was returned to AAA hockey in his first year of eligibility. He made one appearance for Hull. He was traded to the Beauport Harfangs late in the 1992-93 season.

With Beauport in 1993-94, his first full season, he tallied 59 goals and 107 points in 66 games. He was named to the QMJHL First All-Star Team and the CHL Third All-Star Team. The Harfangs reached the league semifinals.

Chicago picked him 90th overall in the third round of the 1993 draft. The pick came from the deal that sent Dominik Hašek to Buffalo. Dazé finished 1994-95 with four games and two points. He scored at least 20 goals every year from 1995 through 2003. Even when injuries cut seasons short.

Last year, in 74 games, he scored 153 points. That was the best line in the league, fourth in Art Ross history for a Chicago player. This year, 127 points in 82 games. A 26-point drop, and still fifth among all scorers.

What the Book says about the man who exceeds his own ceiling

Daze's faceoff grade is 51. His fighting grade is 50. His speed on the backcheck, his SPBI, reads 52. His shot power, 94, and his hockey sense, 93, are what a 127-point season runs on. So are his deking, 91, and his puck control, 91. He is the player you find on a third-round Thursday night in a Montreal hotel and hold for a decade.

He has played his entire career in Chicago. The record opened with him already a Blackhawk. No trade, no waiver claim. He was drafted by this club and stayed.

Jocelyn Thibault89 was 90 overall in net, with a 2.91 GAA in 70 games. Alexei Zhamnov84 had 111 points at center. Steve Sullivan84 had 100 on the wing. Theoren Fleury86 was 86 overall in 60 games. That is not a bad supporting cast. It is not a Cup cast either.

Chicago Blackhawks carries a $39.27M payroll. Revenue: $78.58M. Profit: $31.73M. Attendance: 15,161 per game, tickets at $77. Chicago is not in a position to chase anyone. They are in a position to lose someone, in July, on a Thursday night, to a club with more money and fewer scruples.

The market is thin at the top and wide at the bottom

The league's players with one year remaining, ranked by points, read like this: Ilya Kovalchuk92 at 138 points, $1.10M. Dany Heatley91 at 131 points, $1.10M. Todd Bertuzzi89 at 128 points, $2.60M. Daze at 127 points, $3.00M. Mike Comrie84 at 119 points, $1.00M.

Daze is the fourth-highest scorer among unrestricted forwards with one year remaining. He is also the most expensive at his tier. His salary is the highest among the first four names on that list. A club that signs him in July is paying $3.00M for a player whose Book grade, 87, sits inside the top 30 in the league. His potential, 78, means the Bureau no longer expects growth. At 87 overall, that is a fine thing to be.

The question for any suitor: how long can a man with a 51 faceoff and a 50 fight grade keep scoring at this rate? How many more years before his body tells him what his grades have always whispered?

The kids behind him

Mark Bell84 is 23, with a foot injury, out until May 19. Kyle Calder78 is 23, 80 overall, 22 points in 66 games. Igor Radulov80 is 22, 74 overall, 12 points. These are the young players on the roster who will watch Daze walk into a meeting room in late June and decide whether to stay or go.

Daze's path from Beauport to the top of the league's scoring column is the path those players were told about on their first day in camp. It is also the path that ended, for him, with a contract that doesn't fit and a club that can't fix its payroll. He is the answer to the question every rookie asks at 19: what does this look like at the end? For Daze, at 29, the answer is 127 points, $3.00M, and a summer that will tell him whether Chicago will match.

The rookie draft is 28 days away. Free agency closes on July 28. Daze's contract expires before either date, or at least that is what the sheet says. One year left. And he has already played every game he has left on this deal, except the ones that matter most, the ones in July when a club in a bigger market puts a number on a napkin and asks him to call back in the morning.
