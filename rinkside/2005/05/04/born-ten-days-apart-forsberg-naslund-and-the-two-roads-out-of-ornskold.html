Born Ten Days Apart: Forsberg, Näslund, and the Two Roads Out of Örnsköldsvik

They shared a high school, an electrical company, a junior championship, a national team. Now they are both 31, both out in the first round, and both with two years left on contracts that cost a factor of two.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideWednesday, 4 May 2005
ShareCopy link

Peter Forsberg95 was born on 20 July 1973. Markus Näslund was born on 30 July 1973. Ten days and one small city in northern Sweden sit between them. They played on separate youth teams, then together on the Ångermanland all-star squad at TV-pucken in 1988, then together at Modo, then together for Sweden at the 1996 World Cup, the 1998 Olympics, and the 1998 World Championship that Sweden won. At 18 they worked the same summer at the same electrical company. Näslund's mother worked there. Forsberg's father, Kent, worked there. Two boys from the same road, heading to the same building each morning.

Thirty-two years later they are both still playing, both still captains or alternates, both just eliminated from the playoffs in the first round, both with exactly two years left on contracts that differ by $5.50M. Forsberg earns $9.90M at Colorado Avalanche. Näslund earns $4.40M at Vancouver Canucks. Neither is hurt, neither traded. Both are 31, and the spring of 2005 left them on the outside watching.

The numbers this season

Peter Forsberg played 82 games, scored 60 goals and 69 assists for 129 points at 21.4 minutes a night. His overall grade is 95, his base is 97, and the Bureau's Development Index has him one below that base. His morale is 100. He has played for Colorado since before the record opened on 2004-12-01, and the record does not know how long he has been there, only that he was there when the ledger started.

Markus Näslund89 played 82 games, scored 74 goals and 64 assists for 138 points at 20.1 minutes a night. His overall grade is 89, his base is 92, and the Index has him right at base. His morale is 93. He is the captain of Vancouver. He has been there since before the record opened as well.

Näslund outscored Forsberg by 9 points this season. The salary gap runs the other direction by more than two to one.

The playoffs they will not want to discuss

Colorado was the first seed in the West. The Phoenix Coyotes Coyotes, the eighth seed, beat them in seven games. Forsberg played 7 games, 2 goals and 4 assists, 6 points, at 19.9 minutes a night, down from his regular-season 21.4. The Avalanche had won the Presidents' Trophy the year before, 117 points, and last spring they went 24 games deep before losing the Final to Toronto. This spring lasted 7 games. The games the record shows for that series: COL won 3-2 in overtime on 2005-04-10, lost 2-3 on 2005-04-12, won 5-2 on 2005-04-14, lost 1-3 on 2005-04-16, lost 4-3 on 2005-04-18, and lost 3-4 at home on 2005-04-20.

Vancouver was the fourth seed. Edmonton Oilers was the fifth. The Oilers took it 4 games to 2. Näslund played 6 games, 8 goals and 5 assists, 13 points, at 24.2 minutes a night, up from his regular-season 20.1. He carried the team, and the team did not carry him. The last game, on 2005-04-19, Edmonton won 3-1.

One man scored 6 points in 7 games and his team lost a Game 7 at home. The other scored 13 in 6 and his team got swept in the last three. Forsberg's playoff points-per-game is worse than his regular-season pace. Näslund's is better. The $9.90M man had the smaller spring.

What the Book says about what they are

Forsberg's grades are extraordinary in three places: Deke 99, Herz 99, Pass 99. Puck 98, Accuracy 95, Speed 95. His lowest grade is Fight, at 50. Potential 61. At 31, the Book says what he is, and it does not expect him to become more.

Näslund's top grades: Deke 96, Puck 96, Accuracy 94, Herz 94, Pass 94, Shot Power 92. His lowest are Fight at 50 and Injury at 50, Speed in a shootout at 52. Potential 53. The Book has him slightly lower than Forsberg across the board and sees less runway for both of them.

Two men, ten days apart in age, whose weaknesses on the page are almost identical. Neither of them will win a fight. Neither of them is going anywhere.

What the money says

Colorado Avalanche pays $71.56M in total payroll for 112 points and a first-round exit. That is $0.64M per point. Vancouver Canucks pays $35.16M for 99 points. That is $0.36M per point. Vancouver has the cheapest payroll in the league and the best per-point figure. Colorado has the third-highest payroll and the 13th-best per-point figure. Forsberg's $9.90M is the single largest line on the Avalanche roster that I can see in the contract-year table, though he is not in that table because he has two years left, not one.

Vancouver turned a profit of $40.84M on a payroll of $35.16M. The club earns more from tickets and revenue than it spends on players, and Näslund's $4.40M is a fraction of what a player who scored 138 points might command in July. He has two years left. He will be 33 when they end.

Forsberg's $9.90M is locked for two years. He will be 33 when that contract runs out too. Both men have the same number of seasons left on paper, and the one earning less produced more this year and in the spring.

The thing that makes it worth writing

They were never rivals in any way that mattered in the NHL. Forsberg went from Philadelphia to Quebec in the Lindros deal in 1992, a trade the background sheet records as one of the most one-sided in sports history: five players, two first-round picks, and $15M sent to Quebec for Eric Lindros. Forsberg was in the package. He became an Avalanche. Näslund was drafted by Pittsburgh, left for free agency within weeks of his Modo contract expiring in 1993, and ended up in Vancouver. The roads split in 1993 and they have not crossed in the league since.

But they grew up on the same road. They went to the same high school. They worked the same summer at the same company when they were 18, before either was drafted, before either was a professional, before the Lindros trade or the free agency that sent Näslund to the Pacific coast. They won junior championships together at Modo. They won a World Championship together for Sweden. Now they are both 31, both out early, both on two-year deals that will end the same summer, and the city that raised them is far from either building.

The draft was 1991. Forsberg went sixth. Näslund went sixteenth. The Hockey News had Forsberg 25th on its board and called him "a solid second rounder who could move into the first." The Flyers' GM defended the pick. The media did not believe them. Both men made the league look foolish in opposite directions: the one everyone overvalued became a 95-grade center playing for a Presidents' Trophy team, and the one everyone undervalued became a captain scoring 74 goals.

They will be 31 together for one more season. Then both contracts run. Then the two boys from Örnsköldsvik become free agents in the same year, and whatever roads are left to walk, they walk at the same time.
