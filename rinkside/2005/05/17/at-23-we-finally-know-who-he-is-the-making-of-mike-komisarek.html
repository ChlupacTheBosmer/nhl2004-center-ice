At 23, We Finally Know Who He Is: The Making of Mike Komisarek

Twenty-four games and three points last year. Eighty-two and eighty-two this year. One year left at $1.10M to find out which one is the real him.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideTuesday, 17 May 2005
ShareCopy link

Mike Komisarek87 played 82 games and scored 82 points this season. His club won 21.

The Bureau's Development Index has him at +6, tied for the biggest form jump on the board. The Book's base grade is 77. The overall today reads 82. The potential grade is 92. In 2003-04, Komisarek suited up 24 times for Montreal Canadiens, logged 11.8 minutes a night, and finished with 1 goal and 2 assists. One season later, he is the second-highest-scoring defenseman in hockey.

From West Islip to the Forum

Komisarek grew up in West Islip, New York, on the south shore of Long Island. He played varsity hockey at St. Anthony's High School, then moved to the New England Jr. Coyotes of the EJHL, then to USA Hockey's National Team Development Program. The University of Michigan took him from there. He played two seasons for the Wolverines, helped them to a CCHA title and two NCAA tournament runs, and was named a First Team All-American in 2002. The Canadiens picked him seventh overall in the 2001 draft. He signed on July 24, 2002, and played his first NHL game the following fall.

That path, the junior-to-college-to-top-ten path, is supposed to produce someone who is at least competent at 23. It rarely produces someone who scores 82 points on a 21-win team.

The Year That Wasn't, and the Year That Was

The 2003-04 season is a footnote. Twenty-four games, three points, 11.8 minutes a night. A depth defenseman getting cup-of-coffee minutes in a season that meant nothing to anybody. The following October he would log 30.4 minutes a night.

This season: 15 goals, 67 assists, 82 points, 40 penalty minutes. He led all defensemen in assists. The only defenseman with more total points is Nicklas Lidstrom of Detroit Red Wings, who scored 86 at a 1.05 points-per-game pace. Komisarek's pace is 1.00.

What the Book Said and What It Did Not

The Book had him at 77 before this season started. That is a grade for a 22-year-old depth defenseman: good body (75 in, 225 lb), a checking grade of 92, a speed grade of 89, an aggression grade of 88. The weak numbers tell you the ceiling the Book drew: a fight grade of 51, a shot power of 52, an offensively-driven backcheck of 55. The Book saw a defender. The 67 assists came from somewhere the Book hadn't looked.

The Development Index adds 6 to the base. That is the Bureau saying the form is here, right now, at this level. It does not say whether it stays. The potential grade, 92, is the Bureau's measure of how likely further development is, and Komisarek sits near the top of it. Whether this is him or the high-water mark, July's contract will make the answer public.

The $1.10M Question

At $1.10M with one year remaining, Komisarek is the cheapest 80-point defenseman the desk can find. The cost-per-point query the desk ran on defensemen who scored 60 points or more makes the gap visible:

21 Wins and a Room He Did Not Build

Montreal Canadiens finished 21-46-15, with 66 points. They scored 238 goals and allowed 295. They did not make the playoffs. The team that drafted Komisarek seventh overall has built no structure around him. Jose Theodore93, the best player on the roster at 91 overall, has one year left at $5.50M. Richard Zednik87 at 85 is the closest forward. Andrei Markov, his defensive partner at 81, is the only other man within three grades of the top of the board. The dressing room is not Komisarek's to carry.

He is 23, and he came from West Islip through St. Anthony's, through the NTDP, through Ann Arbor, through 24 games of nothing, through 82 games of everything. The potential is 92. The form is +6. The checking is 92. The speed is 89. The number that will define next season is the salary on the new page.
