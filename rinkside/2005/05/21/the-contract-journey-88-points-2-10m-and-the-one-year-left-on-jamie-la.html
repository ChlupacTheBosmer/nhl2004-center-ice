The Contract Journey: 88 Points, $2.10M, and the One Year Left on Jamie Langenbrunner

New Jersey booked $45.96M of profit on the third-cheapest points in hockey. Its captain scored the most of his life for $2.10M. He turns 30 in July.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideSaturday, 21 May 2005
ShareCopy link

Jamie Langenbrunner88 has one year left on a $2.10M contract, a career high of 88 points, and a birthday between him and free agency.

The rest of New Jersey Devils sits the same way. The Devils bought 114 points on $45.15M of payroll, $0.40M a point, the third-cheapest rate in the league. They turned $98.74M of revenue into $45.96M of profit, the second-best number in hockey behind Toronto Maple Leafs. The room that did it is expensive now, or it is gone, and the calendar has free agency closing on 2005-07-28.

Langenbrunner turns 30 on 2005-07-24, four days before the money changes hands across the league. He is the captain. He has been the best thing in the building all season, and he has one year to be paid like one.

He came to New Jersey inside a package that made him

Before the contract, there is the road. He scored 148 points in 70 games at Cloquet, Minnesota, won the AP's state player of the year award, skipped his senior year, and went 35th overall to Dallas in 1993. Two winters at Peterborough came next, then an NHL debut on April 9, 1995 against St. Louis, and he was still a Star in 1999 when Dallas beat Buffalo for the Cup. He scored 10 goals and 17 points that spring and finished behind Mike Modano84 and the Conn Smythe winner, Joe Nieuwendyk, on the Stars' scoring chart.

On 2002-03-19 the Stars sent Langenbrunner to New Jersey with Nieuwendyk for Jason Arnott, Randy McKay and a first-round pick. The man who had skated beside him through the best postseason of his life was in the same box. That is how he arrived: not as the franchise, but with one.

Three years later he wears the C, and the trade reads differently depending on which side of it you work at.

Ankle in December, 88 points by April

He lost three weeks early this season to an ankle, hurt sometime between 2004-12-04 and 2004-12-07, back on the roster on 2004-12-30. He finished with 69 games, 39 goals, 49 assists, 20.1 minutes a night. The 88 points are the most he has ever scored in one season, and he did it carrying 45 penalty minutes on a man whose Book grades list FIGH 51.

The spring did not follow. The Penguins took the series in 6, and the seventh seed went out and beat the second. Langenbrunner had one goal and 4 points in 6 games, the quietest he has been in any postseason on the record. The Bureau's Book has him at 86, up from nobody's forecast, with his HERO grade at 91, the highest number on his card.

He was a 35th-round pick out of a town of a few thousand who skated with Gretzky's record books, a Cup, and a trade that shipped him inside a package. At 29 he scored a career year and lost in the first round.

Brodeur is safe for four years, and he earned it: 46 wins, a 2.42 GAA, .882 in 66 games at 91 overall. Underneath him the building is a fuse. Friesen, Gomez and Marshall have no years left on their contracts. Elias has one at $4.90M after 36 points in 37 games. Langenbrunner has one at $2.10M after 88 points in 69.

Read the bottom four rows and you have New Jersey's season in one column. The Devils scored $0.40M a point because Gomez cost $1.00M and Langenbrunner cost $2.10M and Marshall cost $1.50M. The same accounting that built $45.96M of profit built a summer where four cheap good forwards on the roster can leave at once.

The gate will help. 17261 people bought in a night at $80, the Devils' attendance was the third-best in the league, and the profit line says the building can carry more. The question is not whether New Jersey Devils can pay Langenbrunner. The question is who the general manager signs first, because the same checkbook does not clear five names by July.

What a career year costs at 30

The Bureau's Book gave him 86 with a base of 89, which means the Index reads him level, no heater, no slide, just a season's work. His morale sits at 94. He has no trophies on the record with this club, one Cup with another, and a contract that will look like a clerical error the moment he signs the next one.

Every club in this league runs on someone's cheap year. New Jersey ran on four of them and made the second round of the ledger behind Toronto. The rookie draft is 18 days away, on 2005-06-08, and it cannot solve the middle of this roster. Only a signature can, and the man whose 88 points bought all of it turns 30 while the ink dries or doesn't.

If you are New Jersey Devils, you pay him the same week the season ends. If you are Langenbrunner, you have already said everything you can say, in 69 games, for $2.10M.
