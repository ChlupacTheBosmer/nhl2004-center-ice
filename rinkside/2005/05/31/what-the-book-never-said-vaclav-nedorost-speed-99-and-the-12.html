What the Book Never Said: Vaclav Nedorost, Speed 99, and the +12

The Bureau's Development Index has him as the biggest riser in hockey. He scored 25 points. His contract expired, and Florida has 58 days to decide what a 99-speed center costs.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideTuesday, 31 May 2005
ShareCopy link

The Bureau's Development Index has Vaclav Nedorost83 at +12, the biggest jump on the sheet. He is 23. He makes $1.00M. His contract expired when the season ended, and what the calendar has as free agency closes in 58 days.

Nedorost scored 13 goals and 12 assists in 69 games this season at 13.4 minutes a night. The Book reads his overall at 84, his speed at 99, and his passing at 96. Nobody who drafted him 14th overall in 2000 expected that line, and most people in Florida stopped watching his column after the 2003-04 season, when he played 7 games and scored once.

The question Florida faces is what a 99-speed center who scored 25 points costs a club that finished with 76 points, 16 overtime losses, and a head coach who said the available player pool was empty. The Panthers' payroll is $53.89M. Their per-point cost is $0.71M. They made $13.59M in profit. Nedorost carries the highest speed grade on the roster, and he has no deal for next year.

The road out of České Budějovice

Nedorost was born on 1982-03-16 in České Budějovice and came up through the local club there. The Colorado Avalanche took him 14th overall in the 2000 draft. He crossed to North America for the 2001-02 season, split time between their AHL affiliate and the NHL roster, and played 25 games in Denver for 2 goals and 2 assists. The 2002-03 season brought 42 more games.

On July 19, 2003, Colorado traded Nedorost and Eric Messier to the Panthers for Peter Worrell and a second-round pick. A first-rounder from three years prior, a man who had played 25 games in his first NHL season and 42 in his second for 4 total points, went for a second-rounder. The Avalanche kept their draft capital. Florida got a center with speed and almost nothing else in the file.

A season that was mostly the playoffs

His first year in Florida barely happened. Nedorost played 7 regular-season games at 7.1 minutes a night and scored 1 point. He killed shifts, sat extra periods, and stayed on the fourth line all season. The Panthers made the playoffs anyway. On the first-round roster he played 5 games, scored 1 goal, added 2 assists, and logged 9.6 minutes a night. More minutes in the postseason than the regular season had given him, which is what a coach does with a player he trusts but could not dress in March.

The Development Index was not at +12 that spring. His base grade sat at 75, and the Book left him there through a season where the only evidence anyone in Sunrise had of his ability was a handful of playoff shifts that flashed and then stopped.

The leg, and the rise

This season was Nedorost's first full one, or nearly. A leg injury put him out between 2005-03-09 and 2005-03-11, with a return date of 2005-04-24. He finished the year at 69 games, 25 points on 13 goals and 12 assists, 13.4 minutes a night.

The Index has him +12 above his base of 75. The next riser on the Bureau's list is Marian Gaborik99 of Toronto Maple Leafs at +11.

The grades the points hide

The Book's per-attribute grades explain what 25 points do not. Nedorost's speed reads 99. His passing reads 96. Agility 95, puck control 95, shot power 95, acceleration 93.

His fighting grade is 50, heroism 60, determination 61. The game can push him off the puck. Among Florida's other centers, Olli Jokinen84 reads speed 92 and passing 81. Joe Nieuwendyk reads speed 85 and passing 86. Nedorost carries the highest speed-passing pair on that center chart by seven points in speed and ten in passing.

The room and the contract

Florida finished with 76 points, 236 goals for, and 16 overtime losses, the most in the league. Their head coach, Boris Van Ryswyk, spoke about it after a game on 2005-05-29.

"We were right there eleven times in a row and we couldn't put the thing away," Van Ryswyk told The Tunnel's Marcus Bell. "You watch the last ten minutes and it's the same thing over and over, we can't close."

Later in the same interview he addressed what his front office has to work with. "I know the pool is empty. Everybody knows the pool is empty. It's not a secret."

Jokinen has 4 years left on his deal. Nieuwendyk has none. Nedorost sits at $1.00M with nothing left. He is the cheapest player in the lineup by a wide margin, and the one the Index has rising fastest. His morale reads 100, his potential 94. No deal, and 58 days until what the calendar has as free agency closes.
