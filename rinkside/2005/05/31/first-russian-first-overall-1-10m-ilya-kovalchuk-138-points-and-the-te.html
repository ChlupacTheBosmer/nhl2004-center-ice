First Russian, First Overall, $1.10M: Ilya Kovalchuk, 138 Points, and the Team That Cannot Afford What He's Worth

He was born in Tver and wore a number for a Soviet legend he never saw play. Four years later he scores 138 points for a team whose third-best scorer makes more than he does.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideTuesday, 31 May 2005
ShareCopy link

The piece

Ilya Kovalchuk86 grew up in Tver, Russia, a city on the Volga two hours northwest of Moscow. As a boy he played for Spartak Moscow in the 1997 Quebec International Pee-Wee Hockey Tournament. He chose number 17 the way Russian kids chose numbers back then, out of love for someone older: Valeri Kharlamov, the Soviet winger who scored the goals nobody in the West was supposed to know about. Kovalchuk was 18 in the summer of 2001 when Atlanta took him first overall, the first Russian ever selected at the top of the draft. He scored 29 goals as a rookie and missed 17 games with a season-ending shoulder injury. He scored 38 the next year. Then 72.

This year he scored 63 goals and 138 points over 79 games at 20.0 minutes a night. He is 22. He makes $1.10M. He has one year left.

The Bureau's Book has him at 90 today, up 5 on the Development Index. His shot power and speed are both 99, his acceleration 98, his agility 97. His fighting is 50. His durability is 51. You do not need a strong frame when everyone else is trying to get out of the way.

The team that made $25.97M because two wingers were cheap

Atlanta Thrashers finished the regular season with 84 points, 31 wins, 37 losses. They missed the playoffs. Their payroll was $36.79M, the second lowest in the league, behind only Vancouver Canucks at $35.16M. They turned a profit of $25.97M on revenue of $70.29M. Their cost per point was $0.44M.

Those numbers look efficient on a spreadsheet. They mean something different once you see who is on the books.

Kovalchuk scored 138 points for $1.10M. Dany Heatley85, his partner on the wing, scored 131 for $1.10M. Between them, $2.20M bought 128 goals on a team that scored 274. That is 46.7 percent of every goal the Thrashers put on the board this season, from two men on one year each.

The third-best scorer on the roster is Sami Kapanen76, a left wing on $3.60M who scored 104 points. He earns more than twice what the team's best player costs.

Byron Dafoe79, the goaltender who started 68 games, earns $2.40M.

Two wingers moving in opposite directions

Right now, Heatley carries a base grade of 91 and sits at 88, 3 below his base on the Index. Kovalchuk has a base of 89, sits at 90, up 5. Same salary, same team, one year from free agency. One is above what the Bureau expects. The other is below.

Heatley scored 131 points. If that is below his base, what does a Heatley playing to his base look like? The Bureau is not saying he gets worse. It is saying he should have been better.

Kovalchuk's potential is 85. That is lower than his current grade of 90. It means the Bureau thinks the room to grow from here is narrow, because he already arrived. He is 22 and the Book says he is already the 2nd-best under-23 player in the league behind only Marian Gaborik99.

With one year left on both contracts, Atlanta is about to learn what these two men cost. They cost more than $1.10M. They cost more than $36.79M in payroll, which is what the whole roster costs.

The arc

Kovalchuk came to North America at 18. The first Russian taken first overall. The Thrashers drafted him and he was supposed to be the cornerstone before he could shave properly. He broke his shoulder as a rookie, played 65 games, and had 51 points. The next year: 38 goals, 67 points. Then 72 and 134.

Then 138, at 20.0 minutes a night, on a team that has nothing else.

The injury that sat him out in March was a shoulder, sometime between March 25 and March 30, back by March 30. His durability grade is 51, which sits below fighting at 50 in a list that otherwise reads 99, 99, 98, 97, 94, 94. The Bureau says he is breakable and fast and the rest of it is fine. So far this year the Bureau is right about the fast part.

Morale is 87. Not perfect. Not unhappy. A 22-year-old who scored 138 points on a team that won 31 games is probably somewhere between proud and frustrated, and the Bureau puts that at 87.

Free agency closes in 58 days. The draft is in 8. Atlanta has to decide whether $36.79M buys a contender around two wingers who cost $2.20M and score nearly half the team's goals, or whether the next money goes to a defenseman, a center, a goaltender, anyone who can make the third line matter. The profit line says the money is there. The cost per point says it already spent wisely. What it has not done is win.

Kovalchuk wears 17. It is for Kharlamov, who played in a league that does not exist anymore, for a country that does not exist anymore, in a city four hundred miles from where this kid was born. He is in Atlanta now, Georgia, scoring at a 143 pace on a team that won 31 games, making the kind of money that gets rewritten before he turns 23. And one year from now, someone is going to pay him what he actually costs.
