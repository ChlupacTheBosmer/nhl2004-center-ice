Morale 100, Base 97, One Year Left: The $32.11M Denver Cannot Hold

Peter Forsberg is the anchor of a contract cliff with no parallel in the league. Four players at 86 or above, all expiring, all content, all walking in the same summer.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideWednesday, 12 October 2005
ShareCopy link

Peter Forsberg89 sits at morale 100. The Development Index has him one point below his base of 97, which means the Book calls him the best player on the Colorado Avalanche roster and a half-step off his own standard. His contract has 1 year left at $9.90M. Last season he scored 60 goals and 69 assists for 129 points in 82 games at 21.4 minutes a night. This season he has 1 game, 1 point, 20.0 minutes. None of those numbers describe a man who wants to leave.

The problem is not one player. Colorado has $32.11M of expiring contracts across seven men, four of them rated 86 or above. That is the richest, most concentrated free-agent class in this league, and it belongs to the fourth-highest payroll at $68.11M. The club that finished last season 43-23-0 with 310 goals for and 246 against, that lost in 7 playoff games, now faces a summer where its four best forwards all become available on the same day.

The room is fine, every man at 100, and that removes the usual exit story. A man at morale 100 is not being pushed out. He is a man the club would re-sign if the math worked.

The boy who was picked too early

The 1991 draft belonged to Eric Lindros89. Quebec took him first overall, he refused to sign, and his mother told him to wait. Philadelphia, six picks later, took a stocky kid from Örnsköldsvik who had 17 points in 23 senior games in Sweden. The Hockey News had ranked Forsberg 25th in its draft preview and called him "a solid second rounder who could move into the first." The Philadelphia media spent the summer calling the pick a waste.

The trade that sent him to Quebec included five players, two first-round picks, and $15 million for Lindros. In hindsight, that deal built a decade. Forsberg stayed in Sweden three more years. His father coached him at Modo. They won the 1998 Olympics together and the 1998 World Championship. In 1993, with Modo eliminated in the quarterfinals, he won the Guldpucken as Player of the Year and the Guldhjälmen as MVP, the award his peers decide.

The Book has passing at 99, deception at 99, heart at 99, puck control at 98. Fighting at 50. Those numbers describe someone who decided, as a teenager on the High Coast, that hockey was a game you solved, and everything else was furniture.

What the arithmetic says

Colorado earns $36.35M in revenue against a $68.11M payroll. The profit line reads $27.91M. Attendance runs 14,945 a night at $82 a ticket. Prestige points sit at 657. Zero Cups. The last playoff run lasted 1 round.

The club makes money. It fills seats. It has never won the thing. And it is about to watch four 86-plus players leave at the same time. Kariya alone at $10.11M is more than the entire Atlanta Thrashers payroll of $39.32M divided among however many men they carry. Selanne at $6.94M and age 35 is a bridge, not a foundation. Hejduk at $2.50M and overall 88 is the one who might actually stay, because the number is low enough that Colorado can carry him alongside a 95-overall left winger.

The Colorado Avalanche last-season record: 129 points for Forsberg, 5th in the league behind Jaromir Jagr90 at 145, Ilya Kovalchuk91 and Markus Naslund91 at 138 each, and Dany Heatley90 at 131. The Hart went to Naslund. The Art Ross went to Jagr. Forsberg finished the season the way he has finished most of them: just below the very top, in the company of men who score for more money or fewer years.

His playoff line last spring: 7 games, 2 goals, 4 assists, 6 points, 19.9 minutes a night. Mike Modano86 had 24 points in 21 games for Dallas Stars. Jagr had 21 in 15 for Washington Capitals. The Avalanche went out in 2-4 and Forsberg went home with 6 points. That is not a man failing. That is a first-round exit where the best passer on the team gets 7 games to show what he does.

The question that costs $9.90M

At 32, the base of 97 will not improve. The Development Index shows a man holding steady, one point below his own floor, which after fifteen seasons in this league is rare in itself. His potential reads 61.

Forsberg has played his whole career in the shadow of a decision someone else made. Philadelphia picked him early. Quebec traded for Lindros and got him as the remainder. He was the man who came along with the prize. The trade became legendary and he was inside it, a component, a name in a column of five players and two picks and $15 million.

Now the decision is his, or at least it is his agent's. One year at $9.90M with a base of 97 and a morale of 100. Colorado's payroll has $32.11M walking out the same summer. The club that employed him before the record opened in December 2004 now has to decide whether a 99-passing left winger who is not getting better but is not getting worse either fits inside a budget where Kariya costs $10.11M and Selanne costs $6.94M and the rest of the room needs to cost something too.

Forsberg grew up idolizing Håkan Loob. He played minor hockey beside Markus Naslund, born ten days apart, same town, same summer job at the same electrical company at 18. He came to North America as the second-best thing in a trade and built a career that made the first-best thing look like the deal. At 32, with 1 year left, he is no one's remainder. He is the man a club either keeps or loses, and $9.90M is the distance between those two things.

The season is 1 game old. The calendar has free agency closing on 2006-07-28, which is 289 days away. Colorado has until then to decide whether the best passer on its roster is worth the money his passing costs. Everyone in that locker room, at 100 morale, is waiting to find out.
