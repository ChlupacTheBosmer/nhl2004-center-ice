The Reinvention of Dan Blackburn

He started 17 straight at 19 and broke. The man they traded for is now 38 and sharing a crease with him. At 22, Blackburn is +6 on the Index with 98 potential and no contract.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideTuesday, 18 October 2005
ShareCopy link

Mike Dunham89 is 38 years old. He has been in this league since 1990, longer than most of the men guarding his crease have been alive. He is the Rangers' best goaltender today at 87, his contract expires in July, and his morale reads 94. Behind him, in the same blue and orange, sits Dan Blackburn87: 22, overall 83, potential 98, and $1.10M a year with zero years left on his deal.

They were never strangers. Dunham was traded to New York in 2002-03 specifically to take weight off Blackburn's shoulders, and now the two men share a room in the building on Seventh Avenue while the rest of the league looks at what the Rangers have built and asks how a $92.95M payroll produced zero Cups.

Part of the answer is sitting on the bench.

What happened when he was 19

Blackburn came out of Montreal as a prospect who was already too good to wait. Taken 10th overall in 2001, he walked into an NHL dressing room at 18 and backed up Mike Richter for 31 games, a number no backup at that age should have been handed. He finished that season with a 3.28 GAA and a .898 save percentage and earned a spot on the All-Rookie Team.

Then Richter's head would not let him play. Post-concussion syndrome, the kind that does not clear on a timetable anyone can control. Blackburn went in and started 17 consecutive games. He won a shutout in overtime against Calgary in November 2002, his only career clean sheet. And then, as happens to a boy asked to carry a franchise at 19, the legs went, the reads went slow, and the confidence thinned.

The Rangers traded for Dunham. They brought in a 35-year-old journeyman to eat the starts, to let Blackburn play at a rate his body could learn from. He finished 2002-03 with a 3.17 GAA, a season played on a leash someone else held.

The three quiet years

Three games in 2003-04. Two so far this season, 41.5 minutes a night, 0-1, 4 goals against. Dunham has played three and carries the starter's load while Blackburn watches and waits and stays healthy enough to be worth the roster spot.

The Development Index says something the games do not. He sits at 83 today against a base grade of 80, which means the Bureau's form term reads +6. Potential sits at 98. Intelligence is 91. The glove and the left pad are both 88, the shot stop too.

These are not the grades of a backup. They are the grades of a man two or three years from something, if the body cooperates.

What the room costs

The Rangers spend $92.95M, the highest payroll in this league. Toronto Maple Leafs spends $89.94M, St. Louis Blues spends $69.76M. The per-point figure reads $18.59M. Their revenue is $32.53M, their profit is $20.44M, their attendance is 14,195 at $82 a ticket. No Cups. The previous playoff run lasted 0 rounds.

The expiring contracts tell the rest. Nine men are on one-year deals in that room, and together they carry $36.00M. Dunham at $3.10M. Alexei Kovalev90 at $8.57M. Eric Lindros89 at $4.50M. Petr Nedved88 at $4.60M. Brian Leetch88 at $8.20M. The Rangers will spend the summer replacing them, or watching them leave, or some negotiation of both.

And in the middle of that room sits a 22-year-old goaltender with 98 potential making $1.10M.

The question the calendar asks

Dunham is the last man to hold the crease while Blackburn gets there. When his contract ends this summer, or when his body says it is done, the Rangers hand the net to a man with 5 NHL appearances in the last two seasons, because no one else in that room can carry it.

Blackburn's morale is 92, the Bureau's Index has him at +6, his potential is 98. He was drafted 10th overall at 18, earned an All-Rookie Team spot at 19, broke at 19, and spent three years learning not to break.

The Rangers traded for Dunham to save him. The saving is nearly done. The 38-year-old beside him has 1 year left and a morale of 94 that says he does not care where the puck goes after that. What happens next belongs to the kid who already knows what it costs to carry a city at an age when nobody should.

He is 22. He is +6. He has 4 goals against in 41.5 minutes a night. Somewhere between the shutout in overtime against Calgary and this quiet October on Seventh Avenue, he became the most valuable thing the Rangers have and the thing they have used least.
