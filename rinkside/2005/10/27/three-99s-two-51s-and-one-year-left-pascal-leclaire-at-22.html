Three 99s, Two 51s, and One Year Left: Pascal Leclaire at 22

The second-best player under 23 in this league makes $1.10M, plays in front of a room that cannot score, and has no contract for next season.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideThursday, 27 October 2005
ShareCopy link

The piece

The League Scouting Bureau gives Pascal Leclaire89 a 93 overall. It gives him 99 in agility, 99 in glove, and 99 in speed. It also gives him 51 in positioning and 51 in pad displacement. A goaltender who never quite gets his body where he wants it, but whose body is fast enough to forgive the mistake every time. He has 77 starts behind him across two full seasons and a partial third, 7 goals against in 4 games this season, and no contract for next year.

Pascal Leclaire turns 23 on November 7. He sits second among every player under 23 in this league, behind only Marian Gaborik99 at Toronto. Among goaltenders his age or younger, he is not second to anyone. His base grade is 83. The Bureau's Development Index has him at +9, which is the same reading that Ales Hemsky83 got in Edmonton last week and that Vaclav Nedorost80 got in Florida. But Hemsky plays in a room with Ryan Smyth88 who scores at a 105 pace. Nedorost has the 99 speed that turns a 75 into an 80. Leclaire plays in Columbus, where the room has managed 19 goals in 7 games and given up 15.

The road that made him

He grew up in Repentigny, Quebec, not far from Montreal. He played his junior hockey in the QMJHL: Halifax Mooseheads, then the Montreal Rocket, four seasons between 1998 and 2002. Columbus took him eighth overall in 2001, the first goaltender selected in that draft. Ilya Kovalchuk86 went first, and is now the best-scoring player at 22 in the league. Leclaire went first at his position, which in 2001 meant he was the safest pick in the room.

He came out of junior at 19, played 24 games that first season, won 11 of them. The team gave him 49 games last year. He won 23, lost 25, and gave up 131 goals at 60.5 minutes a night. Nobody outside Columbus thought that was special. The Book thought so, because the base grade went from wherever it sat the year before to 83.

This season he has played 4 games. Three wins, one loss, 7 goals against. If he qualified for the goaltending table, he would sit between Roberto Luongo95 at 1.61 and Martin Brodeur95 at 1.71. He does not qualify because he has only 4 starts. Columbus has a backup named Marc Denis86 who sits at 89 in the Book, which is the kind of luxury that keeps a 22-year-old from logging the volume he needs to build a season.

What the grades say

The three attributes at 99 tell you where the saves come from: quickness, hands, recovery. The 97 in speed confirms the legs are there. The 97 in shot reading says he sees the play early. The 94 in five-hole says the bottom is sealed when his body is in the right place.

The 51 in positioning says he is not always in the right place. The 51 in pad displacement says when he does move, the pads do not cover what they should. The 51 in fighting is about what a goaltender does when a man comes across the crease to make a point. A man with three attributes at 99 and three at 51 saves the impossible shot and concedes the routine one. He makes 40 saves a night and loses 3-2.

That is what Columbus looks like from the crease. The team has 11 points through 7 games, which puts them tied for sixth in the league. They have scored 19 in that span. Their best forward, Geoff Sanderson82, is 33 years old and sits at 86. They have no one else on the roster above 86. At Toronto on October 12, they managed 1 goal in a 4-1 loss.

The money problem

$1.10M. That is what a 93 overall goaltender costs in Columbus right now. The contract has 0 years left. Come July, he is free.

The club has $62.03M in payroll, $36.52M in revenue, $23.63M in profit. They are solvent. They are not rich. A 93 goaltender signing a new deal at the top of the market takes $7M, maybe $8M, and that is a serious question for a payroll that currently holds Geoff Sanderson at $6.34M on a 33-year-old body. The front office must decide whether this team is good enough to build around or good enough to sell pieces before the window narrows.

Leclaire does not make that decision. He plays 60.0 minutes a night when he starts. His morale is 90, which is not a man who hates it there. The Index says he is better than the 83 base. The Book says the potential is 94. He is one point below the ceiling the Bureau believes he can reach, and he is doing it in a sweater that gives him nothing to protect.

At 22, in his third season, the man from Repentigny who was the first goaltender drafted in 2001 has the hands and feet of the best in this league and the positioning of a man still learning where to stand. The 93 says he is already better than most. The 51 is the gap that will define where he stops. And the $1.10M says the price changes this summer whether Columbus is ready or not.
