The Morning Before Coffee: Vincent Lecavalier, $2.60M, and the Year That Makes Him Expensive

First overall at 18, stripped of a captaincy at 21, broken and broken again. At 25, the contract his team cannot avoid is the one he signed before he knew what he was worth.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideMonday, 24 October 2005
ShareCopy link

Michael Burt thinks about the contract before he finishes his coffee, every morning. He said it on The Tunnel this week, the way a man admits a thing he has been holding for weeks: "You've got a 25-year-old center at $2.60M, an 86 in the Book, and a year on the clock. The moment that number moves up six, the moment the League Scouting Bureau edition changes and he's in the high 80s, the money changes a lot. So yes. I think about it before I have my coffee, if I'm being honest."

The money changes a lot on Vincent Lecavalier90, and the morning Burt drinks his coffee and runs the math is Tuesday, October 24, 2005, five games into a season where Lecavalier has scored 7 points in 26.6 minutes a night at morale 100 and his club is 3-0-2.

The first pick

The summer of 1998 produced a sentence that followed Lecavalier for seven years. Art Williams, the owner who had just bought the Tampa Bay Lightning, stood at a podium on draft day and called the 18-year-old center from Ile-Bizard, Quebec, "the Michael Jordan of hockey." The boy had won the QMJHL's rookie awards in his first year at Rimouski. He was fast enough to make a 6-foot-4 frame look like a mistake. He had the hands.

He did not have, at 19, the kind of steadiness a franchise asks of its first pick. He got the captaincy. He kept it for two seasons. Before 2001-02 began, Lightning management decided he was too young for the letter and took it back. He was 21.

Three months later, on January 14, 2001, a slap shot from the Philadelphia Flyers broke his foot. He missed 14 games and finished that season with 51 points. It was the first injury the public remembered, and it would not be the last.

The broken seasons

Last February, between the 16th and the 19th, a leg went. He was out six weeks, back on April 2. He finished 2003-04 with 105 points in 59 games, and the team made the second round of the playoffs, where Lecavalier added 7 points in 7 games before the run ended.

The season before that, his best, he put up 138 points in 82 games. 21.3 minutes a night. 32 penalty minutes. He was 22 and he looked like the Jordan of hockey, for one stretch, the way he did in the 2004 World Cup when he won the Canadian team's MVP award and the team lifted the cup.

That season is the ceiling now. The Book has him at 86. His potential is 86, which means the Bureau does not think it moves. His form is 0, dead on his base. None of that matters on the ice in October, where 7 points in 5 games and a 26.6-minute role say more than a Book grade can: the man is playing the most minutes of his career, and he is good enough to deserve them.

The contract and what it costs

Tampa Bay Lightning pays him $2.60M. That is the number Burt runs in his head every morning. The club's payroll sits at $59.79M, fifth-lowest in the league. Lecavalier is 4.3% of it. If the Bureau's next edition moves him up even two points, the entry money ends. If he returns to the 90s he touched in his best year, the money doubles or more.

For comparison: Peter Forsberg95 is 32, a 95 overall at $9.90M with one year left and morale 100. Paul Kariya89 is 31, an 89 at $10.11M with one year. Daniel Alfredsson89 is 32, an 89 at $11.40M. David Legwand89, 25, sits at $8.37M on one year with a 90 overall. Lecavalier is 25, one year, $2.60M, 86 in the Book, morale 100, and the coach who said "I watch him and I think this is the year the number jumps again. The question isn't whether he's the guy. The question is what he is next year when the Bureau puts him somewhere north of 86 and..."

He trailed off there. Burt did not finish the sentence on The Tunnel.

The room around him

Nikolai Khabibulin91 is 89 in the Book and has won 3 of 5 starts at 2.00 GAA. Martin St. Louis88 is 84 and playing right wing. Cory Stillman86 is on the left wing at 82. This room did not let a first pick flounder. The captaincy came off because the organization decided he was 21 and needed the letter more than he did. The organization has spent four years building around him. He is 25 now, and the letter is gone from his résumé, but the minutes are 26.6 a night and the points keep coming and the morale sits at the top of the scale.

The decision

The contract-year class in this league is deep: 30 players with one year left and an overall of 84 or above. The top of that class makes $11.40M. The bottom makes $0.90M. Lecavalier makes $2.60M. He will not make $2.60M after this season. The Bureau will publish its next edition. If his overall moves, his agent gets a phone call. If it holds at 86, his team has another year at a number they will not get again.

Burt said he will not sit him down. "I let him play through it. You don't sit a man down in the middle of a run like this and put numbers in front of him. He's got 7 in 5, he's got his legs, morale is 100. I want that staying exactly where it is through March. But it's on my list. Top of the list."

Top of the list. In Tampa, that means someone has already opened a folder, run the payroll forward, and set aside a line that currently reads $2.60M. The coffee gets cold before it's done.
