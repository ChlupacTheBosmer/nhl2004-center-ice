The Cheapest Room and the Most Expensive Problem: Edmonton, $42.61M, and the Goaltender Who Cannot Close

Two men rising on the Bureau's Index faster than anyone at their age. A payroll no other club can go below. A goaltender at 1 and 5 who makes the rest irrelevant.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideMonday, 24 October 2005
ShareCopy link

The payroll is $42.61M. No club in this league spends less. Edmonton Oilers carries the cheapest room in hockey, and the cheapest room in hockey has 17 goals for and 22 against in the first six games because a goaltender named Tommy Salo86 is 1-5 with a 3.24 GAA and a save percentage of 0.851. The young men rising on the Bureau's Development Index will not save a team that cannot close a game with Salo standing in net.

The risers

Ales Hemsky81 is 22, shoots right, and the Index has him up 9 points. His base grade is 75. The Book today shows 83. He plays 19.3 minutes a night and has 4 points in 6 games. His potential is 90, which means the Bureau thinks he is not done at 83. On the night of October 22, the Index published its new edition and Hemsky's number jumped nine places, second only to the 11 that Vaclav Nedorost84 gained in Florida and the 11 that Marian Gaborik99 put up in Toronto.

Jani Rita81 is 24, a left winger, and the Index has him up 8. Base 76, today 83, potential 86. Two points in 6 games at 19.3 minutes. Both of them are the kind of player a cheap roster is built to develop: late draft picks or second-round talent that another GM passed on, given 20 minutes a night in a room where nobody is fighting for a top-six slot.

Neither is the problem. The problem is between the pipes.

The goaltender

Salo is 88 in the Book. He was the fourth-round pick of 1993, #118 overall. Last season he played 71 games, won 37, posted a 2.90 GAA. This season he has 6 games, 1 win, 5 losses, a 3.24 GAA and a 0.851 save percentage. The jump from 2.90 to 3.24 is half a goal per game. Over 6 starts that is 3 goals, and 3 goals is the difference between 1-5 and 4-2.

The veterans who carry

Ryan Smyth86 is 29, 88 in the Book, and has 9 points in 6 games at 23.0 minutes a night. He was the sixth pick of the 1994 draft. He plays left wing and the room follows him.

Mike Comrie84 is 25, 86 in the Book, 8 points in 6 games, 22.8 minutes a night. The fourth-round pick of 1999, #91 overall, producing at a 109 pace on a room whose payroll could not cover a single season of Peter Forsberg89.

Smyth and Comrie are cheap because the payroll is $42.61M total and 34 men share it, and the system does not ask more of the cheapest room in hockey.

The business

Revenue is $32.52M. Profit is $21.70M. Attendance is 14,481 a game at $81 a ticket. Prestige sits at 1026, seventh-highest in the league. The club has never won a Stanley Cup. Last season they reached the second round of the playoffs, 5 wins in 12 games. They have $42.61M and 1026 prestige points and two 23-year-olds rising nine and eight points on the Index, and they have allowed 22 goals in 6 games.

What the cheap room buys

The cheapest payroll in the league buys 19.3 minutes a night for Hemsky and Rita. It buys Smyth and Comrie at their entry-level numbers because the club has no choice but to keep them there. What it does not buy is a goaltender who can hold a lead.

The Index says Hemsky and Rita are going up. It says nothing about Salo, who is not on the fallers list and not on the risers list. His form is 0. His overall is 88. The 1-5 record comes from the form, not the grade. No payroll this low is going to replace a 0.851 save percentage, and no room that scores 17 and concedes 22 before November is going to wait for one to develop.

The wins are there in the games where the offense outworks the back end. The 17 goals for say that works sometimes. The 22 against say it has to work every single night, because no one behind it is going to bail the room out.
