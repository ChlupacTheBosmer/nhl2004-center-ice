The Man They Traded for a Center: Daniel Briere, 6 Points, and the $3.59M Question

Phoenix let him go for Chris Gratton. Four years later he leads the league, his team pays $14.35M a point, and his contract expires in July.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideSaturday, 15 October 2005
ShareCopy link

The piece

Daniel Briere80 scored his second goal of the night in the third period against Chicago Blackhawks on October 13, and the Sabres won in overtime. Three games, 6 points, the most in hockey. He is 5 feet 9, 178 pounds, and four years ago the Phoenix Coyotes traded him for a center named Chris Gratton and a fourth-round pick.

That trade still echoes through Buffalo Sabres. The Chris Gratton they wanted back was never the reason. They just wanted out on a player they thought was too small, too soft, too something. He was born in Gatineau, Quebec, in 1977, and he played midget hockey there before Drummondville took him in the QMJHL draft in 1994. The Phoenix Coyotes selected him 24th overall in the first round of the 1996 draft, and he went back to Drummondville for one more year before turning pro.

What came next was four years of being passed around between the Coyotes and the Springfield Falcons of the AHL. In his first full professional season in 1998 he put up 92 points in 68 games for Springfield, won the Dudley "Red" Garrett Memorial Award as the league's top rookie, and was named a First Team All-Star center at that position. He is still the third-leading scorer in Falcons franchise history. None of that was enough to keep him on an NHL roster. He earned a consistent spot late in the 2000-01 season, and Phoenix gave him 60 points in 2001-02. Then, at the 2002-03 deadline, they sent him to Buffalo.

Briere began off-season work with Hugo Girard, a Canadian World's Strongest Man competitor who grew up in Gatineau, the same streets, the same rinks. Girard was there because Briere could not out-muscle the league. He had to build a body that would not break. The grades on the Bureau's Book show what that work bought him: Deking 93, Passing 90, Faceoff 88, Puckcontrol 88, Acceleration 87, Agility 86. His Fighting grade is 50. His Injury Resistance is 52. He has played every game this season, all 3, and 82 last year.

Buffalo is paying him $3.59M with one year left. His base grade on the Book is 83, and the Development Index has him at +1 today, so the overall reads 82. His morale is 92. He leads the Sabres in scoring through 3 games with 6 points on 2 goals and 4 assists, which projects to 164 points over a full season. The team's payroll is $57.39M, its cost per point is $14.35M, and that ranks fourth-cheapest in the league. The club pulled in $27.37M in revenue on 16,447 seats and a $78 ticket, and booked $17.67M in profit.

The roster around him is loaded with men on short leashes. Chris Drury85 has 4 points in 3 games and 0 years left on his contract. Alexei Zhitnik81 is at 0 years. Joel Bouchard77, the defenseman with 4 points of his own through 3 games, is also at 0. Briere has 1 year left. The cheapest points in hockey are sitting on a house of cards that could blow up in July.

His best season before this one was 2003-04: 82 games, 30 goals, 65 assists, 95 points. That was the year after the trade, his first full year in Buffalo, and it was the kind of number that makes a general manager wonder about the phone call he made in March. He did not finish among the league's top ten scorers, but 95 points at 26 years old on a team that missed the playoffs put him in a conversation Phoenix had already stopped having.

The Sabres are 2-1-0 through 3 games. Their goals for and against sit at 11 and 9. They scored 4 goals in regulation at Chicago on October 13 and won in overtime. Briere had the game-winning goal. His ice time was 22.3 minutes a night, same as last season.

The room behind him

Owen Nolan86 is 33 and has 5 points in 3 games at 22.7 minutes. Martin Biron85 is 90 overall in the crease. Tim Connolly80 is 82 at center behind Briere. The room is good. Everyone in it knows what his contract says, and almost none of them have more than a year left.

Briere turns 29 in October. He has spent nine professional seasons being the player someone else did not want. In Phoenix he was the small center they traded for a center they thought they could trust more. In Springfield he was the AHL rookie they sent down. In Gatineau he was the kid who needed a strongman to build a body that belonged in the league. Now he leads the league through a week on a team that pays $14.35M a point.

One year left. $3.59M. 164-point pace. The Sabres have until July to figure out what that costs.
