At 29, the Book has him at 96: the $0.60M goaltender nobody priced

Marty Turco grades highest among goaltenders, carries Dallas on a fifth-rounder's salary, and has one year to make the market notice.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideTuesday, 4 January 2005
ShareCopy link

The Book gives Marty Turco92 a 96. No goaltender in this league grades higher. He makes $0.60M. His contract expires in seven months.

Marty Turco is 29 years old. He played 33 games this season and won 21 of them. His goals-against average is 3.16, his save percentage .862. Those are not the league's best numbers. The overall grade is. The overall grade is what the Bureau's Book puts on a man after it weighs everything it can measure, and for a goaltender the grades that carry the most weight are glove, butterfly slides, and reading the play before it arrives: 99 glove, 98 slide, 98 intelligence. The consistency grade is 51. The toughness grade is 50. Neither is a skill, and the Book cannot move them.

The road he took

Nobody drafted Turco in the OHL. He was a 17-year-old in Sault Ste. Marie, Ontario, who signed with Cambridge Jr. B in 1992, played two seasons in the OHA, and accepted a scholarship to the University of Michigan. He won two NCAA titles there. He was Tournament MVP in 1998. Dallas took him 124th overall in the fifth round of the 1994 draft, the same year they already had Ed Belfour93 in net.

After Michigan, Turco went to the IHL's Kalamazoo Wings and won their Rookie of the Year in 1999. Dallas made him the starter after the 2001-02 season. Belfour left for Toronto. In 2002-03, Turco's 1.72 goals-against average set a modern NHL record. He started the All-Star Game the next year. He went 45-23 in 70 games, allowed 206 goals, and lost the first round to Colorado.

This season he is at 21 and 11 in 33 starts. The Development Index has him one above his base of 94.

Turco is the highest-rated goaltender in the class, and the cheapest by a wide margin. Roman Cechmanek in Los Angeles grades 91 and makes $3.30M. Ed Belfour, Turco's predecessor in Dallas, makes $6.50M at 39 with a 92. Jose Theodore makes $5.50M at a grade of 89. Turco, at 96, makes what a backup makes on a team with a clear starter.

The room around him

Dallas Stars has 55 points. They sit second in the payroll at $66.88M. Bill Guerin85 leads the league at 84 points in 41 games, a 168-point pace. Mike Modano86 has 75 in 37 at 34 years old. Brenden Morrow82 is 25 with 54 points and one year left on $1.30M. This is a team built to win now, paying premium money up and down the forward lines, and its goaltender makes the least money of any player on the roster.

Marty Turco told The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk after Dallas beat Detroit 3-2 on December 29 that the difference between his home and road record this season comes down to sleep. "Nine hours in your own bed, I'll take that over the bus every night of December. You come in, the legs are better, you don't feel like, you know, you were jostled around for six hours." He was talking about the schedule, and about knowing what you can fix.

When asked about inconsistency, the same conversation: "Some nights I'm great, some nights I'm just, I'm there. I'm not gonna, I'm not gonna sit here and tell you I'm something I'm not. But I look at 19 wins, and I look at what this group's doing, and the inconsistency hasn't cost us that many. Maybe a couple. You'd like to have them back."

He had 19 wins at that point. He has 21 now.

What 96 costs

The Bureau's Book grades the glove at 99. That is the single highest attribute grade any goaltender in the league carries. The slide is 98. The reading of the play is 98. The endurance is 96, which matters because he started 70 games last season and 33 of Dallas's 42 this year. He is the man between the pipes when it matters, and the grade says so, and the contract says $0.60M.

The last time a goaltender left Dallas in free agency, he was 36, still playing at 92, and Toronto paid him $6.50M. That man was Ed Belfour. He still plays at 92, on a one-year deal in a city that has won a Cup since he arrived.

Turco will be a free agent in a market that saw Belfour get that number at 36. He is 29. He grades four points higher. He played 70 games last season and 33 in 42 this season. The consistency grade is 51 and it will not move and he knows it. But 51 consistency does not stop a team from needing you every night, and it does not stop a general manager from watching the playoffs and wondering what a 96 costs when the market sets the price instead of a fifth-round pick from 1994.

He sleeps better at home. Dallas is 13 and 3 there. The road is where the legs go and the 51 shows up in the column.
