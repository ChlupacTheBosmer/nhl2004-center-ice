At 31, the league's best scorer is on the cheapest team in the playoff race

Markus Naslund is putting up a 162-point pace on a $34.94M payroll, and the Book says he will not get better than this

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideWednesday, 19 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Markus Naslund91 scored his 49th goal of the season three nights ago in Minneapolis, the one that made it 4-3 with the game still in the balance. He took a pass from the half-wall, stepped into the slot, and put it top corner. Twenty seconds of watching the net ripple, and then he skated back to the bench like any other night in January. Thirty-one years old, leading the league, putting up a pace no leading scorer has touched before.

91 points in 46 games. That reads as 162 over 82. He scored 133 in 82 games last season, a career year by any measure. He is on pace to blow through that number by 29 points, and he is doing it on a club that spent $34.94M this year and is getting $0.57M per point, the cheapest production in the league.

Vancouver Canucks sits sixth in the standings with 61 points, the last club in the Presidents' Trophy conversation, on a payroll that would make the floor in Detroit look generous. The Canucks went 25-39 last season and 245-305 in the score column. This year they are outscoring opponents 33-32 in the last ten and winning games they had no business winning a year ago. None of it happens without the man wearing the C.

The Bureau's number that nobody reads

The Book has Naslund at 91. His base grade is 92. The Development Index has him at zero: no movement up, no drift down. The potential column reads 53, which is where it gets sobering. A potential grade is not a ceiling, but at 53 the evaluators think his development is close to done. He is not going to get better.

He doesn't need to. A 91-grade player scoring at a 162 pace is the best combination of rating and production in the league. Todd Bertuzzi95 has the higher grade at 95, and Bertuzzi's 83 points in 46 games (a 148 pace) are fine. But 162 and 148 are different animals.

The sheet cannot answer whether he can hold this. His 20.0 minutes a night are reasonable, not punishing. His morale sits at 95. His contract has two years left at $4.40M. At 31, with the potential at 53 and the base at 92, he is the player he is right now, and this is the season that counts.

From a town that makes too many players

Örnsköldsvik, Sweden. Population small enough that the same electrical company employed Markus Naslund's mother and Peter Forsberg95's father. They worked there at 18, played junior hockey for Modo on the same line, and were born within ten days of each other in 1973.

Craig Patrick, Pittsburgh's general manager, drafted Naslund 16th overall in 1991 and said that night that the Swedish winger was a more natural goal-scorer than Jaromir Jagr90, who had just finished his rookie year with the club. That claim aged strangely for about twelve years. It looks like prophecy now.

Naslund stayed in Sweden two more seasons after the draft, then walked away from Pittsburgh's contract offer when it expired in the spring of 1993 and became a free agent. What happened between then and this season is not in the record. What is in the record is a 31-year-old captain, a 195-pound left winger who shoots left, grades 98 on deking and 98 on puck control, and grades 50 on fighting. He is a technician, and the technician is scoring more goals in 46 games than he scored in the first 60 games of his career year last season.

The room around him

Todd Bertuzzi at 95 is the second-best player on this club and the most expensive thing they own at $2.60M with one year left. Dan Cloutier88 at 88 is the goaltender, on $1.00M with no years left, which means he is a free agent in June. Ed Jovanovski87 at 87 is out with a knee until 2005-02-19. Trevor Linden85 at 85 is the other veteran, $3.80M with two years left, the man who came back to this club after years in New Jersey and New York.

The bottom of the roster carries Daniel Sedin79 at 75 on $0.90M and Henrik Sedin74 at 74 on $0.90M, both 23, both from the same region of Sweden where Naslund and Forsberg came from. Henrik is out with a shoulder through 2005-01-22. The two young Sedins and Naslund share a geography: Örnsköldsvik and the surrounding Ångermanland district produced all of them. The Canucks' roster carries that map on it.

What the room costs

The club's profit is $28.80M, third in the league, on a payroll that would be second-to-last if Minnesota were not spending $29.97M. Their attendance is 14816 per game at a ticket price of $78. Revenue is $55.95M. They take in more than almost everyone and spend less than almost everyone, and the difference is real.

Vancouver's prestige points are 423, which puts them in the middle of the league. They have won zero Cups. Last season's playoff run lasted 7 games, 3 wins and 4 losses, eliminated in the first round. The Presidents' Trophy belongs to Dallas Stars, the Conn Smythe went to Gary Roberts85, the Stanley Cup went to Toronto Maple Leafs. None of that machinery is set up in Vancouver yet.

Naslund knows this. His contract has two years left, he is 31, the potential is 53. If the Canucks are going to be a team that matters in April, they need to matter in the next 12 to 14 months, which means Bertuzzi's free agency in June, Cloutier's free agency in June, Jovanovski's return in February, and the Sedins maturing on a timeline that nobody controls. The captain is putting up the best numbers of his life right now, and this window depends on decisions the club has to make by spring.

He is not going to get better. He is already as good as he will ever be, and as good looks like 162 points, a first-place spot in the Art Ross race, and a club spending half of what Detroit spends to stay within striking distance of a playoff berth. At 31, on the cheapest roster in the playoff picture, Markus Naslund is the best player in this league who has no more seasons like this left in him.
