The $11.20M Man Is the Best Player in the League

Washington spent three years trying to trade Jaromir Jagr. At 32, he's the highest-paid man in hockey and the one nobody can stop.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideSaturday, 1 January 2005
ShareCopy link

The Capitals tried to trade Jaromir Jagr88 all through 2003. Ownership called every GM in the league, worked the salary cap, dangled first-round picks. Nobody wanted the contract. Nobody wanted the problem. The man on $11.20M was supposed to be a dead weight, a relic of a front office that thought buying veterans would be faster than developing them.

Six months later, he leads the league.

Jaromir Jagr has 80 points in 38 games for Washington Capitals, a 173-point pace over 82. That is the highest pace in the league, ahead of Mike Modano86 at 169 and Bill Guerin85 at 168. He is 32 years old. He has six years left on the contract nobody wanted.

The Bureau's Book has him at 89 overall, two points below his base of 91. The Development Index says his form is zero. He is not playing above his level. He is playing at the level they wrote down for him fifteen years ago, the year he came out of Kladno at 18 and walked into Pittsburgh and never left as a supporting player.

The four years Washington made him carry a room

Jagr arrived in Washington in July 2001, traded from Pittsburgh for three prospects and a defenceman he would never see play another NHL game. The Penguins were rebuilding. Washington was supposed to be the place where his numbers stayed elite while a team finally built around them.

That did not happen. In his first full season in the capital, the Capitals missed the playoffs for the first time in a decade. For the first time in his career, Jagr did not finish among the league's top scorers. He did not make the All-Star team. The team that had bought him was already looking for the door by the time he walked through it.

Washington finished sixth in the East in 2002-03, lost to Tampa Bay in the first round, and ownership spent the summer shopping its captain. They could not move him. The salary was too large, the years too many, the room around him too thin.

So he stayed. He put up 151 points last season, a 151 pace, good for fourth in the league behind Glen Murray79, Eric Daze81, and Sergei Samsonov81. That was a very good year. It was not this year.

What the Book says about 32

His deke is 99. His speed is 98. His acceleration is 96, his passing 96, his puck-handling 96, his shot power 95. These are not the grades of a man managing a decline. They are the grades of a man still doing the things that made him the best right-winger in the history of the position. The 1995-96 records for assists and points by a right-winger still stand. His hands have not left.

What has left is the fighting grade, 50, and the faceoff, 51. He is not going to win a battle along the boards or take a draw in his own end. He never was that player. What he is, at 32, is the fastest man on the ice with the puck and the smartest reader of a two-on-one. At 22.3 minutes a night, that is enough.

Peter Forsberg95 talked about what one extra beat does this week, about his own center in Colorado. "When Joe is not there, I have the man on me all night," Forsberg told The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk after Washington beat Philadelphia 6-5 on December 27. "With him, I get, uh, maybe one beat more. One beat is everything." Jagr has always lived in that one beat. The difference is that the rest of the league has finally caught up to what the Book knew in 1991.

The room around him

Washington is 18-13-5, 45 points through 38 games. They are in the playoff picture, which is not the same as being good. Olaf Kolzig92 is 18-16 with a 3.48 goals-against and a .846 save percentage. Peter Bondra85 has 61 points in 33 games at age 36. Sergei Gonchar82 carries the blue line.

This is not a contender. It is a team with one extraordinary player and a collection of good ones, the same collection they had when they tried to trade him. The difference is that Jagr has decided to carry them instead of waiting for someone else to do it.

He is the captain. The letter came in Pittsburgh when Ron Francis left for Carolina in 1998. He carried it through four scoring titles, a Hart, an Olympic gold in Nagano. He carried it through the trade, the years in Washington where the room asked nothing of him because the room was not asking anything of anyone. He is carrying it now, at 32, on the biggest contract in hockey, with six more years before the cap room clears.

The pace nobody predicted

It would be the highest single-season pace since Glen Murray's 168 last year. It would be a 173-point season if he keeps this rate through February, March, April.

At 32, on a room that is 18 and 13, with a goals-against of 139 in 38 games, with a Development Index that reads zero, he is playing like a man who decided the contract did not matter anymore. The Capitals wanted out. They could not get out. So he stayed, and the only direction left was through.

The picture the league has of Jaromir Jagr is a man who was expensive and underachieving in the wrong city. The picture from the last 38 games is the best scorer in hockey, still the fastest, the most patient, the only man who reads the play a beat before it arrives. The $11.20M looks different from here.
