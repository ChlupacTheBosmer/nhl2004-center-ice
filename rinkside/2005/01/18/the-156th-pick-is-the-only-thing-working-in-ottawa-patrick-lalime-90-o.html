The 156th Pick Is the Only Thing Working in Ottawa: Patrick Lalime, 90 on the Book, and the March That's Coming

The general manager told The Tunnel he would trade his best goaltender rather than lose him for nothing. The goaltender has not said a word.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideTuesday, 18 January 2005
ShareCopy link

The piece

Michael Hui said it on The Tunnel this week, plainly and without hedging. "Patrick's done a good job, he's been our guy, and if we're not in a position to re-sign him at a number that makes sense, then I'd rather trade him in March and get something back for the pipeline than lose him for nothing in July. I've had that conversation with myself. I haven't had it with him yet."

That is a general manager, at 15 wins and 21 regulation losses, saying that the man in his crease is a tradeable asset. The man in his crease is Patrick Lalime90, 30 years old, 90 on the Bureau's Book, and the reason the Ottawa Senators Senators are not mathematically dead.

Lalime came from nowhere. Drafted 156th overall in the sixth round of 1993 out of St. Bonaventure, Quebec, a town nobody scouts. He worked into the Ottawa crease, took the job, and won 33 games last season. This year, his numbers look different. 14 wins, 20 losses, a 3.51 goals-against average, a 0.840 save percentage in 37 starts. His base grade on the Book is 92, but the Development Index has slid him to -1, putting his overall at 90. One notch down, and the team behind him has fallen a great deal further.

The arithmetic of a tailspin

Ottawa has lost 8 of 10 heading into this week. They scored 23 goals in that stretch and allowed 32. Lalime has faced what his teammates have given him, which is not much. The offense that carried them last year is gone, the back end that held the line is slower, and the team is playing for fourth place in a division where fourth is a long way from the post-season.

The GM's other line tells you more than the trade talk. "I don't have a player I can look at and say, there's the top-end talent we build the next five years around. That's why we're fifteen and twenty-one." A man with a 96 overall and 68 points plays in Toronto. A man with a 91 overall and 82 points plays in Atlanta. The Senators have Daniel Alfredsson91 at 88, Martin Havlat89 at 86, and a 22-year-old goaltender named Ray Emery80 who has started 2 games. None of them, in the general manager's own words, is the foundation.

What the market does with a 90

Goaltenders with a 90 on the Book are rare. Martin Brodeur91 is a 92, Tomas Vokoun90 is a 92, Roman Cechmanek91 is a 91. Lalime sits just under all three. His salary, $1.90M, is less than half of what Cechmanek costs in Los Angeles. The difference between him and those men is one thing, and it is not talent: he is 30, and the Index is pointing down.

A club at the deadline looks at a goaltender's age, his save percentage, and the number of years left on his contract. Lalime answers the second question at 0.840. He answers the third with one. He fails the first. A contender needing a playoff backup gets a discount. A contender needing a playoff starter looks elsewhere.

The trade deadline is 60 days away. Enough time for a room to decide what it is, and enough time for a goaltender to find out.

The man who came from St. Bonaventure

There is a version of this story where Lalime never plays a game in the National Hockey League. Sixth round. 156th overall. A kid from a small town in western Quebec who went to the University of Maine and waited. Most sixth-round goaltenders wash out by 24. He took his time and won the job.

He is not the best goaltender in this league. The Bureau's Development Index has Jean-Sebastien Giguere97 at 97, Marty Turco94 at 96, Roberto Luongo94 at 94. Lalime is a 90. But the 90 is what it is, and it belongs to a club that cannot give him enough to matter.

What he has said

Nothing. Not to The Tunnel, not to anyone who has published it. The GM said he has not had the conversation with him yet. The goaltender is in the building every day, preparing for games that Ottawa keeps losing, carrying a 0.840 save percentage on a team that outshot its opponents only twice in its last ten games and won one.

A 90 on a team of 15 wins means showing up at 6:00 on a Thursday morning in January when the standings say you should not bother. It means the 2,259 minutes he has played this season, 132 goals against, 14 times that the work held and 20 times it did not.

The conversation is coming. The general manager said so himself. Sixty days to the deadline. Six months to free agency. The arithmetic is simple, and the arithmetic does not care that he is a sixth-round pick from St. Bonaventure, Quebec who walked into a building nobody wanted and made it his own for three seasons. The arithmetic says: move him, get something, start again.

And the goaltender keeps working. You come back and you play.
