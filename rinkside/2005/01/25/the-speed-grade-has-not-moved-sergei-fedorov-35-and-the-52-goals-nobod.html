The Speed Grade Has Not Moved: Sergei Fedorov, 35, and the 52 Goals Nobody in Anaheim Could Have Predicted

He left the Soviet Union at 20, won the Hart at 24, won the Cup at 27. Now he leads the league in goals on a club that has never won a playoff series, and the Bureau's speed number is the same number it was in 1994.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideTuesday, 25 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Sergei Fedorov86 scored 52 goals in 48 games. Nobody in this league scored more. Not Jaromir Jagr90 with 44, not Bill Guerin89 with 48, not Markus Naslund91 with 50. The league's leading goal scorer is a 35-year-old center playing for a club in Anaheim that has never won a playoff series, on a payroll of $41.01M, at $0.72M a point.

He is in his fifteenth season. He defected from the Soviet Union in July 1990 during the Goodwill Games in Portland, and he has been scoring in this league ever since. In 1993-94, Wayne Gretzky watched him destroy the Rangers and said later that he had never seen a player dominate a game that way. Gretzky thought Fedorov was the best player in the game at that point. He was 24.

That was eleven years ago. The speed grade on the Bureau's Book today is 94. It was 94 when he won the Hart. His shot power reads 91, his decision grade is 90, his acceleration is 89. These are the numbers of a man in his mid-twenties, printed on the career sheet of a man born in Pskov in 1969, and they are the reason he can still separate from a defenseman at the blue line with enough time to read the goaltender's hip and put the puck five-hole before the stick gets down.

What changed is everything else.

The reinvention at the far end

Last season Fedorov carried 76 goals and 55 assists in 82 games. A complete center who could score from the slot and set up a winger from the half wall, the way the Selke Trophy in 1996 was supposed to describe him: the best two-way forward in hockey, 39 goals and 107 points in 78 games while never losing a faceoff or a board battle.

This season: 52 goals and 22 assists in 48 games. His goal rate has climbed from 0.93 a night to 1.08, his assist rate has fallen from 0.67 to 0.46. He is carrying 23.4 minutes a night, up from 21.1 last year. He is playing more and making fewer plays.

The Book's development potential is 61. The Index has him at minus 1 on his base grade, which sits at 94. The current overall reads 86. He is slightly below what the Bureau believes he truly is, but the truth is still a number that puts him above every other player in Anaheim except Jean-Sebastien Giguere93, whose 93 is the only other grade in the room above 90. The next best Anaheim skater is Erik Cole73 at 75.

Nineteen points between Fedorov and the second-best player on the roster.

The room that cannot win a series

Mighty Ducks of Anaheim sits at 19-15-9, 57 points, 151 goals for, 149 goals against. They are alive. Their last ten reads WWLOTLWWWWWL, seven wins and two overtime losses in ten games, a room that has found something. But the franchise has never won a playoff series. The previous playoff run record reads 0. The Stanley Cup has never come to Anaheim.

Fedorov won it twice in Detroit, in 1997 and 1998. He led the Wings in playoff scoring in 1997 with 20 points in 20 games. He scored five goals in one game against Washington on December 26, 1996, all five of them, a 5-4 overtime win. The $38 million offer sheet from Carolina in 1998 was supposed to make him the richest man in hockey. Detroit matched it in February of that year.

Before this chapter came the Cup years, the offer sheet, the five-goal game. This one is quieter. A man on $6.30M with four years left, scoring 52 in 48 for a club that pays $79 a ticket and plays in front of 14,511 people per game. His morale is 97. The Bureau has never listed him injured. No body part, no onset window, no return date. The file is clean.

At 35, the file being clean is the story. The fight grade is 50, the injury resistance is 50, the shot blocking is 52. He has never been the man who stands in the slot and eats a puck off the shin. But his body, which left a country at 20 with a letter hidden inside a media guide, still arrives every night.

What the 94 means

The speed grade of 94 puts him among the fastest skaters in the league. At 35, with a potential grade of 61 (the Bureau's own way of saying there is no room left to climb), he is spending the speed now. Every goal this season is the same sequence: a first step that beats a defenseman to the slot, a release that catches the goaltender's glove moving the wrong way before the stick gets down. In Detroit the game went through his hands. Now it goes through his feet.

The 22 assists tell you where the hockey went. It went into 52 goals.

Nobody in Anaheim built a room around him. His best linemate by the Book is a 75. The team scores 429 goals across all players this season and he has 74 points of them. The club is 19 and 15 and 9 because he is 52, and the gap between what he gives them and what they give him is why they are 57 points on a $41.01M payroll and still alive in January.

He will be 36 in December. The contract runs four more years. The speed grade will move eventually. Not yet.
