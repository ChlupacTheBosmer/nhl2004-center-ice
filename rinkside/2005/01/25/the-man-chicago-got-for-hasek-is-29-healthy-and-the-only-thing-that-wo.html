The Man Chicago Got for Hašek Is 29, Healthy, and the Only Thing That Works

Eric Daze was the 90th pick in a goaltending trade. Eleven years later he's the best skater on a 39-point club, scoring at a 141 pace on $3.00M, and his contract expires in June.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideTuesday, 25 January 2005
ShareCopy link

The 90th pick in the 1993 entry draft came to Chicago inside a package. The Blackhawks sent Dominik Hašek to Buffalo and received Stéphane Beauregard and that selection. The pick became Eric Daze87. Eleven years and one month later, Daze is the best hockey player on the Chicago Blackhawks Blackhawks, and nobody in that building has figured out what to do about him.

He is 29, with 84 points in 49 games (37 goals and 47 assists) at a 141 pace. He is on $3.00M with one year left on his contract. The Book has him at 87, a shade below his base of 89, with nothing on the Development Index in either direction. His morale is 94. He has not been on the injured list.

The Blackhawks have 39 points. That is the lowest number in the league, tied with St. Louis Blues. Their last ten games read WLOTLLLWWLWW, 22 goals for, 22 against. They score almost nothing and give up almost the same amount. Daze is the difference-maker on a club that has no other one. He is the leading scorer, the second-highest overall player on the roster behind Jocelyn Thibault87's 90, and the man whose 37 goals are more than half of what the next two forwards combine for.

The arithmetic of a cheap contract

Chicago's payroll is $39.27M, the fifth-lowest in the league. The pending-free-agent group at the end of this season is four players totaling $13.00M: Daze at $3.00M, Alexei Zhamnov84 at $4.20M, Steve Sullivan84 at $3.00M, and Thibault at $2.80M. That $13.00M is a third of what the club is spending right now, and all of it is walking unless the front office re-signs.

Daze's $3.00M over 84 points works out to $0.04M per point. Zhamnov is at $0.06M per point on $4.20M and 72 points. Sullivan is at $0.05M on $3.00M and 64 points. Daze is the most efficient producer in the room, and by a clear margin. The Blackhawks are paying less per point for their leading scorer than they are for anyone else who scores.

The question is what Daze costs when the market re-prices him. He is 29. He had 153 points last year. He has never won a trophy. The Book says his potential is 78, which means the Bureau does not expect him to climb from here. He is the player he is, entering the season when most clubs ask a winger at his grade to take more than $4M.

A career built in lines

Daze grew up in Laval, Quebec. He went to Beauport in the QMJHL after a brief, unhappy stay with Hull, and in his first full season there he scored 59 goals and 107 points in 66 games. He won the QMJHL's sportsmanlike award and its personality of the year. The Blackhawks took him 90th, in the third round, with the pick that came back from Buffalo in the Hašek deal. He arrived in the NHL in 1994-95 with 2 points in 4 games and spent the rest of the decade being shuffled across lines. Doug Gilmour one year, then off. Martin Rucinsky, then Kyle Calder80. A pulled groin in January 1999 that cost him six games. Nothing dramatic, nothing that made him a headline. He scored 20 or more goals in every season between 1996 and 2003, through line changes and coaching changes, through the slow grind of a club that kept missing the playoffs.

Then 2003-04 happened. 74 games, 70 goals, 83 assists, 153 points. The Hart Trophy conversation that season was Ryan Smyth86 at 163 and Glen Murray80 at 168, and Daze finished behind both of them. He did not get the trophy. He got the $3.00M extension instead, which was fair at the time because everyone figured a 153-point season from a 90th-pick winger was a one-year thing.

It was not a one-year thing.

What the room looks like without him

Chicago has no top-end center. Zhamnov is 34, on $4.20M with one year left, and the Index has him flat. Sullivan is 30, same situation, $3.00M and one year, 64 points. Theoren Fleury86 is at 86 on the Book. Mark Bell84 at 82 is the closest thing to a young core player on a forward line. Behind them, the depth chart is almost blank. Jon Klemm is a 75. Igor Korolev73 is a 72 on $1.60M and has played 26 games.

If the Blackhawks let Daze walk in July, they lose their leading scorer, their best cost producer, and the only player on the roster who can make a goal happen without a system built around him. They keep their $3.00M. They also keep 39 points and start the next season with the same roster and the same problems.

The club has made $26.01M in profit this season on $57.03M in revenue, and their $39.27M payroll is the fifth-lowest in the league. The money is there. What the Blackhawks do not have is a player who makes them spend it in a direction that wins games, and Daze is the closest they have ever come.

The 90th pick turned into that. The goaltender went to Buffalo, kept winning, and never came back. The kid from Laval stayed in Chicago, moved to the second line in 1998, the third line in 2000, the first line last season when it mattered most, and now sits in a dressing room with the second-best overall grade in the building at an age when the league starts counting down. His contract expires in June. He is scoring 84 points on $3.00M. The club is winning a third of its games. Neither of those numbers is going to change by itself.
