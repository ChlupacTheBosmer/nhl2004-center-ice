At 29, We Finally Know Who He Is: Eric Daze and the 12 Years It Took to Reach the Top of the List

He was drafted with a pick Chicago got for Dominik Hašek. He scored 20 goals for eight straight seasons. Last year he scored 70. Now the Bureau says the mountain is behind him, and the contract runs out in June.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideWednesday, 9 March 2005
ShareCopy link

Eric Daze83 has spent 11 seasons being the player Chicago expected him to be. Good, not great. Reliable, never thrilling. In 2003-04 he finally stopped being that man, put up 153 points in 74 games, and the rest of the league noticed. Now his contract costs $3.00M and expires in June, and the Chicago Blackhawks Blackhawks have to decide whether the year that changed everything was the beginning or the peak.

The Bureau's Book gives him an overall of 87 today. His base grade is 89, and the Development Index has him at zero, meaning no upward or downward movement this edition. The potential grade sits at 78. The Bureau is not calling that a ceiling. They are saying there is a small chance he improves. Most likely, 87 is what he is. Most likely, 87 is the most he will ever be again.

He is 29, born in Montreal in the summer of 1975. He played midget in Laval, then went to the Hull Olympiques in the QMJHL, then to Beauport, where he scored 59 goals and 107 points in 66 games in his third junior season and won the CHL's Top Prospect Award. Chicago took him 90th overall in the 1993 draft with a pick that came from the trade that sent Dominik Hasek away. The Blackhawks got Stéphane Beauregard and a fourth-round selection. They used that selection on a kid from Quebec who would spend the next decade scoring 20 or 30 goals a year and being exactly what his draft position suggested he would be.

The Year That Broke the Pattern

In 2003-04, Daze put up 70 goals and 83 assists in 74 games. The 153 points put him third in the league in scoring, behind only Glen Murray86 and Ryan Smyth82. He had never scored more than 34 in a season before. The Development Index did not move on him then; the overall was already high, the potential had not changed. He simply played a season none of us saw coming, and 153 points is a number you cannot explain away.

This season he has 108 points in 68 games. The pace is 130 over 82 games. He has been on the scoresheet at a rate that would have seemed like a career year five seasons ago. The pace is down from the 170 he set last year, which was an outlier so violent that no reasonable person builds a contract around it. But 130 is still the best number on the Blackhawks. No one else on the club is within 15 points of him.

Theoren Fleury82 is out with a knee, back on 2005-03-20. Alexei Zhamnov80 has 94 points and is carrying more center ice than his contract suggests he should. Steve Sullivan80 has 82. Daze is the top scorer, the highest-paid player on the roster at $3.00M, and the only man on this team who has scored 50 goals in a season. Twice, if you count last year.

The Money

Chicago's payroll is $39.27M. The club turns $27.34M in profit, which is the sixth-largest margin in the league. The cost per point is $0.67M. Daze's $3.00M buys 36 points per million this season, measured against 108 points in 68 games. The Blackhawks could replace him at half the salary with a man who puts up 55 points and the cost per point barely moves.

But that is not who they would have. The Chicago Blackhawks Blackhawks play in a building that seats 15,137, charge $77 a ticket, and drew 59 points last season with a first-round playoff exit. They are a $39.27M club pretending to be a $25M club, and Daze is the single asset that makes the pretense visible. Without him they are a payroll. With him they are a team that can put 130 or 140 or, in some improbable year, 170 points on the board.

The contract-year table puts him at morale 91. He is not going to force his way out, and he will not play badly to earn a change of scenery. He will show up, score his goals, and let June decide. That is the man they drafted with a spare fourth-rounder in 1993, and the man they got for Hašek, which is a sentence that still sounds strange 12 years later.

What the Index Does Not Say

The Development Index has Daze at zero. That means the Bureau sees no reason to move him up or down from 89. The overall reads 87 today, two below base. That gap is a rounding error. His speed is 88, his shot power 94, his deking 91. His faceoffs are 51. His fighting is 50. He will not become a two-way center, and the 95 is not coming. What he will do is keep shooting the puck past people with hands fast enough to matter.

The potential of 78 is the number that haunts a June decision. If Chicago extends him at $5.00M or $6.00M, they are paying franchise money against a probability that says 78 is more likely than 90. If they let him walk, they are letting go the best player on the roster for a number that might not be replaceable at any salary this side of $8.00M.

The trade deadline falls on 2005-03-19, 10 days away. After that, Chicago either signs him or loses him. There is no third option that keeps the room in one piece.

Daze will play 68 games this season whether Chicago signs him or not. He will score goals because he is a man who has scored goals every year since he was 20 years old, in Chicago, in front of people who pay $77 to watch. Nobody doubts he will do it. What matters is what it costs to keep him doing it, and whether $3.00M becomes $6.00M or zero by August.
