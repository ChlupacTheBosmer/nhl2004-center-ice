The 124th Pick Dallas Chose Over Belfour: $0.60M, 96 on the Book, and a Consistency Grade of 51

Marty Turco is the cheapest player rated 90 or above in this league. Three years ago, Dallas let Ed Belfour walk to make room for him.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideMonday, 21 March 2005
ShareCopy link

Marty Turco94 costs $0.60M. Ed Belfour93 costs $6.50M. The gap between them on the Book is four points. Dallas chose the gap.

That was after the 2001-02 season. The Dallas Stars Stars had a Hall of Fame goaltender with a Cup ring and a Stanley Cup Final appearance on his resume. They had a 27-year-old fifth-round pick who had spent the better part of three years in Kalamazoo. The Stars made the younger man the starter and let the older one sign in Toronto. Three years in, everyone calls it a gamble.

The trade deadline passed two days ago. Turco did not move. No club traded for him, no club called a package he and Dallas would discuss. He will finish the season in Dallas on a contract that expires in June, which makes him the single most cost-effective player in this league: 96 on the Book, $0.60M, and one year left.

The road through Kalamazoo

Turco was not drafted by the OHL. The kid from Sault Ste. Marie, Ontario, a 71-inch left-catcher at 183 pounds, signed with Cambridge of the Junior B ranks in 1992 because nobody in junior major wanted him. Two seasons later he accepted a scholarship to the University of Michigan. He won two NCAA championships there, was named the CCHA Tournament MVP in 1998, and made the first All-Star team in 1997. Dallas took him 124th overall in the fifth round of 1994, while he was still a sophomore in Ann Arbor.

After four years at Michigan, he went to Kalamazoo in the IHL. He won the league's Rookie of the Year award in 1999. Then Dallas called, and in 2002-03, his first full season as the starter, he set a modern NHL record with a 1.72 goals against average. The Stars lost to Anaheim in the conference semifinals. Nobody cared about the GAA record in April.

The number that holds him back

The Bureau's Development Index has him at +1. His base grade is 94, his current overall is 96. He is exactly as good as his baseline says, maybe a hair above it. The Index does not see him climbing further.

The number that matters for June is his consistency grade: 51. His toughness reads 50. His fighting grade reads 51. The first two tell you what the eye already knows, that a goaltender does not get hit or hold a line in the corner. The consistency number is the one that decides whether a club commits eight years and $7M a season to him. The Bureau has three years of data on every goaltender in this league. Men with a consistency grade at or below 52 do not hold the top of the Book for long. Tomas Vokoun90 has 50. Martin Brodeur91 has 50. They are both good enough right now that it does not matter. It will matter in two or three years.

This season Turco has played 61 games, won 37, lost 22, recorded 1 shutout, and carried a 2.65 goals against average on a save percentage of 0.872. Last year: 70 games, 45-23, no shutouts, 206 goals against. He played 15 playoff games last spring and Dallas lost to Colorado in the first round.

The contract-year class

He sits second in the Bureau's contract-year class by overall grade, behind only Marian Gaborik98 of Toronto Maple Leafs at 97. The first is a 23-year-old right winger on $1.10M with a +6 on the Index. Turco is a 29-year-old goaltender on $0.60M with a +1. Gaborik's potential reads 90. Turco's is 94. Neither has a contract for next season.

Below them in the class, the salaries climb fast. Todd Bertuzzi93 at 95 costs $2.60M. Ed Belfour at 93 costs $6.50M. Martin Brodeur at 93 costs $6.70M. Turco is the cheapest player in the entire league carrying a grade of 90 or above.

The man in Toronto

Belfour is 39. He has played 34 games this season, won 29, lost 5, recorded 6 shutouts, and carries a 1.95 goals against average with a save percentage of 0.930. He is the most efficient goaltender in the league per minute on the ice, and he is on a one-year deal at $6.50M. His morale is 99, the highest number on the contract-year list.

Dallas let him go to Toronto in the summer of 2002. The Stars simply stopped offering and the man left. Now both goaltenders are free agents in June. One costs ten times what the other does. The one who costs less is rated higher. The one who costs more might be better right now, per game, per minute, when his body lets him play. But he can only play 34 games. The younger man has played 61.

One year

Dallas Stars has a payroll of $67.99M, 97 points, and $7.92M in profit. The per-point cost sits at $0.70M. The Stars are not a club that stretches. Their general manager has kept the roster lean and the top of the payroll manageable. Turco at $0.60M is the foundation of that structure. The moment he leaves, or the moment his consistency grade drags the overall below 92, the whole shape of the team changes.

The Bureau's potential grade reads 94. That is not a ceiling. It is a probability, the likelihood that a man reaches the next step. At 29, with 61 games this season and 70 last season and a GAA record set two years ago in his first year as a starter, the probability is no longer a prediction. He has been there since 2002-03.

The Book says he is not consistent. CONS 51 means the room knows he will give them 30 wins some nights and 2 on others. The 2.65 GAA is a career low. The 37 wins are not a career high. He played 61 games; last year it was 70. The shutout is 1, after 0 the year before.

He will finish the season in Dallas. In June, every club in this league will call the same phone number. Whether Turco is worth $0.60M was answered three years ago. What remains is whether a club hands a man with a consistency grade of 51 a contract that runs past his 35th birthday.
