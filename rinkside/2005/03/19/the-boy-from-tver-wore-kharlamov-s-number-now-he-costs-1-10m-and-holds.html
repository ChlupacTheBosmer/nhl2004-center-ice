The Boy From Tver Wore Kharlamov's Number. Now He Costs $1.10M and Holds Atlanta Together.

Ilya Kovalchuk is 21, 124 points, one year from free agency, and the cheapest player in hockey with his production. The club that found him has 131 days to decide what that means.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideSaturday, 19 March 2005
ShareCopy link

Tver is 174 kilometers northwest of Moscow. Ilya Kovalchuk90 was born there on April 15, 1983, in a city where the hockey rink is small and the winters are long enough to practice in until your fingers go numb. He played two seasons in the Vysshaya Liga with Spartak Moscow before he was 18 years old. In 1997, at 14, he played the Quebec International Pee-Wee Tournament. He wore number 17 then, and he wears it now, for Valeri Kharlamov, the Soviet superstar of the 1970s.

A small city, a big name on his back, 174 kilometers from the nearest thing he could call a real league.

On June 24, 2001, Atlanta Thrashers made him the first Russian ever drafted first overall in this league. He was 18. Three months later in his first NHL season, a shoulder ended it. Seventeen games gone, a season cut to pieces. He finished with 29 goals and 51 points in the games he could play, still not 19.

Since then: 38 goals the next year. 72 goals the year after. And this season, in 71 games, 58 goals and 66 assists and 124 points, a pace of 143 over 82, at 20.2 minutes a night, at 21 years old. The Atlanta Thrashers payroll is $36.79M, the second lowest in the league behind Vancouver Canucks. Its cost per point is $0.47M, which puts it fourth in the league's efficiency table. The man carrying the arithmetic costs $1.10M and has one year left on his contract.

Ilya Kovalchuk does not appear on the injured list. He is not traded. The Atlanta Thrashers record shows him in the lineup on what the calendar has as the trade deadline, today, and the hockey-gazette called it four days early: still on Atlanta's roster after the deadline, and that prediction settled as a hit tonight.

The Development Index says he is playing above himself

The Bureau's Book has Kovalchuk at a base grade of 89. The Development Index adds 5 to that today, which puts his overall at 92. He sits among the risers on the Bureau's list, the youngest name in a column that runs through Marian Gaborik96 at 23 and Pierre-Marc Bouchard77 at 20. The Index says his current form runs ahead of what the Book thinks his game truly is: 21 years old, playing better than the Bureau thinks he can.

His strongest attributes on the Book are speed and acceleration, both at 99, with agility and shot power also at 99. He is the fastest player in this league with a puck on his stick. His fighting grade is 50, the lowest on his card. His injury resistance is 53. Those two numbers carry his history: the kid who broke his shoulder at 18 and the man who never learned to take a hit because nobody in Tver or Moscow ever asked him to. He has not been hurt this season. Seventy-one games, 20.2 minutes a night, 58 goals, no trip to the injured list. The 53 sits on his card unanswered.

The linemate who is almost as cheap

Dany Heatley88 is 24. He is on the contract-year list at $1.10M, one year left, 68 games played, 115 points, a pace of 139, an overall of 90. He and Kovalchuk play together. Each costs $1.10M.

The club's third-best player is Byron Dafoe82, a goaltender at 83 overall. After that: Sami Kapanen80 at 82, Patrik Stefan79 at 81. The gap between the top two forwards and the rest of the roster is 8 points on the Book. No other club in this league has a wider gap between its two best players and its third-best.

Atlanta Thrashers has made $24.07M in profit this season on $63.41M in revenue. Its attendance is 14,357 per game at $78 a ticket. It has never made it past one round of the playoffs. This season it has 78 points in 82 games, which puts it in a position neither the front office nor the fans quite recognize: good enough to be in, cheap enough to stay, stuck in the middle with two players who will demand triple their current salary come June.

What 131 days looks like from Tver

Free agency closes on 2005-07-28. That is 131 days. The Atlanta Thrashers front office has until then to decide what Kovalchuk is worth, and what Heatley is worth, and whether a $36.79M payroll can stretch to cover both without breaking the thing that makes it work.

Last season Kovalchuk scored 72 goals in 82 games and shared the Maurice Richard Trophy with Jarome Iginla94 and Rick Nash81. This season he has 58 goals in 71 and is the third-highest goal scorer in the league behind Markus Naslund91 at 66 and himself, by pace. His points are 124. The player ahead of him is Jaromir Jagr90 at 129, who is 33 and in his 15th season. The player behind him is Naslund at 126, who is 31 and in his 12th.

Kovalchuk is 21.

The Bureau's potential grade on him is 86. The Development Index has his form at +5 today. The Book's base says he will not get much better than what he already is. The Index says he is currently playing better than the Book thinks he can. So he is near a ceiling the Bureau believes it has already found, and his legs are carrying him above where the Bureau put his frame. At 21, the years ahead are about holding the line.

In the room at the Atlanta Thrashers rink, the number 17 belongs to a kid who watched Kharlamov on a small television in a cold city. He crossed an ocean at 18, broke his shoulder in his first season, and has spent three years proving that the number on his back means what it says. He has 124 points at 21, and he costs $1.10M, and the calendar says free agency closes in 131 days. The boy from Tver does not owe anyone an answer tonight. But someone in the building does.
