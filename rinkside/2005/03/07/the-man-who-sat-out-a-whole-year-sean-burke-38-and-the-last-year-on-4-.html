The Man Who Sat Out a Whole Year: Sean Burke, 38, and the Last Year on $4.20M

Twenty seasons ago he forced a trade by doing nothing at all. Now the Book has him at 82, his consistency at 50, and his contract at an end.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideMonday, 7 March 2005
ShareCopy link

Sean Burke88 is 38 years old. He was the 24th pick of the 1985 draft. He played 11 games for the New Jersey Devils in 1987-88, and the last of them, an overtime win against Chicago on the season's final night, was the game that sent New Jersey to its first playoff series. Seventeen seasons later, he has played 53 games for Phoenix Coyotes this year, 62.9 minutes a night, 25 wins, 28 losses, 1 shutout. His overall on the Bureau's Book is 82. His consistency grade is 50. He has one year left on a contract that pays him $4.20M, and that year is this one.

The Development Index has not moved on Burke. His line sits flat, the way it does for a career that has reached its level. Phoenix has 69 points in 65 games, an 87-point pace over 82. The club is 15th in the league, one point ahead of Atlanta Thrashers and New York Islanders, and 39 points behind Toronto Maple Leafs. They did not make the playoffs last season, and Burke is in net through all of it.

The year he chose not to play

In 1991-92, Sean Burke did not play professional hockey. He was 24. He wanted out of New Jersey, and the Devils wouldn't deal him, so he sat out the entire season. Not a single game. He sat in Windsor, Ontario, and waited for a club to call.

They traded him to the HAR on August 28, 1992, for Bobby Holik, a second-round pick used on Jay Pandolfo70, and future considerations. Burke was the Whalers' MVP every year from 1993 to 1997. Then he signed in Phoenix, where he has been ever since.

In 2001-02, his fifth season with the Coyotes, he was a finalist for the Vezina Trophy and the third finalist for the Hart. The 2001 and 2002 All-Star games both had his name on a Phoenix jersey. That was three seasons ago. The Book was higher then. It is 82 now, and his potential reads 59.

Seven straight, then overtime

Phoenix won 7 in a row from February 18 to March 2, the longest active streak in the league's bottom half, before a 3-2 overtime loss at Pittsburgh on Saturday. In those 7 games the Coyotes gave up 11 goals. They had been a .500 club doing nothing before the run started.

Burke was in net for all seven. His 2.84 goals-against average and his save percentage of 0.862 are not the numbers that put him in the Vezina conversation. They are the numbers of a man whose three strongest grades (breakaway at 85, screens at 85, rebound control at 83) are separated from his three weakest (consistency at 50, style at 50, toughness at 50) by more than 30 points. The Book says he can make the save in a scramble and cannot be trusted to make it twice in a row.

The biggest salary on the cheapest payroll

Burke's $4.20M is the largest single salary on the Phoenix roster. Shane Doan86, the team's second-highest-paid man, earns $2.40M at an overall of 80. Mike Sillinger88 earns $1.80M at 82. Brian Boucher83 earns $2.20M at 77. Phoenix's payroll is $47.81M against revenue of $56.72M, a profit of $11.28M, and a per-point cost of $0.69M.

A team that runs at $0.69M per point does not hand $4.20M to a 39-year-old with a consistency grade of 50 for a second year. The contract-year class is full of younger men at lower salaries with higher overall grades: Marty Turco94 at $0.60M and 94, Marian Gaborik98 at $1.10M and 96. Burke does not fit the arithmetic of a second contract. He knows that. He sat out a whole year once because he needed a club to listen to him. This time there is nothing to force and nobody to wait for.

He played 5 seasons for Phoenix and was a Vezina finalist and a Hart finalist in the fifth. His grade today is 82. He has played 53 games this season, 62.9 minutes a night, 158 goals against. He has 17 games left to play. The Development Index has him flat. He is 38. He is still here.
