Six Points in Sixteen Days: The Bureau Moved on Jochen Hecht, and Minnesota Has 15 Days to Decide What That Means

From 68 to 74, from 44 goals to 46, and a contract at $1.10M that expires in June on a club with 16 wins.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideFriday, 4 March 2005
ShareCopy link

On February 16 the Bureau's Book had Jochen Hecht72 at 68, the lowest grade of any player in the league's top 30 in scoring. Today the grade reads 74. That is a six-point move in sixteen days. The grade had fallen behind the production, and the Bureau moved it.

The numbers that forced the move: Hecht has 46 goals and 42 assists in 64 games for Minnesota Wild, an 88-point pace of 113, on 22.8 minutes a night. When the last piece ran, he had 44 goals. Two weeks and two goals later, the grade caught up.

The timing makes the 74 matter more than a six-point adjustment usually would. Hecht turns 28 on June 21. His contract pays him $1.10M and has one year left, which means it expires this summer. The trade deadline is March 19, fifteen days from now. Minnesota has 16 wins, 33 losses, and 7 overtime losses in 64 games. They sit 28th in the league.

The Road to 22.8 Minutes

Hecht is from Mannheim, Germany. He played for Adler Mannheim in the DEL and won two league titles there, in 1997 and 1998, before the St. Louis Blues selected him 49th overall in the second round of the 1995 draft. He was 18 years old and playing in a European professional league when a North American team drafted him.

He played two full seasons for St. Louis, putting up 32 goals and 46 assists across that stretch. On July 1, 2001, the Blues dealt him to Edmonton Oilers for Doug Weight and Michel Riesen. One season in Edmonton, then Buffalo took him in a swap for the 31st and 36th picks of the 2002 draft. Three clubs in four years, none of them keeping him long enough to build a home.

Last season he played 72 games and averaged 11.1 minutes a night. Thirty-six points. Twenty-seven goals, nine assists. A fourth-line winger on a rebuilding roster.

This season: 64 games, 22.8 minutes a night, 88 points. The minutes doubled. The points went up 144.4 percent. The grade went up by six and still reads 74, below every other player in the top 30 except Brett Hull81 (77 at Detroit Red Wings) and Geoff Sanderson83 (79 at Columbus Blue Jackets), both of whom are older and cost more.

What Minnesota Knows That the League Does Not

Minnesota Wild runs the second-highest profit in hockey at $37.61M on a payroll of $37.91M. The club spends just under what it takes in, and its per-point cost sits at $0.69M. Hecht alone accounts for a cost per point of $0.013M, which is the third-lowest among the top 30 scorers, behind only Ilya Kovalchuk92 and Mike Comrie79 at $0.010M each.

The club that made the most efficient use of an entry-level salary on a 27-year-old is also the club furthest from the playoffs. Minnesota's 55 points in 64 games put them 28th. Their goal differential is 196 for and 234 against. Hecht's 46 goals account for 23.5 percent of their total scoring output, a figure no player on a team that low in the standings should be shouldering.

The Question Fifteen Days Out

The contract runs out in June. The deadline is March 19. Hecht's morale sits at 87. The sheet does not record whether Minnesota Wild has received any calls or made any, but the shape of the situation is hard to miss: a player with an expiring deal, a 46-goal season, and a team 28th in the standings, fifteen days from the deadline.

At 68, the argument against trading him was that no serious contender would pay a meaningful return for a player the Book considered average. At 74, with 46 goals and a 113 pace on 22.8 minutes, that argument is gone. Whether Minnesota Wild keeps a player whose $1.10M salary is the best value on the roster and whose 46 goals might be the only reason anyone in Minneapolis watches the remaining 18 games is a question for the front office, not the Book.

Hecht's Development Index sits at zero. His base grade on the Book is 79, which means the Bureau's long-term assessment of him is above what he is playing tonight. His potential is 77. He is 27 years old and 16 years out of Mannheim, and the grade is still not done moving.

He has 18 games left.
