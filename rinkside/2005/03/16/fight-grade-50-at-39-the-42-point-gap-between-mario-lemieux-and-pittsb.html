Fight Grade 50 at 39: The 42-Point Gap Between Mario Lemieux and Pittsburgh

He scores 102 points on a $9.90M salary. His next teammate has 60. The Book says the mountain is behind him, and nobody in the room can reach where he stands.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideWednesday, 16 March 2005
ShareCopy link

The piece

Mario Lemieux85 is 39 years old. He has 102 points in 62 games for Pittsburgh Penguins, a pace of 135 points over 82, good enough for 19th in a 30-club league. His overall on the Book reads 85. His Development Index sits one point below his base. His potential, the Bureau's measure of what remains, is 61.

Those four numbers are the whole story. The rest is the room around him, and the room cannot reach him.

The gap nobody on the roster can close

Martin Straka83 [953] is Pittsburgh's second-best forward. He carries an 82 on the Book and has 60 points in 37 games, a pace of 133. Shane Willis follows with 56 points in 67 games. Eric Meloche75 [654], the Calder winner from last spring, has 52 in 53.

Lemieux has 42 more points than Straka, the next man on the roster. That is a man carrying a room on his back while the room walks beside him.

Pittsburgh spends $44.95M and earns $0.64M per standings point. Lemieux's $9.90M, with five years left on the contract, is the single largest line the club carries. He scores 102 points on a team that earns 70 standings points and $16.39M in profit. The man who once told the press Pittsburgh did not want him badly enough is now more than a fifth of their payroll and a fifth of their offense.

The grades that show what the eyes already know

The Book is a photograph of the present tense. It captures what is true today, and what is true today is this: his pass reads 96, his deke reads 95, his puckhandling reads 92, his hockey IQ reads 93. Those are the numbers of a man who sees the play before it arrives and puts the puck where it needs to be.

His fight grade reads 50. His toughness reads 58. His speed from a standing start reads 52. Those are the numbers of a body that is 39 years old and has been 39 years old for some time.

The speed on the skates is 88, which is remarkable at this age but also a full 12 points below his passing. The separation is the arc in miniature. Everything that runs through his hands and eyes is still among the best in this league. Everything that runs through his legs and shoulders has been leaving for three years, and the leaving is nearly done.

Ville-Emard to the first pick to the first pick who would not shake the hand

He was born on 1965-10-05 in Montreal, in Ville-Émard, a working-class district where his father built a rink on the front lawn so the boys could practice after dark. Before that, wooden kitchen spoons and bottle caps in the basement. The youngest of three brothers. A child who declared at 15 that he would break the QMJHL's records and then did.

Lemieux refused to shake Eddie Johnston's hand at the 1984 draft. He did not put on the sweater. He told the gathered press that Pittsburgh did not want him badly enough. At that time, the Penguins drew fewer than 7,000 people to the Civic Arena. They had been bankrupt once already and the rumors of relocation would not stop.

Now 14,008 people fill the building every night and the ticket costs $79. The prestige points read 366. The profit reads $16.39M. The building is full and the man who once would not shake the general manager's hand is still here, still scoring, still $9.90M on a franchise that was once less than half-full in its own arena.

What the Index is saying

The Development Index moved Lemieux one point below his base this edition. That is a small movement, the smallest the Bureau can register, a whisper rather than a shout. At 39, a whisper is the most you can expect. A two- or three-point slide at this stage would be the loud version of the same truth, and the truth is that the overall of 85 will not go back to 92. The base is 92 and the body says otherwise.

Last season he played 82 games and scored 150 points. This season, 62 games and 102 points. The pace has dropped from 150 to 135, the slide a man at 39 gives, and it is still extraordinary.

The room

Pittsburgh Penguins sits at 70 standings points. The last ten games read: OTL, L, W, W, L, W, W, L, W, OTL. It is not good, it is not bad, and it has the most expensive player in its history, who is also the only forward on the roster who reads above 85 on the Book.

The next-best teammate is Johan Hedberg86 [380], the goaltender, at 82. The second-best forward is Straka at 82 with 24 goals in 37 games before injury. After Straka comes Aleksey Morozov76 [682] at 75, then Meloche [654] at 74, then Dick Tarnstrom at 74 on the blue line. The depth chart is a long, slow slope down from the top and the top is 39 years old.

Lemieux has morale at 96. He is not unhappy. He is not leaving. He has five years left on the most expensive contract Pittsburgh has ever carried and a fight grade of 50 and 102 points and the hands of a 28-year-old in the body of a man who used kitchen spoons for hockey sticks in a basement in Ville-Émard.

The book will not move on him this year. The Index says one point below base. The potential says 61. The contract says five more years at $9.90M. The room says 42 points behind.

He scores anyway. He has always scored anyway.
