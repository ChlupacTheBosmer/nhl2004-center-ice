He Is Already What He Will Ever Be: Martin Havlat, Speed 99, and the $0.90M Question

The Bureau projected an 84. He is 87. He costs less than a backup and has 125 days until free agency closes.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideFriday, 25 March 2005
ShareCopy link

Martin Havlat90 is from Mlada Boleslav, a hockey town in the Czech Republic where the Extraliga keeps its season going through the winter. He left it at 18, stayed home one more year, and arrived in Ottawa for his rookie NHL season at 20. The Senators had taken him 26th overall in 1999. They wanted a winger who could skate. They got the fastest man in the league.

His speed grade is 99. There is no higher number in the Book. Agility: 97. Acceleration: 96. Shot power: 93. Deking: 90. The Bureau has never seen a grade it cannot improve on this player. His potential sits at 84. His base grade is 87. He is three points above the number the Bureau originally projected him to reach, and the Bureau says there is nowhere left to go.

He is 23 years old.

The year that broke the projection

Last season Havlat scored 50 points in 48 games. He was injured for roughly half the schedule, and when he was on the ice the Senators went nowhere: 4 games in the postseason, 0 wins, 4 losses, swept in the first round. This year: 37 goals, 58 assists, 95 points in 76 games. He plays 20.6 minutes a night, and his form on the Development Index sits at +3.

The jump from 50 to 95 is not the sort of thing a man does when the Bureau has already written his ending. The potential number is the likelihood of development. At 84, with a base of 87, the likelihood is close to nil. The Index says he is playing above his base by three, which means he is currently the best version of Martin Havlat, and the Bureau expects him to come back down.

He scored 95 points on $0.90M last summer's contract, which has one year left. Ottawa's profit in 2004-05 is $31.51M. They can afford him. The question the room poses is what to pay a man whose number they have already decided he cannot raise.

The room he carries

Havlat plays on the left side of the second line, behind Marian Hossa84 and beside Daniel Alfredsson91. Hossa is out with a back injury that has a firm return date of 2005-03-29. Alfredsson has 101 points in 76 games at $4.70M. The Senators have the 7th-lowest payroll in a 30-club league at $42.80M, but they are paying for a goaltender (Patrick Lalime88, 89 overall, $1.90M) and a defenseman (Zdeno Chara82, 82, $1.70M) and a center who cannot take a faceoff (Todd White82, 83, $0.90M, and a hand that keeps him out until late March).

Faceoff: 50. Fighting: 50. Speed with the puck: 54. The fastest skater in the league cannot win a draw, cannot finish a check, and cannot carry the puck through the neutral space at the pace he skates without it. He is an open-ice winger. The system that uses him has to create the space for him first, and Ottawa has scored 26 goals in their last 10 games while giving up 37.

That is not a complaint about Havlat. It is the room Ottawa has: a man who does one extraordinary thing well and is paid $0.90M to do it, in a club that has lost seven of ten.

What 99 means

There are 30 clubs in this league. I have never seen a 99 on the speed column before, and the Book caps at 99. His acceleration is 96. The first three strides he takes are faster than almost every man who skates against him. His agility is 97, which means he can change direction at that speed. Deking at 90 means he can put the move on the man already beaten by the speed.

Those four grades together make him the most dangerous open-ice forward in this league when the puck is on his stick and there is open ice ahead of him. His 37 goals in 76 games is a finishing number, and the finishing grades (shot power 93) are not the problem. The problem is the faceoff and the neutral zone, and the fact that the team behind him has scored 26 goals in 10 games while giving up 37.

He will turn 24 in April. He is in his fifth NHL season. He played 48 games the year before this one, and before that he played a full schedule in 2002-03 and finished with 24 goals and 59 points. The line across four seasons reads: rookie year, second year, 24 and 59, 50 in 48, and now 95. The arc is not finished even if the Bureau says it is.

The money

Ottawa has $31.51M in profit. Their payroll is $42.80M, which is the 7th-lowest in the league. Their revenue is $78.73M. They sell 14731 a night at $80. They have 458 prestige points and no Stanley Cups. Their previous playoff run was a first-round exit.

The trade deadline was 6 days ago. Hot Stove predicted Havlat would be dealt before it. He is still here. So is Lalime. So is Chara. The front office did not move a single one of the three players the street expected them to package. Either they could not find the return, or they are building around this core for the spring.

Havlat's contract runs out in June. Free agency closes in 125 days. The Bureau has him at 86 today, with form at +3 and a base of 87. If the Index holds, he finishes at 87 on the Book. If the form falls back to base before the deadline for re-signing, he is 87 with 95 points on a $0.90M salary, a man who costs $0.01M per point, the cheapest production in this league at that level.

What the man would say

This is not a player who talks much. His morale reads 87, which is high without being euphoric. He was suspended twice in 2003-04 for a kicking and a high-sticking, four games total. Ken Hitchcock said after one of the incidents that somebody was going to make him eat his lunch. Nobody has. The speed makes it difficult to get a hit on a man who is already gone by the time the shoulder arrives.

He has no trophies. No playoff series win. His best run was the 2003 Eastern Conference Finals, when he was 22 and scored 2 goals in 4 games. He has never carried a club. He has been the second man, the third man, the winger who gets the puck from the center and puts it in the net at a speed the goaltender cannot track.

The Bureau says that is what he is. Speed 99, potential 84, 87 on the base. A man who arrived at his peak before most players in this league have played enough games to find out where the peak is. Ottawa has 125 days until free agency closes to decide what that costs. His price this year was $0.90M. He scored 95 points. His next contract will not cost that.
