What the Book Never Said: Mike Komisarek, 73 Points, and the 24 Games Nobody Remembered

The seventh pick of 2001 played 24 games last season and scored 3 points. This year he is tied for second among all defensemen in a 30-club league, and Montreal has five days to decide what that means.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideMonday, 14 March 2005
ShareCopy link

Last season, Mike Komisarek76 finished with 3 points in 24 games. He was 22. The seventh pick of the 2001 draft had played 11.8 minutes a night for the Montreal Canadiens and gone almost unnoticed.

This March he is 23. He has 73 points in 71 games. He plays 29.9 minutes a night for a club that has won 18 games. The Bureau's Development Index has him up six, the highest mark on the riser list for any defenseman. The Book has him at 74 today. His potential is 90. He costs $1.10M. He has 1 year left.

The Montreal record has no answer to the question his season poses. What do you do with a 23-year-old defenseman putting up 73 points on a 55-point team? The Canadiens sit in 23rd place in a 30-club league with a payroll of $45.26M and a profit of $18.43M. They did not make the playoffs last season and they will not make them this one. The attendance is 15,527 a night at $77 a ticket. This is a building full of people who remember the name on the chest and are wondering when the name on the back will matter again. For now, it's his. He is the best player on this roster.

Third in the league among defensemen, on a team nobody watches

Komisarek's 61 assists are the number that makes his season real. The 12 goals are fine for a defenseman. The 61 are what put him in a three-way tie for second among all defensemen in the league, behind Nicklas Lidstrom at 78 points.

Gonchar is on a team with 89 points. Ozolinsh is on a team with 83. Komisarek is on a team with 55.

His nearest teammate on the blue line, Andrei Markov75, has 55 points in 61 games. That is the guy who is supposed to share the top pair with him. The gap in production between them is what it looks like when a team has one defenseman and needs another.

The Long Island road to a number nobody expected

Komisarek grew up in West Islip, New York, a Long Island town where hockey is a thing you drive somewhere for. He played in the Suffolk PAL organization, then two years of Varsity hockey at St. Anthony's High School. The New England Jr. Coyotes followed under coach Gary Dineen, then USA Hockey's National Team Development Program, where the Lithuanian coach Aleksey Nikiforov taught him the position. The University of Michigan gave him two seasons: 46 points in 80 games, a CCHA title, two trips to the NCAA tournament, a First Team All-American nod in 2002.

Seventh overall in 2001. Signed in July 2002. First NHL game in 2002-03.

Then 24 games last season. 3 points. 6 penalty minutes. 11.8 a night. A defenseman drafted seventh overall who managed that is a depth prospect with upside, and by last season the upside looked theoretical.

What the card says and what the ice says

The strongest grades on his Book card are checking at 84, speed at 81, aggression at 80, puck handling at 80, and shot power at 79. The weakest are fighting at 51, faceoffs at 52, and heroics at 52. He is a defenseman built for the way the modern game works: fast, physical with the puck, absent from the corners where the enforcers live. Thirty-one penalty minutes in 71 games don't make anyone nervous. Speed at 81 does.

The Development Index has him up six. The Book base grade sits at 77, and the current overall reads 74. The Index is bullish; the Book says the climb is early. His potential is 90, so the Bureau still sees a lot of runway.

The room he is holding up

Richard Zednik79 leads this club with 45 goals and 85 points, and he is 77 on the Book. Jose Theodore85 is the highest-rated player on the roster at 83. The Canadiens have 3 men in the trainer's room: Guillaume Lefebvre72 with a foot out until May 15, Mike Ricci75 with a groin back on March 20, and Chad Kilger75 with an elbow due back on March 22. They are not the reason this team has 55 points.

The reason is that the club has no one else. When your 23-year-old defenseman is the best player you have in the possession game, 29.9 minutes become a sentence he serves every night, and the 73 points are the whole case for keeping him in them.

The prestige score sits at 339, middle of the league. The Cups column reads 0. The Bell Centre has been loud for a long time and it has been loud about a name that has not been in the building for a while. Now there is a man in that building who puts up numbers, and the question is what a club does when the numbers arrive before the plan does.

Five days

The trade deadline falls on March 19, five days from today. Komisarek has 1 year left on a contract worth $1.10M. He played 71 games. He scored 73 points. He plays 29.9 minutes a night for a team that has won 18 games.

Those facts will be read differently in five days. If Montreal Canadiens keeps him, the club has a defenseman on a bargain contract who is already better than the team around him. If the club trades him, the return has to account for a 23-year-old with 73 points, a potential of 90, the Index up six, and $1.10M left on a deal that was signed before any of that was true.

He will not make that decision. He will play his next shift and the one after that, 29.9 minutes on a night when the Bell Centre fills and 15,527 people watch a man do what they did not expect from him. The 24 games are the part nobody mentions. The 71 are the part everyone will remember, whichever way this goes.
