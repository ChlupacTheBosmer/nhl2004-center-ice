The Man Who Won't Stop Growing: Igor Radulov, +11 on the Index, and a Room That Cannot Pay Attention

He went from 3 points in 82 games to 12 in 79 to 10 in 16. The Bureau has him up 11. He makes $0.60M. Chicago has lost 10 straight.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideThursday, 17 November 2005
ShareCopy link

Igor Radulov80 skates through a shift at the United Center and the building is half empty. 15,619 seats on a night when the scoreboard reads 2 and 9 and 4, and the losing streak is 10 games old, and the man who is the best story on this roster is playing 20.0 minutes a night in front of a crowd that has learned to leave early.

Radulov has 10 points in 16 games this season. Seven goals, three assists. Last year he had 12 points in 79 games. The year before, 3 in 82. The Bureau's Development Index has him at +11, tied for the fourth-highest riser in this league among players under 23. His overall reads 74 today on a base of 71. He is 23 years old. He makes $0.60M. His contract has 0 years left.

None of that matters to a club that has lost 10 straight and is outscored 50 to 32. The room around him is 2 wins, 9 losses, 4 overtime losses, 10 points. That is what $48.74M buys per point in Chicago: $4.87M. Only Phoenix Coyotes and Chicago Blackhawks are more expensive in this league per point earned.

The shape of the arc

Three seasons at Chicago produced three different players.

The first year produced 3 points in 82 games. The second broke open: 8 goals, 4 assists, 39 penalty minutes in 79 games, a regular shift that stretched to 11.4 minutes a night. The third, the one running now, is something else. The puck has come to him differently. His PUCK grade in the Book is 84, his best attribute by a wide margin, and the Index says he is 11 places above where the base should be. At Nizhny Tagil, at 73 inches and 186 pounds, the hands arrived first and the body filled in later.

He was drafted 74th overall in 2000, third round. A third-round pick from Russia in that era was a gamble on eyes and patience. Chicago held him through 82 games of nothing, 79 games of something, and now 16 games of the best hockey he has played in this league. He has a head injury on record from last March, a week between April 4 and April 10, nothing that lingered. He was already here when the record opened on 2004-12-01. He has been here the whole time.

The teammates around him tell you what this room was supposed to be. Eric Daze87, 81 overall. Theoren Fleury86, 80. Steve Sullivan84, 80. Alexei Zhamnov84, 78. Two goaltenders at 83: Ari Ahonen89 and Jocelyn Thibault87. These are players whose names meant something in this league and who are now wearing a sweater that gets outscored 50 to 32 in 16 games.

What the GM said

Theoren Fleury and Eric Daze and Alexei Zhamnov are not Radulov's problem. The room is. Chicago General Manager Jason Hughes spoke to The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk after the last game, nine losses into a streak that is now 10, and he did not pretend.

"Not good. You know, you can't dress that up. We're getting outscored and we're losing enough to make the point over and over," Hughes said. "The first period especially, at home, 1 and 8 in our own building, and that's... that's a room where something isn't right."

He was asked about the practice track, the building sheet, the equipment. He said: "Practice. And I'll tell you why, because 1 and 8 at home. Those guys are skating on their own ice and we're losing. That's the practice rink. That's the room you prep in, the lines come together. If they're not sharp when they step on it, something's wrong with the room they came from."

The GM is talking about infrastructure. The room came from the practice rink. The practice rink is at 1. The Index says the player skating in it is 11 places better than he was.

The contract that makes the arithmetic ugly

$0.60M. That is what Igor Radulov costs Chicago right now. He is 23, he is +11 on the Index, he has 10 points in 16 games on a roster that has 10 points in 16 games as a team. His potential is 77 in the Book, which means the Bureau does not think he gets much better than what he is today. He is a finished product who happens to be cheap.

His contract expires in June. A player who is +11 on the Index at 23 and on an expiring deal gets expensive the way someone else pays first. Chicago's payroll is $48.74M. Its profit is $26.43M. It makes $43.59M in revenue. The money exists in this building. It is being spent on the wrong things, or on the right things in the wrong order, and Jason Hughes told a man with a microphone that he probably should have done equipment two years ago.

Radulov will sign somewhere next July. Maybe not here. The 7 goals in 16 games will look different in a room that wins. The PUCK grade of 84 does not change with the sweater. The 20.0 minutes a night might not.

What the room is

Chicago's last 10: loss, overtime, loss, loss, loss, loss, loss, overtime, loss, loss, overtime, loss, loss, loss. 32 goals for, 50 against. The goal differential is minus 18. The streak is 10. The attendance is 15,619 and the ticket price is $79 and the prestige is 823, which is more than Minnesota Wild has and more than Calgary Flames has and does not buy enough wins to fill the lower bowl on a Tuesday.

Radulov plays 20.0 minutes a night. His fight grade is 51. His penalty grade is 55. He does not drop hands or take penalties; 2 PIM in 16 games tells you that. He changes a room by putting the puck in a net, which he is doing more often than he ever has, and by doing it at a cost to his employer of $0.60M, which the employer cannot hold.

The Education of Igor Radulov took three seasons. The Bureau's Book says 74. The Index says 85. The team says 2 and 9 and 4. All three numbers are true at the same time.
