46 Wins Last Year, No Trophy. Eleven and Two This Year, and a 1.85. Martin Brodeur at 33 Is Making It Awkward.

He has been good for 12 seasons. Nobody in the voting room has ever called the best goalie. This year the goals-against average says it for him.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideThursday, 17 November 2005
ShareCopy link

Martin Brodeur95 won 46 games last season. He gave up 174 goals in 66 starts, which is a 2.42 average. The Vezina went to Jean-Sebastien Giguere95, who had 38 wins and a 2.44.

Brodeur is 33. Thirteenth professional season, twelfth with New Jersey Devils. A man who has been the best goaltender on the ice more nights than not, and who has watched a trophy go to a man with eight fewer wins. Twice now. The Jennings went to Roberto Luongo95, who shared a crease in Toronto that allowed 190 goals in 82 games.

This year, the numbers have moved into a different conversation. Brodeur is 11 and 2 in 13 starts. His goals-against average is 1.85. His save percentage is 0.910. He has played 62.5 minutes a game. The New Jersey Devils Devils are on a five-game winning streak and sit fourth in the standings at 25 points, with 31 goals against in 23 games played, the fewest of any club in the top seven.

The five-game run tells you what the crease looks like from the outside. From 2005-10-25 through 2005-11-16, the Devils gave up two goals in five of eight games and three in the other three. They lost one. Brodeur was in net for all of them. The league designates injuries by body part and Brodeur has none on record. He has played through everything and the record carries no gaps.

The Book's grades tell a strange story about Brodeur. His toughness is 50. His consistency is 51. His fighting is 51. A goaltender who has never thrown a punch in this league and whose base grades say he should not be the hardest man to backstop a season. But his endurance is 97, his speed 96, his agility 95, his puck-handling 95. The Book reads a man who is never hit, never tested on durability, and never asked to break down, because the position in front of him bends the game around him.

He has been here since 1993-94, when he won the Calder at 21 and led New Jersey Devils to the Eastern Conference finals. He won the Cup the next spring in the lockout-shortened 48-game season. He was 22. He has been a Devil for 12 full seasons since, drafted 20th overall in 1990 out of Saint-Hyacinthe in the QMJHL, where he made the All-Rookie team and the Second All-Star team. His father Denis played for Canada in the 1956 Olympics and spent 20 years photographing Montreal Canadiens games. Martin was a forward at seven. He chose goaltending that year, told a coach he thought it would be fun, and has not left the position since.

At 33, with three years left on a $6.70M contract, the window that remains is narrow. His potential grade is 74, which is the Bureau's way of saying the ceiling is what you see. He will not grow into a different player. The Development Index has him at +1, which means today's overall of 95 sits one above his base. The form is there but it is not a breakout. It is a continuation. A man who has been 95 for two seasons and is proving that 95 can win you things if the room holds.

The room holds right now. Patrik Elias88 and Jamie Langenbrunner88 are both 88. Jeff Friesen88 is 88 on a one-year, $7.08M deal with morale at 99, which means he will leave in July unless someone finds another $7.08M for him. Scott Gomez84 is 84 at center. Brian Rafalski82 anchors the blue line at 82. Behind them all, Brodeur plays 62.5 minutes and allows 1.85 goals a game.

The payroll is $58.50M against $48.79M in revenue, which leaves $31.10M in profit. The Devils spend $2.54M per point, which is the ninth-cheapest rate in this 30-club league. Attendance is 16,838, ticket price $82, prestige 1,084. They have never won a Cup in the record's span. The last playoff run ended in the first round, two wins in six games.

Morale is the number that catches you. Brodeur sits at 84. Most of the league's top players sit above 90. Lecavalier is at 100. Friesen is at 99. Brodeur, the winningest goaltender in this league's current standings, sits at 84 with three years of a contract left and no reason to leave. A man who is not unhappy exactly, but not settled either. A man who has been here for 12 seasons and has watched the hardware go somewhere else.

The Vezina has criteria. The voters want the lowest average and the most wins and the shutouts. Brodeur has zero shutouts this season. He has played 13 games; the league minimum for the trophy is likely closer to 25. If he stays healthy, if the room holds, if the shutouts come in December and January, he finishes with numbers no other goaltender can match. 1.85 over 40 games is not a 2.42 over 66. The sample size is smaller, but the floor is lower.

Giguere is 4 and 9 at 2.28. Turco is 10 and 6 at 1.80, the only man with a lower average and more games played. Luongo has played 8 games and has a 1.73, but he shared a crease with Mikael Tellqvist73 who has six wins in six starts of his own. Brodeur's crease is his alone. Michael Leighton80 is the backup and has appeared in 14 games across all seasons on record with 3 wins.

This is what the grades have said since his base landed at 95. He is the most durable, fastest, most agile goaltender in this league, playing in front of a defense that allows 31 goals, in a building where 16,838 people watch for $82 and the franchise keeps $31.10M a year. No toughness grade is coming tonight or tomorrow, and no fight either. He will not carry a locker room on a speech.

He will play 62 minutes and give up fewer than two goals and the room in front of him will hold and the record will carry another win.

At some point between now and April, somebody has to give him the trophy.
