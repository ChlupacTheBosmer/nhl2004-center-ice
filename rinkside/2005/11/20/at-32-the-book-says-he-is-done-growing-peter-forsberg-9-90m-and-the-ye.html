At 32, the Book Says He Is Done Growing: Peter Forsberg, $9.90M, and the Year the Money Leaves

Three forwards at the top of Colorado's payroll have one year each. The best of them has 24 points in 19 games, a potential grade of 61, and a team that cannot turn it into wins.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideSunday, 20 November 2005
ShareCopy link

Peter Forsberg89 has 24 points in 19 games. Colorado has 20 points in 19 games. The gap between those numbers is the story of the season at Colorado Avalanche.

The payroll at Denver reads $66.24M. At the top of it sit three forwards with one year each: Forsberg at $9.90M, Paul Kariya83 at $10.11M, and Teemu Selanne80 at $6.94M. All three carry morale above 96 and none of them carries a trade request. They are the kind of money a general manager looks at in March and cannot ignore.

The Book has Forsberg at overall 95, with a passing grade of 99, a deking grade of 99, and speed at 95. He is good enough. Whether the club around him can offer enough to earn a new contract from a man who turns 33 in July is another matter.

The pace is there. The wins are not.

Forsberg is playing 23.0 minutes a night, up from 21.4 last season. He has 12 goals and 12 assists in 19 games, a pace of 104 points over 82. Last season he scored 129. The season before, 101. The line goes up, up, and the team's points total goes 112, and now 20 in 19 games.

Colorado scored 310 goals last season. It has scored 47 in 19 this year. The goals-against went from 246 over 82 to 54 over 19. Missing one man does not explain that. The structure around all three of the top forwards is what is gone.

What the Index says, and what potential means

The Development Index has Forsberg at -1. His base grade is 97; today the overall reads 95. That is one notch below where the Book expects him to be, which for a man carrying 23.0 minutes and a pace of 104 is barely a footnote.

The potential grade of 61 is a different sentence. The Bureau uses potential to mean the likelihood of further development rather than a ceiling. At 61, the Book puts only moderate odds on Forsberg getting better from here. He has been 95 or higher in this league for as long as the record reaches. The speed reads 95. The fight grade reads 50. He will never be the man who wins a board in the corner, and the Book stopped pretending he might be several editions ago.

Three years ago the same overall grade carried 129 points. The grade does not change with the team's record. The points do.

The other two names on the payroll

Kariya is 31, carries $10.11M with one year left, and has 7 goals and 17 assists in 19 games. His overall is 89, his pace is 104, and his Index reads +10. He is the best form among the three expiring forwards right now. Kariya came to Colorado on a long deal and has one year left on the biggest number in the room. He was the first overall talent of his draft class and the Book still grades him at 89 with two years of mileage since the trade.

Selanne is 35, $6.94M, one year left, 10 points in 19 games, overall 86, morale 97. The grades are the lowest of the three, the payroll number the smallest, the age the highest. If Denver sheds a contract in March, the sheet says it is this one.

The fourth name with one year left is Milan Hejduk82: 29 years old, $2.50M, overall 88, 23 points in 19 games, pace 99. He is the cheapest production in the room and the easiest decision. If the club wants to keep two of its four expiring forwards, the sheet makes the case for Forsberg and Hejduk. Whether Hejduk will sign for more than $2.50M, and whether Forsberg will sign for less than $9.90M, are questions for another season.

The town that built him

Forsberg was born in Örnsköldsvik, Sweden, ten days after Markus Näslund. They went to the same high school. At 18, they took summer jobs at the same electrical company: Näslund's mother worked there, and so did Forsberg's father, who also coached him from 1991 to 1994 with Modo and on three Swedish national teams. The Philadelphia Flyers drafted him sixth overall in 1991, the first European taken that year. The pick drew criticism from Philadelphia media. The team's general manager said time would prove them right.

It proved them right. Forsberg went to Quebec in the Lindros trade and never played a game in Philadelphia. The deal became the foundation of a franchise that won a Cup and moved to Denver. The man who was drafted sixth by a club that did not want him built the club that did.

Now the money leaves in a year and the room has to earn him back.

The number that matters in June

Colorado has 20 points in 19 games and sits at 6-7-4. The club that won 43 games last season has won 6 this season, one streak of three and one of two. The next break comes when Saku Koivu86's shoulder heals on 2005-11-29 and Todd Marchant84's knee clears on 2006-01-30. Neither is a Colorado injury. The room is whole but not good enough.

Forsberg's Index grade says one point below base. His potential grade says he will not close the gap on his own. The three men at the top of the payroll cannot win 20 games by December without the room changing around them. And the room does not change when your best three forwards make their money and their contracts expire in the same June.

The Book has Forsberg at 95 and the payroll has him at $9.90M. There are 119 days to the trade deadline and 200 to the draft. None of those dates changes the problem: a team with 20 points and 47 goals, a center who turns 33 in July, and a potential grade that says the best is what he is doing right now.
