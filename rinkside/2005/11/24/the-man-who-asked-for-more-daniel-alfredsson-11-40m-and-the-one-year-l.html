The Man Who Asked for More: Daniel Alfredsson, $11.40M, and the One Year Left

In 1997 he demanded a trade over a $1 million contract dispute. Eight years later he carries the most expensive expiring salary in the league, on a club that costs $4.23M per point.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideThursday, 24 November 2005
ShareCopy link

On 24 August 1997, Daniel Alfredsson91 told the Ottawa Senators he wanted out. The five-year contract they offered him was worth $1 million less per year than the one Alexei Yashin83 had received. He was 24, had won the Calder Trophy, and was already the captain. He walked into the office and asked for more, and the club eventually gave it to him.

Eight years later, he makes $11.40M. That is the highest salary on the list of every man with one year left in this league. The next is Paul Kariya89 at $10.11M in Colorado, then Peter Forsberg95 at $9.90M. The man from Gothenburg who was picked 133rd overall in 1994 sits alone at the top of the expiring class, and the room he leads has 14 points and a payroll of $59.27M.

The Bureau's potential grade on him is 64. That is not a ceiling; it is the likelihood of further development, and 64 means the Book does not expect much more growth from a man of 32. His overall reads 87 today. His base grade is 92. He does not appear in the Bureau's Development Index as a riser or a faller, which places him in the flat middle of the league's form spectrum: settled and done.

What the captain carries

Alfredsson has 15 points in 17 games this season, playing 20.7 minutes a night. Last year he put up 108 points in 81 games at 21.5 minutes a night, his best points season in this league. The drop from 108 to a 15-point pace is not a collapse; it is a 17-game sample against a full-year comparison, and his goals-per-game rate (10 in 17) tracks close to last year's pace (56 in 81). But the point total sits below the league's top 30 scorers, and the contract does not move with the small sample. $11.40M is fixed for one more year.

His morale reads 89. That is not low. In a room where Marian Hossa86 sits at 100 morale with a $2.30M contract and Martin Havlat90 sits at 100 with a contract of a fraction of that, the captain's 89 says the room is fine and the contract conversation is not.

The cost problem

Ottawa costs $4.23M per point this season. Only Phoenix Coyotes is worse at $4.26M. The Senators generate $41.44M in revenue and clear $21.54M in profit, which means the money is there; the points are not. The team has won 6 games and lost 9 in regulation with 2 overtime losses over the 17 games on the record, and the last ten read: 4 wins, 5 regulation losses, 1 overtime loss.

What Alfredsson's contract does to that arithmetic is simple. He accounts for $11.40M of a $59.27M payroll. The Senators built cheap around their captain, and the cheapness is why the payroll can absorb $11.40M on one man without going to the top of this league's spending column. Ottawa sits 12th in payroll among the 30 clubs.

The strategy has a ceiling. The room's best two forwards by overall are 87 and 86. No third man is above 82. When Alfredsson's 87 drops or the line around him cools, there is no one behind them to hold the pace. The goals-for over the last ten sits at 26 against 22 allowed, which is not a disaster but is not a contender's math either.

The room around the C

Martin Havlat is the second-best player on the roster at 86 overall. Hossa is 82. Patrick Lalime90 in net carries a 2.48 goals-against average over 13 games. Zdeno Chara82 is a top-pairing defenseman who played 17 games before going down, still earning a fraction of what the top lines make. The structure works when everyone is healthy and producing. It is brittle otherwise.

Wade Redden81 has a sore elbow and returns 2005-12-22. A month without the top defenseman means the captain cannot lean on the blueline to generate offense from the point. Ottawa scored 6 goals against Colorado on 2005-11-06 in a blowout. It scored 2 against MTL the following week and 2 against BUF the week after. The range of outcomes in this room is wide, and the wide end costs games.

The one year

The contract-year class in this league has 30 names on it. Forsberg's $9.90M in Colorado, Kariya's $10.11M in Colorado, Markus Naslund91's $4.40M in Vancouver, Roberto Luongo95's $1.60M in Toronto. The league looks at these men in June and decides what they are worth going forward. Alfredsson's position in that list is unusual: the most expensive and the one nobody is surprised by.

He has been the Senators' captain since 2000-01. He came to Ottawa as a 133rd-round selection from Gothenburg at 22 years old, won the Calder in his first season, made two All-Star Games in his first two years, and demanded a trade in his third year over a dollar figure. The club kept him. The club made him captain. The club paid him. Now the club has 14 points and a $4.23M per-point bill, and the man who once fought for $1 million more makes $11.40M with one year left and a potential grade of 64.

Ottawa has never won a Stanley Cup. Their last playoff run reached one round. They drew 14,283 per game this year at $82 a ticket, a building that leaves seats empty on most nights for a franchise that has the captain's name on the marquee and the most expensive expiring contract in the league sitting on it. The one year is what is left to prove, and the room around him will be cheaper by June if they let it be.
