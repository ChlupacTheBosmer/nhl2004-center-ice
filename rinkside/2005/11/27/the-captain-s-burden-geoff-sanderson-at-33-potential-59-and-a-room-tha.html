The Captain's Burden: Geoff Sanderson at 33, Potential 59, and a Room That Costs $2.48M a Point

He has scored 30-plus goals in five different seasons for three different franchises. At 33, the Book has him finished. The captain of Columbus leads his team with 23 points in 21 games while the payroll around him produces 25 standings points.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideSunday, 27 November 2005
ShareCopy link

Geoff Sanderson86 turns 34 in February. He wears the C on a roster that has 25 standings points and a payroll of $62.03M. The Bureau's Book gives him a potential of 59, which means the grades should not move again. He is leading this team with 23 points in 21 games, a pace of 90, and nobody else on the roster is close.

He was picked 36th overall in 1990 by the Hartford Whalers, a second-round pick of a franchise that did not know it was about to relocate. Sanderson scored 30-plus goals in six of his seven seasons for that organization, through the move from Hartford to Carolina, until the Hurricanes traded him to Vancouver midway through 1997-98. He landed on Buffalo a few weeks later and watched Dallas take the Stanley Cup in Game 6 of the 1999 Final on a goal nobody still agrees about.

In 2000, the Columbus Blue Jackets selected him in the Expansion Draft. An afterthought of a second afterthought: the first Columbus team was not a team anyone wanted to protect players from. They got Sanderson because nobody else wanted to pay him at the top of a contract, and he gave them 30-plus goals twice, in 2000-01 and 2002-03, while the room around him finished last in the Central Division four straight years.

Columbus traded him to Vancouver at the 2004 deadline. He played the final games of his postseason career in a Canucks sweater, scoring once in a first-round loss to Calgary. Then he came back. Not to Vancouver, not to Buffalo, not to Carolina where it started. Back to Columbus. The club that picked him up as spare parts in an expansion draft now hands him the C.

The Arithmetic of a Captain

Sanderson costs $0.28M per point. The Blue Jackets cost $2.48M per point. Those are the same organization. That gap is what it costs to be the best player on a team that spends like a good one.

Pirjeta and Kalinin cost less per point, but they are on cheap deals. Sanderson's number on his salary is what a player like him costs a club that already owes everyone else. The room behind him: Todd Marchant84 out until January 30 with a knee, Joe Juneau73 out until November 28 with a hand. Two centers gone from a lineup that had three before the injuries thinned it.

Brent O'Connor told The Tunnel's Marcus Bell after the game on November 26 that the per-point number bothers him. "The per point is too high," he said. "That tells me I'm not getting value out of the roster I've built or the rooms I'm running." He also said this about Sanderson: "He's scoring. Twenty-three points in 21 games, that's real. He's giving us everything his number says he can give us."

What the Book Sees

SPEED 91. ACCURACY 90. PUCK CONTROL 90. HERO 90. The attributes that made Geoff Sanderson a 30-goal scorer in six different seasons for three different organizations. The grades that hold him up: FIGHT 50, INJURY 50, SPEED-BEHIND-PLAY 52. He won't hit you and won't take one. He won't beat you to the board from behind. He gets open, he shoots it past you, and he does that at 33 years old on a one-year deal because the grades that matter are still 90.

The potential grade of 59 is the Bureau's way of saying: stop checking. His speed won't go up and neither will his strength. The form term is +1, which means the overall of 84 is almost exactly where the Book expects him to be. Morale 87. He likes it there.

His season pace of 90 sits below the 121 he posted last year (23 points in 21 games, extrapolated, against 121 in 82). But the room he was in last year had Todd Marchant healthy for 82 games and a second line that could carry 20 minutes. The room this year is Tyler Wright80 with 10 points in 21 games at 39 pace and Yanic Perreault78 with 9 in 21 at 35 pace. Sanderson is 21 games into a season where he carries a house made of different material than the one he played in before the trade.

The Contract That Ends

$6.34M, one year left. He is not on the contract-year class table because the Bureau lists the top 30 by overall and his 84 falls below the cut, but he is in it. A 33-year-old captain, potential 59, a team with $2.48M per point and no clear path to a playoff spot. What does a club do with that in March, and what does it do in June?

O'Connor's answer, on the record: "I'm not moving a guy producing like that unless I get back something that makes us better in 2006-07 than he makes us right now. That's the test. I haven't got that test met yet."

The test is a trade-deadline test. He will not pass it by scoring more points, because the points are already there. He will pass it if some GM in another front office decides that Geoff Sanderson at 33, $6.34M, one year left, SPEED 91 and potential 59, is worth a pick and a prospect. The clubs that have tried to buy him over the past five years are Carolina, who let him go in 1998; Vancouver, who got him at the 2004 deadline and sent him nowhere; and Buffalo, who gave him up for nothing in an expansion draft nobody expected to matter.

He is in the fifth city of his career, back in the fourth. He was born in Hay River, Northwest Territories. He is 5-foot-7 and 190 pounds and the Book says he is done. The room costs $62.03M to field and produces 25 standings points. He costs $0.28M a point and he is the captain.
