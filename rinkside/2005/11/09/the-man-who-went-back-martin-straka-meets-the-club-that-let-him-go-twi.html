The Man Who Went Back: Martin Straka meets the club that let him go twice

Drafted 19th overall in 1992, traded away in 1995, bought back as a free agent in 1997, shipped to Los Angeles on a cost-cutting move in 2003. At 33, the captain's C is on a roster that costs less per point than any other in this league.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideWednesday, 9 November 2005
ShareCopy link

Martin Straka83 has 21 points in 12 games for Pittsburgh Penguins this season, and the Bureau's Book has his Speed at 93. He is 33 years old, he makes $4.20M, he has one year left on his contract, and the team around him is 10-2. All of those facts belong to the same man, at 33, still here.

Straka was the 19th pick of the 1992 draft, taken by the club that raised him. A productive first full season turned into a broken one in 1994-95, four goals and 16 points in 31 games, and on April 7, 1995, Pittsburgh traded him to Ottawa for Norm MacIver and Troy Murray. He was traded again by January, waived by the spring, claimed by Florida. The Panthers did not re-sign him. He went where a young man goes when no one will pay him yet: back to Pittsburgh, as a free agent, 1997-98.

He stayed eight years. The 2000-01 season was the one people remember, 27 goals and 95 points with Mario Lemieux85 skating beside him. Then came 2001-02, a season-ending broken leg, and the years after it where the word "speed" started to sound like a memory. On November 30, 2003, Pittsburgh dealt him to the Los Angeles Kings for a prospect and a defenceman. The record calls it cost-cutting.

He is back now. Not as a free agent signing this time, because the record does not know when he returned, only that he was here on the day the ledger opened. He is back with the C on his sweater, 19.9 minutes a night, 21 points, 0 penalty minutes. Morale 100. The Development Index reads zero, which is the Bureau's way of saying this is the real thing: no heater, no cold streak, no borrowed time. The base grade is 88. What you see is what the Book says he is.

The cheapest value in this league

Pittsburgh Penguins carries a payroll of $48.92M, which is the fifth-lightest in a 30-club league. Twenty points so far, and $2.45M per point. That is the lowest cost-per-point number on the sheet.

The GM, Eric Lippmann, said it plainly to The Tunnel after the Florida game on November 6: "Martin Straka is 87 in the Book, he's got 21 in 11 games, he's on $4.20M. You find me another 33-year-old doing that for less."

The sheet reads 86 and 12 games, both updated since the interview. The argument does not move: a 33-year-old captain on $4.20M, at a 144-point pace, on a team that turns money into points cheaper than anyone else in this league.

What makes the number strange is what it sits on top of. Straka's Fight grade is 50. His Toughness is 52. He has 0 penalty minutes in 12 games. The 93 in Speed belongs to a man the broken leg was supposed to have taken the word away from, and the Book does not care what people remember. It has watched him skate 19.9 minutes a night and it wrote 93.

The room that learned to win without spending

The Penguins are 10 and 2 over their first 12 games. They gave up 4 goals on opening night to Toronto, then won 10 of 11. The only other loss in that span was 3-2 at Detroit on October 25, one goal in a building on the road. They are 20 points in the standings, which is the fourth-most in the league, behind only New York at 24, Toronto at 22 and Dallas at 22.

Eleven of those twelve games were decided by two goals or fewer. Three of them were 3-2. The room is finding ways to win close ones, and a 33-year-old on a one-year deal at 19.9 minutes a night is finding the second goal when the first one has stopped coming.

Lemieux is 40, and the Index says he is slipping. Johan Hedberg86 is 10 and 2 with a 2.39 GAA at age 28. The back end is young and thin, with Brad Chartrand73 out until November 23 with an ankle and Rico Fata70 out until January with an abdomen. The building is at 1 for equipment, at 1 for practice, at 2 for the workout track. Lippmann said so himself: the coach is fighting the room, not the opponent.

What July costs a man like Straka

One year left at $4.20M. Morale 100. He will be 34 next November. The Development Index has him at zero form, the Book has him at 88 base with potential 76, which the Bureau reads as finished growing. A finished 88 on a one-year deal at $4.20M is the single best contract-to-output ratio on this roster.

The problem is what happens when a man scores at a 144 pace and his own GM says "I'm not going to stand here in November and tell you I'm chasing a name in March." The room knows he is on a one-year deal. The room knows the payroll is $48.92M and the building needs work. The room knows what $2.45M per point means: it means nobody else on this list is a luxury.

Straka left Pittsburgh a second time on a Tuesday in late November, two seasons ago, for a prospect the record cannot track and a defenceman from Plzen. He came back again. He is wearing the C. The hands are 91. The legs are 93. The rest is 50s, and has always been 50s, and none of it matters when the puck is on his blade at 19.9 minutes a night and the team in front of him is 10 and 2.

He was here before the record opened. He was here before the record closed. The question is whether $4.20M is the right price for a man who costs less than the room he plays in.
