18 Goals in 20 Games, $8.37M, and 1 Year Left: David Legwand Forces Nashville's Hand

The second pick of 1998 lost a year to mononucleosis, spent six seasons as good instead of great, and now leads this league in goals on the cheapest payroll per point in the sport.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideSaturday, 26 November 2005
ShareCopy link

David Legwand90 was born in Detroit in 1980 and drove to Grosse Pointe North High School every morning to play hockey on the way to becoming the second player chosen in the 1998 NHL entry draft. Nashville took him. San Jose, whose first-round pick the Predators had traded for the second and the 85th slot, took the 1st. Legwand has lived in the shadow of that trade ever since.

He arrived at training camp that fall, got mononucleosis, and went back to the Plymouth Whalers for another OHL season. He came back in 1999-2000, still 19, and spent the next five years producing 98 points, 116 points, 84 points, and then a 41-game season of 59 points. This year he has 18 goals in 20 games. Nobody in this league has scored more.

David Legwand sits at overall 90, which is his base grade of 88 plus 3 on the Index. His Speed is 99, his Agility 96, his Fighting grade 50. These numbers describe a player the Book has trouble placing: too fast to be a center on a checking line, too soft to be a winger in the corners, too good to hide anywhere else.

The pace that changes the argument

At 31 points in 20 games, Legwand carries a 127 pace. Last season he scored 98 in 82. That 98 made him a very good center; the 127 makes him the best player on his club by a wide margin.

He is not the points leader. Markus Naslund91 of Vancouver Canucks has 32. But Legwand has 18 goals in 20 games against Naslund's 17 in 20, and he is playing 21.5 minutes a night on a team that is 10-4-2 and sits third in the league in points at 30.

The Nashville Predators payroll is $51.38M. The club has 30 points. That is $1.71M per point, the lowest figure of any club in this league. Pittsburgh is next at $1.75M, Dallas at $1.76M. Nashville is producing points at the cheapest rate in the sport, and the man carrying the most expensive salary on the roster, at $8.37M with one year left, is the one producing them at the highest clip.

What the Book says about a 25-year-old past his potential

The Bureau puts Legwand's potential at 85. His overall today is 90. He is already five grades above what the Book thought likely. The Development Index has him at +3 this month, the base grade catching up to the man.

At 25, he is old enough to stop growing and young enough that the contract is the whole story. The league's expensive men with one year left are Daniel Alfredsson91 at $11.40M, Paul Kariya89 at $10.11M, and Peter Forsberg95 at $9.90M. Legwand is fourth on that list. Kariya carries a 113 pace, Forsberg a 113 pace. Legwand's 127 pace is the highest of any of them.

Nashville cannot let him walk. The club's revenue is $38.18M, which generates $17.97M in profit, but the room cannot absorb a replacement for a 127-pace center at any price the market would charge for one.

The room that built him

Nashville Predators's best teammates, by grade, are Tomas Vokoun92 at 92, Martin Erat86 at 86, Scott Walker85 at 85, and Scott Hartnell79 at 84. Vokoun makes $0.90M. The Predators are outscoring opponents 59-50. Their goaltending has been Vokoun's 2.32 goals-against in 14 starts, good enough for 10-4.

The structure works only as long as the 90 plays like a 90. Legwand's Speed of 99 and Fighting grade of 50 mean the game is always faster for him than it is for the man checking him, and he never lets it slow down to make the hit.

From Plymouth to here

The boy who won the Red Tilson Award as the OHL's most outstanding player, who scored 54 goals and 51 assists in his draft year, who became the first NHL player to score on a penalty shot in overtime on December 23, 2000 against the Rangers, has taken seven seasons to arrive at a point where the league's scoring table lists his name in the first row by goals.

He is from Grosse Pointe, played Little Caesars midget, went back to junior when he got sick at 18 and came back at 19 and was told to put on weight.

The contract year will decide what he becomes. At 26, with a potential the Book already says he has exceeded, there is no development left to wait for. There is only the money, the team that found him second in a draft that got the first wrong, and 62 games still to go before June.
