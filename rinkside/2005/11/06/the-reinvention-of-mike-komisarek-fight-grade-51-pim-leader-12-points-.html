The Reinvention of Mike Komisarek: Fight Grade 51, PIM Leader, 12 Points in 11 Games

He was a First Team All-American at Michigan. Now he leads the league in penalty minutes a game, plays 29.5 minutes, and makes $1.10M with no contract for next season.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideSunday, 6 November 2005
ShareCopy link

The League Scouting Bureau gives Mike Komisarek81 a Fight grade of 51. He leads this league in penalty minutes per game at 4.18, ahead of every man who hits harder than he does. His Checking is 87, his Aggression 82, his Speed 93. He plays 29.5 minutes a night at 23 years old for a Montreal club that sits at 13th in the league's standings, and he has 12 points in 11 games on a payroll that spends $54.89M without finding a center who can carry him.

In his first 24 NHL games, spread across his debut and a partial call-up, Komisarek drew 6 penalty minutes. That is 0.25 a night. Last season, in 82 games, he carried 40 PIM, 0.49 per game. This year, 46 minutes in 11 games. Four point one eight a night, more than eight times his pace as a rookie, in a single offseason.

He is from West Islip, New York. Suffolk PAL, then St. Anthony's High School, then the New England Jr. Coyotes under Gary Dineen, then USA Hockey's National Team Development Program. He went to the University of Michigan and was named a First Team Division I All-American in 2002. Two seasons, 46 points, 145 penalty minutes, a CCHA title, two trips to the NCAA Championship. The Montreal Canadiens Canadiens took him seventh overall in 2001.

What the Book says and what the ice says

The Bureau's base grade on Komisarek is 77. His overall today reads 80. The Development Index has him at +10, tied for fourth among risers in the league. Potential 90. Morale 89. Those numbers describe a man the room is building toward something, but they do not explain what changed in the body.

The grades on the sheet: Speed 93, Shot Power 91, Acceleration 88, Checking 87, Agility 84, Aggression 82. Faceoffs 54. Fight 51. Short Pass 52. He is a defenseman who moves like a winger, hits like a winger, and cannot win a draw to save his life. In a league where most top-four defenders are graded above 70 on Faceoffs, Komisarek is 16 points below that line and still playing 29.5 minutes.

He does not fight. The penalty minutes are not fisticuffs. They are the consequence of a man with 87 Checking and 82 Aggression deciding to use both on every play, and the officials answering with two minutes. His Aggression grade is 31 points above his Fight grade.

The money problem

Komisarek makes $1.10M. His contract has 0 years left, which means he is a restricted free agent in July. At his current pace of 89.5 points over 82 games, he is costing his club $12,297 per point. For comparison, the second-cheapest payroll in the league belongs to Vancouver at $46.36M, and they pay Markus Naslund87 $4.40M at a pace of 164 points. That is $26,829 a point for a Hart Trophy winner.

Komisarek is a defenseman on an entry-level number with no year remaining. The Bureau has his potential at 90. He is 23. The Index is at +10 and climbing. None of that is on a sheet a Montreal general manager can ignore in July.

The room around him

Montreal's best player today is Jose Theodore87, the goaltender, at 87. Richard Zednik81 is 80 at right wing. Michael Nylander77 is 76 at center. The top defenseman in the room who is not Komisarek does not appear on the Bureau's list of under-23 risers, the development index, or the scoring leaders. The club sits at 9 points in 14 games and has gone OTL, OTL, W, L, W, L, L, L, L, W over its last ten.

Komisarek is playing the most minutes on the team because there is no one else, and that is why a 23-year-old with 54 Faceoffs is logging 29.5 minutes a night in a league that asks its top defenders to win draws against first lines.

Where the PIM rank sits

Last season's PIM leaders were the men you expect: Darren McCarty77, Ian Laperriere68, Tie Domi70. All forwards. All men whose Fight grades sit in the 80s or 90s. Komisarek finished their season at 0.49 per game, well below every one of them. This year he is at 4.18, ahead of every man on that list's pace, and he does it with a Fight grade of 51. The closest comparison among defenders on the sheet is Steve Staios70 at 2.73 and Ossi Vaananen79 at 1.92, both men with higher Aggression grades and more experience in the corners.

A man graded at 93 Speed and 91 Shot Power decided the shortest distance between the blue line and the scoreboard runs through the penalty box, and his coach is letting him go there 29.5 minutes a night. The Index caught it. The Book has not.

At $1.10M, with 0 years left, and the Development Index at +10, Mike Komisarek is the cheapest top-pairing defenseman in hockey, and Montreal has until July to find out what the market says about that.
