The Room Turned on the Road: Brandon Reid, 30 Points in 23 Games, and the Cheapest Contract in the Building

He weighs 165 pounds, has 99 speed and a fight grade of 50, and the St. Louis Blues have won 8 straight without him leaving their building for a single one of them.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideWednesday, 30 November 2005
ShareCopy link

Brandon Reid73 sits tied for 5th in this league in points. He weighs 165 pounds. He makes $0.60M. The Bureau's Book grades his fight at 50, the floor, and his speed at 99, the ceiling. Both are true at once, and the number that should make a general manager in St. Louis lose sleep is the salary line.

Reid has 30 points in 23 games for St. Louis Blues, a pace of 107 over 82. Seven players on the league's scoring table carry 30 or more points this season. The other six: Markus Naslund87, David Legwand89, Keith Tkachuk84, Richard Zednik83, Jarome Iginla94, Paul Kariya89. None of them weighs 165. None of them makes $0.60M. None of them has a fight grade of 50.

The St. Louis Blues Blues won their eighth consecutive game on 2005-11-27 at Nashville, 3-2. Every one of those eight wins came on the road. They lost 1-5 to the Rangers at home on November 11, lost 2-4 to Minnesota at home two nights later, and then left the building and did not lose again.

Kirkland to 208

Reid was born on March 9, 1981 in Kirkland, Quebec. Six feet tall on the sheet, 165 on the scale. The Bureau drafted him in 2000 with the 208th pick of the seventh round. The grades explain why nobody took him earlier. Acceleration 99. Speed 99. Agility 98. Puck control 89. Accuracy 88. Faceoffs 88. And then the bottom: checking 50, fighting 50, toughness 50. The floor on every attribute that keeps a man safe on the ice.

He has played through that. Twenty-three games this season, two penalty minutes total. His first pro season produced 70 points in 76 games at 19.1 minutes a night. Last year he put up 58 in 73 at 14.3 minutes, which means the coach trusted him four and a half more minutes a night this year and he answered with a higher pace.

The record shows him with Vancouver Canucks before the log opened on 2004-12-01, and with St. Louis arriving on 2005-03-04. He spent the spring and summer of 2005 in a new room and came back with his ice time up and his points-per-game up and his salary still $0.60M. His only time on the injured list this record: an abdomen issue, out from a window between January 19 and January 22, 2005, back on February 9. Three weeks. He played through the rest of that season and started this one with 11 goals in 23 games.

$0.60M Against $1.99M Per Point

The St. Louis Blues payroll sits at $69.76M. The club holds 35 standings points. That makes the cost per point $1.99M, the 7th-cheapest ratio in this 30-club league. Reid contributes 30 of those points on a $0.60M contract that has zero years left. The Bureau's cost per point for Reid is $0.02M. The rest of the roster is not close.

Keith Tkachuk has 31 points at $9.00M, a cost of $0.29M per point. Chris Pronger80 has 17 at $9.50M, costing $0.56M per point. Doug Weight79 has 15 at $8.10M, costing $0.54M per point. Pavol Demitra77 has 22 at $5.46M, costing $0.25M per point. The roster carries $69.76M in total salary and produces 35 points. Reid's $0.60M produces 30.

Tkachuk is 33 and the Book says 90. Pronger is the Norris winner from last season. Weight and Demitra carry the middle. None of that is in dispute. What the table shows is a 24-year-old on an expiring entry-level deal producing at a rate no one else on this roster approaches per dollar spent.

The Book Has Him Up Four

The Bureau's Development Index carries Reid at +4. His base grade is 77; his overall reads 79. The potential grade sits at 94. He is 24. The Index measures how far today's overall sits above the base, and four points on a 77-base kid from the seventh round means the Bureau sees something it did not see at the time of the Book.

Morale 100, which tracks with what happened after the March trade from Vancouver: a new city, new linemates, a room with Tkachuk and Pronger and Weight, and 11 goals in 23 games on the second-least money on the roster. The man is 165 pounds, was drafted in the seventh round, and does not care that his linemate weighs 210.

The eight wins tell the same story. The St. Louis Blues Blues scored 32 goals in those 8 road games and allowed 15. They beat Nashville 3-2, San Jose 3-2, Pittsburgh 3-1, Philadelphia 5-2, Washington 4-3, Florida 3-1, Tampa Bay 4-2, Atlanta 4-3. Six of the eight were one- or two-goal margins. They won in buildings where the home team had the crowd and the travel and the rest, and they won with the cheapest forward line in hockey feeding off a $0.60M contract that expires in June.

What June Costs

Zero years left on the deal. The St. Louis Blues front office holds nothing on him after the spring. A 24-year-old with 107-point pace, speed 99, potential 94, and a morale grade of 100 does not stay at $0.60M once the calendar turns to summer. The league's cheapest rooms are Dallas at $1.61M per point and Pittsburgh at $1.63M. St. Louis at $1.99M is not far from that floor. Losing Reid means rebuilding that floor from a new salary that will not be $0.60M.

The room turned on the road. Eight straight wins in eight different buildings. The man carrying the most dangerous line in the league weighs 165 pounds, has a fight grade of 50, and will be a free agent before the leaves turn next October. The Blues have 35 standings points and $1.99M per point. The cheapest contract in the building produces 30 of them.
