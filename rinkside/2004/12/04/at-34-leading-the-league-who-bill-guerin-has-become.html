At 34, leading the league: who Bill Guerin has become

Fifty-eight points in twenty-seven games, a 176 pace, and a Dallas club built around the oldest men in the room.

Carol Bingham
Features writer · RinksideSaturday, 4 December 2004

The top line of the scoring table usually belongs to a young man. The sheet knows this. Look at the names under the first line and you get a portrait of a league in the middle of a passing: Ilya Kovalchuk90 is twenty-one, Dany Heatley88 is twenty-three, Brad Richards85 and Mike Comrie78 and Vincent Lecavalier84 and Scott Gomez83 are all twenty-four. The future is bright and it wears a youth jersey.

But the first line belongs to Bill Guerin89, who is thirty-four years old, who plays right wing for Dallas Stars, and who has fifty-eight points in twenty-seven games. One hundred and seventy-six pace. Thirty-two goals. Twenty-six assists. Twenty minutes and four seconds of ice a night, and an overall grade of 89, which the Bureau gives a man when it believes what it is watching is not a flash but a truth.

The thing that makes a writer sit down and try to write about this is not the pace, though the pace is remarkable. It is not the goals, though thirty-two at his age is a sentence worth finishing. It is the table. The whole table. Because Guerin is not an exception — he is the front of a procession.

Eight of the top eight. Not a single man under thirty-one. This is not one aging star having a late blooming; this is a league's forward groups, the ones that have been doing this for a decade and a half, looking up and deciding they are not finished. The Bureau's Book gives Guerin an 89. It gives Modano, also thirty-four, a 90. It gives Jagr an 88 and Forsberg an 89. These are not consolation grades. These are the grades of men who are better right now than they were two years ago.

Dallas is where two of them landed. Mike Modano90 has forty-eight points in twenty-three games, sixteen goals and thirty-two assists, twenty-one minutes a night, and a 90 overall that is the highest grade on the Dallas Stars roster. Behind them in the crease stands Marty Turco96, twenty-nine, twelve wins and eight losses, one shutout, a goals-against average of 3.28, and an overall grade of 96 — the highest number on the entire sheet, tied with Marian Gaborik96 in Toronto. Turco is in the last year of a contract that pays him six hundred thousand dollars. Read that again. A 96-grade goaltender making pocket change, guarding a net for a team that sits third in the standings with thirty-five points.

The Dallas room is full of this contradiction. The oldest men are carrying the scoring, the youngest are carrying the goaltending budget, and in between stands Brenden Morrow86, twenty-five, thirty-five points, an 86 overall, also in the last year of his contract at $1.30 million. The architecture is clear: two thirty-four-year-olds up top who refuse to be old, a goaltender making a fraction of what his grade should command, a left wing turning into a franchise player at an entry-level number. Dallas Stars has thirty-five points, seventh in payroll at $66.88 million, and a per-point cost of $1.91 million that is almost embarrassing for a club with this much production. The last ten games tell the rest: seven wins, two losses, one overtime loss, forty-two goals for, thirty-five against. A room that turned, and stayed turned.

What makes me keep coming back to Guerin, though, is the shape of the thing. A career is a line you draw on a page, and most of them bend downward after thirty. They bend toward the penalty box, toward the fourth line, toward the minutes that drop from twenty to fifteen to twelve and then to a conversation in a hotel lobby about a tryout. Guerin's line bends up at thirty-four. Thirty-two goals. Twenty-six assists. A pace of one hundred and seventy-six. The Bureau looks at him and writes 89, which is a grade that means we were wrong to count you out and we are writing it down so nobody forgets.

There is one other thirty-four-year-old on the scoring table worth naming. Alexei Zhamnov83 has forty-four points in twenty-four games for Chicago Blackhawks, a 150 pace, an 83 overall. And Teemu Selanne81, also thirty-four, has forty-four in twenty-five for Colorado Avalanche, a 144 pace, an 81. Both are good years. Neither is Guerin's year. The gap between the best thirty-four-year-old and the second-best is fourteen points in three fewer games, and it is wide enough to make you ask whether what you are watching is a trend or a man who has simply decided, at an age when most have made peace with the slide, that he will not go quietly.

The sheet will answer that question in its own time, the way it answers all of them — slowly, in columns, with a final tally that either vindicates the season or files it as the last bright one. But for now, in early December, the first line of the scoring table says Bill Guerin, thirty-four, right wing, Dallas. And the second line says Jaromir Jagr88, thirty-two. And the third says Peter Forsberg89, thirty-one. And the room is quiet for a moment, and then it is not, because the old men are still here, and they are still the best thing in the building.
