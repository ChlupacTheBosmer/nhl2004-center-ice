The Jordan Promise, Six Years Late: Vincent Lecavalier at 24

Drafted first overall at 18, called the Michael Jordan of hockey, stripped of a captaincy at 21. Now the pace is 153 and the room is finally built around him.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideWednesday, 29 December 2004
ShareCopy link

Vincent Lecavalier90 is 24 and playing at a 153 pace. Sixty-seven points in 36 games, 26 goals, 41 assists, 23.0 minutes a night. His Book grade sits at 90, one above his base on the Development Index, and his morale reads 100. Six years of waiting is the story underneath all of that.

The prophecy and the patience

In 1998, a new owner in Tampa Bay Lightning stood at a podium and told the assembled media that his first-overall pick, a center from Ile-Bizard, Quebec, would be the Michael Jordan of hockey. Lecavalier was 18 years old. He had just finished a rookie season at Rimouski where he won the QMJHL's top rookie forward and top rookie overall trophies. The words landed on a teenager and stayed there for six years.

He played 68 games in 2000-01 and finished with 51 points. He broke a foot blocking a slap shot in January of that season. He was given the captaincy. Lightning management stripped it before 2001-02, deciding he was too young for the letter even as a high-calibre player.

Last season was the turn. 82 games, 63 goals, 75 assists, 138 points. He was 23 and the number was the number they had waited for. Then he went to the World Cup, scored for Canada, and came home named the tournament's MVP.

A 153 pace through 36 games this season. The Book's per-attribute grades tell you what a scout would tell you in the room: HERO at 98, SPEE at 97, SHPW at 96, ACCE at 95, AGIL at 94. He sees the ice before the play arrives and he goes there faster than the body that was supposed to get him there at 18. His fight grade is 51. His speed on the backcheck is 52. Those are the margins between the player they drafted and the player he has become, and he has decided which ones matter.

The room that turned

Tampa Bay Lightning is 18 wins, 11 losses, 3 overtimes, 47 points through 36 games. Two wins in a row heading into late December. The goal differential is +10, 136 for and 126 against. The payroll is $45.79M, the cheapest per point in the league at $0.97M alongside Nashville Predators. Revenue is $52.24M and profit is $24.62M. Tampa Bay is building something on a budget that most clubs would not recognize as a budget.

Around Lecavalier, the room has found its shape. Martin St. Louis87 is 33, undrafted, and carries 57 points in 36 games at a 130 pace with 87 overall in the Book. Cory Stillman84, the 6th pick of the 1992 draft, has 16 goals and 41 assists, 57 points, the same pace. Nikolai Khabibulin92, the 204th pick of 1992, is 18-12 with a 3.22 GAA and a 0.851 save percentage. Three players who were not supposed to be enough, all of them now the men beside the man who was.

Lecavalier was drafted first overall and took six years. St. Louis went undrafted and is 33 years old, still finding new ways to score at a 130 pace. Stillman was a top-10 pick and is now the third-best scorer on his own team. Khabibulin was a seventh-rounder and leads the Lightning in wins. None of them arrived on schedule except the one who was supposed to be Michael Jordan.

Two years left and a number that will not stay small

The contract reads $2.60M with two years left. That is 5.7 percent of Tampa Bay's payroll for the league's ninth-best scorer at a 153 pace, the highest-graded Lightning player, the center around whom the structure is built. When the deal expires, he will be 26 and the market will do what markets do to men who score 153 per 82 and grade 90.

He is 24. The Michael Jordan comparison was made when he was 18 and it stayed too long in the building. Vincent Lecavalier stopped being a prophecy sometime around the 60th game of last season, when the 138 points finished and the room had been waiting for him all along.
