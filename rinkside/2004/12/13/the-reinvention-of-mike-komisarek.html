The Reinvention of Mike Komisarek

A year after scoring 3 points in 24 games, the 22-year-old has 31 assists in 29 and plays 29.4 minutes for a team that needs him everywhere.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideMonday, 13 December 2004
ShareCopy link

24 games and 3 points, 1 goal and 2 assists, at 11.8 minutes a night. That was Mike Komisarek74 a year ago, the fourth-pairing hitter on a Montreal Canadiens team that asked him only to be hard. This season he has 4 goals and 31 assists in 29 games, 35 points, and he plays 29.4 minutes, nearly every minute that matters.

Thirty-one assists. A year ago the league's highest-scoring defenseman finished the season with 82 points over a full 82 games. Mike Komisarek has 35 in 29, a rate that would reach roughly 80 over a full schedule, and the shock of it is that the scouting report on him came off the left side of the sheet: written in front of the net, in the corner, anywhere a body was needed.

This is the season's least noticed breakout. The Development Index has him up six, and the Book now reads 76, a point above Andrei Markov73, the next-highest-rated defender in the Montreal room. All of it is happening on a team going nowhere. Montreal is 9-17-1 for 23 points, with 100 goals for and 125 against, and the best story in the dressing room is a 22-year-old in the last year of a $1.10M deal who has become the player they cannot take off the ice.

He was drafted 7th overall in 2001 to be exactly what he was supposed to be. Two years at Michigan left him with 46 points and 145 penalty minutes in 80 games, a First Team All-American by 2002, a CCHA title and two trips to the NCAA tournament. The Long Island road ran through Suffolk PAL, St. Anthony's High School, Gary Dineen's New England Jr. Coyotes and the national development program. Every stop confirmed the same thing: he could hit, he could clear the front, and the puck could live somewhere else.

The NHL agreed for a long time. In the 2003-04 season Montreal used him 24 times, gave him 11.8 minutes, and watched him earn 3 points. There was no shame in the year; he was 22 and physical, and teams give that age to the crease. But nobody handed him the puck to move, because nobody had ever seen him move it.

Ask him about the wait and he describes it plainly. "You sit down there long enough and you learn the game from the wrong side of the boards. You watch what the puck does before it comes to you. Then one day the minutes come and nothing in the game surprises you."

This season everything changed at once. The team slipped, the depth went thin, and the kid drafted to play 11.8 minutes started playing 29.4. The points came in a rush, 31 of them as assists, in territory the Book's defenseman grades did not anticipate.

There is a small private symmetry to the route. As a boy he played the 1996 Quebec International Pee-Wee Tournament with the New York Islanders' minor team, a Long Islander getting his first look at the province that would draft him 7th overall. He grew into an American defenseman in Montreal, the city that talks about its blue line the way other cities talk about the weather, and he plays the game with the hitting he brought from home and the passing he discovered late.

The hitter kept his hands

The odd thing is that he is still the hitter. Mike Komisarek has 71 penalty minutes this season, and his strongest grades in the Book are the ones nobody drafts a playmaker for: CHKG 86, AGGR 82. He clears the front the way he always did. But the Book also reads PASS 79 and PUCK 82, the two numbers that explain the assists, and those are the grades Montreal's scouts never had a reason to look for.

His description of the two jobs is just as plain. "The hitting was always the job. The puck is the part nobody handed me, so I never let it go when it came."

The minutes explain part of the leap. The Index explains the rest: +6 on form, a base of 77, potential of 90 in the Book, and a grade climbing as the calendar turns.

The one good story in a lost room

Montreal's season is 9-17-1, and the dressing room has spent December answering for the other part of the record. When a team slips, the minutes go to whoever can carry them, and the 22-year-old from West Islip turned out to be that player. He is paid $1.10M this season, the final year of an entry-level deal, playing 29.4 minutes a night. A club searching for a way back has found its blue line near the bottom of the standings, in the draft pick it already held.

The Book moves slower than the game, and here it is catching up. Mike Komisarek is 22, he plays 29.4 minutes, and the club that drafted him 7th overall to do a small, hard job is watching him do a much larger one.
