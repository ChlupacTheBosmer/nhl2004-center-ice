The Contract Journey: Martin Havlat, $0.90M, and the speed that grades 99

A 26th-overall pick from Mlada Boleslav is playing at a 121.7 pace with one year left on a deal that would barely cover a second-line center's parking.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideWednesday, 22 December 2004
ShareCopy link

The Book grades Martin Havlat84's speed at 99. No other skater in the league carries a higher number in that column. The man who carries it earns $0.90M.

That gap is the story in Ottawa Senators this December. Havlat, 23, is in his fifth NHL season, all of them with Ottawa, and he is playing the best hockey of his life on a contract that expires in a year. Forty-six points in 31 games is a pace of 121.7. Last year he finished with 50 points in 48 games, a pace of 85.4. The Development Index has him at +3 form, reading 86 overall. His morale sits at 92.

He was 26th overall in 1999, taken from the Czech Extraliga by a Senators team that had been in the league for six years and had never finished above .500. Havlat stayed another season in Mlada Boleslav, then crossed the ocean for his rookie year in 2000-01. Four years later he is still in Ottawa, still at $0.90M, and still the fastest man on the roster by any measure the Bureau publishes.

The minutes are the same. The production has leapt. His assists have climbed more than his goals, 29 against 17, which is what happens when the speed that grades 99 starts finding men instead of just finding ice.

His shot power sits at 93, his agility at 97, his puck control at 89, his dangle at 90. The numbers that drag him down are faceoffs at 50 and fighting at 50, neither of which matters much for a winger who plays 21 minutes and does not see the dot on most shifts. His speed 99 and his acceleration 96 together describe a player who arrives at the blue line before the defense can set. One second of open ice is all he needs, and the Book says nobody is faster than he is in getting to it.

What happened last year

The 2003-04 season gave Ottawa 50 points from Havlat, which was good but not this. He played 48 games and was suspended twice, once for kicking and once for high-sticking, four games total. Ken Hitchcock, coaching the Flyers at the time, said after a high-sticking incident that "somebody is going to make him eat his lunch." The words made the wire in February. They followed him into the summer.

He was 22, with 24 goals the year before, his second consecutive 20-goal season, and a run to the Eastern Conference Finals in 2003 behind him. The talent was visible. The consistency was not. The suspensions made it look like he had chosen the wrong kind of attention, and the $0.90M stayed where it was.

What is happening now

This year the 46 points come in 31 games with the same legs, the same hands, and a center in Daniel Alfredsson86 whose overall of 88 means he is finding the seams before they close. Marian Hossa83 at 85 on the right side adds another set of hands. Todd White81 at 83 gives the third line an anchor. Patrick Lalime88 at 90 in net means the games stay close, which means the space opens in the third period, which means a man with speed 99 gets a lane.

The room knows what he is. His morale of 92 is high enough that he is not looking sideways or asking for a trade. He is 23 years old, playing 21 minutes a night, and producing at a pace that would put him among the league's top scorers if he held it through 82.

That pace is 121.7. The top scorer in the league right now is Bill Guerin83 at 178. The gap between Havlat and the top of the table is roughly the gap between a very good season and a Hart. He is 51 games from a full schedule. Nobody knows what 51 more games will look like. The last time a 23-year-old Czech winger jumped from an 85.4 pace to a 121.7 pace in his fifth NHL season, the leap did not hold for 82. It held for 48.

But the speed does not leave. The Book has his potential at 86, level with his current overall. The Development Index reads +3, meaning the form term is sitting above his base grade. He has already shown Ottawa what he can be at 86. The next jump, if there is one, comes from the 51 games between December and April that no one can predict.

What it costs

The contract year is the one that matters. $0.90M with one year left means that on July 1, 2005, Martin Havlat is a free agent unless Ottawa moves. The best players in the contract-year class who earn less than $1M and produce more than 40 points are rare. Havlat is the only one in the top 30 by overall who earns under $1M. The next-cheapest man in that class is Jason Bacashihua81 at $0.60M, a backup goalie who has played 1 game.

The market will find him. A 23-year-old winger with a 99 speed grade, 46 points in 31 games, and no character questions since the suspensions two years ago is not going to sign at $0.90M. If he holds this pace through March, Ottawa will face a decision in the summer that goes like this: match what another club offers, or watch the fastest skater in the league leave for someone else's building. His hometown is Mlada Boleslav. He crossed the ocean at 19 to play in Ottawa. He has never asked for anything, not publicly, not once in five years. The asking price this summer will not be one he sets.

The speed is 99. The salary is $0.90M.
