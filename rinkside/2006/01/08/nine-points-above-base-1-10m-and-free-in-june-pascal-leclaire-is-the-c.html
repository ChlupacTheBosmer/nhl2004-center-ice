Nine Points Above Base, $1.10M, and Free in June: Pascal Leclaire Is the Contract Columbus Cannot Let Go

The Book had him at 83. The Index put him at 93. The Blue Jackets have about five months to figure out what that gap costs.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideSunday, 8 January 2006
ShareCopy link

Pascal Leclaire93 is 23 years old with a 1.89 goals against average through 30 games. The League Scouting Bureau's Development Index puts him nine points above the Book's base grade, so the man playing in front of Columbus's blueliners right now is a better goaltender than the one the Book ever wrote down. He earns $1.10M. His contract ends in June.

The Blue Jackets are 51 points in 40 games. They are on a 2-game win streak and have won 7 of their last 10, allowing 87 goals in 40 games for 2.175 a night, third-best in the league. They have done this without Todd Marchant84, out with a knee since November and not expected back until 2006-01-30. They have done this without Espen Knutsen82 and Tyler Wright80, both hurt between January 4 and January 5, both out past February 19. The goaltender is the best player on the ice every night and he is cheaper than most of them.

The Book gives him an overall of 93 today, tied with Martin Brodeur93. His base grade is 83. The difference is the Index, which reads nine. His potential is 94, which the Bureau defines as a likelihood of further development, and that number sits one point above where he already is. The Book expects more.

What the grades say and what the numbers do

Leclaire's attribute grades read like a mistake. Agility: 99. Glove hand: 99. Speed: 99. Reactions: 97. Slide: 97. Five-hole: 94. Then the bottom: fighting: 51. Paddle: 51. Positioning: 51. He is a cat in a phone booth and he makes every other save in hockey look like a reflex test. A coach who needs him to hold his blue paint and let the puck hit his chest protector will learn what those 51s mean.

He does not need them. Columbus allows the fewest goals in the league per game among the top three, and he does it on a save percentage of 0.904, third among goaltenders with 5 or more starts. The Blue Jackets allow 2.175 goals a night. Brodeur's Devils allow 2.184. The gap between New Jersey's defense and Columbus's is about a third of a goal a game. The gap between their goaltenders, by the Book, is zero.

Last season Leclaire played 49 games and went 23-25-1. The year before that, 24 games and 11-10-1. This season through 30 starts he is 19-11-1 with a 1.89 GAA and a 0.904 save percentage. It is the jump the Index has been measuring all season, nine points up, and it is happening in a sweater that will not exist for him in October unless someone in the Columbus Blue Jackets front office sits down and decides what he is worth.

The contract and the room

Leclaire's deal is $1.10M with zero years left. In a league where Roberto Luongo95 at 95 and $1.60M has one year remaining, and Marty Turco95 at 95 is the subject of its own contract story in Dallas, a 93-goaltender at $1.10M on an expiring deal is the cheapest top-tier asset the league carries.

The Blue Jackets' payroll is $62.03M. They have 51 points. That is $1.22M per point, which sits near the efficient end of the table, helped by exactly this kind of contract. Geoff Sanderson86, 33 years old, 51 points this season at a 105-point pace, also has one year left at $6.34M. He and Leclaire are the two most important players on this roster and both contracts end within the year. Sanderson has the Hart Trophy's cousin, a 95 morale rating, and a 22.3 minutes-a-night workload. Leclaire has a 95 morale rating and a practice rink nobody mentions because nobody complains about it.

But they don't have a top center. Marchant has been out since November with a knee, and his 84 overall is the highest among the three injured men. Knutsen and Wright both landed in the last week, ribs and abdomen. Columbus has 4 players on the injured list, tied for fifth-most in the league. They are playing a 13-game homestand stretch of their schedule with depth lines making 20-minute contributions from people the Book rates at 72 and 73.

What June asks

The Bureau's Book had Leclaire at 83. A player at 83 earns maybe $2.50M, maybe $3.00M. A player at 93 earns $5.00M or more, and the comparables above him in the goaltending table are Marty Turco and Roberto Luongo, both rated 95, both on larger numbers. Columbus built a $62.03M payroll where the goaltender is the cheapest elite asset and the team allows 87 goals. If Leclaire is gone, the next version of this payroll needs to spend $5.00M to get the same save.

He was the first goaltender selected in the 2001 draft, 8th overall. He came from the QMJHL, from Halifax and then the Montreal Rocket, from Repentigny, Quebec, where he played pee-wee hockey at 13. He has been a Columbus Blue Jacket since before the record opened in December 2004, with no trophies and no injuries on record. His game is a 99-grade glove hand, a 51-grade paddle, and a contract that expires in a little under five months.

The Index will keep moving. The Book will eventually revise the base up, the way it revised for Vaclav Nedorost84 from 75 to 84, the way it did for Mike Komisarek81. But the revision takes an edition, and the free-agent market does not wait for editions. Columbus has about 70 days before what the calendar has as the trade deadline, and about 151 before the draft, to either extend the deal or watch the best goaltender on the roster walk into a summer where his 1.89 is a price tag nobody wants to pay for a 23-year-old with 51 positioning.
