Potential 66, Overall 93, and Free in June: Daniel Alfredsson Has Been 27 Points Past the Book's Number for a Decade

The 133rd pick in 1994 demanded a trade because a teammate made more. Eleven million a year later, the projection that was supposed to cap him reads 66.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideSunday, 15 January 2006
ShareCopy link

Daniel Alfredsson93 was the 133rd player taken in the 1994 draft, a fifth-round pick out of Gothenburg whom the Ottawa Senators selected in the middle of an evening. The League Scouting Bureau's Book gave him a potential of 66. He is 33 years old, he carries the captain's C on a sweater in Ottawa, his overall reads 93, and he will be a free agent in June.

The gap between 66 and 93 is 27 points, the distance between a bottom-line regular and a top-20 player in the league. The Bureau has been wrong about it since Alfredsson was old enough to shave.

This season he has 45 points in 44 games, an 83.9 pace, on $11.40M with one year left on his contract. His morale sits at 94. His grades read 99 on deking, 99 on passing, 97 on hero. The man who was supposed to be a 66 is one of the two best skaters on the Ottawa Senators roster, and the only one ahead of him, Martin Havlat92, arrived years later with a first-round pick's draft pedigree and an overall of 92.

The trade demand that started the money

On 24 August 1997, Alfredsson demanded a trade from Ottawa. The reason was a five-year contract worth $1 million less annually than what Alexei Yashin83 received. He was 24, a Calder Trophy winner two seasons earlier, with back-to-back All-Star Games on his résumé. The organization had handed him a number and told him it was fair.

He stayed. The C went on his sweater, Yashin eventually left, and the money grew. Eight years later, the $11.40M he carries is the number Alfredsson always believed he should have had, arrived at through patience and production.

The seasons that broke the projection

His personal-best year came in 2001-02: 37 goals and 71 points in 78 games. The Book, if you believe it, projected a career high near the 66 range. Alfredsson beat it by five goals before he turned 30.

In 2003-04 he scored 32 goals and 80 points in 78 games. The Ottawa Senators lost to Toronto Maple Leafs in the first round, and Alfredsson managed one goal and three points in seven games. He went home, came back, and put up 108 points in 81 games last season.

This year's 83.9 pace sits below last season's 109.3, which was a career year by any measure. It is still a points-per-game pace from a 33-year-old captain playing 21.4 minutes a night. The fallers column on the Development Index does not include him. The Index reads +2, which means the Bureau acknowledges he is slightly above his own base of 92. They are still calling 92 his base. He has been past 66 for so long that the projection has become a historical footnote, the way a first-round pick's grade at eighteen becomes one.

What Ottawa has around him

Martin Havlat at 92 is the only teammate with a higher overall. Ray Emery91 sits at 91 with a +10 on the Index. Patrick Lalime90 is at 90. Marian Hossa86 carries 86 and one year left. Zdeno Chara85 is 85 with 78 penalty minutes. The payroll is $59.27M, the cost per point is $1.32M, and the last ten games read LWWWLWOTLWWL.

Ottawa is not a contender in this league. Forty-five points through their games puts them in the middle of the pack. What they have is a captain whose contract expires in five months and whose morale is 94.

The number that does not matter anymore

The Bureau's Development Index measures form against base. The Book's potential measures what the Bureau thought a player could become when he was young. Alfredsson's potential of 66 was set when he was 21, a year into his NHL career, the season after his Calder. He has been above it every season since.

At 33, on $11.40M, with the C on his chest and 94 in morale, the question is whether Ottawa Senators pays a 33-year-old with a Potential of 66 franchise money again, or whether they let the man who demanded a trade over a million dollars walk for free.

He is 27 points past the projection, and has been for most of his career. The number 66 is a record of how wrong the Book was, and how little it mattered.
