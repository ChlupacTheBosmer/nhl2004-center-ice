Potential 58, Overall 93, and Free in June: The Book Told Us Who Todd Bertuzzi Was When He Was Nineteen

He is the best skater on a $46.36M payroll, his contract expires in five months, and the number that was supposed to cap him has been wrong for ten years.

Carol BinghamFeatures writer · RinksideTuesday, 10 January 2006
ShareCopy link

The Vancouver Canucks Canucks have the second-smallest payroll in this league at $46.36M. They made $27.69M in profit last season on $58.49M of revenue, which means the room is careful. And in the middle of that careful house lives a 30-year-old right wing whose overall grade is 93, whose contract pays him $2.60M, and who is a free agent in June.

Todd Bertuzzi89 has 55 points in 41 games this season, a pace of 110 over 82. He scored 128 points last year. The season before that he put up 101 in 63 games. Three consecutive seasons above 100 points, and the Bureau's Book has him at Potential 58. That number was set when he was a teenager in the OHL, and it has not moved since. It says he is done growing. It says the 93 he carries today, the highest grade on the roster above Markus Naslund87's 91, is about the best he will ever be.

The cheapest 93 among skaters

A 93 on an expiring deal is rare across this league. Among all players in the contract-year class, Bertuzzi is the highest-graded skater at 93. The next best forward on an expiring contract is Naslund at 91, and he costs $4.40M. Peter Forsberg95 at 89 costs $9.90M in Colorado. Bertuzzi at 93 costs $2.60M. Only a goaltender, Roberto Luongo95 at 95 for $1.60M in Toronto, is cheaper per grade-point among skaters and goalies combined.

The Vancouver front office knows this. They know it in the way that men know things they cannot say aloud at a dinner where someone from another GM's office might be listening. Bertuzzi's morale is 100. He has not been injured once on the record. He has played 41 of 41 games this season. He takes 22.5 minutes a night and scores at a clip that puts him tied for fifth in league scoring, ahead of Ilya Kovalchuk86 and Joe Thornton84.

His Development Index reads 0. He is exactly where the Book says he is. That has been true for two seasons now, and it has been true because the Book set the number high enough to be flattering and low enough to be safe. Potential 58 for a man who just scored 128 points. The number was wrong when it was set, and it is wrong now.

What Sudbury gave him and what the Islanders kept

Bertuzzi grew up in Sudbury, Ontario. The Guelph Storm took him fifth overall in the 1991 OHL Priority Selection. Marc Crawford, then the general manager of the Cornwall Royals, passed on him. Crawford has said it plainly: maturity issues, a big kid who had not grown into his body. That was the scouting report on a fifteen-year-old. It stuck.

He scored 54 goals for Guelph in his draft year, a single-season team record. The New York Islanders took him 23rd overall in 1993. He did not sign with them for two more years. When the deadline to re-enter the draft approached in July 1995, the Islanders' GM made it known he would take Bertuzzi again with the second overall pick if no deal was struck. They signed him moments before midnight. Four years, $4.6M.

The room turned on a wing

Vancouver's payroll is $46.36M. That puts them ahead of only Edmonton Oilers, at $42.95M, in the whole league. Their cost per standings point is $0.97M. They sit with 48 points and run one of the more efficient operations in the sport at converting dollars into wins.

The four players on this roster who clear 87 in the Book are Bertuzzi at 93 ($2.60M, 0 years left), Naslund at 91 ($4.40M, 1 year left), Dan Cloutier86 at 90 (1 year left), and Ed Jovanovski84 at 88 ($2.30M, 1 year left). All four have contracts that expire this June. The Canucks face a summer where their top four players by overall grade could all walk. Bertuzzi is the first domino because his deal ends first, 0 years left, and because his price is the most absurd in the building.

The Book's Potential 58 will be the number the other side uses. It will sit in the binder across the table from the agent's assistant and glow. Potential 58 means he cannot get better, that you are paying for a peak that has already arrived, that the next contract is the last one that matters.

He is 30 and won't improve. He is also tied for fifth in the league and the highest-graded skater on his club. Both things are true.

$0.02 a point

Bertuzzi's 55 points this season against $2.60M works out to $0.05M per point. Last season's 128 points against the same salary came to $0.02M per point. Even his pace-adjusted cost sits at $0.02M per point. The man is scoring 1.34 points a game on the second-cheapest team in hockey, and the contract that pays for it will not exist in September.

What happens in the spring depends on one question: do you pay a man for what he is, or what the Book says he will be? The Book said 58 a decade ago and stopped talking. Bertuzzi has answered it every night since, with the patience of a man who has been misread his whole career.
