I Saw Him When: The #3 Pick From 2001 Has a 99 and 2 Points

Alexander Svitov's physical grades are elite. His production hasn't moved in four years. He's at San Jose now, on a one-year deal.

Pete LarocqueScouting editor · The Prospect FileFriday, 13 January 2006
ShareCopy link

League sources moved Alexander Svitov75 from Tampa Bay Lightning to San Jose Sharks between the two snapshots this week. He is 23. He was the third pick of the 2001 draft. His potential on the Bureau's Book reads 99. He has 2 points in 24 games at 9.1 minutes a night this season.

That potential has sat at 99 the entire time I have been tracking the Book. Four years, three clubs, 13 points in the record, and the ceiling number refuses to come down. His base overall is 73. The Development Index has him at +4. That puts him at 75 on the board today. The gap between 75 and 99 is what I am writing about.

The physical grades are real

Svitov's Book grades read: 94 balance, 93 acceleration, 93 agility, 92 speed, 84 shot power, 82 toughness. Those are numbers you see on All-Star forwards. They are printed under a 75 overall and 2 points in 24 games, and the Bureau still says 99. At his weakest he has 50 fight, 54 endurance, 56 offensive discipline. The body is extraordinary. The game around the body is not.

His career in this league runs: 47 games and 4 points in his first recorded season, 64 games and 7 points in 2003-04 (his career high), 24 games and 2 points this season at 9.1 minutes a night. He scored 3 goals in 64 games two years ago. He has 2 goals in 24 games now.

The 2001 class left him behind

The first round of 2001 produced one generational forward, a top-pairing defenseman, a franchise goaltender, and a second-line center. It also produced the third pick, who cannot crack a scoring line at the bottom of the Pacific Division.

Spezza is 5 points in 26 games too, but he is behind Chistov and Upshall at Toronto and playing third-line minutes for a contender. Svitov is on a rebuilding club that has won 13 games in 44 tries.

San Jose bought the body

San Jose Sharks sits at 13-22-8, 36 points, 127 goals against. They are last in the Pacific by 9 points. The roster has Patrick Marleau88 at 87 overall and Evgeni Nabokov89 at 89 in net. Between them, the middle is thin. Svitov's 92 speed and 93 acceleration, if they ever attach to 20 minutes and a role, would change the shape of San Jose's third line. Right now he would be fourth center by grade and ice time.

His contract runs $1.00M with 1 year remaining. Free agency closes 196 days from now. San Jose is not a club that keeps a 23-year-old at $1.00M on a one-year deal and then re-signs him unless he shows something in the remaining 20 games or so on the schedule.

He missed time with an elbow injury between late November and early December. The return date was 2005-12-09. That accounts for some absence. It does not account for 2 points in 24 games across two seasons with Tampa Bay and whatever comes next.

The potential is a bet, not a promise

The Bureau prints the potential as a likelihood of development. It does not move down just because a player fails to produce. I have watched the Book for four years and I have never seen a 99 get revised in a direction I could name. The number is a floor on belief, and the floor is very high on Svitov.

I saw him when the 99 first appeared. I believed it. His first season was 4 points in 47 games and I told myself he was adjusting. His second was 7 in 64 and I told myself the minutes were too low. This is his fourth year in the league and the minutes are still 9.1 and the points are still in single digits. The 99 says the Bureau thinks there is a 99 overall inside him. I think the 99 is a typo in the development model, or it is a grade for a player who skates like him and plays 15 minutes with a role and a line he fits. That player does not exist in San Jose's lineup this week.

He is 23 with one year of contract left. If the production does not arrive by spring, the $1.00M expires, the 99 stays on the sheet, and the Bureau moves on to the next 2006-class kid who skates like the wind.
