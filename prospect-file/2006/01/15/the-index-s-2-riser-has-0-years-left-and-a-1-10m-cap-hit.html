The Index's #2 Riser Has 0 Years Left and a $1.10M Cap Hit

Mike Komisarek's base is 77. The Development Index has him at +16. Montreal sits at 45 points and what the calendar has as the trade deadline is 38 days out.

Pete LarocqueScouting editor · The Prospect FileSunday, 15 January 2006
ShareCopy link

Mike Komisarek85 has the second-biggest move on the Development Index in the league. The Index reads him at +16 above his Book base of 77, putting his displayed overall at 85. He is tied with Jason Bacashihua97 at Dallas Stars on the risers list. Only Marian Gaborik99 at Toronto Maple Leafs, up 18, has moved further.

$1.10M, 0 years left, Montreal Canadiens sitting at 13-17-9 and 45 points in the Northeast, 27 back of Toronto. He plays 31.7 minutes a night on a team with no path to anything in February.

A 97 and a 51 on the same page

The Bureau's physical grades stop you cold. Speed 97. Checking 96. Shot power 95. Acceleration 93. Aggression 91. Agility 89. He is 6'3", 225 pounds, right-shot. Born in West Islip, New York. Two seasons at the University of Michigan, 46 points and 145 penalty minutes in 80 games, a CCHA title, a First Team All-American nod in 2002. Montreal took him seventh overall in 2001.

The mental grades go the other direction. Fight Sense 51. Short Pass IQ 52. Offence Board IQ 55. The Book can reconcile those two pages only by keeping his base at 77. A defenceman who skates like that and reads like this will produce 33 points in 44 games, not 55. His potential sits at 90, which is 13 points above his base. The Index says he is already 8 points above it. If the potential is honest, there are 5 more points inside him. If the form is a heater, the floor drops back to 77 the moment the Index resets.

What the production says

He has been with Montreal since before the record opened on 2004-12-01. He played 24 games as a 20-year-old and scored at 0.13 points per game. Last season he played 82 and scored at 1.00 points per game, 15 goals and 67 assists at 30.4 minutes a night. This season: 10 goals, 23 assists, 33 points in 44 games at 0.75 points per game at 31.7 minutes. His per-game rate dropped. His penalty minutes nearly doubled, from 40 to 68.

That is a 23-year-old defenceman on a $1.10M expiring deal logging the second-highest ice time on his team while his production rate declines. The Index measures the gap between his current displayed overall and his base. He can be playing above his base and still below what his prior season suggested.

The trade-deadline case

Montreal's best player by overall is Jose Theodore89 at 89. Komisarek at 85 is the second-highest displayed number on the roster. His base grade of 77 is the third-highest. The gap between Theodore and the next man is 10 points, and most of Montreal's remaining roster sits in the 70s.

A team that needs a top-pairing defenceman for 31 minutes a night, a 97-speed skater who can hit, block and shoot from the point, and a contract that expires in the summer, is looking at the cheapest option the league has to offer. The trade deadline is 38 days out. Montreal is 45 points, tied with Ottawa Senators at the bottom of the Northeast. They have no realistic path to a playoff spot from here.

What they do have is the second-biggest Index riser in the league on a $1.10M expiring deal. A buyer gives up a second-round pick and maybe a prospect. A seller holds a man whose base is 77 and whose displayed number says 85, and whose potential says 90, and whose ice time on a 45-point team says nothing is going to change by April.

I had Igor Radulov76 at Chicago Blackhawks as the cheapest Index riser in the league three weeks ago. Radulov is $0.60M with a base of 71 and a potential of 78. Komisarek costs $1.10M. He also has a potential of 90, a base of 77, 31.7 minutes a night, and a physical profile that Radulov does not approach. Somebody should call Montreal.
