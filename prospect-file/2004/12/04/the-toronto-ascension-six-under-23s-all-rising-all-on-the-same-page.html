The Toronto Ascension: six under-23s, all rising, all on the same page

No other team in the league has more than 2 players under 23 with positive form on the Development Index right now. Toronto has 6.

Pete LarocqueScouting editor · The Prospect FileSaturday, 4 December 2004
ShareCopy link

I wrote about Petr Chlup81 on Friday — the 18-year-old centre already skimming 11.1 minutes a night for the league's best team, the youngest player on the Book's U-23 board with an overall of 82 and potential at 95. One kid. I stopped there.

I should not have stopped there.

Pull up the Development Index risers and look at the team column. Marian Gaborik95 is +6. Rick Nash80 is +5. Rostislav Klesla81 is +5. Brad Richards84 is +4. Chlup is +3. Stanislav Chistov79 is +3. 6 Toronto players under 25 with positive form, 4 of them in the top 10 risers league-wide. No other franchise has more than 2 on that list.

This is not a rookie story. This is not a single breakout. This is an entire cohort developing on the same calendar, in the same system, on the same team that sits 18-5-0 with 147 goals for and 76 against and a 10-game win streak.

6 positive, 3 flat, 0 negative. That last row matters. Every single Toronto under-23 on the sheet is either developing or holding steady. Not a single one has regressed.

What the potential numbers mean.

Gaborik is the everyone sees: 96 overall, +6 on the Index, 34 points in 25 games at 18.4 minutes a night. He is 22 and he is the best player on the best team in the league. But his potential is 90. He is already past it. The Book says the ceiling is there and he is touching it.

The players that make me sit up are the ones below him. Klesla at 82 overall with a 97 potential and +5 form. Nash at 81 with a 95 potential and +5 form. Chlup at 82 with a 95 potential. Chistov at 80 with a 95 potential. Upshall and Eminger both sitting at 99 potential with form flat — not falling, just waiting.

I distrust a big frame with a low potential number. I also distrust a high potential number with negative form. Toronto's kids have the potential AND the form. That combination is what makes me think this is not a window — it is a door that stays open.

The age curve is absurd.

Gaborik turns 23 in February. Nash is 20. Chlup is 18. The Gaborik-Nash-Chlup line, if the coaching staff ever assembles it, will not hit its combined prime for several years. Klesla is 22 with a 97 potential. Eminger is 21 with a 99 potential on the blue line. The back end is not a problem either.

And the draft capital: Toronto Maple Leafs holds Detroit Red Wings's first-round pick this June. They already have 5 picks in the draft (first included). They are winning the Northeast by 18 points and accumulating more assets.

What I am saying.

Most teams build through a handful of young players breaking out. You find your Nash, you find your Klesla, you build around them. Toronto has 6 kids under 25 with positive development, 3 more with elite potential holding flat, and a 22-year-old with a 96 overall who is already the best player in the league. The Book's potential figures on this group — 90, 95, 97, 95, 95, 94, 99, 91, 99 — do not describe a team peaking. They describe a team still climbing.

I saw him when. I saw all of them when. And I think I am going to be writing about them when for a very long time.
