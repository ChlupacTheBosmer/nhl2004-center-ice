I Saw Him When: Petr Chlup and the 18-year-old running Toronto

The youngest player on the Book's U-23 board is already a difference-maker on the league's best team — and the rest of that team's youth wave makes this look like a dynasty in embryo.

Pete Larocque
Scouting editor · The Prospect FileSaturday, 4 December 2004

I saw him when he was seventeen in a Junior Bureau snapshot, a skinny center who could barely hold the puck on his stick in transition and could not be separated from it in the offensive zone. They said he'd need two years. He needed fourteen games.

Petr Chlup82 is eighteen. He sits at 82 overall on the Book's U-23 board, the youngest name on the sheet, with a 95 potential figure that the Bureau rarely hands to a teenager. He's producing eight points in fourteen games at 11.1 minutes per night, and the Index has him three ticks above his base — a teenager outplaying the projection.

What makes Chlup's emergence so disorienting is context. He's not carving out a role on a contender. He's doing it on the Toronto Maple Leafs, which after twenty-five games sits at 18-5-0 with a league-best 147 goals for and 76 against, riding a ten-game win streak and a 14-3 home record. This is not a team that needs an eighteen-year-old center. This is a team that found one and kept him in the lineup.

The Toronto youth board reads like a mock draft layered onto a playoff roster. Marian Gaborik96, 22, at 96 overall with a +6 form spike — he's the best player on the entire U-23 sheet by a clear margin. Rick Nash81, 20, 81 overall, +5 on the Index, 22 points in 25 games at 12.9 minutes. Rostislav Klesla82, 22, 82 overall, +5 form, 97 potential. That's three Toronto players inside the top nine risers on the Index this period, which is almost absurd concentration.

Below them sits a second wave that makes the depth terrifying. Scottie Upshall77, 21, carries a 99 potential figure — the highest on the U-23 board shared with Steve Eminger75, 21, a defenseman with the same ceiling and ten points in twenty-four games. Nikita Alexeev76, 22, at 94 potential. Stanislav Chistov80, 21, 80 overall and +3 on the Index despite only fourteen games and 9.5 minutes a night. Vaclav Spacek75, 19, a rookie center at 75 overall with 91 potential, already at 16.0 minutes.

Five players under 22 with 94 or higher potential on the same roster. I cannot remember seeing that number in one building.

And Toronto owns Detroit Red Wings's first-round pick in June. If Detroit's current 8-9-4 slide and L5 streak holds, that pick moves up. The Maple Leafs are stacking the present and the future on the same timeline.

The Chlup question now is not whether he stays in the lineup. It's how long before the minutes come up. He's at 11.1 per night in a system that gives its other young players 13, 14, even 16. The Bureau's 95 potential suggests they've already penciled in a growth curve. If the Index keeps reading +3 or better, the Book's overall will follow — and an 82 overall at eighteen is the kind of number that makes you redraw the whole board.

I saw him when he was small. He's still small. He doesn't need to be bigger. He just needs more ice, and Toronto is too good to say no.
