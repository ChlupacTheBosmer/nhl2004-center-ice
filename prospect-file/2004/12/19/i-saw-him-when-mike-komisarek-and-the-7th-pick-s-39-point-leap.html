I Saw Him When: Mike Komisarek and the 7th Pick's 39-Point Leap

The 2001 draft's seventh selection had 3 points in 24 games last year. He has 39 in 32 now, the biggest jump on the Development Index for a defenseman, and a contract that expires in 12 months.

Pete LarocqueScouting editor · The Prospect FileSunday, 19 December 2004
ShareCopy link

Mike Komisarek78 [217] scored 3 points in 24 games last season at 11.8 minutes a night. He has 39 points in 32 games this year at 29.8 minutes, and the Development Index has him at +6, tied for the biggest form jump in the league. He is the only defenseman on the risers list. All five others are forwards.

He is 22. His potential sits at 90. His contract pays $1.10M with 1 year left. Montreal Canadiens is 9-19-2, last in the Northeast at 24 points, and on a 6-game losing streak. The Canadiens' most improved asset is a defenseman who had 3 points twelve months ago.

Where he sat in 2001

Komisarek went 7th overall in 2001, behind Ilya Kovalchuk86 [509], Dany Heatley84 [377], Marian Gaborik96 [308], Alexander Svitov71 [1026], Stanislav Chistov80 [518], and Scott Hartnell77 [367]. Four drafts later, here is where the top seven sit:

Komisarek's 39 points lead every defenseman from that first round by a wide margin, and his 80 overall is the second highest among picks 4 through 7. The 7th pick from 2001 has outscored the 4th, 5th, and 6th picks by 38, 32, and 30 points respectively this season.

What he is

Komisarek is 75 inches, 225 pounds, shoots right. His Book grades tell you what kind of defenseman produces 39 points at that size: Komisarek carries a 90 in checking, 87 in speed, 86 in aggression, 86 in puck handling, 85 in shot power, and 83 in passing. His weakest three grades, faceoffs at 51, short-handed breakouts at 52, and offensive breakouts at 55, are special-teams reads that barely touch what a top-pairing defenseman does on a nightly basis.

A 90 in checking on a man who skates at 87 and handles the puck at 86 is the profile that breaks out when the ice opens. That is what 32 games at 29.8 minutes looks like: the coach has stopped easing him and put him in the fire, and he is scoring at a rate no young blueliner in this league can match. Barret Jackman77 [432], the next defenseman on the under-23s list, has 17 points in 31 games.

The team around him

Montreal Canadiens has lost 6 in a row. The recent games read like a body blow: a 5-4 overtime loss to St. Louis Blues, a 5-3 loss at Ottawa Senators, a 6-2 loss to Boston Bruins at home. The Canadiens are scoring 109 goals in 32 games and giving up 141. Komisarek's plus-minus is not on the sheet, but the 32-goal differential is a team problem that his 39 points are not solving.

On that roster, his 39 points lead all defensemen. Andrei Markov77 [620] has 36 in 29 games at the other blue line. Saku Koivu83 [498] has 35 in 23 games, Richard Zednik81 [1107] has 50 in 32. The room is thin enough that Komisarek is carrying the offensive load on the back end against a team structure that is leaking goals. His morale sits at 87, which tells you the room has not turned on itself yet.

The money question

One year left at $1.10M. The 2003-04 version of Komisarek, 3 points in 24 games, would have re-signed cheap. The 2004-05 version, 39 points in 32, will cost more. He was drafted 7th overall out of the University of Michigan, where he logged 46 points in 80 games and made First Team All-American in 2002. The skill was there for three years. The jump is the minutes and the trust.

If the Index holds and the 39 points carry through 82 games, the 7th pick in 2001 is on a Norris trajectory. If the room collapses and the 6-game slide stretches into the teens, $1.10M is the cheapest defenseman on the market come July.
