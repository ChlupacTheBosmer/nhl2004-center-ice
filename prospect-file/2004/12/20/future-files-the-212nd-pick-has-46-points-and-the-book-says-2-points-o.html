Future Files: The 212nd Pick Has 46 Points and the Book Says 2 Points of Room Left

Vrbata is producing at an under-23 elite rate in Carolina. The problem is what the potential column says about what comes next.

Pete LarocqueScouting editor · The Prospect FileMonday, 20 December 2004
ShareCopy link

Radim Vrbata79 was taken 212th overall in 1999, in the 8th round. He is 23 now, has 46 points in 33 games for Carolina Hurricanes, and the Bureau's Book has him at 79 overall with 81 potential. Two points of room left. None of the other under-23 skaters producing at his rate have that problem.

Vrbata's 46 points in 33 games sit behind only Ilya Kovalchuk86's 55 in 31, Dany Heatley84's 51 in 28, Marian Gaborik96's 47 in 33, and Martin Havlat84's 46 in 30 among under-23 skaters with at least 25 games on the sheet. He skates 21.4 minutes a night on a team that has won 12 games in regulation and 7 more in overtime. He is a 2nd-line right wing with 1st-line point production and a ceiling the Bureau has essentially stamped.

I saw him when in 2001, when the first-round guys around him were still finding their game. The eighth-round label followed him through the system. He's 79 in the Book now and the potential figure won't move much, which is a warning about the next layer more than a comment on Vrbata.

The man with the room to grow is watching from the 4th line

Pavel Brendl78 has the highest potential number of any Carolina under-23: 93. His overall is 78. The Development Index has him at +3 this month, the only one of the three young Carolina Hurricanes forwards with a positive form reading above +1. He scored 21 points in 82 games last season and has 18 in 33 now, a 45-point pace.

Brendl plays 13.9 minutes a night. For comparison, Vrbata gets 21.4. Tomas Kurka77 gets 20.5. Brendl is the 2000 draft's 4th overall pick, and he plays fourth-line minutes on a 45-point team. The form is there. The ice time isn't.

Brendl turned 23 in February. He's a young league player whose 93 potential is going unused because the veterans ahead of him are playing well. Rod Brind'Amour is 83 in the Book. Petr Sykora is 82. Sandis Ozolinsh is 86. That's the top of the depth chart and it's locked until those men leave.

Kurka is past his number

Tomas Kurka, drafted 32nd overall in 2000, carries a 77 overall against a potential of 75. He is two points above the Book's likelihood figure, which means the Bureau expected less than he's delivering. His form reads +1, flat enough to ignore. Twenty-three points in 33 games on 20.5 minutes is respectable bottom-six production.

Kurka is the kind of player the 2000 second round was supposed to yield, and the number says he's already given Carolina what they got.

The pipeline question: where does the next one come from?

Carolina took Marcel Janota73 7th overall in 2004. The kid is 20, has dressed once, played 60.0 minutes in that one game, and sits scratched. The 7th pick from last June is a healthy backup watching Kevin Weekes (90 overall) and Arturs Irbe (77) work the crease. Janota carries 89 potential in the Book, the highest number on the Carolina Hurricanes roster for any player under 25, but the path to the ice goes through a 68-game veteran and a 32-year-old Latvian.

The 2004 draft's third-round pick, Yves Bertrand61, has yet to dress. The rest of the young forward group is Hnat Domenichelli (23, overall 75, form -1) and Tomas Kurka. There is no 20-year-old forward on the roster with a potential above 77.

Carolina Hurricanes is 12-7-7, 45 points, 3rd in the Southeast. They've gone to overtime 7 times, the most of any team in the standings. They are competitive right now and the core that got them there is aging: Brind'Amour, Sykora, Ozolinsh, Hedican, O'Neill. The three under-23s are contributing, but the ceiling on Vrbata and the minutes problem on Brendl mean the Bureau's long-term grade on this young group is uncertain.

The 45 points tonight is built on veterans winning close games. The 7 overtime losses tell you how close. The 93 on Brendl's potential line is the only number on the roster that points toward an answer, if someone hands him the ice time to show it.
