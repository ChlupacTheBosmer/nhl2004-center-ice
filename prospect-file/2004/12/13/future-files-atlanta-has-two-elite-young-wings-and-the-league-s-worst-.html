Future Files: Atlanta Has Two Elite Young Wings and the League's Worst Record

Kovalchuk and Heatley are the Book's second and third-rated skaters under 23. The club behind them has 18 points.

Pete LarocqueScouting editor · The Prospect FileMonday, 13 December 2004
ShareCopy link

Ilya Kovalchuk90 has 48 points in 27 games. Dany Heatley88 has 44 in 24. The club that dresses both, Atlanta, has 18 points, the fewest in the league, off a 5-17-2 record.

The Bureau's Development Index this edition has Kovalchuk up five on his base and Heatley up one. The Book grades the pair second and third among skaters under 23, behind only Marian Gaborik98. Gaborik's club has won 22 of 29 and leads the league in points. Atlanta has won 5 of 27. The form is real and the season is spent, which makes the next draft the one asset that still moves the needle, and Atlanta holds its own first-rounder.

They carry the work at 20.1 and 21.5 minutes a night. Kovalchuk is 21, Heatley 23. No forward under 24 scores more, and only Martin Havlat88 reaches 44 points alongside them. The waste shows up when the club records sit beside the names:

Havlat scores his 44 for a club planted in the East playoff pack at 29 points. The two Thrashers do the same volume for the club with the fewest points in the league. The Bureau's 40-name under-23 list carries exactly two Atlanta names, and they are the two forwards. No young defenseman of the Book's kind serves this club, and no young goaltender. The top end of the core is all wing.

The goals are not the problem

Atlanta has scored 100 goals in 27 games. The 122 it has allowed is the season. Two elite wings score straight into that hole, and the Bureau's list says nothing behind them wants to stop the bleeding. Age splits the two as well. Heatley is 23, at the old end of the under-23 board, and the Book's potential on him is 85 against an 88 overall, so his climb is mostly done. Kovalchuk is 21 with the same 85 potential against a 90, and the Index says he is playing above that number right now. The sell case, if there is one, names Heatley first, and it has to explain what replaces 44 points.

What the pick has to be

The draft question sits clean, because Atlanta owns its own first and at 18 points that pick lands near the top of the round. You keep a 21-year-old with 48 points in 27 games, or you trade him and answer what the return replaces. The Index is the better guide: the form on Kovalchuk is up five, the strongest reading on any elite forward this edition. You do not sell that at 21 because the club behind it cannot keep the puck out. You keep the wings and spend the pick on the side of the roster with nothing on the Bureau's board.

Last June gives the caution. Atlanta's top pick was Vyacheslav Sbruev66, a 20-year-old left wing at 31st overall, and he sits scratched at 66 in the Book with 64 potential. The club spent its board on the position it already owned. The next first cannot repeat that bet: the wings are in ink, and the name below them should be a defenseman or a goaltender, because that is the end of the roster with nothing behind it.
