The 2004 Draft, Six Months In: 11 of 30 First-Rounders Are Dressed

Timander leads the class with 17 points. Abid has played 33 games at 9.7 minutes. The minutes are the real measure, and most of the class is not getting any.

Pete LarocqueScouting editor · The Prospect FileFriday, 17 December 2004
ShareCopy link

The 2004 draft class is 6 months old. Of the 30 first-round picks, 11 are dressed, meaning they are on an active roster and getting games. The other 19 are scratched, which in the Bureau's language means they are not playing. That is a 37 percent activation rate, and it is low for a class this age.

The 11 who are playing are split between a handful getting real minutes and a handful getting a look. The minutes column is the one that matters.

Timander at 17.1 minutes and 17 points in 24 games is the class's obvious early return. He was the 2nd pick, and he is playing like it. Salminen at 17.3 minutes with 9 points in 24 games is the quiet one. He was the 5th pick, a defenseman, and the minutes tell you Pittsburgh Penguins is building around him even if the points line does not look like a top-5 selection yet.

Spacek, the 1st pick, has 14 games at 16.4 minutes and 9 points. That is a strong per-game rate, but 14 games is not a sample. The Book has him at 74 overall, 91 potential. The potential number is high because the scouts saw something in the speed and the vision that has not fully translated to the scoreboard yet. 16.4 minutes in 14 games means he is getting a real look, and that is the important part.

The minutes problem

Abid has played 33 games. That is more than any other player in the class. But at 9.7 minutes a night, he is getting a fourth-line load. Timander's 24 games at 17.1 minutes is a completely different role. The 28th pick has been on the roster for 9 more games than the 2nd pick, and the 2nd pick is still ahead in total ice time. That is the gap between a player who is integrated and a player who is a body in a sweater.

Sulku is the worst case. 25 games, 7.4 minutes, 5 points. That is a player who is dressed, who is on the roster, who is getting called up for the odd game, but who is not developing. 7.4 minutes is a third-line even-strength shift.

Salei, a defenseman, has 3 games at 17.3 minutes. He is essentially not in the league yet, despite being dressed.

What 19 scratches means

The 19 who are not playing include the 7th, 8th, 9th, 10th, 11th, 12th, 14th, 16th, 17th, 19th, 20th, 21st, 22nd, 23rd, 24th, 25th, 26th, 29th, and 30th picks. The Bureau's potential grades on the scratch list include 94 (Nylander, who is technically dressed but has 6 games), 89 (Janota, scratched), 85 (Prommersberger, scratched), 84 (Zholtok, scratched), 96 (Stahle, scratched).

Teams are keeping their 2004 picks in the minors until they see something they cannot ignore. The ones who broke through, Timander, Spacek, Salminen, Abid, are the ones who made their case in a preseason or an early stretch. The others are waiting.

That is normal for a draft class. But 11 of 30 is low, and it tells you the 2004 class is being evaluated more carefully than the 2003 group was at this point. The scouts are not rushing. The question for the next 6 months is whether the 19 who are scratched are going to get their minutes, or whether the class is going to split into 11 who made it and 19 who are still looking for a door.

The draft was in June. It is December. The scouts have the luxury of waiting. The players do not.
