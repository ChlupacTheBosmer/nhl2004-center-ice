I Saw Him When: Pierre-Marc Bouchard

Speed 88, deception 86, checking 50. The 8th pick in 2002 is a skill profile with no floor, and 10.8 minutes on a bad team is the question.

Pete LarocqueScouting editor · The Prospect FileFriday, 17 December 2004
ShareCopy link

I saw him when he was 18, in a Sherbrooke rink that did not have heat in the second period, and I remember the puck going through two defenders and coming back to his stick like it was on a string. He was not the biggest guy on the ice. He did not need to be.

Pierre-Marc Bouchard79 is 20 now, with the 8th pick in the 2002 draft behind him and a contract that is 1 year left at $1.20M. He plays center for Minnesota Wild, a club with 23 points and the fourth-worst record in the Northwest. The Book has him at 75 overall today, which is his base of 74 plus 6 on the Development Index. Potential 86.

The grades tell you what he is

Speed 88. Deception 86. Pass 86. Puck 86. Accel 85. Agility 85. Then the drop: checking 50, fighting 50, toughness 50.

That 36-point gap is not a rounding error. It means he has not engaged the defensive side of the game in any measurable way. The 86 potential is in the feet and the hands: the first step, the change of direction, the pass that arrives before the defender reads it.

The question with a profile like this is whether the floor exists. A player with 50s across the board in the physical categories can develop a motor. Or the 50s can be permanent, and the player can become a one-directional asset who wins in transition and disappears when the puck is in his own end.

The minutes are the problem

Bouchard has 13 points in 32 games at 10.8 minutes a night. Last season: 10 points in 36 games at 11.0. The numbers are flat, which is what you expect from a 20-year-old center getting a thin shift load on a team that is 9 and 20 on the season.

The Development Index is up 6. That is the biggest jump on the Board for a Minnesota player this month, and it tells you the Bureau sees him moving in the right direction even if the points line does not show it yet. But 10.8 minutes is survival, not development. A player with 88 speed and 86 deception needs to be on the ice when the game is fast, and 10.8 minutes on a team that concedes 127 goals in 32 games means he is getting the fast stretches but also the garbage stretches, the even-strength resets after a power-play kill, the shift where the game is 3 goals up and nobody is trying anything.

What the 86 means

Potential 86 is the number you give a player you believe will be a regular, a top-six piece on a good team, a guy who scores 50 to 60 points a year at the right age. It is not elite.

The 50s in the physical categories are the part of the file that keeps you up at night. Because the 86 is in the speed, and speed ages, and if the 50s never move, the player becomes a guy you start on your second line in even strength and pull when the game gets tight. A fine player, but not an 86.

The 86 is the player who develops a motor, starts checking, wins the faceoff in his own end and makes the first pass out clean. He becomes two-way not through strength but through speed, choosing to be late on the backcheck.

I do not know if Bouchard is that player. The feet and hands are there. The minutes are not, and 10.8 minutes on a team with 23 points is not going to answer the question. The answer is going to come when he gets 18 minutes on a team that wins, and the 50s start to move, and the 86 becomes what it is supposed to be.

Until then, he is a 75 with 6 on the Index and a profile that says the ceiling is in the skating and the floor is nowhere. That is what I saw at 18 in a rink without heat.
