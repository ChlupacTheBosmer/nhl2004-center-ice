The 93 on the Roster at Columbus: Pascal Leclaire Is the League's Best Young Goalie and the Clock Is Running

Leclaire is 84 overall, +4 on the Index, potential 93, 22 years old, 14 games in, and his deal expires in 12 months. Columbus has 35 points and no plan anyone can see.

Pete LarocqueScouting editor · The Prospect FileFriday, 24 December 2004
ShareCopy link

Pascal Leclaire82 is 84 overall. That puts him fifth among all under-23 players in the league, behind only Marian Gaborik96 (97), Ilya Kovalchuk86 (86), and the two goalies Ari Ahonen84 and Jason Bacashihua85 at 85. His potential is 93. His Development Index form is +4. He is 22 years old, he has played 14 games this season at 59.9 minutes a night, and his contract has 1 year left at $1.10M.

Columbus Blue Jackets has 35 points, a 10-14-1 record, third in the Central. The team behind him will not make the playoffs. The Bureau's grades settled years ago that Leclaire is good. The question now is whether Columbus builds around him, or whether they move him while his value is peaking and his salary is a bargain.

What the grades say

Leclaire's rebound control sits at 74, agility at 91. Those are the two numbers that matter most for a modern goaltender. Rebound control means the second-chance pucks do not kill you. Agility means you recover on the scramble plays. The rest of his save grades are not published on the Bureau's current card.

Leclaire is the starter. He has been since the team took him 8th overall in 2001. The record shows him at 9-4-0 this season in 14 appearances at 58 minutes a night in the game log. The roster around him is the problem.

The minutes tell the story

At 59.9 minutes a night, Leclaire is playing every game he starts, no pull, no breather. On a team with 10 wins, he is the reason they have 35 points instead of 28. The Bureau has him at +4, which is what you see watching him: he is playing above his base because the team needs him to be.

A 22-year-old goaltender taking 60 minutes a night on a 10-win team is absorbing every scramble, every third-period push from opponents who smell blood. If his form drops in February, if his overall falls below 80, his trade value drops with it. The window is now.

What the trade market looks like

Leclaire is $1.10M on an expiring deal. That is a rental with a young core attached. Any contender in the Eastern Conference that needs a goalie gets a 22-year-old with 84 overall and 93 potential for one year of salary and prospects. The contenders in the East: Toronto Maple Leafs at 58, New Jersey Devils at 47, New York Rangers at 48, Carolina Hurricanes at 49. All four have playoff-caliber rosters. None of them has a goaltender under 25 with Leclaire's potential figure.

The counterargument is the one every rebuilding GM tells himself: we just got him, he is young, we build around him. Columbus took him 8th overall in 2001, three years ago, and the roster around him has not caught up.

The 93 is the whole argument

A potential of 93 on the Book means the Bureau sees a high likelihood Leclaire reaches an overall in the mid-90s. That is Vezina territory. Rick DiPietro83 carries a 99 potential at New York Islanders, and Ray Emery80 carries a 97 at Ottawa Senators, and Dan Blackburn73 carries a 97 at New York Rangers. The 93 puts Leclaire in that group, and he is the only one of them playing every minute on a team that cannot win.

He turns 23 in March. His team control runs out this summer. Whoever has the 93 in June either built something around it or paid for it in picks and prospects. Columbus has 35 points and owns all five of their own picks, one first included. They hold no picks belonging to another club.

The simplest read: if Columbus Blue Jackets is not a 40-point team by February, they should not be holding a 22-year-old goalie with a 93 potential and an expiring deal. They should be shopping him at the deadline, collecting two first-rounders and a top prospect, and starting over with better assets than a 10-14-1 record.
