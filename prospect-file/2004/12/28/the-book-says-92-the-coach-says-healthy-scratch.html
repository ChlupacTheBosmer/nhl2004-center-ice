The Book Says 92. The Coach Says Healthy Scratch.

Four of the five highest-potential players in the 2004 first round aren't in the lineup. The class's leading scorer has a potential figure of 66.

Pete LarocqueScouting editor · The Prospect FileTuesday, 28 December 2004
ShareCopy link

Andreas Stahle63 carries the highest potential number in the 2004 draft class: 96. He has not played a league game. Roger Nylander71 sits at 94. He has played 6. Mika Sulku65 is at 92, the third-highest figure among first-rounders last June. He plays 7.5 minutes a night for Calgary Flames and is scratched on most nights. All three are sitting on losing records, and none of them is going to change that before the trade deadline.

The top five potential figures in the 2004 first round belong to Stahle (96), Nylander (94), Sulku (92), Vaclav Spacek74 (91) and Marcel Janota72 (89). Those five have 54 games, 18 points and 2 dressed slots between them. The class's two leading scorers, Kristian Timander69 and Tuomo Pihlman69, carry potential figures of 66 and 56. The coaches are playing a different hand than the [[team:DET|Bureau's]] Book is dealing.

Sulku is the case that stings. The 4th pick overall, 20 years old, potential 92, overall 65. Twenty-eight games, 5 points, 4 goals, 7.5 minutes a night. Calgary is 11-20-4, 28 points, 4th in the Northwest. The Flames are bad enough to lose on and still can't find room for a 4th-overall pick with the 3rd-highest upside projection in the class. Mika Sulku is a healthy scratch.

Andy Murray put it plainly when asked about LA's young players earlier this month, and the quote travels: "These kids, they've been ready. They've been ready for a while. We just had to find the space." The space is the problem, and not every coach thinks the 92-potential kid deserves it over the 66-potential one who's putting pucks in the net.

Nylander is in the same slot at San Jose Sharks. Potential 94, 6 games, 2 points, 9.7 minutes a night, scratched. San Jose has 37 points and is in the Pacific playoff hunt. The Sharks have no reason to dress a 20-year-old project over a proven depth forward, and the 94 doesn't buy him a spot on the third line.

Stahle hasn't played at all. Columbus already has Pascal Leclaire84 (potential 93) in net and Columbus Blue Jackets at 38 points, and a 19-year-old defenseman with a 96 potential sits in the press box. The Book thinks he might be the best player in the class. The coach hasn't given him a shift.

The producers have the lowest numbers

Timander leads the 2004 class with 17 points in 27 games at 16.6 minutes a night. His potential is 66. Pihlman has 17 points in 28 games at 11.9 minutes. His potential is 56. Tuomas Lydman70, the 10th pick, has 14 points at 16.8 minutes. Potential 52. These three are the coaches' answer to the question "who helps us tonight," and the Book is telling them there isn't much there to build on.

Spacek is the exception that proves the rule. First pick, 91 potential, 11 points, 14.7 minutes, dressed. But he plays for Toronto Maple Leafs, a 60-point team that can dress a 19-year-old because the margin is wide enough to absorb a mistake. The other four high-potential players are on teams with 28, 37, 38 and 49 points. Those coaches don't have that margin, and the potential figure doesn't manufacture one.

Karel Duba74, the 3rd pick, has potential 51, the lowest of any first-rounder. He's 19. He has 7 goals and 9 points in 27 games at 9.1 minutes a night in [[team:LA|Los Angeles]]. He's dressed, he's scoring, and the Book says he won't be much better than this. The coaches don't care. He puts the puck in the net tonight, and that's what a coach buys.

The potential number is a projection of upside. The lineup card is a verdict on what a player is right now. Six months into the 2004 class, the coaches have cast their votes, and they aren't going with the numbers that say 92.
