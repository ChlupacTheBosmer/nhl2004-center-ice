The Pick Board: Who Actually Controls June, 121 Days Out

Detroit holds 6 picks and 2 firsts. Toronto and Phoenix hold the least, and they did it to themselves.

Pete LarocqueScouting editor · The Prospect FileMonday, 7 February 2005
ShareCopy link

The piece

The June draft is 121 days away, and the board I keep in public has stopped tracking only prospects. A pick you do not own on draft night is worth nothing, so this edition is about paper. Right now Detroit Red Wings controls more of it than anyone: 6 picks and 2 firsts, per the Bureau's Contract Ledger. That is the top of the asset table, and it came from trades.

The ledger lists every pick sitting with a club other than the one that earned it. Read the column marked holder, not the column marked original, and the picture changes.

Detroit owns the 2005 first round twice: its own, plus the one belonging to Toronto Maple Leafs. Washington Capitals holds Toronto's 2nd. Pittsburgh Penguins holds its 3rd. Florida Panthers holds a supplemental pick off Toronto as well. Four clubs picked Toronto's June clean, and Toronto is 86 points, the best record in the league. It is a good team that decided the present was worth more.

The ledger keeps Toronto Maple Leafs at 1 pick and 0 firsts. No other club in the league is below the 5-pick floor. One is, twice over.

The other is Phoenix Coyotes. It sits at 49 points, a bottom-of-the-table club that ought to be accumulating, and instead holds only 4 picks with 1 first, the fewest first-rounders of any team that has one. New York Rangers owns Phoenix's 2nd and 3rd. Phoenix got back a New York 4th, which is not an equal trade on any board I write. A bad team that trades away its mid-round futures is spending the one asset that tanking actually pays out, and spending it cheap. Phoenix has a 1st to draft with and not much else.

The other three clubs on 6 picks, Pittsburgh Penguins, Florida Panthers, Washington Capitals and New York Rangers, each hold 1 first. They did not sell the pick that matters. Detroit is the only club holding two, and it sits at 63 points, a real team adding to its capital rather than liquidating a rebuild.

Why this reads different from a standings story

A points table tells you who is good today. A pick table tells you who gets to be good in June, and a pick you have already given away is not a hedge. The 5 clubs with 6 picks include 4 that are at or near .500, hoarding through trades, plus Detroit with the second first. The clubs at the bottom of the standings, Calgary Flames at 42 and Minnesota Wild and St. Louis Blues and Chicago Blackhawks at 46, do not appear in the transfer ledger at all, which means they still own their own June. Those are the teams that will pick highest and keep every dollar of it.

So the June order and the June ownership are two different races. The best picks belong to clubs nobody traded them from. The most picks belong to clubs that traded other people's picks. Toronto sits in neither group, with 1 pick and a standing the rest of the league is chasing.

I will keep updating the board in public. The line I am watching is Phoenix: a 49-point team with 4 picks that keeps trading away rounds 2 and 3 is making the same mistake in reverse that a contender makes when it buys a rental, only Phoenix has nothing to win this year.
