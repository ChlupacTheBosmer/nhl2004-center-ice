The 59th Pick From 2000 Has 96 Speed, 94 Shot Power, a 97 Potential, and 8 Points in 40 Games

Ivan Huml's tools say top-six winger. Boston's lineup says 12.4 minutes. The gap is the story his contract has two years to answer.

Pete LarocqueScouting editor · The Prospect FileWednesday, 23 February 2005
ShareCopy link

Ivan Huml70 has 96 speed, 94 shot power, and a 97 potential grade in the Book. He has 8 points in 40 games at 12.4 minutes a night for Boston Bruins.

Boston drafted him 59th overall in 2000. He turns 24 in September. His contract pays him $0.60M and runs 2 more years. The Book has his tools graded higher than almost anyone under 25 on this roster. Whether a man playing bottom-six minutes can make a 97 potential mean something in the standings is the only question left.

The grades say one thing, the minutes say another

The Book's attribute page for Huml reads like a power forward's: 96 speed, 94 shot power, 82 passing, 75 puck handling. His checking is 60, his accuracy 65. The physical tools are elite; the hockey sense lags them by 30 points on speed alone.

That combination produces a player who can burn a defenseman in a foot race and then put the puck in the third row. Boston's coaching staff has decided that's a 12.4-minute winger, and with Joe Thornton85 at 91 overall, Sergei Samsonov81 at 87, and Glen Murray79 at 85 ahead of him on the left side, it's hard to argue with the lineup card.

Huml's Development Index is +3, which means his 76 overall reads three above his base. He's trending up in form. The Book disagrees on direction: his potential grade of 97 is 21 points above his current overall, the widest gap of any Bruin on the U-23 sheet.

The back cost him December

A back injury put Huml on the shelf from somewhere between December 4 and December 7, and he returned December 15. That's a window of roughly 2 weeks, maybe 3 games. In a 40-game sample at 8 points, those absences matter more than they look on paper. A man scoring at that rate needs every shift he can get to stay on a sheet.

Last season he played 65 games at 8.7 minutes a night and scored 5 points. This season's 12.4 minutes and 8 points in 40 is growth, but it's growth from a baseline so low that the slope still reads as a bottom-liner.

The contract is the clock

$0.60M with 2 years left means Huml is controlled through age 25. If the potential breaks, Boston gets 2 more seasons of top-six production at a price no contender can match on the open market. If it doesn't, he's a 25-year-old bottom-six winger on a dead deal.

Boston has 69 points, 2nd in the Northeast, 29 behind Toronto Maple Leafs. They aren't rebuilding. A 23-year-old with 96 speed either fits the top six or doesn't, and that call belongs to a coach trying to win now.

Morale is 92. He's fine. But 12.4 minutes a night for a 97-potential winger says Boston hasn't figured out what to do with him yet.
