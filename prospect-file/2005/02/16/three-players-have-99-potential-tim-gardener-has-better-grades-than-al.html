Three Players Have 99 Potential. Tim Gardener Has Better Grades Than All Three.

The Junior Bureau's potential column points three ways at the top of the 2005 board. The tool grades point one.

Pete LarocqueScouting editor · The Prospect FileWednesday, 16 February 2005
ShareCopy link

The Junior Bureau puts three players at 99 potential for the June draft: Ola Jardemyr, Petr Beier, and Petri Makiaho. Tim Gardener carries 98. Gardener beats all three in speed, accuracy, shot, passing, and checking. He ties Makiaho at 78 in puck handling. No 99 in the class has a grade above his in any of the six categories.

The potential grade measures how likely a player is to develop. A 99 says the Bureau believes that player's ceiling is higher and the odds of reaching it are greater than for a 98. The tool grades describe what a player can do today, on the page, in digits. A general manager holding the first pick has to decide which column to draft from. At the top of this class, those columns point in opposite directions.

What the six grades actually say

Jardemyr is a defenseman with a 50 passing grade. Born in 1986, he is the youngest player in the top four. His best mark is 71 in checking. His 51 accuracy means the puck does not land where he aims it more often than not. A club drafting him is betting that the 50 becomes a 65 and the 51 becomes a 65 in three or four years. Pete has seen a version of that bet in every draft for twenty years, and it does not land more often than it misses.

Beier has a 50 checking grade at left wing. Every prospect grade bottoms at 50 on the Bureau's scale, and Beier sits at the floor on the physical side. His puck handling at 72 is the only grade above 65. Speed, accuracy, shot, and passing all sit between 62 and 65.

Makiaho is the strongest of the three 99s. 78 puck handling, 71 passing, 69 speed, 69 accuracy, 68 shot. He is the only one of the three who looks like a first-line center on his current tool sheet. The 50 checking grade will worry a NHL general manager. He still trails Gardener in speed, accuracy, shot, passing, and checking.

Gardener: 75 speed, 78 accuracy, 73 shot, 74 passing, 78 puck handling, 83 checking. Born 1985. Jardemyr is younger than him by a year. The Bureau put him one spot behind the three 99s. Gardener is the player who does the most right now, and the gap between what he can do today and what the 99s can do today is not close.

The floor problem

This pattern does not stop at four players. Mirko Karg has 93 potential in the 2005 class. His grades read 51 speed, 52 accuracy, 50 shot, 63 passing, 50 puck handling, 50 checking. Four of six categories sit at the floor. His 63 passing is his only above-floor grade outside the low 50s. Wayne Gensel sits at 89 potential with five 50s and a 66 checking grade. The Bureau projects. A draft board has to decide whether you draft the projection or the player on the page.

Pete reads the tools. If a player has a 50 in four categories, the Bureau is telling you they like what he might become more than what he is. A draft board is a scouting document.

112 days

What the calendar has as the rookie draft falls June 8. Calgary Flames sits at 46 points, the lowest total in the league, with no first-round pick from 2000 to 2003 visible on an active roster in the current records. Calgary has the best odds of the top pick and the most at stake in a draft where the potential column and the tool grades disagree about who the best player is.

The Junior Bureau revises with every edition. These numbers will move before June. The question at 112 days is whether the gap between what the Bureau projects and what the tool grades show stays this wide when the board locks.

Pete's board: Gardener at 1. Makiaho at 2. Jardemyr at 3, because 12 months of age can move a 50 and he has them. Beier at 4.
