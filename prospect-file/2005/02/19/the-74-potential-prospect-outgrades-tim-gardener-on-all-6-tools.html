The 74-Potential Prospect Outgrades Tim Gardener on All 6 Tools

The Bureau's potential column and its tool grades rank the top of the 2005 class in close to opposite order

Pete LarocqueScouting editor · The Prospect FileSaturday, 19 February 2005
ShareCopy link

Mika Tirkkonen has a potential grade of 74 on the Junior Bureau's 2005 board. Tim Gardener has a 98. Tirkkonen's tool grades read higher than Gardener's on speed, accuracy, shot power, passing, hands and checking. All six.

ToolTirkkonenGardener
Speed7675
Accuracy8378
Shot power7473
Passing7974
Hands8378
Checking8483

The widest gap is accuracy, 83 to 78. The narrowest is shot power, 74 to 73, but Tirkkonen still wins it. He is a left winger, born 1985. The Bureau's potential column drops him below a player he tops on every attribute the tool grades measure.

I saw him in the fall. The tape backs every number on that line. I do not know what the potential column weighs that the tools do not. It is probably frame, or skating stride mechanics that the speed grade flattens, or the quality of competition. Whatever it is, the model is discounting what the scouts put on the page after watching him.

The 99s do not have the tools either

Andreas Vorobjev, center, born 1986, potential 79. Speed 80, accuracy 76, shot power 79, passing 78, hands 85, checking 56.

He outgrades Petr Beier on all six tools. Petri Makiaho on all six. Ola Jardemyr on five of six, losing only checking, where Jardemyr reads 71 and Vorobjev reads 56.

The board reads: Beier 99, Makiaho 99, Jardemyr 99, Vorobjev 79. The tools read: Vorobjev is better on every attribute that Beier and Makiaho carry, and better than Jardemyr on all but one.

That is the same pattern one layer up. The Feb 16 board showed Gardener's tools beat all three 99 names. Now Tirkkonen's tools beat Gardener's. The ranking the tools produce runs almost exactly opposite the one the potential column produces, all the way down from the top.

The single-tool cases

Stéphane Laperriere has the highest shot power grade on the board. 87. The next closest at that attribute reads 79. His potential is 73.

Jussi Nurminen reads 87 in checking. His potential is 74.

The two players with the most dominant single attribute in the 2005 class have potential grades in the low 70s. The Bureau looked at their tape, gave them elite marks in one area, and then dropped them below the fold in its forecast.

Where my board moves

I draft players, not forecasts. The tool grades tell me what the scouts saw on tape. The potential column is a projection of where a player lands in the league, and projections miss. Every year some of them do.

Tirkkonen sits inside my top 12. Vorobjev sits inside my top 20. The potential column says those kids should land later. I have made this call before and I have been wrong before, and I will make it again because the tool grades are the one column the Bureau publishes that comes off a man's skates and onto a page.

If you trust the potential column, the 2005 draft runs 99, 99, 99, 98 and then falls to the mid-90s and the high 80s. If you trust the tools, Tirkkonen and Vorobjev belong in the conversation at picks 4 through 12 and the board reshuffles.

The Junior Bureau revises its class rankings before the draft. 109 days out. I want to see what the next edition does with these two.
