The 2004 First Round Finished Its Season. The Lowest-Potential Picks Outscored the Highest.

Three first-rounders graded below 57 potential combined for 81 points. The four highest-graded skaters managed 29 points.

Pete LarocqueScouting editor · The Prospect FileTuesday, 17 May 2005
ShareCopy link

Picks 3, 9, and 10 of the 2004 draft produced 81 points. Picks 1, 4, 12, and 19 produced 29. The Bureau graded the first group at 51, 56, and 52 potential. It graded the second at 91, 92, 94, and 96.

Karel Duba75 scored 15 points in 54 games for Los Angeles Kings at 10.1 minutes a night. Tuomo Pihlman76 scored 34 in 73 for Minnesota Wild at 12.7. Tuomas Lydman77 scored 32 in 76 for Mighty Ducks of Anaheim at 18.1. The three carry the lowest potential grades in the entire first round. They also logged the most games and the most points of any group of 2004 first-round skaters.

On the other side of the board: Andreas Stahle76, 96 potential, played 4 games for Toronto Maple Leafs and recorded 0 points. Roger Nylander68, 94 potential, played 18 for San Jose Sharks and had 7 points. Mika Sulku70, 92 potential, played 40 for Calgary Flames and had 5. Vaclav Spacek81, the 1st pick, 91 potential, played 50 for Toronto Maple Leafs and had 17.

The coaches voted differently than the Bureau

The relationship between potential and playing time in the 2004 class runs backward. I sorted every first-round skater by the Bureau's potential grade and tracked games played. The top five by potential played 4, 18, 40, 50, and 20 games. The bottom five played 54, 76, 1, 73, and 6. The players the Bureau projected to develop furthest got the least ice, and the players it projected to stay where they are got the most.

That doesn't make the Bureau wrong. Potential measures development likelihood, not current readiness. Duba at 74 overall and 51 potential reads like a finished product: a solid bottom-six winger who scored 9 goals and won't score 20. Stahle at 71 overall and 96 potential reads like a raw defenseman with room to grow. The Bureau described these players. The coaches decided who dressed.

Who's at fault here

The coaches at Minnesota Wild, Los Angeles Kings, and Mighty Ducks of Anaheim put 19-to-21-year-olds on their lines and got production. Minnesota Wild finished 79 points. Los Angeles Kings finished 79. Mighty Ducks of Anaheim finished 97 and reached what the calendar has as the conference finals. The coaches at Toronto Maple Leafs finished 133 points and the coaches at San Jose Sharks finished 76. Toronto Maple Leafs didn't need a 19-year-old center. San Jose Sharks didn't trust a 20-year-old winger at 11.3 minutes.

Spacek getting 50 games at Toronto Maple Leafs was reasonable usage for a Cup contender. Stahle getting 4 was not. Toronto Maple Leafs drafted those two players one round apart in the same draft and played one in a bottom-six role for half a season and the other in four games. Toronto Maple Leafs now holds 1 total pick in the 2005 draft and 0 first-rounders. The 2004 class and the players already in the system are the future. The future can't develop in 4 games.

The Bureau gave Duba a 51 because it believes he is what he is. It gave Stahle a 96 because it believes he could become something. Duba scored 15 points and Stahle sat in the press box. A 96 potential that doesn't get on the ice is a number. A 51 potential that produces 15 points is a player.

The 22-day window

The 2005 draft is 22 days away. St. Louis Blues holds 8 picks and 2 first-rounders. Detroit Red Wings holds 6 picks and 2 firsts. Washington Capitals holds 7 and 1. Toronto Maple Leafs holds 1. If Toronto Maple Leafs loses the conference finals series to Washington Capitals on 2005-05-18, the front office walks into the draft with nothing but its own late-second-round pick and a first-rounder it can't play.

The 2004 class is about to enter year two. Pihlman turned 21 in September. Lydman turned 21 in February. They're not projects anymore. They're 21-year-olds with a full season in their legs and a 52 or 56 potential next to their name. The Bureau will revise those numbers in June. If a player puts up 40 points at 21, the potential grade moves up, whatever the Bureau said before the season started.

The ones sitting on the bench, Stahle at 96, Nylander at 94, Sulku at 92, are the ones whose grades will hold or drop based on whether they dress next October. A potential number means nothing at 4 games. It means nothing at 18. The coaches decide what a prospect is, not the Bureau.
