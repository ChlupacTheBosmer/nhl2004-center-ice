LA Found a 97-Potential Player at #49. It Found a 51 at #3.

Two second-rounders are +6 on the Index. The 2004 first-rounder has the lowest ceiling grade in the entire class.

Pete LarocqueScouting editor · The Prospect FileSaturday, 21 May 2005
ShareCopy link

Mike Cammalleri87 was the 49th pick of the 2001 draft. He posted 60 points in 61 games for Los Angeles Kings in 2004-05, carries a 97 potential grade in the Book, and sits +6 on the Bureau's Development Index. Karel Duba81 was the 3rd pick of 2004. He finished with 15 points in 54 games, carries a 51 potential grade, and sits 0 on the Index.

Both play center ice for the same club.

LA finished 79 points, fifth in the Pacific. The club's three highest-graded under-23 players by potential are Cammalleri at 97, Alexander Frolov79 at 95, and Jared Aulin77 at 94. Aulin went #47 in 2000. Frolov went #20 in 2000. Cammalleri went #49 in 2001. None of them was a first-round pick.

The first-rounder they do have from recent years is Duba, and his 51 potential is the lowest ceiling grade on record for any player drafted in the first round of 2004. The next lowest are Tuomas Lydman77 at 52 and Topi Lehto69 at 52. Duba's grade sits below both.

The Index tells the story the draft card doesn't

Two LA players finished the season on the Bureau's Development Index risers list. Cammalleri at +6, overall 83 on a base of 79. Aulin at +6, overall 75 on a base of 71. Two second-rounders from different drafts, both climbing, both with potential grades above 90.

That's how LA builds: find them late, develop them. The club's roster also carries Cristobal Huet, a goaltender taken #214 in 2001, and Lubomir Visnovsky, a defenseman taken #118 in 2000. The first round is where the pattern breaks.

Frolov went #20 in 2000, first round, and his 95 potential says the Bureau thought he was special. He scored 55 points in 62 games at 22. He also carries a +3 on the Index, which means the form is there. He's the only first-round pick LA took in the 2000s that worked. Duba, the one they drafted after him, is the floor of the class.

No first-rounder in June

LA holds 4 picks in the 2005 draft and 0 first-rounders. STL holds LA's first. The Blues already have the most draft capital in the league and a development record I wrote about Thursday: 8 picks, not a single under-23 above 70 overall. Now they hold another first.

For LA, the approach is the one that has worked: rounds 2 through 7. The scouting department has produced three under-23s with potential above 90 from picks 20, 47, and 49. It has also produced Duba at #3, a player the Bureau now grades as having less upside than any other first-rounder from 2004.

I called Cammalleri for 55 points this season. The settlement table says 20. The final line says 61 games, 60 points. I'll take the miss on the record. The player scored 60.

Duba played 54 games as a 20-year-old and produced 15 points at 10.1 minutes a night. His potential grade, 51, sits below the Bureau's floor for a professional hockey player. He has a job, he's getting ice time, and the organization that drafted him 3rd overall in a class that contained Spacek and Sulku and Stahle will have to decide what to do with him before he's old enough to leave on his own.

The draft is 18 days away. LA will fill its 4 slots, probably in the middle rounds, and probably find something useful. That is what it does.
