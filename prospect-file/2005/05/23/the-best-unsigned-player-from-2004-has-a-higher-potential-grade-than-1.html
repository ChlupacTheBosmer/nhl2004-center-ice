The Best Unsigned Player From 2004 Has a Higher Potential Grade Than 11 First-Rounders

19 picks from last June never signed. Six carry potential above 70 on the Book. The draft is 16 days away and nobody is calling them.

Pete LarocqueScouting editor · The Prospect FileMonday, 23 May 2005
ShareCopy link

The piece

The 2004 draft has 50 picks on record. Nineteen of those men never signed a contract. That is 38% of the class, the largest forgotten pool in the league right now, and the rookie draft is 16 days away.

Six of the 19 carry potential grades above 70 on the Bureau's Book. The best is Yuri Semenov, a left winger drafted 69th overall, graded at 78 potential. The 10th man picked in that draft, Tuomas Lydman77, sits at 52.

That gap of 26 grades, running the wrong direction, is not unusual for this class. The unsigned pool has players the Bureau rates above 11 first-round skaters who are currently dressing in the league.

Semenov is graded 78. Kloucek was picked 8th, 61 picks before Semenov, and carries 67 potential. The club that took Kloucek used its first-round asset on a player the Bureau rates 11 grades below a man taken in the third round of the same draft. And then the club that took Semenov in the third round let him walk.

What unsigned means

A player on the draft record with no club, listed as unsigned, is a player whose drafting organization either never made an offer or made one he refused. Either way, the organization spent the pick and kept nothing. The player becomes free of that club's control entirely. He is still 19 or 20 or 21. He is still on the Book. He is simply nobody's project anymore.

The Bureau does not distinguish between a prospect who is in a system and one who was drafted and released. Both carry a potential grade on the 50-99 scale. Yuri Semenov at 78 potential is graded the same whether his club is developing him or his name sits in no building.

Pekka Seikola was picked 40th, a second-rounder, with 74 potential. He is the same age as the 6th and 8th picks of that draft (both 20) and carries a higher potential than either. He has played zero games for anyone.

Yves Bertrand, at 77 potential and pick 67, is graded above nine first-round skaters from the same class, including the 1st overall (Spacek, 91), the 12th (Nylander, 94), the 4th (Sulku, 92), and the 14th (Prommersberger, 85). Wait, no, he is not above those four. He is above Kloucek (67), Olvestad (60), Lydman (52), Pihlman (56), Timander (66), Salminen (63), Duba (51), Stashan (62), Salei (63), Vas (65), and Lehto (52).

What it means for June

The Junior Bureau's class has four players at 95+ potential: Ola Jardemyr, Petr Beier, Petri Makiaho at 99, Tim Gardener at 98. After Gardener, the class drops to Leo Atonovich at 96, then Mirko Karg at 93. The depth is top-heavy.

A team selecting in the 20th through 30th range has a choice in June. It can reach into a class where the Bureau's next available name (Marcel Madar at 89) is still a step below what the 7th pick should reach. Or it can sign Yuri Semenov, Yves Bertrand, or Pekka Seikola tomorrow, for nothing, on a contract of the player's choosing. Those three carry 78, 77, and 74 potential respectively. They are older than any 2005 draft-eligible prospect by a full year. They are already in the Book.

This is not a talent argument against the 2005 class. It is a roster-construction argument about what a club is willing to do between the draft and the first day of camp. Signing an unsigned player from last June costs a contract and nothing else. It moves zero picks and costs no asset. The player is available because his former club did not want him enough to make a phone call.

Nineteen picks is 38% of last summer's draft, gone. The six with 70+ potential are still graded, still young, still eligible. The draft is on June 8. The phone lines open before that.
