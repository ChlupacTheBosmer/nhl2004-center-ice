St. Louis Has 8 Picks in June. Its 8th-Overall Pick Has 3 Points.

The Blues hold the most draft capital in the league. They have also failed to develop a single under-23 into a player the Bureau grades above 70.

Pete LarocqueScouting editor · The Prospect FileThursday, 19 May 2005
ShareCopy link

St. Louis Blues holds 8 picks in the 2005 draft. Two of them are first-rounders: their own and the one they took from Los Angeles Kings in a trade that landed them the deepest war chest in the league. The draft is 20 days away. The question is what 8 picks buy a club that just proved it cannot develop a first-rounder.

Vladimir Kloucek69 went 8th overall in 2004. He is 19. He played 70 games this season for St. Louis, the most games any 2004 first-rounder appeared in. He scored 2 goals and 3 points. His overall is 70. His potential grade is 67, which places him below nearly every name the Junior Bureau has ranked for the 2005 class. Kloucek logged 13.4 minutes a night and went invisible.

The pattern is bigger than one bad rookie season. Among all clubs in the league, St. Louis fields no under-23 the Bureau rates above 70 overall. The Development Index has no St. Louis name on its riser list. The Book's top 40 under-23s carries no Blues player. The roster that finished 26-35-8 for 86 points, fourth in the Central and 23 points out of a playoff spot, is built entirely on men whose best seasons are behind them, with one 19-year-old center as the sole young regular. He scored 3 points.

Compare the 2004 first round to Kloucek's cohort. Tuomo Pihlman78 went 9th and scored 34 points in 73 games for Minnesota Wild. Tuomas Lydman73 went 10th and put up 32 points in 76 games for Mighty Ducks of Anaheim. Vaclav Spacek81 went 1st and had 17 points in 50 games for Toronto Maple Leafs. Kloucek played more games than any of them and produced less than all of them.

The draft board is the only asset the Blues built.

St. Louis Blues's 8 picks come from a pattern of trades. They hold Los Angeles Kings's first-rounder, Calgary Flames's second-rounder, and Vancouver Canucks's fourth-rounder on top of their own five. The Blues traded established bodies to accumulate picks, a common path for a mid-table club looking to get younger. But accumulation without development is just a slow way to liquidate the roster. Every round the Blues pick, they spend a resource that other clubs convert into roster players. Toronto Maple Leafs has 9 under-23s graded at 70 or above. Tampa Bay Lightning has 3. Minnesota Wild has 2. St. Louis has none above 70.

The 2005 draft class has four prospects above 95 potential according to the Junior Bureau: Ola Jardemyr, Petr Beier, Petri Makiaho, and Tim Gardener. If the Blues hold on to both firsts, they could take two of those four. Good outcome, except that it means two more players entering a system that just managed 3 points from the 8th pick of a top-heavy class.

The right half of that table (potential) tells you what the Bureau thought. The middle columns tell you what actually happened. Kloucek's 67 potential put him in the bottom third of the class, and he scored 3 points anyway. The picks that produced real offense (Pihlman at 9, Lydman at 10) came from clubs that gave them 73 and 76 games respectively. St. Louis Blues gave Kloucek 70 games and 13.4 minutes a night. He earned that ice time. He did not repay it.

St. Louis traded bodies for picks through 2004-05 and built the deepest draft board in the league. The 2004 first round told them what happens when a top-10 pick lands on a roster with no development infrastructure around him. The question for June is whether the Blues draft for the ceiling or the floor. Eight picks suggest they trust the process. Three points suggest the process does not exist yet.
