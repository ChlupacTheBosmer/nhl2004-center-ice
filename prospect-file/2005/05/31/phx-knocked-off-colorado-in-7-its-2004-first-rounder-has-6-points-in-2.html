PHX Knocked Off Colorado in 7. Its 2004 First-Rounder Has 6 Points in 28 Games.

The draft is 8 days away. The Coyotes hold 4 picks, traded away their 2nd and 3rd, and their development group tops out at 77 overall.

Pete LarocqueScouting editor · The Prospect FileTuesday, 31 May 2005
ShareCopy link

Phoenix Coyotes beat Colorado Avalanche 4 games to 3 in the Western first round as the 8th seed, then lost to Dallas Stars 4-2 in Round 2. They went 40-31-9 for 93 points and knocked off the 112-point top seed in 7.

It did that with Mike Sillinger (88 overall), Sean Burke (88), Shane Doan (86), and Ladislav Nagy (86). The hot-stove desk called it on 2005-03-16: "PHX trades none of Burke, Sillinger or Doan before what the calendar has as the trade deadline." That call is settled, a hit. PHX rode its veterans in and out of the playoffs.

The problem starts with what comes next. PHX has 5 players on its roster who entered the league in the 2000 or 2004 drafts. The best overall among them belongs to Levente Szuper, a goaltender, 77, drafted 116th overall in 2000. The best scorer is Jeff Taffe76, 75 overall, 11 points in 82 games. Their 2004 first-rounder, Yves Mckay70, recorded 2 goals and 6 points in 28 games at 7.9 minutes a night. Their 2004 second-rounder, Tobias Hellkvist67, never suited up.

PHX spent $47.81M for 93 points this season. That puts them mid-pack in salary and 13th in points among the 30 clubs. Colorado Avalanche spent $71.56M for 112. Toronto Maple Leafs spent $60.04M for 133. PHX sits well below both in spending and well behind both in production. The 8th seed won on structure.

The club's financial margin reads $2.44M between revenue and payroll. Revenue is $43.44M. Attendance is 14,549 per game. Ticket price is $79. PHX cannot simply write a bigger check next October.

Four Picks and a 2nd Already Gone

The Coyotes hold 4 picks in June with 1 first-round selection. Their 2nd and 3rd belong to New York Rangers, listed on the pick-transfer ledger as PHX originals held by New York. They will draft once in the first round and maybe once or twice after, depending on how far the first goes.

The 2005 class opens with 3 prospects at 99 potential and 1 at 98, per the Junior Bureau's class ranking. PHX's pick position, at 14th in the standings (93 points), puts them roughly 9th or 10th overall depending on playoff compensation. That is late enough to miss all four top prospects. The next available player with 96 potential, Leo Atonovich (LW, born 1985), carries a 50 in accuracy and a 50 in checking. The next after that, Mirko Karg (RW, 1986), sits at 93 potential with three attributes at the 50 floor: speed, accuracy, and puck handling.

PHX does not have the draft capital to gamble on a projection with holes at the floor. With 4 picks and zero 2nd- or 3rd-rounders to trade for movement, their GM has to pick well in Round 1 and hope Rounds 4 and 5 produce bodies who sign.

What the Veterans Leave Behind

Sillinger is 34. Burke is 40. Doan turns 28 in October. Nagy is 26. The core that knocked off Colorado carries an average age north of 32. PHX has no internal replacement at center, no projected top-pair defenseman under 25, and no goaltender under 25 with a potential grade above 72.

Krystofer Kolanos, the 2000 first-rounder at #19, played 31 games and managed 4 points. That is 6 seasons of development and a 72 overall. He scored at a 0.13 pace in the league. The same 2000 draft produced Marian Gaborik99 at #3 and Dany Heatley90 at #2. PHX passed on both.

Jeff Taffe played all 82 and scored 11 points. A 75 overall at 25, drafted 30th in 2000 by the same club that watched Scott Hartnell84 go 6th and Ron Hainsey77 go 13th. Taffe is their best young center by overall and by production. That tells you where the pipeline sits.

Mckay at 19, the #15 pick last June, is the only player on this list whose potential grade (77) suggests the club drafted for tomorrow. But 7.9 minutes in 28 games, 6 points, 2 goals. The Bureau's Book grades him 69 overall. He played 28 games, then went scratched. There is no trajectory to read in a sample that small.

Hellkvist never played. Potential 72, overall 63, a goaltender buried on the depth chart behind a 40-year-old. He was the 45th pick in 2004. He is 19.

The Question for June 8

PHX won 40 games on $47.81M while Colorado Avalanche spent $71.56M for fewer wins and an earlier exit. The Coyotes won Round 1 in 7 games because their goaltending held and their top line outscored the opposition in close ones. This is a survival model, and it runs out when Doan, Sillinger, and Nagy move on.

The draft is 8 days away. The Coyotes hold 4 picks, their best player from the last 6 drafts rates 77 overall, and their financial margin is $2.44M. PHX needs to find someone in Round 1 who grades above 85 potential and who fills a position that does not currently exist on this roster. With one shot at it and no trade assets to move back into the top 5, the margin for error in June is about as thin as it gets in this league.
