I Saw Him When: Komisarek Went From 3 Points to 82 in One Season

A 7th-overall pick who managed 3 points in 24 games last year just led all defensemen. He costs $1.10M and becomes a free agent in one year.

Pete LarocqueScouting editor · The Prospect FileWednesday, 25 May 2005
ShareCopy link

I had Mike Komisarek82 on a board in 2001 when Montreal Canadiens took him 7th overall. I had him on a board again in January 2005, when he had 3 points in 24 games and 11.8 minutes a night. I stopped writing about him in February. He scored 79 more points after that.

The final line reads 82 games, 15 goals, 67 assists, 82 points, 30.4 minutes a night. Chris Pronger finished with 82 points. Sergei Gonchar had 81. Komisarek tied the Norris winner for the lead among defensemen, on a team that won 21 games.

The jump nobody predicted

Last season, Komisarek played 24 games. He recorded 1 goal, 2 assists, 6 penalty minutes. The Bureau's Book had him at a base grade in the low 70s. His potential was 92, but the gap between 92 potential and 72 production looked like the kind that takes four years to close.

This season he logged 82 games at 30.4 minutes a night. The Development Index has him at +6, base 77, overall 82 today. His checking grade is 92, speed 89, aggression 88. The physical tools were always there. What changed is that someone put him on the first pair for a full season and he responded with the most productive year by a defenseman in the league.

The contract is $1.10M. It has 1 year left. MTL finished 66 points, dead last in the Northeast, and it will need to pay a player at market value for the first time in his career while it is still trying to figure out what it is.

The numbers against the field

Komisarek's 67 assists led all defensemen. His 82 points against the top blueliners:

Komisarek is the youngest man on that list by two years. He is also the cheapest. Pronger's deal is in its final years of a long-term contract. Gonchar, Lidstrom, and Desjardins are all on multi-year money that reflects their track records. Komisarek is on $1.10M because his rookie-scale deal has one year left and nobody at MTL has signed an extension yet.

What the Bureau sees

The Book's potential grade is 92. That number is the Bureau's read on how likely he is to reach his ceiling, not the ceiling itself. The base grade sits at 77. The Index has added 6. So the current overall of 82 is the Bureau saying: this man is performing six grades above where we thought he was in the summer, and we still think there is 10 grades of room above that.

For context, the only under-23 player with a higher base grade plus the Index boost is Marian Gaborik99 of Toronto Maple Leafs, whose base is 91 and who sits at 98 today with +6. Komisarek's 77 plus 6 puts him at 82. The gap between those two is 16 grades. The gap between where Komisarek was in October and where he finished in April is 10 grades, and the Bureau hasn't fully priced it in yet.

The room behind him

MTL has Jose Theodore89 at 91 overall, Dwayne Roloson84 at 86, Richard Zednik82 at 85, Andrei Markov at 81, Chad Kilger77 at 81, and Craig Rivet75 at 80. That is not a terrible roster. It produced 21 wins. The problem was 66 points spread across a group that could not find a way to win close games (6 overtime losses, 46 regulation losses).

Komisarek thrived in that chaos. At 30.4 minutes a night against the top pairing minutes the team could ask of anyone, he produced at a Norris-caliber pace. The question for June, and then for July, is whether MTL can pay him. One year left on a $1.10M deal for a man who just scored 82 points as a 23-year-old defenseman. The salary cap prices what a player is today, and the Bureau's 92 gets weighed against that. The next GM in Montreal decides how.
