The 99-Potential Prospects Have Floors at 50. The 98 Doesn't.

Gardener's worst attribute is 73. Every 99 has a hole at the absolute floor. The draft is 10 days away and some teams need October help.

Pete LarocqueScouting editor · The Prospect FileSunday, 29 May 2005
ShareCopy link

The piece

The Junior Bureau's 2005 class has four names above 95 potential. Three of them sit at 99. The fourth, Tim Gardener, sits at 98. On the potential scale, the gap is one grade. On the current attribute grades, the gap is enormous.

I've been tracking these four since the Bureau opened its rankings this spring. The potential numbers tell you who might become a star. The per-attribute grades tell you who could dress for an NHL game in October, and right now those point to different names.

Here are the four, attribute by attribute:

Jardemyr has a 50 on passing. Beier has a 50 on checking. Makiaho has a 50 on checking. All three sit at the absolute floor of the scale in at least one attribute. Gardener's lowest number is 73, on shot power. His highest is 83, on checking.

The potential numbers go 99, 99, 99, 98. The floors go 50, 50, 50, 73. The ranking inverts.

Who needs help in October

This matters because of who's picking early. The clubs with the thinnest under-23 cores have the least margin for a prospect who needs two or three years of seasoning before he can handle a top-six role.

Detroit has one under-23 on its roster: Niklas Pesonen62, a 61-overall forward who managed 5 points in 27 games this season. The Red Wings hold 6 picks in June, including 2 first-rounders. They were swept 4-0 by Dallas Stars in the first round. Their roster is men whose best seasons are finished, and their only young player grades below 62.

Vancouver has one under-23 at the same average level. Colorado, which won 112 points and lost to the 8th seed in round 1, has two under-23s averaging 64.5. None of these clubs can dress a prospect who passes at 50 or checks at 50 and expect him to survive a 48-game stretch.

The readiness case

Gardener's lowest attribute is 73. That's a number a 19-year-old carries into his first NHL training camp and survives. The other three have a 50 somewhere, which is the Bureau telling you this player still gets separated from the puck along the boards or can't make a breakout pass under pressure.

Makiaho's puck control at 78 and passing at 71 are genuinely good. His 50 on checking is not survivable at the center position in a 7-point series. These are real grades, not a data error.

Beier's 50 on checking is the same problem on the wing. Less structural, but a winger who can't hold the zone or win a board battle is a healthy scratch waiting to happen against any of the 15 teams that play physically.

Jardemyr's 50 on passing concerns me most for a defenseman. Checking you can coach. Size develops. You cannot coach a defenseman who can't make the first breakout pass under forecheck pressure out of his own zone. That's a system problem, and it's a long fix.

What this means on June 8

If I'm Detroit with 2 firsts and a roster that needs bodies in October, Gardener goes first. If the team picking ahead of me takes him, the second choice depends on whether I'm rebuilding (take Makiaho, develop the puck-handling, fix the checking over 2 years) or I'm trying to compete now (I take no one at 99, I take Gardener if he's there, and if he's gone I trade down and reload for next year when the class might be better).

The Bureau will tell you these three 99s have the highest potential in the class. I believe it. But potential is a 7-year projection. I'm drafting for a roster that lost 4-0 this spring and has one player under 23 who grades above 61.

The 98 is worth more than the 99s right now.
