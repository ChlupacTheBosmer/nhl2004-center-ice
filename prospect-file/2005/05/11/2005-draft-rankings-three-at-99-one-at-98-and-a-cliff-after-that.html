2005 Draft Rankings: Three at 99, One at 98, and a Cliff After That

The Junior Bureau's class has four prospects above 95 potential and then drops eight grades by the sixth name.

Pete LarocqueScouting editor · The Prospect FileWednesday, 11 May 2005
ShareCopy link

The rookie draft is 28 days out. The Junior Bureau's class rankings carry four prospects above 95 potential: Ola Jardemyr, Petr Beier, Petri Makiaho at 99, and Tim Gardener at 98. The fifth name, Leo Atonovich, sits at 96. The sixth, Mirko Karg, drops to 93. Below that, the class thins fast. Fourteen of the Bureau's top 40 sit at 79 or below.

That gap between 98 and 96 is where the June conversation starts. Three men carry the same potential grade, and two of them are centers. Petr Beier and Petri Makiaho both scored 99 from the Bureau. Makiaho is the higher-end scorer of the two at the junior level, carrying 78 puck management and 71 passing on the Bureau's per-attribute sheet. Beier's 72 puck management is within six points of him, but his 65 passing and 50 checking make him a wing project.

Ola Jardemyr is a defenseman, born 1986, and carries 71 checking. A defenseman with 99 potential and above-60 checking is what you take first if your rebuilding team needs a puck-mover who can also finish a hit. His 50 passing, though, is the number that keeps me awake. The Bureau gave him 61 speed and 51 accuracy, and 50 passing. Those are the grades of a man the Book sees as a future top-pairing stay-at-home with a 99 ceiling. That ceiling might be a projection artifact.

I distrust a big defensive frame with low skill grades. I've been distrusting it since the first time I saw a 6'4" defenseman with 50 passing projected above a 5'10" forward with 78 puck management. The Book's potential grade measures how likely development is, not what the path looks like.

The rest of the top ten

Tim Gardener at 98 is the safest bet in the class. 78 accuracy, 78 puck management, 83 checking on a winger born 1985. There's no hole in his per-attribute line. If your team needs a goal scorer who can also hold the blue line clean on a penalty kill, Gardener is the name.

Leo Atonovich at 96 has the widest spread I've seen at that potential grade: 83 checking, 63 puck management, 50 accuracy. That's a power forward whose hands might never catch up to his body. The potential is there. The per-attribute sheet says he has to develop three things, not one.

Mirko Karg at 93 has 50 speed, 52 accuracy, 50 puck management, and 50 checking. I keep going back to him because the 93 potential sits on a foundation where four of six attributes are at the Bureau's floor.

The late-birthday question

Makiaho and Beier are both born 1985. That makes them eligible to be 19 or 20 at the draft, depending on the birthday. The Bureau's per-attribute grades reflect what they've done against younger competition. If either carries a December or January birthday, those grades face older men next season and might dip. Draft them as high as you want, but expect their pro numbers to be ugly before they're pretty.

Jardemyr is 1986. He'd be 18 or 19 at the draft. His 71 checking against younger boys is the number that suggests his body is already ahead of the competition. His 50 passing is the number that suggests his hands aren't.

Where the class ends

From rank 11 onward, the Bureau's potential grades flatten. Leonard Greulich and Sami Sundblad both carry 82. Karel Strbak, 82. A defenseman, a center, and a goaltender at the same potential grade with 62, 56, and 50 checking respectively. The class is deep enough to fill a roster with 90+ potentials. It is not deep enough to fill the top six with any one of them. The per-attribute sheets make that case more sharply than the potential figures do.
