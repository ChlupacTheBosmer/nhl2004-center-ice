The 2004 Draft Spent 4 First-Round Picks on Goalies. They Have Played 5 Games.

Marcel Janota, Torsten Schmitt, Mathieu Landry, and Leo Lopatin went 7th, 17th, 22nd, and 23rd. Janota appeared once. Schmitt never suited up. Landry appeared once. Lopatin appeared three times.

Pete LarocqueScouting editor · The Prospect FileWednesday, 11 May 2005
ShareCopy link

The 2004 draft used 4 first-round picks on goaltenders. Between them they played 5 regular-season games. None of the four is dressing tonight for a club still in the playoffs.

That's 4 of 28 recorded first-round selections. One in seven. The Bureau gave them 89, 69, 63, and 68 potential respectively, and the league gave them 5 starts between them across 82-game seasons.

The one with a future

Marcel Janota has the only potential grade in this group that matters. At 89, he sits in the Bureau's second tier, below the 97-potential names the File already tracks, but above the 80-range where most of the league's backup goaltenders live. He played 60 minutes in 1 game. He is 21. He is scratched for a club that lost 4-0 to Washington in Round 1 and went home.

The three without one

Torsten Schmitt never played. Zero games. He sat the entire 2004-05 season in Chicago for a club that finished 78 points and missed the field. His potential is 69, the lowest of any goaltender the Bureau bothered to rank in the top 100 of the class.

Mathieu Landry appeared for 96 minutes in a single start for Florida. The Panthers finished 76 points and lost 11 in a row to close the season. His potential is 63. Florida just lost its 11th straight and starts next season trying to develop him or give up on him.

Leo Lopatin played 3 games at 45.3 minutes a night for Washington. He is scratched behind Olaf Kolzig87 and Josh Harding74, both of whom dress. Lopatin is 19. His potential is 68. He will be 20 at the start of next season and behind two goaltenders, one of whom just posted a 1.00 GAA in 2 playoff wins.

The league has this problem in every dressing room

The 2004 class is not an outlier. Three goaltenders under 23 carry 97 potential in the Book. Maxime Ouellet82 played 13 games. Ray Emery80 played 3. Dan Blackburn79 played 3. The league gave 19 starts to three men the Bureau rates as the most likely goaltenders to develop among anyone under 24.

Ouellet is 23, blocked by Roberto Luongo94 at Toronto Maple Leafs. Emery is 22, blocked by a healthy starter in Ottawa. Blackburn is 21, blocked in New York. None of them is bad. All of them are waiting.

The 2004 goaltenders compound this. The draft took 4 more young bodies at the position, placed them on clubs with established starters or established mediocrity, and the season ended with 5 appearances logged.

What the 2005 class adds

The Junior Bureau ranks 2 goaltenders in its top 40 for June. Sami Sundblad carries 82 potential, born 1986. Karel Zizka carries 73 potential, born 1985. Neither grade is the kind that forces a club to clear a spot.

If the 2005 draft follows the same pattern, the number of first-round goaltenders on NHL rosters without regular starts grows again. The four men from 2004 will enter next season with a combined 5 games of experience, an average age of 20, and zero paths to ice time at their current clubs.
