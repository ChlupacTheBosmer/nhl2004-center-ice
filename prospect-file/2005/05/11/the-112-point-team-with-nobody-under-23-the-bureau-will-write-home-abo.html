The 112-Point Team With Nobody Under 23 the Bureau Will Write Home About

Colorado finished first in the West. It has zero players in the Bureau's top 40 under-23. The rebuild starts now, with 1 first-round pick and a roster of men whose best seasons are behind them.

Pete LarocqueScouting editor · The Prospect FileWednesday, 11 May 2005
ShareCopy link

Colorado Avalanche scored 112 points, won the Northwest by 13, and lost in the first round to Phoenix Coyotes in 7 games. The Colorado roster has no player under 23 who appears in the Bureau's top 40 by overall grade. The team finished second in the overall standings, 21 points behind Toronto Maple Leafs, and carries no young asset the scouting department can build a sentence around.

I checked the U23 list three times. The Bureau ranks 40 men by their current overall. The clubs represented are TOR (7), FLA (4), LA (2), MTL (2), ATL (1), CBJ (1), NYI (1), DAL (1), NSH (2), PHI (2), MIN (2), SJ (1), BUF (1), TB (1), CAR (1), WSH (1), CHI (1), BOS (1), PIT (1), ANA (1). Colorado does not appear.

A front office traded for a window instead of building one. The 2004 draft gives Colorado Yury Stashan69 at #26, overall 65, potential 62, 0 games played. Marcel Ondruska at #56 in the second round: overall 64, potential 74, 36 games, 0 goals, 7 assists. Those are the two best things the Colorado developmental pipeline has produced in the last two drafts that appear anywhere near a roster, and neither would crack a top-line role in this league.

What they have left

The pick table shows Colorado holds 5 picks and 1 first in June. That's the middle of the pack. No extra first, no borrowed second from a rebuild trade. Just what a 43-23-6 team earns.

Phoenix, Vancouver, and Detroit share the same problem: a roster that produced a 90+ point season without a single under-23 the Bureau considers among the 40 most valuable young players in the league. Dallas at least has Jason Bacashihua87, 22, 83 overall, 90 potential. One game played this year, but he's there.

The 7-game series against no future

Phoenix Coyotes beat Colorado in the first round in 7. The Coyotes carried a 40-31-9 regular season and won the series anyway. Colorado's scoring this spring: no Colorado player appears in the playoff scoring lines the desk has published for round 1. The names I can pull from the broader record for this series: the playoff journey section lists no individual Colorado scorers.

The depth carried them. Depth doesn't carry you twice.

The window and the cost

Colorado's roster is built for a window that closed. The Presidents' Trophy went to Dallas Stars, 109 points, and Dallas is in the Western Conference Finals with Marty Turco93 in net. Colorado, with 3 more points, is home. The difference: Dallas had Bacashihua at 83 overall on a 1-game sample. Colorado had nobody.

The draft is 28 days away. Colorado will hold its 1 first, probably somewhere around 20th-25th overall given the standings. The 2004 class's #26, Stashan, went 0 games. That's the precedent for what Colorado does with mid-first-round capital: it sits.

The Bureau's top 40 U23 list has 1 goaltender from New York Rangers with 97 potential and 3 games. It has Ray Emery82 from Ottawa Senators with 97 potential and 3 games. It has 14 names from Toronto Maple Leafs alone across all categories. Colorado could trade every remaining asset on its roster, take the worst pick available in June, and still not have what Toronto already has in its system. That took four years of competing without building underneath.

Their next season begins 145 days from now. The window is either open or it isn't, and the Bureau's U23 top 40 says it shut while they were scoring 310 goals.
