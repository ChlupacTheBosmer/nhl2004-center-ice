The Potential 99 Club Plays 34 Games at Tampa Bay and Scores 3 Points

The Book's maximum grade sits on two Lightning centers who cannot stay on the ice. The same grade is printed on three Toronto players who can barely stay in the lineup either.

Pete LarocqueScouting editor · The Prospect FileThursday, 17 November 2005
ShareCopy link

Five players under 24 carry the Bureau's highest potential grade: 99. They play for two clubs. At Tampa Bay Lightning, the two men with that number are both centers, both 23 years old, and between them they have 3 points in 34 games.

Alexander Svitov73 has 2 points in 17 games at 9.0 minutes a night. Brian Sutherby73 has 1 point in 17 games at 9.1 minutes a night. The Development Index has Svitov at +4 on his base grade of 73. Sutherby sits at +1 above his base of 76. Both land at 77 overall today. Both carry a 99 potential, which is the probability the Bureau prints for a player whose current grade is not his ceiling.

Svitov was the 3rd pick of the 2001 draft. He is 6 feet 3 inches, 198 pounds, from Omsk. His Book grades are extreme: balance 94, speed 94, acceleration 93, agility 93. The bottom of his page reads fight 50, endurance 55, hockey IQ 56. He is one of the fastest skaters on the Lightning roster and the least engaged player on it. The $1.00M deal runs through this season.

Sutherby went 26th in the 2000 first round, one year before Svitov went third. He is a year younger than his draft slot suggests (born in 1982, so 23 as of November 1). His form is +1 on the Index, barely above his base. 1 point in 17 games tells you he is filling a roster spot, not playing a role.

The Tampa Bay core around them is good: Vincent Lecavalier86 at 90 overall, Martin St. Louis84 at 88, Nikolai Khabibulin89 at 91. Those players carry the Lightning's 6-4-5 record. The two centers with the highest potential grades in the building are sitting fourth-line minutes and contributing nothing.

Three more sit at Toronto with the same number

Steve Eminger82, a 2002 first-rounder (12th overall), is the best of the three at 82 overall with +5 on the Index. He plays 19.9 minutes a night but has only 2 points in 7 games since returning to the lineup. Scottie Upshall78, 6th overall in 2002, has 3 points in 9 games at 5.1 minutes a night. His Index form is +1. Carlo Colaiacovo76, 17th overall in 2001, has 2 points in 18 games at 12.5 minutes, form +3.

The symmetry is ugly: each club has 34 player-games from its 99-potential men. Toronto's three produced 7 points on those games. Tampa Bay's two produced 3. Neither total justifies the grade the Book is printing.

The number is a probability, not a prediction

The Bureau's potential figure reads as the likelihood that a player's overall grade continues to rise toward a ceiling. A 99 means the Book is almost certain this player has more in him. It does not say the more is coming soon, or this year, or before the contract expires.

The players who separate from their current grades at this stage of the season share one trait the 99 club does not: minutes. Petr Chlup87, undrafted, 19 years old at Toronto, has a potential of 95 and carries 15 points in 18 games at 20.8 minutes a night. He is 87 overall, +4 on the Index, and getting the ice that makes development happen.

Svitov turns 23 in November. He is in the last year of a 1-year deal at $1.00M. If he does not crack the Lightning's top six, the club has a choice between paying a 99-potential center at a raise or losing a 77-grade player at a reasonable price. The Development Index says he is climbing. The 9.0 minutes a night say the coach does not believe it yet.

Sutherby's 26th overall pick was the last first-rounder in the 2000 draft's first round. Five years later, the 3rd, 6th, 8th, 12th, and 17th picks from that same first round (Svitov, Upshall, Leclaire, DiPietro, and Colaiacovo respectively) carry higher overall grades or more ice time. Sutherby sits at 77 with a form of +1 and 9.1 minutes, the same grade and minutes as Svitov, two picks above him on the draft board.
