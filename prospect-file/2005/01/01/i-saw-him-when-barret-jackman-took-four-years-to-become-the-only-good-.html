I Saw Him When: Barret Jackman Took Four Years to Become the Only Good Player in St. Louis

The 1999 17th pick is up 5 on the Development Index at 83 overall. The 2004 8th pick has 1 point in 27 games and a potential figure of 67.

Pete LarocqueScouting editor · The Prospect FileSaturday, 1 January 2005
ShareCopy link

The piece

Barret Jackman88 has 22 points in 37 games at 31.3 minutes a night for St. Louis Blues, the highest minutes-per-game figure among all under-23 defensemen in the league, and he sits 5 spots above his base on the Bureau's Development Index. At 23, he was the 17th man off the board in 1999, a kid from Winkler, Manitoba who took four full seasons to become the player his scouting report promised in June.

Pete Larocque saw him in the fall of 1999, a rangy blue-liner who could hold the puck on his tape for three seconds and then make a clean read across the width of the zone. He wasn't the fastest. He wasn't the strongest. He did something with his head up that made other people look like they were playing in a phone booth. I wrote that down in a notebook I still own, in a shorthand I can barely read, and I have been waiting four seasons for the league to agree.

Now the Index has him at +5 and his overall reads 83, with a potential figure of 89. That last number is the one I care about. Eighty-nine is the distance between where he is and where the model thinks he can still go. Six points of room on a 23-year-old defenseman logging 31.3 minutes leaves the door open.

The 8th pick in the same room has a potential figure of 67

Vladimir Kloucek78 went 8th overall in June. He is 19, has played 27 games, logged 11.3 minutes a night, and put up 0 goals and 1 point. His overall reads 70, his form is flat, and his potential figure is 67. That is the Bureau telling you this player is not going to be much more than what he is right now, at 27 games and 19 years old. The 8th man off the board in a draft where the top three picks all have potential figures above 90. The gap between those numbers is the whole reason I keep this draft board in public: I want to be wrong in front of people.

STL picked Kloucek with the 8th selection and Karel Kraft at 57 in the second round. Kraft is 19, scratched, 61 overall, potential 59. Two picks, one season, and the sum of their league production is 1 point.

I do not know what the room told them in June. I know what the numbers say now. An 8th-overall pick with a 67 potential figure and 1 point at 19 is a draft miss that nobody wants to name in December because it means the whole scouting department has to explain itself in March.

One under-23 player on the board

Jackman is the only STL player on the under-23 list. The league publishes 30 teams' worth of young talent in that table, and St. Louis enters exactly one name into it. The closest team by record, Chicago Blackhawks, has Steve McCarthy81 at 75 overall and +4 on the Index, plus Igor Radulov76 at +5 form. Chicago is 9-20-6 and they at least have a body on the page. St. Louis is 8-21-3, on a five-game losing streak, 121 goals for and 165 against, and the entire under-23 story is a single defenseman having a career year because there is no one else to carry 31.3 minutes.

What Jackman's season actually is

Twenty-two points in 37 games at 31.3 minutes a night are top-pair numbers on a bottom-five team. His overall of 83 makes him the 8th-best under-23 defenseman on the list. His 89 potential, if it materializes, makes him a Norris-grade option. The Index has him moving. The team around him is not.

STL's 5-11 home record tells you something. A defenseman who logs 31.3 minutes at 23, whose team gives up the most goals against in the Central division, is eating a workload that should be split across three or four younger bodies. There aren't any.

Jackman's contract status is not on my sheet, so I will not guess at it. What I will say is this: the 1999 draft sent him 17th, and it took four years and a full season as a top-pair minutes-eater for the development model to acknowledge what was visible in the fall of 1999 on a cold rink in Manitoba. I saw him when. It took the Bureau four years to catch up. The 8th pick in 2004 has a potential figure of 67 and 1 point in 27 games. The model looked at him in the summer and already knew.
