The 19th Pick Has a 97 Potential and Zero Games

Andreas Stahle carries the highest potential figure in the 2004 first round. Columbus has not put him on the ice once.

Pete LarocqueScouting editor · The Prospect FileSaturday, 22 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Andreas Stahle69 has the highest potential figure of any 2004 first-rounder on a league roster. The Book says 97. He has played 0 games.

That number puts him above Roger Nylander65 (94, 6 games for San Jose Sharks), above Tuomo Pihlman69 (92, 39 games for Minnesota Wild), above every other first-round pick from last June who made a roster. The Bureau's projection says this kid could reach an elite level. The coach has given him 0 minutes across 47 games.

The Blue Jackets give up 148 goals in 47 games on a blue line that leans on veterans. Sydor, Yushkevich, and Smehlik are past their primes and still taking regular shifts.

Stahle's current grades read PENA 79, AGGR 76, PASS 75, SHPW 75, TOUG 75, ENDU 74. He is 75 inches, 212 pounds, shoots right. The weak marks are DEKG 51, FACE 51, FIGH 51. Those are a 20-year-old's numbers from the Junior Bureau's pre-draft evaluation. He has a passing grade above most of the men he is waiting behind.

What the potential figure means

A 97 potential on a 67 overall is what the Bureau thinks is left to develop. The Development Index has him at 0. The Index measures form, and form requires ice time. He is invisible to it.

He has a $0.97M contract with 3 years remaining on a rookie deal. Columbus has him under team control for the duration. The organization does not need to rush him into a bottom-pairing role on a 17-21-2 team. The coach would say the structure works without a 20-year-old, and he'd be right. The Blue Jackets are 50 points, third in the Central, and winning games with the veterans. Kalinin at 78 anchors the top pair. Spacek and Tallinder at 74 are solid middle-pairing men.

What the argument costs

The 97 potential was set before he turned 20, by evaluators watching him in junior. Every year without NHL ice time turns the projection into guesswork. The Bureau gave him enormous headroom. Each season he watches from the bench eats into it.

CBJ is a middle-of-the-pack team with no path to the top of the Central and no reason to think the veterans in front of Stahle will produce better results next year. The defense gets younger only if someone plays the kid.

He has morale at 100. He is 20 years old and earning $0.97M to watch hockey from the bench. The 19th pick from last June carries the highest ceiling the Bureau projected onto that draft. Columbus has 137 days until the draft, 56 until the trade deadline, and the kid has 0 games. I saw him when he was a prospect. I have not seen him since.
