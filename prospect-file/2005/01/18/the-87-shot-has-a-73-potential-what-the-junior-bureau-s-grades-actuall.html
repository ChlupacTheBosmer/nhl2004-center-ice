The 87 Shot Has a 73 Potential: What the Junior Bureau's Grades Actually Say About June

The best individual attributes in the 2005 class belong to the kids the Bureau rates lowest. The three 99s cannot match them.

Pete LarocqueScouting editor · The Prospect FileTuesday, 18 January 2005
ShareCopy link

The piece

The best single attribute the Junior Bureau has published for the 2005 draft class reads 87. Stéphane Laperriere's shot power sits there, at the top of a 40-man board. His potential figure is 73. The three players the Bureau has at 99 carry shot power grades of 68, 62 and 59.

Potential figures measure the room the Bureau thinks remains. The attributes measure what stands in front of you. In June, a team with a top-10 pick decides which number it is betting on.

Three kids the Bureau has capped

Laperriere is a right winger born in 1986. He will be 18 on draft day, the young end of the class. Shot power 87, speed 81, puck handling 80, passing 78. Checking 50.

Andreas Vorobjev, a center also born in 1986, carries the highest single attribute in the entire top 40: puck handling at 85. Speed 80, shot power 79, passing 78. Checking 56. His potential sits at 79.

Mika Tirkkonen is a left wing, 1985. Four attributes at 76 or above: speed 76, accuracy 83, puck handling 83, checking 84. Potential: 74.

These three sit at 79, 73 and 74. Nobody above them on the potential board carries a puck-handling grade higher than 78. Nobody carries a shot power higher than 73. Laperriere and Vorobjev, at the bottom of the top tier, have the best tools the Junior Bureau can see right now.

The 99s have room

Petri Makiaho, one of three at 99 potential, has puck handling at 78, passing at 71, speed at 69. Petr Beier's puck handling is 72, his checking 50. Ola Jardemyr has speed at 61, accuracy at 51, passing at 50.

The Bureau believes these three can outgrow their current attributes. A 99 means it sees a lot of room to grow. Whether a team's development staff and system can get there is a different question, and the Bureau does not answer it.

Tim Gardener, at 98 potential, sits between the two groups. Speed 75, accuracy 78, shot power 73, passing 74, puck handling 78, checking 83. No single number spikes the way Laperriere's 87 or Vorobjev's 85 do, but nothing falls below 73. He is the most complete profile in the top 10 by attribute spread, and still 21 potential points above where Laperriere sits.

What the potential figure does not mean

The Bureau's potential is the likelihood of development. A 79 on Vorobjev does not mean he tops out at 79 overall. It means the Bureau reads his current attributes as close to where they will land. An 85 puck handling at 18 is already an 85. The forecast is that it becomes an 88, maybe a 90. Jardemyr's 63 puck handling at 18 is a 63 today. The forecast is 90.

Both are estimates. One measures distance already covered, the other the distance remaining.

I have looked hard at the gap between Vorobjev's 85 puck handling and his 56 checking. Twenty-nine points. The 1986 birth year puts him at 18 in June, which is on the right side of the age window. The puck handling is there, I can see it. The checking is where I worry. A kid with 85 hands and a 56 check has a path to the league if the compete level arrives. I have also seen the 18-year-old with 80 hands who never adds the rest, plays parts of four seasons, and is gone. The Bureau's 79 is a caution.

The June question

A team in a window drafting in the mid-teens might want Tirkkonen. He has 83 accuracy and 84 checking today. His speed is 76, not 81, but the rest of the profile says he contributes immediately.

A team in a full rebuild picking in the top 5 might take Makiaho at 99 potential and 69 speed. He may not be NHL-ready for two seasons.

The 2004 first round showed what the other path looks like. Kristian Timander73 went 2nd overall in June. He sits at 70 overall today with a potential of 66, below his current overall, which means the Bureau says there is nothing left to develop. He has 17 points in 27 games at 16.6 minutes a night for the New York Islanders and is scratched.

Eight months ago.
