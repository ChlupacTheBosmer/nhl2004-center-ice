The 2003 Calder Winner Has 88 Speed and 30.9 Minutes in a 14-Win Season

Barret Jackman lost last year to a shoulder. The Index has him 5 above his base, and St. Louis has him at $0.70M with 3 years left.

Pete LarocqueScouting editor · The Prospect FileMonday, 31 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Barret Jackman86 is up 5 on the Bureau's Development Index at 23 years old, playing 30.9 minutes a night, and carrying the 14-26-4 St. Louis Blues Blues off the blue line. His overall is 77. His potential reads 90. He is on $0.70M with 3 years of team control left.

That profile sits on the same line as the players the File has been tracking all January: a high-potential young player getting minutes the standings don't explain. Except Jackman has something none of the others carry: the Calder Trophy. He won it in 2003, narrowly beating Rick Nash85, a 22-year-old defenseman from Trail, British Columbia with a plus-23 rating and the second-most ice time among rookies.

The background record says he missed most of the 2003-04 season with a chronic dislocated shoulder. 15 games. The Calder winner barely played, and the conversation ended.

This season it hasn't. Jackman has played 50 games, scored 25 points, and logged 30.9 minutes a night. The per-attribute grades explain why a 14-win team gives him that much: 88 speed, 86 shot power, 85 checking, 84 balance, 84 toughness, 83 agility. Those are the numbers of a top-pairing defenseman on any team in the league. His faceoff is 50, his fight is 53, but nobody asks a 6'2", 200-pound defenseman to center a faceoff.

The minutes place him among the league's busiest young defensemen

The under-23 blue line in this league has 7 players with meaningful ice time. Here they are sorted by points:

Komisarek is his own case: 59 points from the blue line, a number that belongs on a first-pairing center. Behind him, Jackman and Dennis Seidenberg are tied at 25 points. Jackman plays 30.9 minutes to Seidenberg's 18.9. Jay Bouwmeester logs 31.8 minutes, the most of any young defenseman in the league, and carries 19 points for Florida. Jackman's 25 points in 50 games on 30.9 minutes is the production a 17th-overall pick should be delivering by year five.

The next time St. Louis faces a decision about Jackman, he will be 26 and free of all constraints. The $0.70M contract buys them 3 more years of control at a price the market will not match. That is the asset the Blues have and haven't talked about, sitting at 30.9 minutes a night while the rest of the division ignores them.

The Index has a read

The +5 jump means the Bureau has his form at 5 points above the Book's base. The base is 80. His current display reads 77, which means the overall is below the base even with the Index boost. The Bureau sees form running ahead of the pre-injury base. The base hasn't moved.

What that usually means: a kid came back from injury, stayed healthy, logged heavy minutes against top competition, and performed at a level the pre-injury base can't yet account for. The overall catches up.

Jackman's morale is 98. He's 23. STL has 44 points and allows 202 goals in 50 games. None of those things change his contract status or his minutes, but they change the urgency of what comes next.

The 1999 draft gave St. Louis the 17th pick. It took 6 years and a dislocated shoulder before the man it selected started looking like a top-pairing defenseman. The Index has him 5 points above where the model thinks his base should be. At 23, with a 90 potential and $0.70M left on his deal, the File has seen this shape before in other men. Either the club re-signs him at market and gets a franchise defenseman, or they don't and the rest of the league finds out.
