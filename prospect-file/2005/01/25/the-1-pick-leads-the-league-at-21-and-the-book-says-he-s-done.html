The #1 Pick Leads the League at 21 and the Book Says He's Done

Ilya Kovalchuk carries the league's best point total and a potential figure 7 below his overall grade. He is 21 years old.

Pete LarocqueScouting editor · The Prospect FileTuesday, 25 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Ilya Kovalchuk92 has 87 points in 48 games for the Atlanta Thrashers Thrashers. The Bureau's Book has him at 92 overall today, up 5 on the Development Index. His potential sits at 85.

The potential is below the overall. The Book thinks the 1st pick from 2001 is already better than he will ever be again. He is 21.

Ilya Kovalchuk arrived in Atlanta with a 134-point season in 2003-04, 72 goals in 82 games, a Rocket shared with Jarome Iginla. This year he is putting up 41 goals and 46 assists through 48 games at 20.3 minutes a night. That pace is the whole argument against his own grade.

The contradiction at the top of the under-23s

The Bureau's potential figure measures the likelihood of development from today's base. Every other player in the under-23 top 40 carries a potential above his overall. The Book grades what a young player is and the potential grades what he might become. Kovalchuk breaks the frame. His potential of 85 is 7 points below his current 92, and 4 below his base of 89.

Gaborik is the only other under-23 whose potential trails his overall, and his gap is 6 with a different story: Gaborik is 22, already carries a 96, and the Book is simply saying the ceiling is real but close. Kovalchuk's gap carries the weight of his age. He is younger than Gaborik and further into the negative.

What the attribute grades say

The Book gives Kovalchuk four 99s: speed, acceleration, agility, shooting power. His defensive grade sits at 96. His checking is 50. His fighting is 50. Those are the numbers of a player the Bureau has already finished painting.

He shoots right, stands 6'4" at 202 pounds, and has played every game Atlanta has scheduled. No injuries on record, no time lost. Morale 92. On a $1.10M contract with 1 year remaining. If the Book is right and the potential is the ceiling, then Atlanta has a 21-year-old scoring center's worth of production on a bargain contract and the only remaining question is whether he can stay here.

Atlanta is 15-25-2

The Thrashers sit 44 points in the Southeast, 23 points behind Tampa Bay. Their two first-round picks from 2000 and 2001 have 167 points between them. Dany Heatley91, the 2nd pick from 2000, carries a 90 overall and has 80 points in 45 games. Together they form the most productive forward pair in the league on the division's worst team.

Atlanta also owns the 6th-most picks across five rounds (6, including 1 first) and holds Toronto's first from the 2004-05 season. The 1st pick from 2001 is scoring at a clip that should make the Book revise him upward, and instead the potential figure says no. He is what he is, 92 today, and the Book would rather bet against improvement than against a kid putting up 87 points in 48 games at 21.

I saw him when the Thrashers took him first overall in 2001, the first Russian to go there. He scored 29 as a rookie, missed 17 games with a shoulder, came back to put up 67 the next year, then 134. The trajectory was not up anymore. The Bureau had him figured before he figured himself out. The question this season poses is whether the Book grades a player's body and forgets the hands. Four 99s should mean something to a potential number, even at 21, even with a 50 checking grade and a 50 fight.

The record says he is already there. The record also says he is getting better. Both cannot be true in the Bureau's arithmetic.
