The 8th Pick Has a 90 Speed and a 52 Check

Pierre-Marc Bouchard leads the 2002 first round in points among forwards who aren't the 1st pick. His body grades explain why he plays 11.5 minutes.

Pete LarocqueScouting editor · The Prospect FileTuesday, 25 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Pierre-Marc Bouchard77 leads the five players from the 2002 first round still wearing a league sweater in points this season, among forwards not named Rick Nash83. Twenty-one points in 49 games at 11.5 minutes a night for Minnesota Wild, a 16-25-3 club. The Development Index has him at +6, tied with Marian Gaborik98 and Mike Cammalleri83 for the highest form reading among under-23s.

He is the lowest-potential 2002 first-rounder on the Board. The Book puts him at 87. Nash is 95. Jay Bouwmeester78, 95. Scottie Upshall79 and Steve Eminger77, 99.

The hands are better than the pick

The Bureau's attribute grades put Bouchard above Nash in 3 of 4 core skill categories. Nash holds a 1-point edge in speed: 91 to 90. Bouchard is 5 points ahead in deking, 16 in passing, 11 in puck control.

A 16-point gap in passing between the 1st pick and the 8th is the widest grade spread in that draft. Nash carries 30 points in 36 games and 12.9 minutes. Bouchard carries 21 in 49 and 11.5. The per-game numbers sit close enough that the skill profile suggests Bouchard should be producing more per shift than the player taken 7 spots ahead of him.

Bouchard's base grade is 74. The +6 on the Index lifts him to 77, still below Nash's 81. The Index is the form term, not a ceiling. A +6 means the Bureau is seeing something in the current games that the base grade hasn't caught up to yet. Five other under-23s sit at +6: Gaborik, Cammalleri, Mike Komisarek82, Vaclav Nedorost78, Jared Aulin75. Gaborik's base is 91, Bouchard's is 74. Same +6, different starting point.

The floor grades explain the minutes

Bouchard's FIGH reads 50. TOUG 50. CHKG 52. Those are the bottom of the 50-99 scale. The Bureau prints 50 on the same attributes for 16-year-olds in the Junior Bureau who have never been through the middle of the ice. A coach reading a center with 88 deking and 50 toughness is looking at a player he can dress for 11 minutes on a checking wing or a sheltered center shift, and not for 18 minutes driving play against a top pairing.

Minnesota Wild lines him up third, behind Wes Walz84 at 84 overall and Michael Nylander80 at 78. Walz brings a different body: 95 speed, 86 deking, 81 puck control, and grades high enough in the physical categories that the coach trusts him along the half-wall. The coach is building around what the grades say a player can absorb, and that's 11.5 minutes.

A year left on the deal

He turns 21 on April 27. The contract reads $1.20M with 1 year remaining, which puts him in restricted free agency after the 2005-06 season. A breakout year and the $1.20M becomes a number the Wild cannot hold. A quiet year and the 87 potential sits in the same file it occupies now.

The passing and puck-control grades at 88 say the ice will open for him if the coach lets him carry the play. The 50 and the 52 say the coach is not going to do that before the body catches the hands. Whether the minutes follow the form is a question the next 33 games answer, and nobody in Minnesota seems inclined to ask it.
