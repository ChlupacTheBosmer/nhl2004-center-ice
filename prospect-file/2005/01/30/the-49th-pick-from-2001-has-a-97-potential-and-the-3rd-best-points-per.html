The 49th Pick From 2001 Has a 97 Potential and the 3rd-Best Points Per Game in the Class

Mike Cammalleri scores at 1.10 a night and sits behind only Kovalchuk and Komisarek in the 2001 draft class. Twenty-eight first-rounders trail him.

Pete LarocqueScouting editor · The Prospect FileSunday, 30 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Mike Cammalleri83 was picked 49th overall in the 2001 draft, 19 spots into the second round. He is up 6 on the Bureau's Development Index, carries a 97 potential on the Book, and sits 3rd in the entire 2001 class by points per game at 1.10. Only Ilya Kovalchuk92 at the 1st pick and Mike Komisarek80 at the 7th score at a higher rate. The other 28 first-rounders from that class trail him.

Mike Cammalleri has 33 points in 30 games for Los Angeles Kings at 21.6 minutes a night on a $1.10M contract with one year left. Last season he had 20 points in 69 games at 0.29 points a game and 11.8 minutes. The Index has tracked the jump from 79 base to 83.

The grades say what the line already told you

His strongest attributes are DEKG at 93, PASS at 93, PUCK at 90, AGIL at 89 and SPEE at 89. His checking sits at 65, his fighting at 50. The Book's 83 overall is the gap between his hands and his body. The 97 potential means the Bureau thinks the hands carry the rest.

Not the full first round, but enough to make the point. Spezza at No. 2 has 0.41. Svitov at No. 3 has 0.09. Weiss at No. 4 has 0.17. Cammalleri's 1.10 is more than double Spezza's rate and twelve times Svitov's.

Richmond Hill to Ann Arbor to the second round

Cammalleri grew up in Richmond Hill, Ontario. He chose the University of Michigan over the OHL to keep college eligibility. At 15 he was the OPJHL rookie of the year with the Bramalea Blues. At Michigan he led the team in goals twice and reached the Frozen Four in 2001. The Kings took him 49th that June. He came back for another college season because the fit wasn't right, and came to Los Angeles with a full degree and a second-round badge.

What the 97 potential costs LA

Cammalleri's contract has one year left at $1.10M. The Index has him at +6, the highest riser on the sheet for a skater from the 2001 class after Kovalchuk. His 33 points in 30 games on that salary is the cheapest production in the draft class. Kovalchuk earns more, and so does Komisarek.

LA is 14-24-6, 5th in the Pacific division, 48 points in 51 games. The team is not going anywhere this season. But the 49th pick from 2001 is on a pace that makes him the best value in the class, and the Book has him at 97 potential with 83 as his current grade. The desk that drafted him in the second round got a first-round player, and might have gotten the steal of the draft outside of Kovalchuk.
