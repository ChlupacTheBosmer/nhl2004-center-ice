The 208th Pick Has More Points Than 30 First-Rounders From 2000 to 2003

Brandon Reid is 5'8", 165 pounds, and carries a 99 speed on a $0.60M contract. The Book has him at 77 with 94 potential and nobody in Vancouver is talking about it.

Pete LarocqueScouting editor · The Prospect FileWednesday, 19 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Brandon Reid75 [1211] has 47 points in 46 games for Vancouver Canucks. That is more than 30 first-rounders from the 2000, 2001, and 2002 drafts currently on league rosters. He was picked 208th in 2000.

The four who have more points from that group are Ilya Kovalchuk92 (82), Dany Heatley90 (75), Marian Gaborik96 (69), and Mike Komisarek80 (56). Reid sits fifth. He is not a first-rounder. He is not a second-rounder. He is the 28th pick of the 7th round, taken at a position where scouts barely have time to write a paragraph.

What the Book says about the 208th pick

Reid carries a 99 speed and a 97 acceleration. Those are the 2nd-highest speed and the highest acceleration among the 40 best under-23s by overall in the league. His 94 agility sits behind only Kovalchuk's line among skaters in that group. His overall is 77, up 1 on the Development Index, with a potential figure of 94. The Book sees room.

The rest of the page tells you what he is not. Checking 50. Fighting 50. Toughness 50. He is 68 inches, 165 pounds, shoots right. He has 2 penalty minutes in 46 games. You do not build a power forward out of these numbers. You build something else.

The minutes tell the real story

Reid plays 14.0 minutes a night. He has 47 points in those 14.0 minutes. Vancouver is 25-13-5, 61 points in 46 games, sitting atop the Northwest. Todd Bertuzzi93 [68] carries a 95 overall, Markus Naslund89 [701] sits at 91. Reid is not the best player on this team. He is the best value on it.

His contract reads $0.60M with 1 year remaining. That is what the 208th pick costs. The average first-rounder from 2000 or 2001 on a league roster today is producing fewer points and costing more. I do not have their contracts, so I will leave the dollar comparison there and let you find your own.

The question is July. What the calendar has as free agency closing falls 190 days from today. Reid turns 24 in March. A 23-year-old with 47 points, a 94 potential, and a 1-year deal at $0.60M will not sign for $0.60M again. Vancouver's cap situation is not in this brief, but the league's Collective Bargaining Agreement has a restricted free agency mechanism that gives them control. They will exercise it.

The draft-position column is a fiction

The 208th pick has outscored Raffi Torres70 (15 points), Scott Hartnell79 (15), Vaclav Nedorost77 (12), Stanislav Chistov80 (12), Scottie Upshall77 (17), and 25 other first-rounders from three drafts. Not one of them was picked lower than 30th overall.

I wrote on January 18 that the best individual attributes in the 2005 class belong to kids the Bureau rates lowest. Reid is the same story wearing a league sweater. The 99 speed is real. The 47 points are real. The 14.0 minutes are what a coach gives a man who weighs 165 pounds and has a 50 in every physical attribute the game still values on the fourth line.

VAN plays Edmonton Oilers on Wednesday. Bertuzzi, Naslund, and Brendan Morrison82 will get the coverage. Reid will get 14.0 minutes and a 2-pointer or two and nobody will mention his name in the postgame. That is fine. He is up 1 on the Index and the Book has him 17 points of development above where he sits. I will be watching when the potential catches up to the speed.
