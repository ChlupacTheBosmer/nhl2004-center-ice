2005 Mock Draft 1.0: Three 99s and the Grades That Don't Match

The Junior Bureau's top potential figure belongs to three players whose current attributes tell three different stories

Pete LarocqueScouting editor · The Prospect FileMonday, 10 January 2005
ShareCopy link

The piece

Three prospects for the June draft carry a potential figure of 99. None of them, taken by the numbers the Junior Bureau publishes alongside it, has the current tools to look like the 98-rated player right behind them.

Petri Makiaho is a centre born in 1985. His potential is 99. His best current grade is a 78 in puck handling, and his worst is a 50 in checking. Petr Beier, a left winger also born in 1985, carries the same 99 and his best grade is 72 in puck handling. Ola Jardemyr is a defenceman born in 1986; his best grade is a 71 in checking and his worst is a 50 in passing.

Then there is Tim Gardener. He is rated 98 potential and his lowest grade is 73, in shot power. His best is 83 in checking. Every one of his six current attributes sits above 72. Each of the three 99s carries at least one grade at 50.

The gap between the number and the player

The potential column tells you what the Bureau thinks a man might become. The grades tell you what he is right now. Those two things rarely line up, and the draft question is which one you believe.

Makiaho, Beier and Jardemyr have potential figures that say 99 and grade profiles that include a 50 at checking or passing. Gardener's potential is one point lower but his floor is a full 23 points above the floor any of them has posted.

The late-birthday edge

Jardemyr is the only 1986 birth year among the four. In a draft where the 2004 first-rounders already in the league are 19 and 20, a player turning 19 in the spring carries more development runway than a player who turned 20 last summer. The Bureau factors that into the potential column, and it may be part of why Jardemyr's 99 is attached to a grades profile that looks more raw than either 1985 forward's.

That does not make him the safest pick. It makes him the one whose number is most likely about a body that has not finished filling out yet.

If the standings held

St. Louis sits at 31 points. Chicago and Montreal and Calgary sit at 32. Atlanta, Minnesota and Phoenix sit at 36. If the draft order fell where these records sit today:

1. STL takes Petri Makiaho. They have Barret Jackman83 on defence carrying the load and the 2004 8th pick at 1 point in 30 games. A centre with a 78 in puck handling, even one with a checking grade of 50, is the highest-ceiling player available.

2. CHI takes Tim Gardener. Chicago has scored 134 goals in 41 games, the lowest total in the league outside St. Louis. Gardener is the most NHL-ready forward on the board. His SHPW of 73 and ACCU of 78 say a player who can contribute earlier than the 99s can.

3. MTL takes Petr Beier. Mike Komisarek76 is up 6 on the Development Index at 76 overall with 49 points from the blue line. Montreal needs a top-six forward to go with him. Beier's 72 in puck handling is not Gardener's 78 but it is the second-highest skill grade in the 1985 group.

4. CGY takes Ola Jardemyr. Calgary has scored 130 goals in 41 games, second-last in the league. A defenceman with a 71 in checking and the youngest birth year in the top four projects as the longest build in the group. The 99 says he is worth the wait. The grades say the wait will be long.

What I am telling myself

If I had the first pick I would take Makiaho for the ceiling and accept the checking. If I had the second I would take Gardener because his grades are not a projection, they are a current profile, and the gap between what he is and what he can become is the smallest of any player in this class. That is the one that lets me sleep after June.
