The 2005 Draft's Best Player Has a 99 and No Sweater

Petr Beier went #8 in June. The Junior Bureau grades him at 99 potential. Eight days into the season, he is unsigned.

Pete LarocqueScouting editor · The Prospect FileWednesday, 12 October 2005
ShareCopy link

The piece

Petr Beier has a 99 potential grade from the Junior Bureau and no contract. He was selected 8th overall in June. As of October 12, he is not on any roster in this league.

The Junior Bureau gives out two 99-potential grades in the 2005 class. Beier is one. The other is Petri Makiaho66, a center taken 89th overall by New Jersey Devils. Makiaho's current overall sits at 64. He has not appeared in a game line this season.

The class has 17 first-rounders on record. The highest overall among them belongs to Francois Anderson77, a goaltender from Buffalo Sabres taken 11th. Anderson carries a 79 overall and a 59 potential. He is the closest thing the class has to a finished product, and the Junior Bureau does not expect him to be much more than what he already is.

That 40-point gap between Anderson's potential and Beier's is where the whole draft sits. The Bureau saw 2 first-rounders with a realistic shot at all-star play. One never signed. The other (Leo Atonovich61, #13, Atlanta Thrashers) carries a 61 overall and has not logged a shift.

The unsigned pool is deeper than one name

Beier is not alone. Six players from the 2005 class are unsigned as of this edition: Marcel Ballo (#3, 62 potential), Beier (#8, 99 potential), Leo Glebov (#38, 74 potential), Martin Hobson (#53, 62 potential), Marcus Liv (#84, 69 potential), and Marcel Smrek (#123, 85 potential). Two carry potential above 80. If the Bureau's grades are real, the league is leaving a top-3 prospect and a top-8 prospect on the free-agent market in October.

I wrote in May about the 2004 unsigned class, which had 19 players never signed and six carrying potential above 70. That pool was wide. This one is narrow but deeper: two players above 85 potential, including the single highest-graded man in the draft.

Makiaho is the counterfactual

New Jersey Devils took Makiaho 89th overall. The Junior Bureau had him at 99 potential, tied with Beier. That is an 81-pick gap between two men with the same ceiling grade, and the reason has nothing to do with talent: Makiaho signed. He is on a roster, 20 years old, born 1986, same birth year as Beier. He played 0 games through the first week of the season. NJ's center depth chart kept him scratched.

The difference between Beier at 99-and-unsigned and Makiaho at 99-and-64-overall is a signed contract. Access to development, or nothing.

What the class has done in 8 days

The 2005 class has produced exactly 1 player in the under-23 top 40 by overall: Anderson at BUF. No other first-rounder from this class appears in that table. Tim Gardener74 at #5 is the only 2005 first-rounder to appear in a lineup: 1 game, 0 points, 16.0 minutes for Toronto.

Beyond the first round, 2 players carry overalls above 70: Mika Tirkkonen75 at #134 (R5, New York Islanders, 75 overall) and Jussi Nurminen69 at #102 (R4, New York Rangers, 71 overall). Both are scratches. Neither has played.

The question nobody on a GM's staff is answering

A 99 potential grade means the Junior Bureau believes this player will develop into a 99-overall. Beier has that grade and no team.

The 2004 draft produced a similar case: the best unsigned player from that class (Torsten Schmitt69 excepted) carried a potential above most of the first round. Three of those unsigned 2004 players signed by September and appeared in games this season. That is a 12-month gap between draft and first shift.

Beier has 4 months. If the pattern holds, he is looking at a first professional season in 2006-07 at the earliest. The 2006 rookie draft is 239 days away. Every team that passed on him in June gets one more chance in June to decide whether a 99 is worth the wait.

I keep a board. Beier is on it at the top. He has been at the top since June. Nobody has called him.
