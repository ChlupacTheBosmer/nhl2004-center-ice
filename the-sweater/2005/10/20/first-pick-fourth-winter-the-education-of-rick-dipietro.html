First Pick, Fourth Winter: the education of Rick DiPietro

The first overall pick of 2000 has a base of 79, the Index has him 9 above it, his ceiling is the league's highest at 99, and his contract reads $1.10M with 0 years left

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterThursday, 20 October 2005
ShareCopy link

The piece

The New York Islanders won their third straight on October 17, 4-2 at Philadelphia, and Rick DiPietro86 stopped every puck that mattered. He is 24 years old. He was the first pick of the 2000 draft. His contract has 0 years left, and free agency closes in 281 days.

The League Scouting Bureau's Development Index has DiPietro at +9 this week, the fourth-largest jump among goaltenders. His base grade is 79. His overall reads 88 today. The ceiling the Bureau set on his card is 99, tied with Pascal Leclaire91 for the highest number any goaltender carries.

The card has two ends. DiPietro's top six attributes read: PASS 99, AGIL 97, POKE 95, SPEE 94, GSH 92, GSL 92. His bottom seven: TOUG 50, FIGH 51, PADL 51, POSI 51, STYL 51, CONS 52, FLOP 52. Between those clusters sit nine attributes in the 62-to-87 range, the ordinary work of a goaltender who reads pucks and tracks through traffic. The top and the bottom tell you what happens when something goes wrong.

A goaltender who passes the puck at 99 but positions himself at 51 is a goaltender who gets caught out of structure. A goaltender who is agile at 97 and tough at 50 will make the save no one else makes and then fold on the one that comes from behind. The Bureau's base of 79 averages those contradictions into a single number that undersells the good and forgives the bad. The Index, at +9, says he is finding a way to lean on the 99s more than the 50s. His Islanders are 2-1-0 with 6 points and on a 3-game win streak.

From Lewiston to the top of the board

DiPietro was born in Lewiston, Maine, and moved to Winthrop, Massachusetts, when he was 10. He played the 1994 Quebec International Pee-Wee Hockey Tournament with a team from Beverly. He went to Saint Sebastian's School, then Boston University, where he played exactly one season in 1999-2000. That year he was named Hockey East Rookie of the Year, made the All-Rookie Team and the Second Team All-Hockey East, won the Terriers' co-MVP award, and was named MVP of the Beanpot Tournament, winning the Eberly Trophy as the tournament's top goaltender.

One year at BU. Then the Islanders picked him first overall in 2000.

The debut came on January 27, 2001, against Buffalo. The record shows 21 games in that first stretch: 7 wins, 12 losses, 75 goals against, 58.8 minutes a game. He played 10 games the following year, including one playoff appearance, before the call-up became permanent. In 2003-04, he played 63 games. He won 32, lost 28, recorded 1 shutout, allowed 174 goals, and averaged 59.8 minutes a night. His goals-against average improved from 3.49 in his debut season to 2.36 that spring. The Islanders made the playoffs for the first time during his tenure and won one series before a five-game exit.

What the room gives him

DiPietro's best teammate has an overall of 83: Alexei Yashin83 at center. Behind him on the depth chart sit Mike Peca82 at 82 and Roman Hamrlik82 at 82. The Islanders' top six skaters play for a team with 2 wins, 1 loss, and 6 points through 4 games. DiPietro is 5 grades above his best teammate. He is playing in front of a corps that gives him no margin for the positioning grades that read 51.

His morale sits at 94. The three straight wins in October came against Washington (4-3, home), Nashville (4-2, home), and Philadelphia (4-2, away). In 4 games this season he has 3 wins, 1 loss, and 10 goals against. He averages 61.5 minutes a night, the highest number on his card. The team trusts him enough to leave him in.

The July question

DiPietro plays on $1.10M. The contract has 0 years left. When free agency closes, 281 days from now, every club in the league will know what the Index says about him, what the 63-game season did to his track record, and what a 99 ceiling means at 24 years old.

The Islanders gave him 63 games in 2003-04. They have given him 4 games in this one, and taken 3 wins out of them. He is the cheapest first-overall pick in the league with a ceiling of 99 and no years left. If the Index holds, if the win streak extends past November, if the positioning and toughness grades nudge up the way a 24-year-old's sometimes do when the structure around him improves, the number next to his name in July will not be $1.10M. It might not be close.

The Bureau set his base at 79, which is what they believe he is when the Index resets, and his ceiling at 99, which is what they believe he could become. His current +9 is the largest single jump of his career. The room sits at 94, one of the highest morale readings on the roster. And the 3-game win streak says the Islanders are winning games in October with a goaltender whose positioning reads 51 and whose toughness reads 50, because everything else on his card is elite and he is making the saves that matter before the positioning problem gets a chance to matter.

The fourth winter is the first one where anyone outside Long Island is paying attention.
