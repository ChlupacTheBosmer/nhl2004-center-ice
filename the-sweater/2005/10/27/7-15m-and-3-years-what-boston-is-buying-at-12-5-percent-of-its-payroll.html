$7.15M and 3 Years: What Boston Is Buying at 12.5 Percent of Its Payroll

Glen Murray's contract runs through the season he turns 35. The Book caps his ceiling at 61 and the pace is telling the Bureau it is wrong.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterThursday, 27 October 2005
ShareCopy link

The piece

The Bureau's Book grades Glen Murray82 at a base of 88 and sets his potential at 61. Boston pays him $7.15M a year for 3 more seasons, and the desk's query puts that at 12.5 percent of the club's $57.39M payroll. He is 32. He will be 35 when the contract expires. The Development Index reads 0.

What Boston is buying, right now, is the second-highest pace in the league. Murray's 15 points in 7 games convert to 176 over 82. Only Jarome Iginla86 of Calgary Flames has more points this season, and Iginla plays 22.4 minutes a night compared to Murray's 23.6. The Bruins' captain is the highest-minute player among the league's current point leaders.

The risk on this contract lives in what the 48-game 2003-04 season already told us. Injury resistance on his card reads 50. Fighting reads 50. Shot blocking reads 51. The endurance grade of 94 is the reason he is still here, the reason a 225-pound winger from Halifax born in 1972 is skating 23.6 minutes and leading his club in points. Those protection grades are also the reason this contract has an expiration date no one can see yet.

Boston's payroll at $57.39M puts it mid-pack for a 30-club league. Joe Thornton84 at $10.74M is the team's highest earner, second in the league behind Iginla at $11.09M. Murray at $7.15M sits below the top ten, but at 12.5 percent of his own club's payroll he is a large slice of a mid-size budget. His cap hit competes with every second-line role on the roster.

The season where he won the Art Ross produced 168 points at 22.3 minutes a night. He is on pace to beat that number at 32, in his 14th professional season. The Development Index has him at 0 because the Bureau wrote his potential at 61 and his base is 88. When the base already exceeds the ceiling, the Index has nowhere to go. Murray sits at 81 displayed, 88 base, and the ceiling of 61 is below both.

The arm injury earlier this year carried a return date of January 15. He missed the opening weeks, came back, and produced 5 goals and 10 assists in 7 games. The 48 games in 2003-04 was 34 fewer than a full season. That is what an injury resistance grade of 50 looks like when it cashes in.

Boston Bruins is 4-1-1 through 7 games, four points behind Toronto in the Northeast division, on a four-game win streak. The team is winning with Murray on the top line with Thornton. Sergei Samsonov82 is out with a neck injury until November 9, and that absence has pushed production toward the right wing. The room plays the way it plays because the captain is scoring, and the captain is scoring because the endurance holds.

The 3 years on this contract run through the season he turns 35. The pace of 176 will regress. The question is how far, and whether the 94 endurance keeps him at 23 minutes and 15 points through 82 games when the body starts saying no to 49. A man with injury resistance of 50 does not get to choose the year. The body chooses.

The Bureau set his ceiling at 61. The season the record opens produced 168 points. Two years ago he scored 64 in half a season. Today: a 176 pace, 3 years on the books at $7.15M, a fighting grade of 50, an arm that healed in January. The pace will decide whether this contract was too expensive or too cheap.
