Speed 99, Consistency 50: Pascal Leclaire carries the widest split in goaltending on $1.10M and 0 years left

The 8th pick of 2001 has the league's best tools between the pipes and its worst floor, and the market finds out which one he is in 289 days

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterWednesday, 12 October 2005
ShareCopy link

Pascal Leclaire89 grades 99 in speed and 50 in consistency. Those are the Bureau's numbers, the current ones, sitting on his card today at Columbus Blue Jackets. No other goaltender in the league carries a wider distance between his best attribute and his worst. He is 22 years old. His contract reads $1.10M with 0 years left on it.

The Book says 83. The Index says plus 9, which puts his overall at 91 today. That ties him with Tomas Vokoun90 for seventh among goaltenders, behind Roberto Luongo97 and Jean-Sebastien Giguere97 at 97, Martin Brodeur97 at 95, Marty Turco93 at 93, and Olaf Kolzig92 and Nikolai Khabibulin89 at 92. The potential is 94, so the Bureau sees 3 grades left in the model. His morale reads 90.

The tools and the habits

The speed and agility are both 99, tied for the highest a goaltender in the league has on either attribute. The glove hand grades 98. The snap is 95, the skate is 95, the five-hole is 92. Then: consistency 50, fighting 51, pad stack 51, positioning 51, style 51. The physical instruments are the best in goaltending. The habits that make a goaltender reliable sit at the floor of the scale.

Look at the other goaltenders who share 99 speed. Luongo has it too, and consistency at 51. Josh Harding84 in Washington Capitals grades 99 speed, 54 consistency. Ari Ahonen85 at Chicago Blackhawks is 97 speed, 52 consistency. They all carry wide splits. None of them carries 99 and 50. Leclaire's gap is the widest the Bureau has drawn on any goaltender's card this edition.

A goaltender whose tools are maxed but whose consistency sits at 50 can steal a game and then lose the next one by leaving a post open. The Index moved him up 9 grades this year, the biggest jump among goaltenders. The Book base of 83 says the Bureau still isn't sure the 91 is real.

The 8th pick, four years on

Leclaire grew up in Repentigny, Quebec, and played the 1996 Quebec International Pee-Wee Tournament with the Rive-Nord Elites. The QMJHL gave him Halifax from 1998 and Montreal through 2002, the Mooseheads and the Rocket, two clubs in two cities while he grew into the frame: 73 inches, 185 pounds, left-handed.

The 2001 draft made him the first goaltender taken, 8th overall, to Columbus. Four years later the overall reads 91. The potential reads 94. The contract has nothing left on it.

He arrived at Columbus before the record opened on 2004-12-01; the desk does not have a trade or free-agent date for his first day in the building, only that he was already there. His first partial season gave him 24 games, 11 wins, 10 losses, 1 shutout, 72 goals against at 58.0 minutes a night. Then 49 games in 2003-04, 23 wins, 25 losses, 131 goals against at 60.5 a night. One game this season, a 2-1 loss, 60 minutes, 2 goals against.

The room

Marc Denis88 stands in the other crease. The Book has Denis at 90, one grade below Leclaire's current reading. Two goaltenders above 90 on the same roster is unusual, and the pair share a consistency grade of 50. Sanderson leads the forwards at 84. Todd Marchant80 and Dimitri Kalinin80 grade 82 at the blue line. Espen Knutsen78 and Tyler Wright78 at 80 up the middle.

Columbus Blue Jackets opened the season 0-1-0, 1 goal for and 2 against. Leclaire started the one game played.

A young goaltender in a room like that learns something different from a young goaltender behind a Cup winner. Denis is the veteran presence, one grade away on the Book, same 50 consistency. The two of them know what the other knows: that the tools say you can stop everything and the habits say you will not stop everything. There is no coaching position available on the sheet to say whether Columbus runs a rotation or names one man a starter. What the sheet says is that both dress and both are close enough to split the work.

The contract year

$1.10M is what he costs now. Free agency closes on 2006-07-28, 289 days from today. A goaltender at 91 with 99 speed and 98 glove does not cost $1.10M in an open market. A goaltender at 91 with 50 consistency might.

The Bureau sees the split. The market will too. Columbus will decide whether the 91 this year is the real number or whether the 83 is closer to what they get in a full season of 60 or 70 starts. The Index has him at plus 9, his highest form reading on record. If it holds, the potential ceiling of 94 comes within reach. If it fades to zero, he is an 83 with 50 consistency and no contract, and $1.10M looks generous.

He is the best player on the ice some nights and an ordinary one on others, and nobody in that building, not the coach, not Denis, not Sanderson looking back over a shoulder after a bad change, can predict which one walks out of the tunnel. The save that comes from a 99 skate and a 98 glove is the same save he misses when the 51 positioning puts him two inches off the wrong post.

At 22, with $1.10M and 0 years left and the Index pointing up 9 grades for the first time in his career, the next 60 or 70 games decide whether the league pays for the 99 or the 50.
