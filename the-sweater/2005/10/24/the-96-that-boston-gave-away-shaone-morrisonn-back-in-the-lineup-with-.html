The 96 That Boston Gave Away: Shaone Morrisonn, back in the lineup with 0 years left and 11 grades on the Index

The slap shot that ties the league's eighth-highest mark belongs to the defenseman Boston traded for Sergei Gonchar, who is now back in a Boston sweater and playing 11.3 minutes a night

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterMonday, 24 October 2005
ShareCopy link

The Bureau's Development Index moved Shaone Morrisonn74 11 grades this week. He is 22 years old. His slap shot reads 96, which is the eighth-highest mark on any defenseman in the league, one grade behind Sergei Gonchar, the man Boston traded him for.

Morrisonn plays 11.3 minutes a night. He has 1 assist in 4 games. His contract reads $1.10M with 0 years left. He will be an unrestricted free agent when this season ends, and the Bureau's Book has him at 78 today with a ceiling of 99.

Kamloops to the 19th pick

Morrisonn grew up in Vancouver, played community hockey, then earned a spot on the Kamloops Blazers in the WHL. The Bruins took him 19th overall in 2001. He was 18. A 75-inch, 182-pound left-shot defenseman from the West Coast, drafted into one of the league's oldest markets.

His first full season was 2003-04: 31 games, 1 goal, 4 assists, 10.1 minutes a night. He logged 8 penalty minutes in 31 games. The elbow came sometime between February 13 and February 16 of 2005 and kept him out until April 27. He missed the run into spring and most of what was left.

Then came March 3, 2004. Morrisonn went to Washington in the deal that brought Gonchar to Boston. He was 21. He went as a young defenseman with tools and a contract about to expire. Boston got a proven top-pairing defenseman. Morrisonn got a new room in a city that was trying to build something around Jaromir Jagr89.

The record does not carry how he got back to Boston. He was here already when the transactions opened in December 2004, and he has not left since.

The grades: 96 and 51

The Book prints a split that reads like two different players sharing a sweater. Slap Power is 96. Passing is 87. Puck Handling is 85. Speed is 83. Balance is 81. Toughness is 80.

Fighting is 51. Heroics is 51. Injury resistance is 51.

The first six numbers belong to a defenseman who can walk the blue line and put the puck where the goaltender cannot reach it. The last three belong to a kid who has not yet decided what he is willing to absorb to keep the ice time. At 182 pounds, the 51 in Fighting reads as honest. The 51 in Injury resistance reads like a warning that the elbow is not the last time something gives.

Morrisonn is the 4th defenseman on Boston's depth chart by overall. Bryan Berard is 81, Daniel McGillis is 80, Jeff Jillson is 79, Morrisonn is 78. Behind him sits Jonathan Girard at 76, Nick Boynton at 74.

Four games, 11.3 minutes, 1 point. That is what the 4th defenseman gets when the top three are healthy and the coach wants to win games in October.

The three men above him who are also on the Development Index right now: Komisarek is +10, Klesla is +8, Gonchar is not on the risers list. The Index does not reward a slap shot by itself. It rewards minutes and production.

The room and the minutes

Boston Bruins is 4-1-1 through 6 games, second in the Northeast behind Toronto Maple Leafs. Boston has won three straight. The offense runs through Joe Thornton86 at 90 overall and Glen Murray81 at 85. Sergei Samsonov82, the third-best player on the roster at 86, is out with a neck until November 9.

With Samsonov up the lineup, Boston's penalty kill stretches thin and the coaching staff leans on its top three defensemen for the heavy minutes. Morrisonn gets 11.3. He scores his 1 point when the puck finds him at the point on a power play or when he walks in and releases. The shot is not the problem. Getting 17 minutes a night is.

The Bureau's ceiling of 99 says this man can be the best defenseman on any club in the league. The base of 70 says he is not there yet. The +11 on the Index says the gap is closing, which is what makes the contract worth reading.

July

$1.10M, 0 years left. Free agency closes 277 days from now. Morrisonn will hit the market at 23 with a 96 slap shot, an 87 pass, a ceiling of 99, and a career high of 31 games played in a single season.

Morrisonn's morale is 90. He is on the Index risers. He is the 4th defenseman in a division-leading club. If the Index holds at +11 through November, the overall reads 81. If it holds through the trade deadline in March, and he plays 60 games, the number becomes real in a way that 11 minutes a night cannot make it.

The elbow cost him two months last spring. The trade cost him a season's worth of development in a room that was not his. The minutes cost him nothing except time, and time is what he has the most of at 22.

What the 96 cannot do is put him in the lineup. The 51s might. The Index is pointing up, and the contract is running out. Whether that gets him to the first team before July is the question.
