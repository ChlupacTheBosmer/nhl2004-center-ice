The Long Way Round: Pavol Demitra, the 227th Pick, at 30

Soccer until 15, drafted in the eighth round, traded for a defenseman. The Book's base grade is 89 and the Index has slipped 4.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterSaturday, 29 October 2005
ShareCopy link

The piece

Pavol Demitra81 was a soccer player until he was 15. His father played the game professionally, and the son only turned to hockey as a teenager, late for a man who would put up 101 points in an NHL season.

He is 30 now, an alternate captain in St. Louis, and the line reads 7 goals and 5 assists for 12 points in 8 games, a pace of 123 over 82 games. The Book's base grade for him is 89. The Bureau's Development Index has him down 4, so the overall reads 83. The contract is $5.46M with 3 years left.

An eighth-round pick at 30, still scoring at the top of a division race. The file was written twelve years ago and the Index is the only line that has moved backward.

Dubnica, then Trencin

Demitra began at the senior level with ZTS Dubnica in Czechoslovakia's second tier, where he scored 13 goals and 10 assists, 23 points, in 28 games. The next season he joined HC Dukla Trencin of the premier Czechoslovak Extraliga and scored 11 goals and 28 points in 46 games.

Trencin is his hometown. Marian Gaborik99 was born there too.

He went 1993 #227 (R8), and he began his career in the league with Ottawa. His first NHL game came 9 October 1993, and he scored on Curtis Joseph for his first goal and point. He played 12 games for the Senators that season, with a goal and 2 points, and spent the rest of it with Prince Edward Island in the AHL, where he put up 41 points in 41 games.

The pattern held for two more years. He scored 74 points in 61 AHL games the next season and managed 4 goals and 7 points in 16 games with Ottawa. A year later he had 81 points in 48 AHL games before the recall stuck, and he finished that season with 7 goals and 17 points in 31 games.

Traded for Olsson

To start the 1996-97 season Demitra held out. He played a single game with Dukla Trencin, a goal and two points, then joined the Las Vegas Thunder of the IHL, where he scored 8 goals and 21 points in 22 games. On 27 November 1996 Ottawa traded him to St. Louis Blues for Christer Olsson.

St. Louis sent him to Grand Rapids, and he scored 20 goals and 50 points in 42 games before the call came. His first game with the Blues was 17 March 1997, a 3-2 loss to Phoenix with no points on the sheet. His first two goals for the club came 3 April 1997, both against Tommy Salo88, in a 5-5 tie with the Islanders.

He made his playoff debut 16 April 1997 with an assist in a 2-0 win over Detroit, and his first playoff goal came 22 April 1997 against Mike Vernon, in a 4-0 win in which he added two assists. By 1997-98 he was a full-time Blue, 61 games, 22 goals and 52 points, fifth on the club. By 1998-99 he had 89 points on 37 goals and 52 assists in 82 games, tenth in league scoring, and a place in the 1999 All-Star Game in Tampa, where he scored for the World team.

The grades still read like a first-line player. Passing 92, puck control 91, speed 90, deking 88, and the big-moment grade 90. Accuracy is 88. Two grades stop at 50, fighting and toughness, and checking is 54. The Book caps his ceiling at 61, which is a number the Bureau has not revisited in a while.

He has never been on the injured list the record keeps, twelve seasons of professional hockey without a single body part on the sheet.

The room

St. Louis is 4-1-2 for 12 points, second in the Central behind Columbus on 13, with a win in hand. The Blues have scored 24 and allowed 22, 3-1 at home and 2-2 on the road.

Keith Tkachuk88 is 90, Chris Pronger84 86, Doug Weight83 85 and Chris Osgood83 85. The line Demitra centers or wings is one of four a coach can trust, on a club that has not lost in regulation in five of its first eight games.

"You start late and you learn to keep the puck," Demitra says of the soccer years. "Nobody hands it back to you."

What the Index means

The -4 is small. A base of 89 says the Bureau thinks he is one of the best forwards in the league on his own merits. Four points of form have moved the overall to 83 while he scores at a 123 pace. He is 30, has 3 years left on $5.46M, and just posted a 12-point month. The market is not worried about him.
