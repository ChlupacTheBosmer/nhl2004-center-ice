The 39-grade split: Radulov plays at 80 with a Fighting grade of 51 and a ceiling of 79

His Puck Handling is 90. The man guarding him at 51 is the only one who could stop him, and no club has one on the roster.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterSunday, 16 October 2005
ShareCopy link

Igor Radulov80's card has a 39-grade gap between his best attribute and his worst. Puck Handling: 90. Fighting: 51. Those are two different players, one who can stickhandle through a neutral-zone pinch and one who cannot finish a check when the shift ends. The Bureau has graded both the same man for three seasons.

What changed in October is that Chicago Blackhawks put him on the first two lines. At 21.0 minutes a night, his shift is longer and more varied than the 40-second shifts he played on the fourth line, and the 90 gets used more. At 2 points in 2 games, his pace is 1.00 points a game. His pace last season was 12 points in 79 games. The rate is identical. The minutes behind the rate are double.

The question the Index cannot answer

The Development Index has Radulov at +11. That puts his overall at 80 today against a base of 71. His potential grade reads 79. He is 1 grade past the number the Bureau says he should be able to reach. This happens in small samples. The question is whether the 80 is what he is or what a hot stretch looks like on a man whose true level sits at 71.

There is a third possibility. If 90 Puck Handling is a genuine elite tool, then 71 was always too low, and the Bureau undersold him by a decade because his 51 Fighting made him invisible on the fourth line. A man who never gets the puck cannot score at his hands. Give him the puck for 21 minutes and maybe the 80 is not a fluke but a correction.

The record supports the correction theory in one way and the fluke theory in another. Two games, 2 points, 0 penalty minutes. Two games is a nothing sample. But the mpg jump from 11.4 to 21.0 came from a coach's decision, and coaches do not double a man's minutes on a whim.

What 51 Fighting costs a team in April

Radulov played 6 playoff games last postseason at 8.3 minutes a night and scored nothing. In a playoff series, minutes are earned by trust, and trust is built partly on the ability to hold a zone and take a hit. A man at 51 Fighting and 58 Body Checking does not inspire that trust in the third period of Game 6. The Chicago coaches gave him 8.3 minutes in the playoffs after giving him 11.4 in the regular season.

His potential of 79 likely reflects the playoff ceiling. The Bureau sees a man whose tools make him a good regular-season winger but whose frame and willingness keep him on the third line in June. The 80 he reads today is what October looks like for that kind of man: fresh, confident, with the puck finding him before the checking starts.

The market problem

$0.60M with 0 years left. Morale at 94. He is happy playing 21 minutes and making $0.60M. A club that wants him next July will either pay for the October version or the April version, and no one can tell them which one is real until May.

If a buyer pays for 80 and gets 71, they have overpaid by 9 grades for a 39-grade split. If a buyer pays for 71 and gets 80, they found the cheapest top-six winger in the league at $0.60M. The 285 days before free agency closes will tell, and the Index will keep moving every week until it does.
