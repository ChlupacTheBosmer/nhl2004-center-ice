Igor Radulov, at 23: 21 minutes, $0.60M, and 1 grade past a ceiling the Bureau set at 79

The 74th pick of 2000 played 11.4 minutes a night for two seasons in Chicago. This year he plays 21.0 and the Index has him up 11.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterSunday, 16 October 2005
ShareCopy link

Igor Radulov76 played 10.6 minutes a night as a rookie, then 11.4 as a sophomore. This October he is at 21.0, and the 74th pick of the 2000 draft is producing at 1.00 points a game on a contract that pays him $0.60M and expires this summer.

He is 23, from Nizhny Tagil, Russia, 73 inches tall, 186 pounds, left-handed. The Bureau's Book gives him a base grade of 71. The Development Index has added 11 to that, putting him at 80 today, the second-biggest jump on the riser board behind Vaclav Nedorost80's 12. His potential grade reads 79. He is 1 grade past his ceiling.

Ninety-six more seconds a night is not a small thing for a man who spent two years on the bottom two lines of Chicago Blackhawks. He scored 12 points in 79 games last season. He has 2 in 2 now. The production rate is the same, but he is earning it on a top-six assignment, against the kind of minutes that used to belong to other men.

The grade profile is what separates this from a simple minutes bump. His Puck Handling reads 90, the same number that sits on the cards of players three rounds above him in the draft. His Agility, Deking, Faceoffs, Passability and Heroic grades all read 85. First-line tools, all of them. For two seasons he used them on the fourth line, against other men's fourth lines, touching the puck maybe 15 times a shift, and the Box Score read 3 points one year and 12 the next.

What the ceiling number means

The Bureau sets a potential grade for every player. For Radulov, that number is 79. Today his overall reads 80. A coach in the room would tell you the tools were always there and the minutes were not. What changed is how the coach uses the middle of the card. At 21.0 minutes on Chicago's top six, Radulov puts his hands on the puck in open ice and the 90 and the 85s can breathe. He stays out of the corners where a Fighting grade of 51 would get him eaten alive.

The floor is plain. Fighting 51. Body Checking 58. Puck Checking 58. The gap between his Puck Handling at 90 and his Fighting at 51 is 39 grades, one of the widest attribute splits on any skater's card in the league. He cannot win a board battle against a man who wants to take the puck, and he cannot finish a check. At 73 inches and 186 pounds, the frame does not offer an excuse. The Bureau has always known about the 90 and the 51.

The contract and the clock

Radulov earns $0.60M and his contract has 0 years left. Free agency closes on 2006-07-28, 285 days from now. He will be a free agent at the end of this season.

Morale sits at 94. He is playing the minutes his hands think they have earned. In July the question is whether a club pays for the 80 he is posting now or for the Bureau's 79, once the Index cools.

A head injury in April

The record carries one injury on his card: a head injury with an onset between 2005-03-30 and 2005-04-04, back on 2005-04-10. A 6-11 day absence. He finished the season with 79 games played, meaning the injury cost him at most 3 games. Whatever opened in his role this summer has nothing to do with a recovery.

The room

Chicago's top players today are Jocelyn Thibault83 at 89, Ari Ahonen85 at 89, Eric Daze83 at 87, Theoren Fleury82 at 86, Mark Bell80 at 84 and Steve Sullivan80 at 84. Radulov sits at 80 with the Index, the eighth-best player in the room, behind two goaltenders and five skaters who have more minutes and more points in their careers than he has in three seasons.

He played 6 playoff games last April at 8.3 minutes a night. He scored nothing. This October he is on a different line, playing different minutes, against different men. The 74th pick from Nizhny Tagil, drafted in the third round when the rest of that draft class was still in junior or unsigned, has found a role that suits his hands. The next five months will tell whether his ceiling suits the rest of his body.
