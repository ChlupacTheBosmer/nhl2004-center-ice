Seventh Round, Zero Years: The 208th Pick's $600,000 Winter at St. Louis

Brandon Reid has Speed 99, a 109-point pace, and a contract that expires this spring. The Bureau's ceiling says he has 17 more grades in him.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterSaturday, 26 November 2005
ShareCopy link

The 2000 draft ran seven rounds deep before a club in St. Louis took a 19-year-old from Kirkland, Quebec. Brandon Reid77 went 208th overall. Five years later he sits in the top ten of the league's scoring race at 28 points in 21 games, a pace of 109 over a full season, and his contract on file carries 0 years of term and $0.60M in salary.

He is 24 years old. His morale reads 100. The Bureau's Book has him at 77 today, which is 4 above his base of 73, and his potential sits at 94. That is a 17-grade gap between what he is and what the Bureau thinks he can still become, on a man the draft did not select until the 208th name was called.

What the Book says about the body

The speed grades tell you who he is before the puck touches his stick. Speed 99 and Acceleration 99 put him at the very top of the league. Agility 96 rounds out the first line of his body profile. Those three numbers are the reason a 165-pound right winger from a suburb north of Montreal plays 19.0 minutes a night for a club in first place.

The bottom of the Book reads like a different man. Checking 50, Endurance 50, Fighting 50. The Bureau grades the floor at 50 on any attribute, and Reid sits on it in three categories. He does not finish checks. He does not engage. He goes where the puck isn't and arrives before the puck gets there anyway.

The middle of his game does the work the bottom doesn't. Puckhandling 87, Accuracy 86, Faceoffs 86. Those are second-line numbers, maybe top-six numbers on a night where the legs are fresh. At 19.0 minutes a night, the legs are supposed to fade. The Endurance grade says they should. The scoring line says they haven't.

The jump from 14.3 to 19.0

Last season with Vancouver Canucks, Reid played 73 games at 14.3 minutes a night and scored 58 points. That was a fourth-line role with flashes, 28 goals and 30 assists on a team that made the playoffs and lost in the second round. He dressed for 7 playoff games and added 6 points.

On 2005-03-04 he arrived in St. Louis. The record does not carry the terms of the transaction, only the date. Whatever St. Louis did in March gave this man a larger role. This season he is at 19.0 minutes a night and 28 points in 21 games, a 1.33 point-per-game clip against last season's 0.79. The goals are down (9 in 21 against 28 in 73) but the assists nearly doubled in rate: 19 in 21 against 30 in 73.

That is a different usage. Last season Reid finished 43 of 58 points. This season he is generating 19 of 28 on the pass, which means he is creating for the men around him. In St. Louis the men around him include Keith Tkachuk88 at overall 88, Chris Pronger84 at 84, Doug Weight83 at 83, and Pavol Demitra81 at 81. In Vancouver he was the fastest thing on the ice making it happen alone. In St. Louis he feeds a line that can score.

What the room says

A man with Speed 99 and Fighting 50 learns early that the game is a negotiation he is going to lose in the corners. Reid's answer is to not be in the corners. At 68 inches and 165 pounds, a Checking grade of 50 is physics. A 208th pick does not get sent out to play tough minutes in his first year. He gets sent out to skate circles around men twice his weight, and if he scores enough, the minutes go up.

They went up. From 14.3 to 19.0. The Development Index has him at +4 this season, his best form reading since the record opened. He was with Vancouver Canucks from before December 2004 through early March 2005, played 73 games there in 2003-04, and was moved. The record opened on 2004-12-01 and lists him already in a Vancouver sweater, so we do not know how long he was there before that date. He carried an abdomen injury in January 2005 that cost him time through early February.

Among the St. Louis forwards who appear in the league's top 30, Reid's 19 assists lead the club. His 28 points tie him with Tkachuk for the team lead. At $0.60M with 0 years left on the deal, he is producing at a 109-point pace on a salary that does not reflect it.

The 17 grades

The Bureau's potential for Reid is 94. His current Book grade is 77. That is 17 grades of headroom on a 24-year-old with Speed 99 and Acceleration 99. For comparison, Ilya Kovalchuk90 has a potential equal to his base, a gap of 0, and the desk wrote about that ceiling last week. Reid has the opposite problem. The ceiling is real but far, and the contract expires before he gets there.

St. Louis sits at 13-4-3 in the Central, first place, 31 points in 21 games. Their top line is Tkachuk, Weight, and Reid, and that line is carrying the offense. If the 208th pick signs a new deal this summer at a market rate for a 109-point winger, the cost will not be $0.60M.

The 2000 draft produced Rick Nash85 first overall and Jason Spezza73 second. Reid was taken after all of them, after another 200 names. At 24, he is scoring at a pace that would make most first-rounders from that class look at their own sheets, on a deal that expires this spring. The Index has him at +4, the potential sits at 94, and the contract expires this spring.

He is 24. The 17 grades between 77 and 94 keep the room talking at night. The contract is what they talk about in July.
