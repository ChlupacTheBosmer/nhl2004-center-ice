99 and 50: Ilya Kovalchuk has outplayed his ceiling and run out of room

The first Russian ever drafted first overall is 22, holds the league's top shot power and speed, and the Bureau's potential for him equals his base grade. The contract has no term past this season.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterWednesday, 16 November 2005
ShareCopy link

Ilya Kovalchuk92 has a shot power of 99, a speed of 99, and a ceiling of 84. The Bureau's Development Index has him playing 5 grades above that ceiling right now, which means the 89 on his Book today is a hot streak. The base underneath is 84, the potential the Bureau projected for him at 22 is also 84, and the two numbers meet. There is no room above.

His contract reads $1.10M with 0 years left. He hits the market in July.

The road from Tver

Kovalchuk was born in 1983 in Tver, a city northwest of Moscow on the Volga. He played in the 1997 Quebec International Pee-Wee Hockey Tournament with Spartak Moscow, then played two seasons for Spartak's Vysshaya Liga club before crossing the Atlantic. The Thrashers took him first overall in 2001, the first Russian a league that had drafted for 39 years had ever selected at the top. He wears 17 for Valeri Kharlamov, the Soviet winger who won eight world titles in the 1970s.

Three NHL seasons in, the record says what he is. His rookie line reads 82 games, 72 goals, 62 assists, 134 points. Last season he put up 63 goals and 75 assists for 138 points in 79 games and won the Lady Byng. This season, through 15 games, he has 10 goals and 6 assists for 16 points, playing 22.3 minutes a night.

What the grades build

The Book shows 23 attributes. The top of his list is almost absurd: 99 shot power, 99 speed, 98 hero, 97 acceleration, 96 agility, 94 endurance. Fastest man with the hardest shot, finishes on big nights, legs that do not quit. Those six grades drive everything good on Atlanta's scoresheet.

The bottom of his list tells you why Atlanta has 32 goals for and 43 against. Fighting 50. Injury resistance 50. Shootout backhand 51. Aggression 60. Pass blocking 63. Faceoffs 64. Pressure 77. You cannot ask him to take a hit along the wall, win a draw in his own zone, or chase a loose puck into a crowd without something breaking. Last season a shoulder kept him out between late March and the very end of March, the return date the same week the injury window closed. At 22, the body that cannot absorb contact is the one on the market.

The base of 84 is the average of the 99s and the 50s, and the potential sits there too. The room says he will not get better; the Index says he already is better than the Book thinks he can be.

The room around him

Atlanta Thrashers sit last in the Southeast at 5-8-2, 12 points through 15 games. They score 32 and give up 43, the gap built from a 1-8 road record. Their best skater is Dany Heatley91 at 88, who plays right wing next to him. Byron Dafoe85 holds the crease at 82. Andre Roy74 is out with an arm through January 20 and Yannick Tremblay73 with a leg through December 28.

A 99 shot power needs a body between him and the goaltender, and the bodies Atlanta has are a 74, a 73, a 72 and a 71 on the back end. Heatley at 88 is the only other man in the building who can finish. When Atlanta plays on the road, they lose 8 of 9.

The line of 10 goals in 15 games is his lowest pace in the league. His point total of 16 through 15 is slower than 138 over 79 projected. The Index still reads +5, so the Bureau believes the form is real and the goals will come, but the team around him is asking him to carry more than a man with a fighting grade of 50 can carry.

What 0 years left means at 22

A $1.10M contract with no term past this season puts a 22-year-old with a 99 shot in July. The market will see the Lady Byng, the 72-goal rookie line, the 63 goals and 138 points from last winter. Some team will bid. Another will look at the 50 injury resistance, the 50 fighting, the potential of 84 that equals the base, and pass.

Kovalchuk has no room left. He is what he is: a 99-shot scorer with a 50 body who cannot check or fight and cannot stay whole through an 82-game schedule. The Index will cool, the base will hold at 84. The question is what a club pays for the peaks when the ceiling is already the floor.

He turns 23 in April. The shoulder healed. The contract does not.
