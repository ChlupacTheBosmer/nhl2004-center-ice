Pascal Leclaire runs faster than anyone wearing a goaltender's mask

The 2001 eighth-pick has Speed 99, Positioning 51, and a contract with no term past this season.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterThursday, 17 November 2005
ShareCopy link

Pascal Leclaire93's Speed grade is 99. His Agility is 99. On the Bureau's Book, no goaltender in the league skates faster or turns quicker. The man who does both at 99 is 23 years old, plays in Columbus, and has a Positioning grade of 51.

The Bureau's overall has him at 91 today on a base of 83 and a form rise of 9. The potential sits at 94. The contract reads $1.10M with 0 years left. He can cross-crease on one stride and cannot predict where to set up before the play arrives.

He turned 23 on November 7, ten days ago.

From Repentigny to the eighth pick

Leclaire grew up in Repentigny, Quebec, on the north shore of the St. Lawrence across from Montreal. He played the 1996 Quebec International Pee-Wee Tournament with the Rive-Nord Elites, then went junior in the QMJHL, first with the Halifax Mooseheads and then the Montreal Rocket, between 1998 and 2002. Columbus took him eighth overall in 2001, the first goaltender selected that year.

He came to Columbus before the record opened on December 1, 2004, and has been there since. Columbus Blue Jackets are 7-5-1 through 17 games, fourth in the Central, and Leclaire has appeared in 12, winning 7 and losing 5 with no shutouts.

Last season he played 49 games, won 23, lost 25, and allowed 131 goals. The goals-against average the Bureau computed from those two numbers is 2.7. A goaltender on a team that finished sixth-last in the league (66 points) was never going to collect many wins. The 2.7 is what matters: he was good enough for a bottom-feeder and the team did not improve around him.

The top of that table belongs to a Vezina candidate. Speed 99 and Agility 99 together mean he can recover from any angle, close any gap, get across the crease faster than the puck can reach the far post. Glove 98 means when the recovery works, he makes the save. Screen 95 means he can see through traffic. These are the best athletic grades on any goaltender's sheet this season.

The bottom belongs to a goaltender who should not be starting. Positioning 51 means the Bureau grades him in the bottom sixth of the league at the most fundamental job a goaltender has: being in the right place before the shot arrives. Puck Playing 51 means he is equally poor at handling the puck on a rush or at the blue line, which makes him a liability whenever the opposition dumps and chases or carries it in with speed. Consistency 50 means the Bureau does not expect him to repeat any performance, good or bad.

A goaltender with those athletic grades and those technical grades will make saves no one else in the league can make, then let one go five minutes later that a Grade-70 goaltender stops without thinking. His 7 and 5 with no shutout in 12 starts is what a Consistency of 50 looks like.

The room and the crease

Columbus has two goaltenders the Bureau rates above 80. Pascal Leclaire is 91 today. Marc Denis92 is 87. Denis is 29, the 3rd overall pick in 1996, and has been a full-time starting goaltender in this league for several years. Leclaire's 91, driven by the +9 form rise, sits above Denis's 87 in the overall column. His base is 83.

The Index rise is real. He is the 11th-biggest riser in the league this edition, +9 on a base of 83. What produced it is not obvious from a 7-5 record with no shutout. The Bureau's form term captures something beyond the win column: save percentage on shots he had no business stopping. When Agility 99 and Glove 98 come together on a broken play, the result goes into the form term even if the game is lost.

Columbus is without Todd Marchant84, out with a knee until January 30. Dimitri Kalinin84 is 82 and carries the back end. Geoff Sanderson86 leads the forwards at 84. The team has allowed 35 goals in 17 games. Leclaire is not on a team that is being outshot into submission. He is on a team that is good enough to give him a chance to win, which is what the Index rise reflects: when the volume is reasonable and the defense is competent, Speed 99 and Glove 98 win games.

What the gap costs

You can see it from the press box. A goaltender who sprints across his crease like a forward on a rush, then is wrong-footed by a simple wrist shot from the right circle because he opened too early and was not set. Columbus fans know this. They cheer the impossible save and groan at the soft one in the same period.

What he would say about it, if a man put a microphone in front of him after a 7-save first period followed by three soft ones in the second: the game is faster when you can skate, but positioning is not about speed. It is about reading the play before it happens, and reading is not something you recover from. You either see the cross-crease pass developing at the blue line or you do not. Agility 99 carries you across three feet of ice a half-second too late. The glove is still there. The body just arrived late.

The form term rewards the saves he makes. The base grade remembers the ones he should have been in position to make without moving.

The contract

$1.10M. 0 years left. The season he is playing right now is the last season Columbus controls without negotiating.

At 23, with a base of 83 and a ceiling the Bureau set at 94, he is a goaltender with 11 grades of headroom. The problem is Consistency 50. A team extending him is buying the ceiling and getting the base on most nights. Columbus has already seen that: the 7-5 in 12 starts, the 23-25 last season on a bad team, the 1 shutout in 49 games.

The Index has him at 91 today, and the Index is a form term. It moves. When it moves back toward the base, the 91 becomes an 83, and an 83 with a Positioning grade of 51 is a backup in this league. Columbus knows their goaltender is athletic. What they need to figure out by July is whether the Bureau's Consistency grade is a ceiling or a phase.

The potential of 94 says the Bureau thinks he can still become a 94-grade goaltender. It is what the Bureau believes is possible if everything else comes together, and "everything else" here means 49 grades of improvement across Consistency, Positioning, and Puck Playing simultaneously. Speed 99 solves the consequences of Positioning 51, not Positioning 51 itself. That costs him something on every play, and you cannot ask a goaltender to pay that price for 82 games.

He is 23. He is faster than anyone in the league wearing his equipment. His contract expires this summer. Columbus has the rest of the season to decide what that is worth.
