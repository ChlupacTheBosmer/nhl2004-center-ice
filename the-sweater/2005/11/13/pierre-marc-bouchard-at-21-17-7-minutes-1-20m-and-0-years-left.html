Pierre-Marc Bouchard, at 21: 17.7 minutes, $1.20M, and 0 years left

The 8th pick of 2002 just took the biggest jump of his young career, and the contract on file has no term past this season.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterSunday, 13 November 2005
ShareCopy link

Pierre-Marc Bouchard79 played 11.6 minutes a night last season. This season he plays 17.7. The contract reads $1.20M with 0 years left.

The 8th overall pick of 2002 is 21 years old, carries 5 points in 16 games for the Minnesota Wild, and the Bureau's Development Index has him 9 grades above his Book base of 74. His morale reads 92. The contract has no term past this season.

The grades describe one kind of player. Speed 94, Acceleration 93, Agility 93 at the top. Fighting 50, Toughness 52, Checking 54 at the bottom. Shot Power 89, Deking 86, Passing 86, Puck Control 86. The puck skills sit in the top tier of the league. The physical game sits at the floor.

The jump nobody noticed

His first season on the record ran 36 games at 11.0 minutes a night. His second, the 2003-04 full campaign, ran 82 games at 11.6. This season, through 16 games, he sits at 17.7 minutes a night. That is the biggest single-season expansion of his role.

That is a second-line center's role, and he shares the middle of the ice with Wes Walz82, rated 82, and Saku Koivu84, rated 82, who is out with a shoulder through 2005-11-29. With Koivu on the shelf, Bouchard gets the top-nine minutes and the matchups that come with them. The 2003-04 season showed the coaching staff he could handle 82 games without a scratch on the injury record. This season tests whether he can handle more minutes per game.

The 79 on the Index puts Bouchard behind Manny Fernandez88 at 88 and Walz at 82 among active Wild players. Koivu, at 82, sits with him on the shelf. A 21-year-old who weighs 165 pounds is the 3rd-highest-rated player on the roster right now.

The points line reads 2 goals and 3 assists in 16 games. Richard Park77, the right winger, has 23 points in 16 and leads the club. The production is modest. The Index movement is the signal: 9 grades up, the highest mark among Wild players on the Development Index, and one of the biggest risers in the league this season.

Sherbrooke to the 8th pick

Bouchard grew up in Sherbrooke, Quebec, and played the 1998 Quebec International Pee-Wee Tournament with College-Français Rive-Sud from the South Shore of Montreal. The Bureau put him 8th on its 2002 draft board. He arrived at Minnesota Wild as a teenager and has been there ever since. No trade, no waiver claim, no other sweater shows on the record.

He is listed at 70 inches and 165 pounds, shoots left. A Fighting grade of 50 is the lowest single number on the Wild roster. His Toughness at 52 and Checking at 54 are close behind. The rest of his book reads like a skilled winger's grades bolted to a small center's frame. Speed 94 and Agility 93 get him open. The 165 pounds decide whether he stays open when a defenseman turns, finds him, and leans.

A coach with that player in the lineup has two options. Play him in space, away from the boards, and let the speed create separation before contact arrives. Or put him on the power play, where Shot Power 89 and Deking 86 create danger without a body in the way. At 17.7 minutes a night, the coach is doing both. The penalty minutes suggest the shelter is working: 8 in 16 games, down from 13 across 82 games last season.

Among the 2002 class

Five first-rounders from the 2002 draft carry league jobs today. Rick Nash85, 1st overall, sits at 85 in the Book with 8 points in 16 games for Toronto Maple Leafs. Jay Bouwmeester83, 3rd overall, carries 83 at Florida Panthers. Steve Eminger82, 12th, holds 82. Bouchard's Index-boosted 79 places him 4th of the 5, ahead of Scottie Upshall78 at 78. The Bureau has Bouchard's ceiling at 88. Nash carries 85 as his current grade. A player tracking toward 88 at 21 is not the player who signed for $1.20M.

14 grades and 0 years

The Bureau's ceiling for Bouchard is 88. His base sits at 74. There are 14 grades between them, the space the Bureau set aside for him. The Index has moved him 9 grades up from the base, to 79. At 21, that is a window. His 2003-04 campaign, his first full 82-game season at age 20, produced 14 goals and 29 points with 13 penalty minutes and no injuries. A clean track, a rising Index, and a ceiling that has not moved.

The contract reads $1.20M and 0 years left. Free agency is 257 days away. If the Index holds its position through the spring, if the minutes hold near 17.7, if the points line climbs into double digits, the $1.20M becomes a number no agent takes to a second meeting. The Minnesota Wild sit 4-7-3 with 15 points in the Northwest, 1-7 at home, 5-3 on the road, riding a 4-game winning streak. The 8th pick of 2002 is playing a real role at 21 on a team that wins away from its own building.
