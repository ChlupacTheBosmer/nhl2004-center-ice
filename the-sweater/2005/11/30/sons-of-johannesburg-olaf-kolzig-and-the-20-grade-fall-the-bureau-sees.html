Sons of Johannesburg: Olaf Kolzig and the 20-Grade Fall the Bureau Sees

The Vezina winner from the first season on record is 35, outside the top 20 in goals-against, and carries a ceiling the Bureau puts 20 grades below his base

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterWednesday, 30 November 2005
ShareCopy link

Olaf Kolzig89 was born in Johannesburg on 6 April 1970. That is the only South African birth on any roster in this league. The Washington Capitals selected him 19th overall in 1989, the first round, and sent him to Tri-City where he played the kind of junior hockey that produces goaltenders who score goals on November afternoons. Then came the minors. Several years in the minors.

He won the Jack A. Butterfield Trophy in 1994 as the AHL's playoff MVP with the Pirates, and the Hap Holmes Memorial Award the same year. Washington recalled him in 1995-96 to back up Jim Carey. The next season they traded for Bill Ranford, and Kolzig went back to the end of the bench. In the first game of 1997-98, Ranford got hurt. Kolzig dressed, won the job, and never gave it up.

A rink in a city where nobody plays hockey, to a Vezina Trophy on a shelf in a division where his team now sits last.

What the Book says now

The Bureau's current grade for Kolzig is 83. His base is 92, and the Development Index has him at -2, which puts the printed number six below where the base sits. His potential reads 72, which is 20 grades below his base. The Bureau projects a decline of that size between now and the end of his career. He is 35 years old on a $6.20M contract with 2 years left.

His strongest attributes are ENDU 83, REBC 83, BRKA 82, SPEE 82, SREC 82, 5HOL 81. Those are the grades of a goaltender who still has the tools. His weakest are CONS 50, STYL 50, TOUG 50. Consistency and style sit at the floor of the scale, and toughness does too. A goaltender whose style grade is 50 at 35 is not going to find a new one.

He played 74 games in 2003-04, won 43, gave up 218 goals across 4682 minutes, posted 3 shutouts. That season earned him the Vezina. This season he has played 19 games, won 6, lost 12, given up 51 goals across 1207 minutes, and logged 0 shutouts. He does not appear in the Bureau's top 20 goaltenders by goals-against rate in any qualifying sample.

What the room says

Washington Capitals is 6-12-3 through 23 games, 19 points, last in the Southeast. The team has surrendered 68 goals in 23 games while scoring 58. Jaromir Jagr90, at 84, is the best skater on the roster and has been out with a neck injury since between 2005-11-24 and 2005-11-26, due back 2005-12-01. Peter Bondra87 at 81 and Sergei Gonchar85 at 79 are the other men whose grade says they should be winning. They are not winning.

Josh Harding83, 21 years old, sits behind Kolzig at 77 overall. Harding is +8 on the Development Index. He has played 5 games this season and given up 10 goals in the time he has faced. The Bureau has him rising. The Bureau has Kolzig falling.

A man on a 2-year deal at $6.20M, with a ceiling the Bureau places 20 grades below his current number, is a man whose contract becomes a question the moment the younger goaltender is ready. Harding is not ready yet. He dressed 5 times in the first stretch of the season, which is not a workload that tells anyone anything about what comes next. But the Index says +8, and the age gap is 14 years, and the contract has 2 more years on it.

Kolzig's morale reads 83. It says he is here, he is playing, and he has not found a reason to be otherwise.

The man who was not supposed to be here

The background sheet says he played his first NHL game in 1989-90 and was sent down that same season. He spent four more years in the minors before the Capitals called him up to watch. He sat behind Carey, then Ranford, backup for two full seasons and parts of two more before an injury to another man handed him the crease.

He took the job from a man who got hurt and kept it for eight years. The 1998 All-Star Game, 14 saves on 17 shots. The line brawl in Boston on 28 November 1998 where he dropped the gloves with Tie Domi's teammate Dafoe. The Vezina.

Now the Bureau's Development Index has him at -2 and falling, his potential 20 below his base, and the team around him 19 points in 23 games. He has played every game Washington has needed him for since the record opened in December 2004. He has not been hurt once. There is no injury on the record for this man.

The slow arithmetic of a goaltender whose consistency and style grades sit at 50, who is 35 years old with 1207 minutes this season on a team that has let 68 goals in 23 games. That is what the Bureau believes is ahead. No injury, no trade, no demotion written down anywhere.

He was 19 when Washington picked him, 27 when he became the starter, and 35 now with the ceiling below the floor. The contract has 2 years left.
