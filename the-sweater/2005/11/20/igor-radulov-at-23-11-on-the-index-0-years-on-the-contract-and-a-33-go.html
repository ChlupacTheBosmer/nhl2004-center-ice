Igor Radulov, at 23: +11 on the Index, 0 Years on the Contract, and a 33-Goal Pace on the League's Worst Team

The 74th pick of 2000 went from 10.6 minutes a night to 19.9, scored 7 goals in 17 games, and now plays for July. The Bureau's ceiling says 77.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterSunday, 20 November 2005
ShareCopy link

Igor Radulov76 scored 1 goal in 82 games two seasons ago. This season he has 7 in 17. His ice time went from 10.6 minutes a night to 11.4 to 19.9, and Igor Radulov is sitting on the Development Index with +11, fourth among the league's risers, tied with Shaone Morrisonn78 of Boston Bruins. The Index has him at 74 overall, 11 above the Bureau's base grade of 71. The ceiling, per the Book, is 77.

He is 23. His contract pays $0.60M and has 0 years left.

Chicago Blackhawks is 2-10-4, dead last in the Central, 10 points, 11 straight losses, minus 20 on the goal differential. No one at Chicago Blackhawks is having a good season. The room is thin, the building is behind, and the GM said as much on The Tunnel this month: "We're getting outscored and we're losing enough to make the point over and over. The first period especially, at home, 1 and 8 in our own building."

Inside all of that, a third-round pick from 2000 is turning into something the Bureau never projected, or almost.

The road to Nizhny Tagil and back

Radulov came from Nizhny Tagil, in the Urals, the kind of industrial city that produces hockey players who are already men by the time a North American scout puts a number next to their name. The Bureau drafted him 74th overall in 2000, third round, a pick that lands in the dead zone of the draft: too high for a project, too low for a player anyone expects to see a lineup. The sheet carries no junior or European club before 2004, so the record opens with him already wearing a Chicago sweater. He arrived before December 1, 2004, and no transaction lists the date.

What the record does carry is three seasons. The first, 82 games, 1 goal, 2 assists, 14 penalty minutes, 10.6 minutes a shift-night. The second, 79 games, 8 goals, 4 assists, 12 points, 39 minutes in the box, 11.4 minutes a night. This season, 17 games, 7 goals, 3 assists, 10 points, 2 penalty minutes, 19.9 minutes a night.

That is 17.7 percent of the game in his first season, 33.2 percent of it now.

The grades tell a specific story

Radulov's strongest attribute in the Book is Puckhandling at 84. Four attributes sit at 79: Aggression, Deking, Faceoffs, Hands, Passing. His three weakest are Fighting 51, Penalty 55, and Blocking 58. He does not hit anybody, does not take penalties, and does not block shots. He is a small man (5 ft 7, 186 lb) with a left shot who carries the puck into a zone and gets out of trouble with his hands.

The Puck 84 is the number that matters on the Index. A man with Puck 84 and base 71 is a player the Bureau has been underselling at his role, and the coach has now given him enough ice to prove it. The potential grade of 77 says the Bureau still sees a bottom-six winger. The Index has him at 74, which is 3 below the ceiling.

He has played 6 playoff games, 0 points, 8.3 minutes a night. The head injury in the spring of 2005, a head that kept him out from sometime in late March through early April, cost him those final weeks of the 2004-05 regular season. He had 7 goals in 17 by then and a return date of April 10 that arrived with the season closing around him.

What $0.60M buys at 19.9 minutes

Radulov's salary is $0.60M. At 10 points in 17 games, he is costing his club $0.06M per point. The league's median forward on a roster spot earns more per game played than he earns per game played.

Chicago's payroll is spread across a room where nothing connects. The GM said it plainly on The Tunnel: "I've got a roster that's not good enough and a building that's behind."

Radulov's 10 points on $0.60M is the cheapest production on the club. Mark Bell80, at 78 in the Book, plays 22 minutes a night for more than three times what Radulov makes. Theoren Fleury82 and Steve Sullivan80, both at 80, both on larger deals, are not putting up the numbers a 74 on the Index is posting at this point in the season.

The 7 goals are real, and his goal pace through 17 games sits at 33.8 over 82, which would be a career year by a wide margin. The 1 goal in 82 two seasons ago is not the player he is, but the player he is has a ceiling of 77 in the Book, which means the Bureau would not move him up even if he scored 30.

The room around him

The best players on the roster are goaltenders. Jocelyn Thibault83 and Ari Ahonen85 both sit at 83 in the Book. No one in front of them grades above 81.

Eric Daze83 at 81 is the most experienced forward. Fleury at 80 and Sullivan at 80 round out the room. Mark Bell at 78 is the center on the first line. Torsten Schmitt69, the 17th pick of the 2004 draft, is on the roster but has not played a game. The back end has Deron Quint71 out until November 24 with a groin, and the GM told The Tunnel he has "four guys we trust and that's it."

Radulov is the fifth forward by overall grade on the club, and on the ice he is playing like the second.

What happens in the summer

The contract has 0 years left. He will be a free agent when the league's year ends. A 23-year-old left wing with a 74 overall, a 77 ceiling, Puck 84, and 7 goals in 17 games will get offers. Whether they come from a team that can win or from the same kind of club he is at now is the question.

His morale reads 89, which is high. A man who knows his contract is expiring, who is playing 20 minutes a night on a team that loses 11 straight, who has 1 goal in 82 one year and 7 in 17 the next, has found something the record cannot account for. The room at Chicago Blackhawks is the worst room in the league by the standings. He is in it, and he is better than the room.

The Bureau's Development Index will reset next edition. The +11 that has him sitting at 74 today was built on 17 games and 7 goals, a sample that is large enough to be real and small enough to be fragile. If he cools, the Index drops and the gap between his overall and his ceiling widens. If he keeps scoring, the Index stays, the ceiling holds, and someone else's payroll pays him for it in July.

At $0.60M, the bet is cheap for Chicago. At 77, the ceiling is low.
