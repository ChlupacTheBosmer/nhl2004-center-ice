The Long Way Round: Adam Oates, 43, and the 27-Grade Gap

The oldest man in the league won the Masterton last spring. The Bureau says his ceiling is 53 and his base is 80. He has three years left on $4.52M and morale at 100.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterSunday, 27 November 2005
ShareCopy link

The piece

The Bureau's Development Index puts Adam Oates77 at 77 today, two below his base of 80. His potential sits at 53. That is a 27-grade gap between what he is and what the model thinks he can become. Forty-three years old, three years left on a contract worth $4.52M, playing for Colorado Avalanche, which sits first in the Northwest with 26 points through 22 games.

Oates scored 2 goals and 9 points in 22 games this season, a 33.5 pace over 82. Last year he carried 71 points in 82 games at age 42, a pace of 71.0. He played 19.0 minutes a night in both seasons, and the team that dressed him the same minutes at 43 as it did at 42 tells you what they need from him: the puck moves through his stick on the second unit and he wins the draw that starts the shift.

His grades are blunt. Passing 89. Face-offs 89. Endurance 90. Heroics 95. Speed 79. Agility 81. Checking 50. Fighting 50. Toughness 50. The bottom three are the floor, the lowest number the Bureau prints. He will not hit you or fight you, and he does not care that you know it. What he will do is beat you to the puck on a draw, carry it across the blue line at 79 speed, and thread it into a lane that nobody else on the ice can see.

The gas station and the lacrosse box

Oates was born in Weston, a neighbourhood in Toronto, in 1962. He played box lacrosse for the Etobicoke Eclipse of the Ontario Lacrosse Association, scoring 181 points in 19 games in 1981, the 11th-highest total in that league's junior history. He won the league's scoring award and once put up 19 assists and 29 points in a single game. After one season of Senior A with the Brampton Excelsiors, he quit lacrosse in 1984 to chase hockey full-time.

He dropped out of high school and pumped gas at 19. He played junior hockey for the Markham Waxers and scored 159 points in 1981-82, including 105 assists. No NHL club drafted him. The scouts said he was too slow.

RPI's assistant coach Paul Allen saw him during a 1982 game while scouting someone else. Oates went to Troy, New York, and spent three seasons with the Engineers. He led the team in assists every year. In 1983-84 he set school records in points (83) and assists (57). In 1984-85 he broke them: 60 assists, 91 points. The Engineers went 85-19-1 in his three years. A two-time NCAA All-American, a Hobey Baker finalist, the 1985 tournament's all-star team. Still undrafted.

Five NHL clubs tried to sign him that summer. He took Detroit's offer: $1.1 million over four years, the richest rookie contract in the league in 1985-86. He scored on his first shift in his first NHL game against the Minnesota North Stars on October 10, 1985.

That was 20 years ago.

What the Masterton paid for

The League gave Oates the Bill Masterton Memorial Trophy last spring, awarded for perseverance and inspiration. The trophy came from a 42-year-old man who put up 71 points on 19.0 minutes a night, who had been told at 19 that he was too slow for this game, and who kept doing it for twenty more years.

No injuries on record. Morale reads 100. No draft year, no pick number, no round. The record does not carry a transaction that put him in Colorado; he was already there when it opened.

His teammates this season: Peter Forsberg95 at 95, Paul Kariya89 at 89, Joe Sakic88 at 88, Milan Hejduk88 at 88. A 43-year-old center on the third line, taking draws against men half his age, winning them at a rate the Bureau grades 89.

The three years

$4.52M and 3 years left. The Bureau's potential grade of 53 means the model thinks his body will collapse from 80 to 53 within the term. No injuries. He has played 22 games this season without missing one. Last year he played 82. The year before that he played 68 and then 24 more in the playoffs.

COL sits first in the Northwest. Oates plays 19.0 minutes, takes face-offs, wins them, and passes the puck. His Speed is 79 and the players around him are faster, but speed is not the whole game at 43, and the room knows it. The contract reflects that: a number set by what he gives you, which is the draw, the pass, and the 90 Endurance that lets him do it at 19 a night without fading.

The Index says -2. The pace is half of last year. The goals are 2 in 22. Under all of that, a kid from Weston who pumped gas and played box lacrosse in a building that smelled like sweat and rosin, who was told he was too slow, who spent one summer with a skating instructor, and who is still here at 43, three years into a deal that runs through the end of this decade, with the room's morale reading 100 and the Bureau's model giving up on him by 27 grades.

He does not hit, he does not fight, he does not check. He wins the draw, moves the puck, and goes to the next shift.
