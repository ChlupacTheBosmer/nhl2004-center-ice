The Masterton at 34: Tony Amonte plays 20 a night for a room that has won 16

He won the trophy for perseverance last spring. The Flyers are 16-21-6, the Bureau has his potential at 56, and he has 3 years left at $5.90M.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterWednesday, 26 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Tony Amonte81 turned 34 in August, won the Bill Masterton in June, and walked into training camp knowing the Flyers had finished 40-29 last April and would not look different this time around. They have won 16 games in 48. He has 71 points in 48.

The pace is 121.3 points over 82. The Development Index sits at minus 2, which puts his overall at 81 against a base of 88. The Bureau has his potential at 56. A man at 34, $5.90M a year, 3 years left on the deal, and those are the grades the organization has on him. He is playing good hockey, the room is not winning, and the projection desk says he won't get higher.

Amonte grew up in Hingham, Massachusetts, a town on the South Shore where the rink is the town square. He was 68th overall in 1988, third round, taken by the Rangers. He played three seasons in New York, scored 84 goals, and was traded to Chicago with 7 games left in 1993-94. The Rangers won the Stanley Cup that June without him.

He spent a decade in Chicago, became the kind of player you build a top line around, and played for Team USA in the 1996 World Cup of Hockey. Gold medal, final against Canada. He was 25. After the lockout he signed with Phoenix, lasted half a season, and was traded to Philadelphia near the end of 2002-03. He has been a Flyer ever since. The record opened in December 2004 and he was already in the room.

What the record says about the body

The grades tell you what he is now. A 91 on the deke, an 86 on the pass, an 86 on the puck. He is 72 inches and 200 pounds and shoots left and plays 20.0 minutes a night. His faceoff grade is 50, his fighting grade is 50, his puck battle on the board is 53. He doesn't win draws or drop the gloves or win many puck races along the boards. The 91 deke is the number the rest is built on: receive the puck in space and do something with it the defense cannot read.

Morale reads 94. That is high for a man on a 16-win team with $5.90M on his back and 3 years before he can leave. He is not going to ask out. He will play his 20 minutes, score his points, and the Flyers will carry that contract until it runs.

The shape of the year is the same. Slightly fewer goals, slightly more assists, a hair less ice time. The 118 last season made him one of the best forwards on a team that missed the second round. The 121.3 pace this season has him on the same footing and the team has slid from 40 wins to 16.

The room he is in

Philadelphia is 14-10 at home and 7-17 on the road. The road record is the worst in the Atlantic division and one of the four worst in the league. Jeremy Roenick85 is 85 overall and plays center on a line that carries the first unit. Simon Gagne82 is 24, plays left wing, and sits at 82. Keith Primeau80 and John LeClair81 round out a core that is built around men Amonte's age or older. Michal Handzus81 is 81 at center on the second line. The talent is real. The wins are not coming.

Amonte has never played on a team that won the Cup. The Rangers won it 7 games after trading him. He spent a decade in Chicago watching them miss the playoffs. Phoenix lasted half a season. Philadelphia has won 16 games this year. The Masterton is for perseverance and dedication to hockey. You can win that trophy on a team that has won 16 games. You cannot win the other one.

What the Index says and what it means

The Development Index reads the gap between what a player is doing and what his base grade would predict. At minus 2, Amonte is playing slightly below what a player with an 88 base should produce. That is not a collapse. It is the sound a 34-year-old body makes when the legs take a half-step back. The potential at 56 is the Bureau's long-range read. At 34, it means the organization sees no path back up. The question from here is how long he can keep being it.

He has 3 years left. $5.90M a year. If the Flyers miss the playoffs again, which the standings say they will, that contract has 2 more seasons behind it. No team will take the full cap hit at his age. The deal is not a problem this year. It will be a problem later.

For now he plays 20 minutes and puts pucks in nets at a clip that would make men ten years younger take notes. They walk off a 7-17 road into a room that is not going to change its shape before June. The Masterton sits on a shelf in Hingham or in a den outside Philadelphia or wherever he keeps it. The 121.3 pace tells him what he is doing.
