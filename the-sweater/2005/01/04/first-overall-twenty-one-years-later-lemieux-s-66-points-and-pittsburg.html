First Overall, Twenty-One Years Later: Lemieux's 66 Points and Pittsburgh's 33

He is scoring at a 169-point pace at 39 on a team that has won 10 games, and the Index still says he is below his base.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterTuesday, 4 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Mario Lemieux88 is 39 years old. He has played 32 games this season and put up 66 points. The scoring leaders page puts his pace at 169. The Pittsburgh Penguins have won 10 games. They sit at 33 points, last in the Atlantic, 5 points behind the New York Islanders who are one spot above them. The gap between the man and the room goes back to the night in 1984 when he would not shake Eddie Johnston's hand.

He told the room he was not coming. He told the building, the one that drew fewer than 7,000 people a night before he arrived, that Pittsburgh didn't want him bad enough. Twenty-one winters later he is still here. $9.90M a year, 5 more on the deal. The base of his Book grade is 92. The Development Index has him at minus one, so the overall reads 86 today. He is below his base, by the Bureau's measure, while scoring at a pace only Bill Guerin89 and Jaromir Jagr83 can match among the league's top ten.

Mario Lemieux skates 19.9 minutes a night. Last season he skated 21.8 and finished with 150 points in 82 games. This year the coach has taken almost two minutes off his evening and the points-per-game have gone up. He has 66 in 32 against 150 in 82. The speed grade reads 89. The passing grade reads 97. The deke is 96. The fight is 50. The toughness is 59. None of those numbers moved. The minutes moved, and the pace went up anyway.

He played 82 games last winter at 38. Bill Guerin is 34 and putting up 84 points in 41 games. Mike Modano90 is 34 and has 75 in 37. Brett Hull82 is 40 and has 64 in 38. Lemieux is one year younger than Hull and scoring at a 169 pace against Hull's 138.

The room around him

The Penguins' best forward in the Book is Martin Straka85, at 85, who is on the injured list with a wrist and returns January 27. The second-best is a goaltender, Johan Hedberg85, at 85, who has played 11 games. Aleksey Morozov79 carries a 78. The next three men the desk lists as Lemieux's best teammates wear 75 and 77. This is a room that needs him to carry every shift, and the coach is taking his minutes down anyway, because he is 39.

The Index says he is below base. That is what the form term means. The base of 92 is the grade the Bureau believes he should carry if everything were even. The minus one says something is off. It could be the back-to-backs, or the four injuries the record shows this winter, each one a day or two, body parts not designated. It could be that he is 39 and the legs do not come back the same way at midnight in a building where the crowd is thinner than it used to be.

The pace is 169. The team is 33 points.

Ville-Émard

He grew up in a working-class district in Montreal. Kitchen spoons and bottle caps in the basement. His father packed snow onto the living-room carpet when it was too dark to go outside. By 15 he was in the QMJHL and said he would break every record there. He scored 562 points in 200 games and then told a city that he was not sure he wanted to play for them.

Johnston gave him $600,000 and a $150,000 signing bonus. The Penguins were averaging fewer than 7,000 fans. By the time he arrived, the building was full.

Now the building is full for a man who is 39, who shoots right, who is 76 inches tall and 225 pounds, whose hero grade is 94, whose fight grade is 50. The man who could not be bought by a handshake in 1984 is on a five-year deal at $9.90M a year. The team around him has lost 19 games in 38. He has not lost a step the Bureau can see, except the one the Index sees, and that one is a single point below a base of 92 in a league where the next-highest overall on Pittsburgh's roster is 85.

What the record says

Lemieux has been on the injured list four times in five weeks. December 8 to 11, December 15 to 16, December 24 to 28, January 1 to 3. Each time, a day or two back. No body part designated. He has played 32 of the Penguins' 38 games.

The standings say 33 points, the contract says 5 more years, the Index says he is below base. He is 39 and he plays 19.9 minutes and his deke is 96 and his fight is 50 and the building is loud when he touches the puck in the third period. He did not shake the GM's hand. He is still here, with more money and a team that cannot keep up.
