20 Points, Then 92: The one-summer education of Mike Cammalleri

A second-round pick who skipped major junior for Michigan arrives at 22 on pace for 92 points, wearing an 'A', on a Kings team that has won 13 games

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterTuesday, 18 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Mike Cammalleri81 scored 20 points in 69 games last season. He is on pace for 92 this season. One offseason, and an "A" on his sweater at 22 years old.

The Bureau's Development Index has him at +6, the same reading as Marian Gaborik99 and Mike Komisarek86, the two men this desk profiled in January. Cammalleri's base grade reads 79. The Index pushes the current overall to 83, and the potential grade underneath reads 98. He is the third riser on this edition of the Index at +6, and the only one whose club has won fewer games than a half-season.

Last year: 11 goals, 9 assists, 20 points in 69 games, 11.8 minutes a night. This year: 12 goals, 15 assists, 27 points in 24 games, 20.8 minutes a night. Nine minutes of ice time appeared between October and January. His 12 goals in 24 games put him on a 41-goal pace. His 15 assists put him on a 51-assist pace. He scores 7.9 percent of Los Angeles Kings's 151 goals.

The grade profile explains the minutes. His deking reads 93, his passing reads 93, his puck control 90, his agility 89, his speed 89. His fight grade reads 50. He is a 69-inch, 180-pound left winger who cannot win a puck along the boards and can put the puck in a window from 40 feet. The ice time this season reflects what the coaching staff believes that profile is worth when the game opens up.

The road that produced a 93 deking grade and a 50 fight grade

Cammalleri grew up in Richmond Hill, Ontario, north of Toronto. His best sport as a youth was soccer. He played minor hockey for the Toronto Red Wings in the Greater Toronto Hockey League and attended the 1996 Quebec International Pee-Wee Tournament. When the OHL's St. Michael's Majors drafted him, he declined. He wanted to preserve his American college eligibility.

He joined the Bramalea Blues in Junior A, the Ontario Provincial Junior Hockey League, at 15. He won the league's rookie of the year. The following season he scored 103 points in 41 games and was named the Ontario Hockey Association's top draft prospect. He had already committed to the University of Michigan on a full scholarship, also at 15. He took an accelerated schedule at The Country Day School in King City, Ontario, and graduated at 17.

On October 1, 2001, he played in the "Cold War" against Michigan State University, an outdoor game at Spartan Stadium that drew 74,554 fans, a world record for hockey attendance. He nearly sat it out with a hip-flexor injury. He figured in all three Michigan goals in a 3-3 draw. That winter he scored 23 goals and 44 points in 29 games, led the Wolverines to the CCHA championship, and was named an NCAA first-team All-American. The West Regional named him most valuable player.

The Los Angeles Kings Kings had drafted him the summer before, 49th overall, second round. He stayed in Ann Arbor anyway. When he came north, he arrived as an alternate captain at 22.

The room, the contract, and July

Cammalleri earns $1.10M this season. He has one year left. He turns 23 in June. The room around him reads like a transitional roster: Zigmund Palffy81, 32, at a 87 overall in the Book. Jason Allison80, 87. Adam Deadmarsh81, 87, out with a groin injury through January 21. Roman Cechmanek86 in net at a 91. Alexander Frolov73, 22, out with a neck injury through February 1.

Los Angeles Kings sits 13-21-5 with 43 points, last in the Pacific Division, on a five-game losing streak that stretches from January 7 to January 16. Before that streak, they had won five straight from December 29 to January 6. Cammalleri's morale reads 98, near the top of the Bureau's scale. You do not read a 98 in a locker room that has lost five straight unless the personal season feels like something the record cannot measure.

The Index at +6 puts Cammalleri alongside Gaborik at 96 overall and Komisarek at 80. Gaborik plays on the best team in the East, 34-8-1, with Mats Sundin92 and Brad Richards86 drawing the attention. Komisarek went from 3 points in 24 games to 55 in 46 on a rebuilding club that handed him the top pairing without ceremony. Cammalleri plays on a team that has lost five straight, has two of its top forwards on the injured list, and sits last in its division. The 92.3 pace is real. The team is not.

What his family built

His father Leo is Sicilian and Catholic. His mother Ruth is Jewish. Her parents survived the camps in Poland and Czechoslovakia. He has talked about closeness built through hardship. He chose Junior A over the OHL at 15. He chose Michigan over the majors at 16. He chose to stay in Ann Arbor after a second-round draft pick at 19.

The Book has him at 83. The pace says 92.3. His contract expires in July. He is 22 years old, 69 inches tall, 180 pounds, and plays the best hockey he has ever played in a building that loses more games than it wins.
