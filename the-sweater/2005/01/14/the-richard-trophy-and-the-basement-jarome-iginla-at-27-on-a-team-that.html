The Richard Trophy and the Basement: Jarome Iginla at 27, on a team that has won 13

Last season he scored 92 goals and won the Maurice Richard. This season he has 39 in 37. Calgary has won 13 games in 43.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterFriday, 14 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Jarome Iginla89 is 27 years old, sits 91st in the Book, and has scored 39 goals in 37 games for a Calgary club that has won 13. The Maurice Richard Trophy, the one he won last season for 92 goals in 73 games, is in the organization somewhere. It has not made the record any better.

That gap is this winter in Calgary. Iginla is playing at the level the Bureau predicted for him since 1995, when he was the 11th pick of the first round out of St. Andrew's College in Aurora, Ontario. The Development Index has him one above his base, a modest steady-state rise. He is 91 in a room that has scored 135 goals in 43 games. He has scored 39 of those 135.

Calgary sits 13-23-6, 34 points, the fewest in the Northwest Division, tied with Chicago for the fewest in the West. The home record is 7-15. The road record is 7-14. They are equally unable to win either place. The team has been outscored 171 to 135, a 36-goal deficit across 43 games.

Last season Calgary won 26 and lost 41, 73 points, 268 goals for, 296 against. They did not make the playoffs. The year before that, the year Iginla turned 26, he scored 92 goals and the club still finished at 73 points. The trophy did not change the team. It changed the record book. It gave a room in Calgary a reason to say the name of their captain in the same sentence as the names of players on teams that play in April.

Iginla was born in Edmonton and raised in St. Andrew's, Ontario, and was drafted 11th overall in 1995 by a club the record does not name. By 22 he was in Calgary full-time. By 25 he was the man scoring 48 goals. By 26 he scored 92 and the Maurice Richard came home. None of that, at this scale, produced wins around him.

The Bureau's Book reads 91 overall for Iginla at 27. He is the fourth-highest-graded player on the Calgary roster, which tells you the problem. A man graded 91 is carrying a team whose other pieces do not climb above the low 80s, and whose net has been breached 171 times. His 39 are nearly one third of a team goal total of 135 in 43. The other 96 goals come from everyone else. The team's goal differential of minus-36 is not something that one man who scores 39 fixes, because the goals against are not his responsibility.

The men who play with him say he does not change his face. They say he shows up at 7 in the morning and leaves at 5, that the building is quiet except for his skates on the ice before the first line comes out. They say the 13 wins are not his fault, that they know he knows that, and that it does not make the 23 losses easier to carry.

39 in 37 is ahead of last year's pace. He is not declining. He wears the C on a club that has scored 135 goals in 43 games and given up 171, and the arithmetic of that gap is 13 wins.

The Index has him at plus-one. The Book has him at 91. He is 27 and the team is at 34 points in January with 39 games left. The record books will remember him as the man who scored 39 on a team that could not stop anything. At 91, at 27, with a pace that puts him near 140 if the legs hold, that is the shape of it. The structure around him has collapsed, 171 goals have gone past the goaltenders, and he is still the best thing on the ice.

The other clubs in the Northwest, Colorado at 57, Vancouver at 57, Edmonton at 57, are all ahead. Minnesota at 38 is ahead. Calgary is last in the division with 34 and the closest thing to a moral victory is the two-game home win streak they will not get this week. They are on the road, they are 13 and 23 and 6.

Iginla turned 27 this season. He will turn 28 next June. The contract the Bureau's Ledger shows is not expired. The Development Index will move, the Book will adjust, and Calgary will either get better around him or it will not. The Richard is on the shelf. The wins are not in the record.
