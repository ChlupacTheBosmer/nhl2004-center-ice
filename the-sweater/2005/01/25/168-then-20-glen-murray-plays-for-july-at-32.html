168, Then 20: Glen Murray plays for July at 32

The Art Ross winner has played 14 games this season. His $3.70M expires in July, the Bureau has his potential at 61, and the room around him is missing its two best skaters.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterTuesday, 25 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Glen Murray85 scored 168 points last season and won the Art Ross. He has played 14 games this one, scored 20 points, and is 32. His $3.70M contract has 0 years left.

The Glen Murray of the Boston Bruins went down with an arm injury by December 1 and returned on January 15. Boston has played 48 games. Murray has played 14. He is on the ice 20.6 minutes a night now, down from the 22.3 that carried him through all 82 games during the 168-point season.

The Bureau's Development Index has him at 0 on the form term. His overall reads 85, three below his base of 88. His potential is 61. The potential sits 27 points below the base. That is the Book's arithmetic on a player who had his best year at 31 and is trying to get back on the ice at 32.

Five years in Los Angeles, then the trade that worked

Glen Murray was born in Halifax, Nova Scotia, and the Bruins took him 18th overall in the 1991 draft. He spent five seasons in Los Angeles, scoring at a rate that never put his name in the top ten in the league, before Boston traded for him on October 24, 2001, sending Jason Allison and Mikko Eloranta67 to the Kings for Murray and Jozef Stümpel. He signed a two-year deal in July 2002, scored 92 points in 2002-03, and went to the All-Star Game. The 168 came the year after.

Glen Murray put up 68 goals and 100 assists last season, the most points in the league. He took 23 penalty minutes in 82 games and won the Lady Byng alongside the Art Ross. The Book has his fighting grade at 50. The Lady Byng is what that grade looks like when everything else is there and you still played every shift of all 82 games: shot placement 95, speed 90, passing 86, endurance 94.

The room without its top two skaters

Joe Thornton91, Boston's 91-overall center, has been out with a hand since between January 19 and January 22 and will not return until March 5. Sergei Samsonov87, the 87-overall left wing who had 152 points last season alongside Glen Murray, is out with a groin and is due back on January 30. The Bruins are 19-20-4, fifth in the Northeast, and Murray is the most decorated player still in the lineup.

Head coach Mike Sullivan told The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk after the January 11 win over Toronto about the gap between Boston's home record and its road record: "Maybe we press. Maybe the crowd, the building, there's a different expectation and the guys try to do too much instead of just playing their game. On the road, nobody expects anything, you just go out and compete and it works." Boston is 9-15 at home, 15-9 on the road.

What the Book says at 32

The injury grade is 51. The durability is not there. 82 games last season, 14 in a season that should have given him 80. The potential is 61. The base is 88. At 32, with $3.70M expiring in July, the question Boston faces in the summer is whether the version that walks into an offseason at 33, with an arm that gave out by December, is worth the money. Everyone wants last spring's version. The money has to be paid for this one.

What Murray might say after a practice, if you asked him about the year after the best one: You work your whole career for one year like that. You think, this is it, this is why you came back, why you signed in July. And then your arm is done by December and you're watching from the bench in a building where the crowd can't breathe and the game is tight and you can't help. And then you're 32 and the contract year is here and you try to get it back, shift by shift.
