40 Goals, 21 Assists: David Legwand swapped his game at 24, and Nashville leads the Central

The 1998 second-overall pick went from a center who passes to a center who finishes, one offseason, no warning

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterSaturday, 8 January 2005
ShareCopy link

David Legwand90 scored 22 goals and 37 assists last season. This season, at 24, he has 40 goals and 21 assists in 40 games. The point total moved from 59 to 61. The shape underneath it changed completely.

A center who was a 0.54 goals-per-game player is now a 1.00 goals-per-game player. A center who was a 0.90 assists-per-game player is now 0.53. The Bureau's Development Index has him up three. His morale sits at 97.

Speed 98, deke 91, puck control 91. Those grades were on the sheet in October and they were on the sheet last February. What changed is a decision: he shoots now.

The Detroit road, the Nashville trade

Legwand grew up in Grosse Pointe, Michigan, went through the Detroit Little Caesars Pee-Wee program, played at Grosse Pointe North High School, then went to the Plymouth Whalers in the OHL. In 1997-98, the season before he was eligible for the draft, he put up 54 goals and 51 assists, 105 points, and won the Red Tilson Award as the league's most outstanding player.

David Poile, Nashville's general manager, traded the Predators' first and second-round picks to San Jose to get the second and 85th picks. Legwand went second overall, 1998. Then mononucleosis hit during training camp and he went back to Plymouth for the 1998-99 OHL season. He came back at 19, a small growth spurt, coaches asking him to put on weight.

On December 23, 2000, against the New York Rangers, he became the first player in league history to score on a penalty shot in overtime. He was 20.

What the Book says

The grades tell you what kind of athlete he is: 98 speed, 93 agility, 92 acceleration, 93 hero. A player built for space and timing. His fighting grade is 50, his spin-behind 55, his open-ice battle 60. He does not win pucks in corners or finish checks. He goes around you.

Last season, a man with those physical grades played the way his 37 assists suggest. He entered the zone, drew the defense, found the winger. The 22 goals were the ones that came anyway. This season, the order reversed. He carries it in, he makes the first move himself, and the shot is the first option instead of the last one.

His minutes went up, too: 21.5 a night last season, 23.5 this season. Two more minutes, and the goal total nearly doubled. He decided to shoot.

The room at Nashville

Nashville is 23-10-4, 56 points, first in the Central Division. They score 4.03 a game and give up 3.35. The Predators were 38-30 last season, a playoff team that played 10 postseason games. This year they are better in both directions, and the team's leading scorer is the 24-year-old center who was supposed to be a playmaker.

Tomas Vokoun92, the goaltender, is 90 in the Book and the best teammate on the roster. Scott Walker85 is 82 at right wing. Greg Johnson82, at 80, is on the injured list with a neck injury and a firm return date of 2005-01-11. The depth behind Legwand is fine without being exceptional.

He has $8.37M in salary left, two more years. He is playing the best hockey of his life on a team that is suddenly good enough to make him matter in April.

The pace

At his current rate, Legwand is on a 125-point pace. Last season his 59 in 41 was a 118 pace, which was already a career high for him. The jump from 118 to 125 is small. The jump from a center who passes to a center who scores is not. The 40 goals in 40 games put him on an 82-goal pace over a full season. That is a number that belongs to the Richard Trophy conversations, the ones Legwand was never part of before.

The Index says plus three. The Book says 87. A player with 98 speed and a 91 deke decided, somewhere between the last playoff game and the first practice this fall, to shoot instead of pass. The room around him went from a 38-win team to a first-place team in January. The shape of his own game changed from setup to finish.

He is 24. He was the second player taken in 1998. Five years, a bout with mono, a record-book penalty shot, a growth spurt, and a division title in progress. The man Poile traded two picks for is having, at last, the season the trade promised.
