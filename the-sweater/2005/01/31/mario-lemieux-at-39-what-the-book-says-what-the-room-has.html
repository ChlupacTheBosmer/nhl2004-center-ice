Mario Lemieux, at 39: what the Book says, what the room has

The highest passing grade and the lowest fighting grade on one sheet, $9.90M with 5 years left, and Pittsburgh in fourth place in the Atlantic.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterMonday, 31 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Mario Lemieux88 is 39. The Book gives him 99 on the pass and 98 on the deke. It gives him 50 on the fight and 52 on his speed back. No other man in this league carries those numbers on the same card.

He has played 42 games this season for the Pittsburgh Penguins Penguins. His pace reads 156 points over 82, second only to Markus Naslund85's 158. He is earning $9.90M a year with 5 years remaining on that contract. Pittsburgh has won 16 games.

The Bureau's Development Index has him at minus 1 on the base this edition, putting his overall at 88 against a base of 92. His potential sits at 61.

The man from Ville-Émard

His father, Jean-Guy, was an engineer. His mother, Pierrette, stayed home. They raised three boys in a working-class street in Ville-Émard, a district in Montreal's southwest end. The kitchen spoons came first, then the bottle caps, then a rink packed into the front lawn when the boys outgrew the basement. By 15 he was playing for the Laval Voisins in the QMJHL and telling anyone who asked that he would break the league's records.

He did. 562 points in 200 games across three seasons, 2.81 a night. The Penguins took him first overall in 1984. He did not shake Eddie Johnston's hand or put on their sweater for the photo. He told the room that Pittsburgh did not want him badly enough, then signed for $600,000.

Twenty-one years after that handshake he never gave, he is still there. The record has him on Pittsburgh's roster from before it opened in December, with no arrival date. He has been there so long that the record does not know when he started.

What the grades say at 39

The strongest and weakest numbers on his card tell you exactly what he has become. PASS 99. DEKG 98. HERO 96. PUCK 95. SPEE 91. SHPW 90. His speed is still 91. His shot power is 90. When he separates from a defender in open ice, the puck leaves fast.

Then: FIGH 50. SPBI 52. PCBI 61. He does not fight. He is slow getting back. He does not finish checks. The Bureau is telling you that this man is a pure skill player whose body has stopped cooperating with everything except the puck.

He played 21.8 minutes a night last season. This season he is at 19.7. That is two minutes a game he does not get, or does not want. In those 19.7 minutes he is producing 1.90 points a game, up from 1.83 last year. The Bureau calls that pace 156. Only Naslund is higher.

The room

Pittsburgh is 16-23-5 through 51 games, 51 points, fourth in the Atlantic. They have scored 179 and allowed 199. They sit 1 point ahead of fifth-place Philadelphia Flyers's 50. They are not a bad team. They are not close to a good one.

Behind Lemieux, the best player on the roster is Johan Hedberg85 at 87, the goaltender. Then Martin Straka85 at 85 on the left wing. Aleksey Morozov78 at 79. Eric Meloche77, who won the Calder last spring, at 77. A room built around a 39-year-old center who cannot fight and a goaltender who is the best player on the roster by grade. The Penguins have 4 men on the injured list, including Kris Beech70 (groin, out until March 3) and Tomas Surovy72 (back, out until February 9).

The last two weeks produced a 2-0 shutout at New York on January 28 and a 4-3 home win over Chicago on January 25. Pittsburgh is on a two-game win streak. It does not matter for the standings. It matters for the next ten games, which decide whether this is a 42-point room or a 55-point room.

The contract

$9.90M with 5 years left. The Bureau's potential says 61. If the model is right, Pittsburgh is paying a 9.9-million-dollar salary for a player the same Bureau will rate 61 within a few years. Five years of $9.90M at 39 through 43 is a number that makes a room hold its breath.

His morale is 96. The rib that sat him between January 19 and January 22 is gone; he returned on January 23 and has played since. He is not asking for a trade. The room does not seem to want one.

What the room says

A man who played 150 points last season, who has 80 in 42 this year, who passes at 99 and dekes at 98 and cannot finish a check to save his life, does not need to explain himself in a locker room. The numbers explain him. What he does in those 19.7 minutes is what Pittsburgh will watch until April.

Ed Taylor talks about Marian Gaborik97's future in Toronto. Nobody in Pittsburgh is talking about Mario Lemieux's future, because the contract has 5 years and the potential has a number that makes the talking pointless. What is there to say? He is here, 39, the best passer alive and the worst fighter. The Penguins pay him $9.90M to be both.

His pace is 156. His room has won 16. The difference between those two numbers is the only story in Pittsburgh this February.
