Fourth Season, Best Season: Dany Heatley at 23, on a team that has won 14

39 goals, a $1.10M contract expiring in July, the best hockey of his life, and the worst room in the East

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterWednesday, 19 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Dany Heatley90 has 39 goals and 75 points in 42 games, a 146-point pace that sits above his previous career best. He is 23. He plays for the Atlanta Thrashers, who have won 14 games. His contract pays $1.10M and expires with the season.

The best he has played, in the worst room in the East. The Development Index has him up 1, a small gain for a man the Book rates at 90 with a 98 speed and a 97 shot power.

The road out of Germany

Heatley was born in Freiburg on January 21, 1981, to Karin and Murray Heatley. His father played professional hockey in the German leagues. The family moved to Calgary when Dany was young enough to grow up inside the Alberta minor hockey system. He played midget for the Calgary Buffaloes, scoring 91 points in 36 games in 1997-98 and winning the Harry Allen Memorial Trophy as the league's top scorer. He led the Buffaloes to a bronze medal at the 1998 Air Canada Cup as Top Scorer and Tournament MVP.

He did not play major junior. He wanted college hockey in the United States, which meant staying eligible, which meant junior A. One season with the Calgary Canucks of the Alberta Junior Hockey League: 70 goals, 126 points in 60 games, the league's player of the year award, Canadian Junior A player of the year. Then the University of Wisconsin.

At Wisconsin he was the WCHA Rookie of the Year as a freshman and made the conference's first All-Star team. As a sophomore he earned WCHA second All-Star honours and NCAA West first All-American recognition. He left Madison after two seasons with two years of eligibility unspent. The Atlanta Thrashers took him second overall in the 2000 draft, behind Rick DiPietro82.

The Calder year, the star year, the crash

His first NHL season, 2001-02, ended with the Calder Memorial Trophy. He led all rookies with 67 points and finished second in Atlanta's scoring behind Ilya Kovalchuk92. He was 21. He won the award Kovalchuk was nominated for.

The following year he became a star. 41 goals, 89 points in 77 games, ninth in league scoring at 22 years old. The ceiling looked like 100 points in a year.

September 2003. A car accident. Heatley was hurt. His teammate Dan Snyder was not. Snyder died. Heatley's season did not start until January. He played 39 games and scored 52 points. The Thrashers missed the playoffs.

An orbital bone fracture from an errant puck left his left pupil permanently dilated. He played through that eye the rest of that winter, through the summer, through this season. You can see it when he scores and raises his arms: the left eye is not quite what the right eye is. He has not hidden it. Nobody in the Atlanta locker room treats it as a story anymore.

What the Book says

The grades read like a blueprint for a right wing nobody wants to chase down the wing. Speed 98. Shot power 97. Hero 95. Hands 94. Endurance 92. Acceleration 90. A forward who can separate a defenseman from the puck with one stride, beat you from the slot or the half-wall, and keep doing it on the third shift of a period.

The weaknesses are on the sheet too. Fighting 50. Faceoffs 52. Durability 55. He will not drop the gloves, win a draw in his own end, or take a cross-check and stay on his feet. Those grades shape what a team can ask of him in a playoff series, and Atlanta has not played a playoff series since he arrived.

His base grade is 91. His potential reads 86, below his base. The Index has him at +1, the smallest gain among the men rated 85 or higher this winter. He is at full volume for the first time since the crash.

The room around him

Kovalchuk has 82 points on a 149 pace. Heatley has 75 on a 146. They are first and second on Atlanta's scoring table, the two men who went first and second in the 2001 draft, carrying a club that has not been above .500 all winter.

Atlanta has scored 166 goals and allowed 185. The 14 wins are in the record. The 25 losses are in the record. Nothing in that column supports a team that deserves either of its top two scorers. Behind Kovalchuk and Heatley, Patrik Stefan has 36 points and Sami Kapanen82 has what the numbers give him.

What the room would say

A teammate who shares a line with a man the Book rates 98 on speed, asked what it is like trying to keep up, said it differently than the numbers would: you just go where you think he will be, and most nights you are close. He gets the puck and the whole game changes, everyone in the building stands up and you are already skating before you know where the pass is going. If you are lucky you get it on the tape. If you are not, he scores and you feel like you watched a game from the bench.

$1.10M and July

The trade deadline is 59 days away. Atlanta sits dead last in the Southeast Division. The Atlanta Thrashers are not making the playoffs. What they have is a 23-year-old right wing with 39 goals and a contract that expires in six months, and the choice is to trade him for something that might help later or let him sign somewhere that can afford what he is worth.

$1.10M is the number on his deal. His morale reads 92, which is high for a man on a 14-win team in a contract year. It says he likes his room, likes his city, likes his game. It does not mean he likes the number.

He will not be cheap in July. A 23-year-old on a 146 pace does not take $1.10M twice.
