52 Goals, 21 Assists: Sergei Fedorov stopped passing at 35

The Pearson winner has the highest goal total in the league and the fewest assists he has ever had. The shape of his game changed in plain sight and nobody wrote it down.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterSaturday, 22 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Sergei Fedorov90 has 52 goals in 47 games for Mighty Ducks of Anaheim. That is 1.11 a night. Nobody else on the league's scoring chart has more goals.

The Bureau's Development Index has him one point below base, overall at 90. His potential reads 61. The goal line says he is the most efficient scorer in the league on a club that has won 18 games.

Last season he scored 76 goals in 82, which is 0.93 a night, and set up 55 others at 0.67 a night. The composition was what you would expect from a center: a man who could finish and also make the pass. This year his assists have dropped to 21 in 47, a rate of 0.45. His goals have gone the other direction. He has stopped looking for the second man and started shooting at the first one. His points per game are almost unchanged, 1.55 to 1.60, but the shape of what he does has changed completely.

He is 35. Born in Pskov in December 1969, a garrison town inside the Soviet military district where the national team camped before the border opened. Detroit picked him 74th overall in the third round of 1989, two months after Alexander Mogilny86 walked out of the Soviet squad in Minneapolis. A Red Wings staffer met Fedorov and his friend Vladimir Konstantinov in August 1989 and handed each of them a media guide with a letter folded between the pages. Fedorov defected the following summer, in Portland, during the Goodwill Games, in July 1990. He was 20.

The background sheet carries the peak. The Hart Trophy in 1994, when Wayne Gretzky told a reporter he had never seen a player dominate a game the way Fedorov did, and then, later that same season, that Fedorov was the best player in the game. Two Stanley Cups with Detroit, 1997 and 1998. Five goals against Washington on December 26, 1996, every goal in the game, a 5-4 overtime win. The $38 million offer sheet from Carolina in February 1998, a contract that guaranteed him $12 million the moment his team reached the conference finals, a clause that gave Detroit a head start no other club could spend.

He won the Lester B. Pearson Award last season, the players' MVP, the one his peers picked. At 34 he scored 76 goals and 131 points and his fellow players said he was the best in the league. The Bureau's overall base of 94 agreed.

A 98 speed on a potential of 61

The Book still reads SPEE 98. SHPW 95, DEKG 94, ACCE 93, AGIL 93. A speed of 98 is not what you find on a man the Index has below base. Those are the numbers that let him separate from a defenseman at 35 the way he did at 25, the same gap the same way, the shot finding the same corner. The HERO grade is 92. The game comes to him in the stretches that matter, and he does not give it back.

What the Index reads, probably, is the row underneath. FIGH 50. INJU 51. SPBI 52. Fighting, durability, checking. Those are the attributes that age fastest, and at 35 they age in plain sight. The potential of 61 sits 29 points below his current overall. The Bureau is telling you that whatever Sergei Fedorov is, he already is it, and the line runs down from here. The Index has him one below base on the day he has more goals than anyone in the league.

The room around him in Anaheim is not deep. Jean-Sebastien Giguere97 is 97 in the crease, the highest goaltending grade in the Book. Behind him the roster falls off. Erik Cole79 at 79. Vaclav Prospal80 at 78. Niclas Havelid77 at 77, out with a foot injury through January 23. Fedorov is the best skater in the building by 11 Book points and the oldest man in it by a decade. The club is 18-15-9, second in the Pacific, 55 points, 148 goals scored as a team. Fedorov put 52 of those 148 in. The Ducks sit 7 points behind Dallas Stars in the division. What they look like in April depends on whether a 35-year-old center keeps finding the net 1.11 times a game.

He told a reporter this week that the morning is the hard part. Getting off the bench after a long bus, feeling the knees and the hip, knowing the body is 35 and not the body he had in Portland when he walked out of the Soviet team's hotel at 20. Then the first shift opens up and the gap is the same gap it was ten years ago. He said he does not feel old until the lights come up after the final whistle and the room empties and he is still unlacing his skates.

The contract pays $6.30M a year with 4 seasons left. That is the number the Bureau's Contract Ledger carries for a man with a potential of 61. His morale grade is 97. He is the alternate captain. The Development Index has him below base on the morning after a 3-2 win over Minnesota Wild, the same week his club moved to 18 wins and pulled within 7 points of the Pacific lead.

Fedorov turned 35 on December 13. He has played 47 of the club's 47 games. No injuries on the record, not one night missed. The INJU grade says 51. The games played says 47 out of 47.

The goal line says Sergei Fedorov is the best scorer in the league. The Book says he is a 90 with 29 points to spare above his own ceiling. Both are on the same sheet, in the same column.
