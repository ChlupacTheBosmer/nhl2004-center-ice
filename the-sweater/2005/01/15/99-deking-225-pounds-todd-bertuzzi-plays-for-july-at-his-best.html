99 Deking, 225 Pounds: Todd Bertuzzi plays for July at his best

The Book's highest single-grade hands are on the biggest forward in the league, in the last year of $2.60M, on a Vancouver club that has won 23

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterSaturday, 15 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Todd Bertuzzi95 is 6 feet 5, 225 pounds, and the Bureau's Book gives him 99 for deking. That is the top single-attribute grade in the league. He is 29 years old. His contract pays $2.60M and runs one more year. He has 79 points in 43 games, a 151-point pace, and Vancouver Canucks has won 23 games and sits 3rd in the Northwest at 57 points.

The contradiction is the whole story. A man his size, with hands that grade higher than any other player's in any single category on the Book, playing the best hockey of his career in the year he can leave. The Development Index has him up 2. Morale reads 100. Nothing about this looks like a man who is about to go somewhere else.

The grades that make no sense together

Open his card and the top of it reads like a small forward's: deking 99, puck handling 97, passing 96, accuracy 94, balance 94, hero 94. Then scroll to the bottom and the same card reads injury resistance 50, fighting 51, speed 53. He is a man who can pull the puck off his own stick blade in traffic and a man who gets hurt doing it. The Book says he will slow down from here. His potential is 58, which is below where he already sits at 93. The Bureau thinks he peaked, and he is peaking in January of a contract year.

His 2003-04 line reads 101 points in 63 games. This season he has 79 in 43. The pace is higher. He is carrying more per night than he did in the year that made him an All-Star, and he is doing it on a Vancouver team that is genuinely in it, 15-8 at home, 11-9 on the road, three straight wins as of this week.

Sons of Sudbury

He is from Sudbury, Ontario. The Northern Ontario road to the league goes through cold arenas and long bus rides and nobody telling a big kid that his hands are the thing about him. Guelph in the OHL, then 23rd overall to the Islanders in 1993. The background sheet has him at 54 goals for the Storm in 1994-95, a single-season team record he still holds. The Islanders drafted him, waited on him, signed him a day before he could re-enter the draft, four years at $4.6M, his agent Pat Morris reportedly asking for Brett Lindros money.

He never made it work in New York. He came to Vancouver before the record opened and has been here long enough that nobody calls him new. Markus Naslund91 is the other half of this: 46 goals, 41 assists, 87 points, a 166 pace, 20.0 minutes a night. Two wingers who can score from anywhere on the ice. Vancouver has won 23 because those two wings score and Dan Cloutier87 is between the pipes at 86 in the Book.

What the room says

A man with 99 deking and 225 pounds changes how the game looks from the slot. The defenseman across from him has to decide before Bertuzzi touches the puck. Step up and he pulls it through the gap. Drop back and he has room to walk it in and lift it over a pad. The 48 assists are not playmaking assists in the way a center collects them. They are the second-man pass after the big man has already committed two bodies to stopping him, and the open winger gets the puck with a half-second that nobody else on the ice can manufacture.

Bertuzzi has said enough in his career about his game that you can hear the register: matter-of-fact, a little self-deprecating about the hits he takes, proud of the moves in a quiet way. The feeling of it, for a man who was told his whole life that size was the point, is that the hands are the point. The size just buys the time to use them.

The contract year

$2.60M with one year left. He is 29. The potential says decline. The pace says 151. The Index says up 2. What does July look like for a man whose ceiling is below his floor?

The answer, if the record is any guide, is that Vancouver wins 23 games and makes the playoffs and he scores 40 goals and somebody calls in February with an offer that makes $2.60M look like a rounding error. The alternative is that the injury resistance of 50 catches up to him before July and the room makes its decision without him.

He has been in this league since October 1995, when he scored a wrap-around on his debut against the Bruins. He has been a 100-point man. He has been hurt enough that his lowest grade is the one that measures staying on the ice. At 29, with the best hands in the league on the biggest body in it, he has 79 points and 124 penalty minutes and a sweater that says 44 and a contract that says next July. The rest is what somebody decides to pay for that in the spring.

Trevor Linden85 is still on this team at 83. Brendan Morrison84 sets it up at center. Ed Jovanovski87 is out with a knee through February 19, and that hurts a room that is already missing Matt Cooke75 until late February. But Bertuzzi is on the ice tonight and tomorrow and the night after, and the puck comes to his stick and he does something with it that a 225-pound man has no business doing.
