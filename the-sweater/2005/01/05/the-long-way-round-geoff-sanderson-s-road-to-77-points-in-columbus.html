The Long Way Round: Geoff Sanderson's road to 77 points in Columbus

A 166-point pace at 32, the C on his sweater, and a room that has won 14 games. Fifteen years and five clubs later, the Whalers' second-rounder is the fourth-best scorer in the league.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterWednesday, 5 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Geoff Sanderson86 has 77 points in 38 games. The Bureau's pace column says 166. He is 32 years old, he wears the C in Columbus, he makes $6.34M a year with 2 years left on the deal, and Columbus Blue Jackets has won 14 games.

That last number is the one that makes the first number feel strange. Sanderson is the fourth-highest scorer in the league, behind Bill Guerin, Jaromir Jagr90 and Markus Naslund91, and he is doing it on a club that sits fifth in the Central Division with 44 points. The gap between the man and the room makes more sense once you see how he got here.

Hay River, Northwest Territories. A town on the Mackenzie River where a boy either leaves early or stays forever. Sanderson was born there on a February morning in 1972. He left at 18. The Hartford Whalers picked him 36th overall in the 1990 draft, a second-rounder out of the Western Hockey League, and the Whalers put him in 64 games his first full season.

He scored 30 or more goals every year in Hartford except the lockout season. The Whalers missed the playoffs every single year. Sanderson was 25 when the franchise moved to Carolina, 26 when the Hurricanes traded him to Vancouver mid-season, 26 again when Vancouver moved him to Buffalo before the deadline. In 1999 he wore a Buffalo sweater in the Stanley Cup Final. Dallas won it in triple overtime. Sanderson scored a goal in that series. He was 27.

The Columbus Blue Jackets took him in the 2000 Expansion Draft. He scored 30-plus in his first year there, and in his third. At the 2003-04 trade deadline, Columbus sent him back to Vancouver. He played his final postseason that spring, one goal in a first-round loss to Calgary.

This season he is back in Columbus, and he is better than he has ever been. 38 goals in 38 games. 39 assists. 77 points. 22.1 minutes a night. The Book grades him at 81 overall, which the Index has up one from base. His speed is 89. His accuracy is 88. His hands and puck control both read 88. His shot power is 87. The weakest grades are a 50 in fight, a 50 in durability, and a 52 in slap shot, and none of those three have ever mattered to what he does on a sheet of ice.

He is 190 pounds. He has 66 penalty minutes this season. He is not a physical presence by any grade the Bureau assigns him. What he is, at 32, is a man with the puck who finds the second option before most men see the first. His morale reads 92, which in a room that has won 14 games means either a man at peace with his own line or a man who has stopped counting the wins that belong to other people.

The room around him has Pascal Leclaire93 in goal at 84, Marc Denis92 at 82, Todd Marchant84 at center at 80, and Dimitri Kalinin84 on the blue line at 76. These are not bad players. They are not the kind of players who make a man look like the fourth-best scorer in the league. Sanderson is doing it at even strength, in all situations, on a club that scores 133 goals in 38 games, a number that puts them in the middle of the pack in the Western Conference.

He took a short stay on the injured list over the New Year, something unspecified, back by January 1. One game missed at most. The league does not record the cause. He came back and kept going.

What his teammates would say about playing with Sanderson at 32: you get open and he finds you. Not in a way that surprises you once you have seen it three times, but in a way that still makes the shift easier than it should be. He does not talk much. He has never talked much. He came out of a town that does not produce loud voices, and the twelve years since he left Hay River have not added any volume.

His contract has 2 years left. $6.34M a year is a top-six forward's salary, and on 166-point pace he is the best value on that side of the ledger. The question that follows a man like Sanderson into his thirties is whether the speed holds. The Book says 89 right now. The Index says up one. The numbers on the sheet say he is scoring faster than he scored at 27 in Buffalo, faster than he scored at 29 in his first Columbus run, faster than he scored at 31 when Carolina traded him to Vancouver and then to Buffalo in the same season.

He is 15 seasons into this league. He has played in Hartford, Carolina, Vancouver, Buffalo, Columbus. He has worn five sweaters. He has been traded twice, waived once that he will admit to, and picked up in an Expansion Draft that put him somewhere new again at 28. Fifteen years is a long time to keep arriving. And the thing about arriving for the sixth time is that nobody expects you to be better than you were the first.

He is better. 77 points in 38 games. A 166 pace. 89 speed. The fourth-best scorer in a league that has had four months to sort itself out. Columbus will not make the playoffs. The Central has Nashville Predators at 52 and Detroit Red Wings at 48 ahead of them, and 8 points is a season's work to make up in February. Sanderson will finish the year with something close to 130 points if he keeps this pace through 82, and Columbus will be where they are in April, and the number will sit in the Bureau's record, and nobody in that building will be wearing white in the first round.

That is not a sad ending. Sanderson scored 115 points last season in 64 games with Vancouver and Columbus combined, and the Canucks lost in the first round. He is on pace to break that number by fifty. He is doing it at 32. He is doing it for $6.34M. He is doing it in Hay River's finest export product, which is a man who arrives somewhere new and does not need anyone to tell him what to do with the puck.
