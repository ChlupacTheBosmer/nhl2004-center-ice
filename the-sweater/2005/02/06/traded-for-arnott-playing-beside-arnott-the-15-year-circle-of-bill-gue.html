Traded for Arnott, Playing beside Arnott: the 15-year circle of Bill Guerin

Edmonton chose Guerin over Arnott in 1998. They wear the same sweater in Dallas. At 34, Guerin has 99 points and the Book says he falls 31 grades from here.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterSunday, 6 February 2005
ShareCopy link

Midway through the 1997-98 season, the Edmonton Oilers traded Jason Arnott87 and Bryan Muir to New Jersey for Bill Guerin89 and Valeri Zelepukin. Edmonton wanted Guerin. Arnott was the price. Seven years later, Bill Guerin and Jason Arnott both put on a Dallas Stars sweater in training camp, and nobody at the desk thought to write it down.

At 34, Guerin has 50 goals and 49 assists in 53 games, a 99-point season on a 153-point pace. He is third in the league behind Markus Naslund85 and Jaromir Jagr84, both of whom have played more than he has. Arnott sits on the next line at center, carrying a 33-goal, 64-point season and an overall grade of 85. Edmonton traded one for the other. Dallas has them both.

What the Book says at 34

The Bureau's base grade for Guerin is 92. Today the overall reads 87, down one on the Development Index. His potential is 61. That is 31 grades below where he sits, the largest gap between a current grade and a floor on any man above 30 in this league. The Bureau is telling you he falls. The only question is how fast.

His shot is 92. His speed is 90. Endurance is 88, penalties 88, checking 87. Those numbers built 50 goals at 34 on 20.6 minutes a night. His faceoff grade is 51. His fighting is 52. Nobody on this room cares about either one.

He has an alternate letter on a room that won the Presidents' Trophy last season with 117 points. Dallas is 28-18-2 now, first in the Pacific, 6 points behind Vancouver Canucks and Nashville Predators for the lead in the West. Mike Modano90, 34 like him, is beside him at center with 85 points in 49 games. Marty Turco96 is in net with a 94 overall and 43 starts.

Worcester, 1989

Guerin grew up in Worcester, Massachusetts, 74 inches and 210 pounds, and the New Jersey Devils picked him fifth overall in 1991. He played there from 1991 to 1998 and won the Stanley Cup in 1995. The Oilers traded for him when he was 27, in their prime, for the best body they could get. Edmonton wanted the goals. They got them in patches.

In November 2001, Boston took him back to New England. A 41-goal season followed in 2001-02, and he signed a five-year contract with Dallas. That is year three. The contract reads $8.80M with 3 years left. Through this season he has played 58 games, 93 points last year in a shortened schedule, and now 99 in 53 with 29 games left on the calendar. His career best is here.

50 goals on a shot grade of 92, 49 assists on a passing grade you can find on the sheet. Speed at 90 at 34 years old, on 20.6 minutes, with a morale of 92. He has not been injured. The room around him does not let him slow down, and he does not let it either.

The center he was traded for

Arnott is 85 today. He scored 33 in 51 games last season for Dallas and 33 in 51 this season for the same club. Steady, not spectacular. Edmonton traded him for a 27-year-old with 92 goals in him, and the trade aged badly for the man who left. Or it aged badly for the man who stayed. Depends on whether you think 99 points at 34 is a peak or a last gasp.

The potential line says last gasp. 61 on the Bureau's index, 31 points below the base. The contract says $8.80M x 3 more years. A player with a potential of 61 who is paid at the level of a base-92 is a player whose decline costs somebody real money, and that somebody is Dallas Stars for the length of that deal.

What the room says

A man at 34 who leads his club in goals and sits third in the league in scoring does not hear much about faceoffs. Guerin takes them at a 51 grade and nobody on this bench runs a possession play that depends on winning one. The room scores through Modano and Brenden Morrow86 and Jere Lehtinen85, and when the puck reaches Guerin on the right circle, the 92-shot does the rest.

He has been traded twice, won a Cup, and scored 41 goals in Boston and 41 in Dallas and 50 here. What he has not done, at any stop, is win a trophy the league hands to a man alone. The Hart went to Ryan Smyth80, the Richard to Jarome Iginla91, the Pearson to Sergei Fedorov90. Guerin is third in scoring at 34 and no one is voting.

The calendar has 29 games left. The contract has 3 years left after it. The potential line says 61. He is on pace for 153 points, which would be 30 more than the career high he set last season. The circle closes somewhere between the trade deadline, what the calendar calls March 19, and the day the fall starts.
