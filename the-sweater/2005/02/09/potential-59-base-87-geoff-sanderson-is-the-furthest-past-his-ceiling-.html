Potential 59, Base 87: Geoff Sanderson is the furthest past his ceiling in the league

The 33-year-old captain leads Columbus in scoring by 31 points, carries a pace of 144, and has a fighting grade of 50 in a league that hits him every night.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterWednesday, 9 February 2005
ShareCopy link

Hay River, Northwest Territories, sits on the shore of Great Slave Lake. Population three thousand, give or take a few hundred depending on who counts and when. Geoff Sanderson79 was born there on 1972-02-01, was drafted 36th overall by Hartford in 1990, and has been in this league ever since. At 33 he has 93 points in 53 games for the Columbus Blue Jackets, a pace of 144 per 82, and he is the sixth-highest scorer in the league.

The Bureau's Development Index has him at +1. His base grade is 87, his overall today reads 79, and the potential the Bureau prints beside his name is 59. That gap of 28 grades between base and potential is the widest on any sheet in this league. The Bureau is saying, in the only language it uses, that Geoff Sanderson's best hockey is behind him. He hasn't been listening.

The gap on the room

Todd Marchant78 has 62 points. David Vyborny74 has 60. Dimitri Kalinin74, the highest-rated skater on the roster at 74, has 34. Sanderson's 93 is 31 points clear of the next man, and he has played the same 53 games as Marchant and Vyborny. The Columbus offense has scored 164 goals in 53 games. Sanderson has 49 of them.

He is not a specialist playing 12 minutes. The sheet has him at 21.7 minutes a night, the 14th-highest usage on a club that carries no other forward above 80 overall. His speed grade is 87, his accuracy 86, his shot power 85. The Bureau's form term has him at +1, which means he is playing slightly above his base grade right now, at 33, in his fifteenth season.

The road that produced this

Hartford drafted him in 1990, the second round, 36th overall. He debuted at the tail end of the 1990-91 season, played 64 games the following year, and scored 30 or more goals in every full season through the franchise's move to Carolina, with the single exception of the 1994-95 lockout year. The Whalers and then the Hurricanes missed the playoffs every year he was there. In 1997-98 he was traded twice in one season, first to Vancouver, then to Buffalo. He played on the 1999 Sabres who lost the Final to Dallas on the Brett Hull83 goal. The Blue Jackets took him in the 2000 Expansion Draft. He scored 30-plus in Columbus in 2000-01 and again in 2002-03. He went back to Vancouver at the trade deadline in 2003-04, scored a goal in the first-round loss to Calgary, and came back to Columbus for this season.

Fifteen seasons. Four clubs, five if you count the Whalers and the Hurricanes as two. No injuries on record this season. Ten penalty minutes in 53 games. A fighting grade of 50 and an injury grade of 50, the two lowest numbers on his sheet, printed on a man who plays left wing and weighs 190 pounds.

The contract and the ceiling

$6.34M with 2 years left. The Bureau's potential of 59 is the number that matters in every July between now and then. A man with a base of 87 and a potential of 59 is the Bureau telling a general manager: the next version of this player is worse than the current one. The $6.34M doesn't know that. Neither does the room at Columbus, 19-24-3 and 55 points.

Sanderson's morale is 87. He wears the C. He plays 21.7 minutes a night on a team whose highest-rated skater is a 74 and whose captain is a man the Bureau says has 28 grades left to lose. The Index has him at +1, which means right now, tonight, he is better than the Book's base says he is. The base is 87. The potential is 59. The room has 55 points.

What the room would say

He has been traded twice, survived a relocation, lost a Cup Final on a controversial goal, come through an expansion draft, and ended up back at the club where the ceiling keeps dropping. A captain with that list is not going to tell you he is tired. He is scoring 1.75 points a game at 33 with a fighting grade of 50, which means he takes checks from men who get paid to hit, and he keeps going back for the puck because his speed is 87 and the puck is on the other side.

The CBJ are 19-24-3. They will not make the playoffs. They have 164 goals for and 163 against in 53 games, a gap of 3.09 to 3.08 per game. Games are close. They lose them. Sanderson has done enough point nights to carry a team that nobody in the Central takes seriously, and the Bureau has already written down that the next version of him will be worse. He has not read that page yet, or he has read it and put it back in the drawer.

The trade deadline is 38 days away, what the calendar has as the trade deadline. A man with $6.34M, 2 years left, a potential of 59, and 93 points on a 19-win club is not a trade candidate. He is the captain. He stays. The gap closes another inch every edition, and he keeps scoring.
