The Long Way Round: Pavol Demitra's road from a soccer ball in Trencin to St. Louis

The 227th pick from 1993 leads the Blues in scoring this season on a contract that dies in July, and the Bureau still has his potential at 61.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterMonday, 28 February 2005
ShareCopy link

Pavol Demitra85 played soccer like his father. His father played professionally. Demitra did not pick up a hockey stick until he was 15. The League Scouting Bureau has his potential at 61, a grade it gives to men it expects to struggle at this level. His base grade is 89. He leads the St. Louis Blues Blues in scoring this season with 60 points in 62 games. His contract pays $3.80M, carries 0 years, and expires this July.

What the calendar has as the trade deadline is in 19 days. St. Louis sits 4th in the Central at 20-28-5, 63 points. Nashville has 84 and Detroit has 73. The Blues will not make the playoffs in April. Demitra is the Blues' leading scorer, 30 years old, on an expiring contract, and at $63,333 per point the arithmetic makes him interesting to every contender's war room.

The 227th pick and the road to St. Louis

Demitra started with ZŤS Dubnica in Czechoslovakia's second-tier league, recording 23 points in 28 games. The following season he joined HC Dukla Trenčín in the Czechoslovak Extraliga, scoring 11 goals and 28 points in 46 games. The Ottawa Senators Senators drafted him 227th overall in 1993.

He scored on goaltender Curtis Joseph in his first NHL game on 9 October 1993, one of two points in 12 games for Ottawa. The rest of that season went to the Prince Edward Island Senators of the AHL, where he posted 41 points in 41 games. The following year he put up 74 points in 61 AHL games and appeared in 16 more in an Ottawa sweater. He began 1995-96 in the minors again, scored 81 points in 48 games, and was recalled for 31 NHL games that season.

To start 1996-97 he held out on his contract. He played one game for Dukla Trenčín, then went to the Las Vegas Thunder of the IHL. On 27 November 1996, Ottawa traded him to the Blues for Christer Olsson. St. Louis assigned him to Grand Rapids in the IHL, where he scored 20 goals and 50 points in 42 games, and called him up on 17 March 1997. He played his first game in a Blues sweater that night, a 3-2 loss to Phoenix Coyotes. He scored his first two goals for the club on 3 April 1997 in a 5-5 tie against the New York Islanders Islanders.

He is still there.

The Book reads 89, the potential says 61

Demitra stands 71 inches tall, weighs 202 pounds, shoots left, and turns 31 in November. Speed grades 89, passing 88, puck control 87. Hero-ball, the number the Bureau uses for a player who picks a game up by the scruff in the third period, is 86.

Checking, fighting, toughness, injury resistance: all 50. The Book runs from 50 to 99, and Demitra sits on the floor in four categories. He has nothing to do with hitting, forechecking into a boards fight, or taking a cross-check on the forearm. He does it with his feet and his hands and the 202 pounds go where the play is.

Last season he played all 82 games and scored 43 goals and 101 points. This season he has 22 goals and 60 points in 62 games, a pace of 79.4. The Development Index has him at minus 2, pulling his overall down to 81 from a base of 89.

The potential grade is the one that separates him from every other Blue. The Bureau has Demitra's potential at 61. The 61 is the projection the Bureau made when he was a prospect. The 89 is what he actually became. The gap is the distance between what they saw in a Slovak teenager who had played hockey for five years and what he built across the next twelve.

What the room looks like around him

The Bureau's injury record for Demitra is empty. No body part, no return date, no entry. He has played in every game of both full seasons on record.

His three best teammates carry the rest of the room. Keith Tkachuk90 is the power forward at 86. Chris Pronger86, at 82, is the defenseman who sees the whole ice from the blue line. Doug Weight86 at 82 is the center who finds the open man and has played all 62 games. The crease belongs to Chris Osgood85 and Brent Johnson85, both graded 81.

The team scores 191 goals and gives up 226. It wins 20 games. Demitra plays 19.7 minutes a night, which is a second-forward's load for a man who leads this club in scoring. He does not play the heavy minutes that Tkachuk takes or the defensive-zone draws that Weight does. The coaching staff uses him where he is most effective, between the top line and the checking line, with room to skate.

"I came here from the IHL as a young man who wasn't sure he would stay. St. Louis is the city where that stopped being true."

A player who spent four seasons in the AHL and the IHL before the NHL called, who was traded in November to a club he did not choose, and who has never landed on an injured list in eight seasons is not a man who needs a new city to prove something.

What July asks of him

St. Louis Blues is in a division race the club will not win. The trade deadline falls on 2005-03-19, 19 days from now. The GM can keep a 30-year-old leading scorer on an expiring contract and hope to re-sign him, or deal and take whatever return a contender offers for a man who costs $63,333 per point and plays every night.

What the calendar has as free agency closes on 2005-07-28, 150 days away. A leading scorer with a base of 89, a potential of 61, morale at 100, and a contract that pays nothing after this season fits the profile of a rental. He gives a contender 60-plus points, 19.7 minutes, and a first or second line without a second-round pick or a top prospect in return.

Demitra's road to the NHL went through a soccer ball in Trencin, the Czechoslovak second tier, the AHL, the IHL, a contract holdout, and a November 1996 trade that sent Christer Olsson to Ottawa. He has been in St. Louis since March 1997. The deadline is 19 days away. The contract dies in 150 days.
