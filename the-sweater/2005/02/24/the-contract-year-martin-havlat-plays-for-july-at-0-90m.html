The Contract Year: Martin Havlat plays for July at $0.90M

The cheapest man in the league scoring at 100-plus pace is 23, his ceiling already sits below his grade, and his club wins 21 games.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterThursday, 24 February 2005
ShareCopy link

Martin Havlat88 scores at a pace of 104.1. He carries 80 points in 63 games on a club that has won 21. He makes $0.90M. His contract has 1 year left, and that year is this one.

He is 23. The Bureau's Development Index has him at +3, which means the Book reads his grade as 88 today on a base of 87. The potential line on the same page says 86. He is two grades past what the Bureau thinks he can be, and he is still in his fifth season.

That gap is small. Two grades. A man can live above his ceiling for a while. But the gap is there, and the salary is $0.90M, and July is coming, and Ottawa wins 21 hockey games.

Where the speed came from

Mlada Boleslav sits about 50 kilometres north of Prague. Havlat played in the Czech Extraliga as a teenager before Ottawa selected him 26th overall in the first round of the 1999 draft. He stayed home one more year, then crossed at 19 for the 2000-01 season. Five seasons in, he has never landed on an injured list. The league has never had a body part for him or a return date to set. He is 63 for 63 this season.

The Book puts his skating grades at 99 speed, 99 agility, 98 acceleration. Those are the top three numbers among men who dress tonight. Marian Gaborik98 matches them at Toronto Maple Leafs, and Konstantin Koltsov carries the same trio in Toronto's system, but Gaborik is the one who plays every night. Havlat is the other.

He skates like a man who learned the game on outdoor rinks in a town that freezes in November, and the grades say the translation worked. His shot power is 95, his deking 92, his puck handling 91. The weak end of the page: fighting 50, faceoffs 51, breakaway speed 54. A man with 50 in fighting does not stand up. He goes around. He has done it for 63 games and 20.7 minutes a night and nobody has found a way to stop him because stopping him means being faster, and nobody is.

Havlat carries the highest pace of any man on $0.90M or less. Kristian Huselius74 at the same salary scored at 105 last season for Florida Panthers. This season Havlat is the cheapest triple-digit pace in the league. The man below him on the contract ledger, Mike Comrie79 of Edmonton Oilers, carries $1.00M and a pace of 122.3, but Comrie is a fourth-round pick who out-earned expectations by accident. Havlat was the 26th overall selection. The Bureau expected 86. He is at 88.

What the room says

Daniel Alfredsson90 sits at 90. Marian Hossa87 sits at 87 and carries a back that keeps him out until 2005-03-29. Zdeno Chara84 is 84 at the blue line. Patrick Lalime90 is 90 in net. That is a room with two men at 90 and a captain who has been in Ottawa since 1995 and watches his team go 11-22 on the road.

Havlat and Hossa share a language in that room. They played together in the Extraliga before either crossed. When Hossa's back took him out between 2005-02-06 and 2005-02-07, the line changed shape and the goals kept coming anyway. Havlat has 31 of them through 63 games at a 15.2 percent shooting rate.

His morale sits at 92. A man with morale that high at 23, past his ceiling, on a club that wins 21, does not leave because he is unhappy. He leaves because the money is not there in July and $0.90M is not a number that competes.

The fall the Bureau sees

Potential 86 means the Index expects him below 88 eventually. He is already above it. The gap is two grades, the smallest among the ceiling-gap stories this desk has run: Sergei Fedorov90 at 33, Todd Bertuzzi93 at 37, Ilya Kovalchuk90 at 6. Havlat's fall is the smallest and the soonest, because he is the youngest man in that group by two years and the pace of 104 is the pace of a man still climbing.

The Index has him at +3. He will not stay there. A man scoring at 104 on a 60-point club who turns 24 in April cannot hold the same production against the same attention without the team around him shifting. Ottawa Senators is 15-15 at home and 11-22 on the road. They score 204 goals and give up 217. They are 4 of 5 in the Northeast.

He took two suspensions last season, four games total, one for kicking and one for high-sticking. Ken Hitchcock said somebody was going to make him eat his lunch. He has 13 penalty minutes this season. He is not the man Hitchcock was warning about anymore, or maybe he never was. The 50 in fighting says he will not throw a punch. The 63 games played says he will not fall down either.

July

The contract expires in 1 year. Free agency closes on 2005-07-28, which is 154 days away. What the calendar has as the trade deadline sits 23 days out.

Ottawa can trade him at the deadline for a package of picks and prospects and rebuild around Alfredsson and whatever comes next. Or Ottawa can hold him to July and watch a 23-year-old with 99 speed walk into a market where $0.90M does not exist for a man scoring at 104 pace.

The room says morale is 92. The room does not say where the 92 comes from. A man past his ceiling on a 21-win club, scoring at his highest pace in five years, carries something that has not been recorded yet. The only number that captures it is the one on the contract: $0.90M, 1 year, and the clock running toward a July that will cost ten times what he makes now.
