62 Goals, 61 Potential: Sergei Fedorov at 35 and the fall the Bureau sees

The Pearson winner left Pskov at 20. The Bureau has him falling 33 grades and he is scoring 34.1 percent of Anaheim's goals.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterWednesday, 23 February 2005
ShareCopy link

Sergei Fedorov88 has 62 goals in 61 games. He is 35 years old. He won the Lester B. Pearson Award last season, the one the players give to the best among them. The Bureau's Book carries his base grade at 94. The Development Index has him one grade below that today, at 90. The potential grade is 61.

He scores at a pace of 121 points per 82, which puts him among the league's top 20 scorers. Mighty Ducks of Anaheim has 182 goals this season. His 62 are 34.1 percent of them. The contract runs 4 years past this one, through his age-39 season, at $6.30M a year. That is 18.0 percent of Anaheim's payroll. The distance between the base of 94 and the potential of 61 is 33 grades.

Pskov to Portland

Fedorov was born on 1969-12-13 in Pskov, Russia. Detroit drafted him 74th overall in the third round of the 1989 draft. Alexander Mogilny89 left the Soviet Union that summer, the first Soviet star to cross the border for a North American roster. In August 1989, a Detroit emissary met Fedorov and teammate Vladimir Konstantinov and slipped each a Red Wings media guide with a letter hidden inside. Fedorov walked away in July 1990 during the Goodwill Games in Portland. He was 20 years old.

He made Detroit's center position his own within four seasons. The Hart Trophy came in 1993-94. Wayne Gretzky watched a December game between Detroit and the Rangers and told reporters afterward he had never seen a player dominate a game the way Fedorov dominated that one. Gretzky called him the best player in the game. A Selke followed in 1996. Detroit won the Stanley Cup in 1997 and 1998. On December 26, 1996, he scored all five goals in a 5-4 overtime win over Washington, a line in a record book that rarely gets updated.

The record opened on 2004-12-01 with Fedorov already wearing number 91 for Anaheim. The transactions carry no arrival. The sheet knows 131 points in 82 games last season, a Pearson Award, and 62 goals through 61 this season. What it does not know is the road from Detroit to Anaheim, and that silence is part of what the Book measures: the grades only record what happens between the lines.

Speed 98, fighting 50

The Book at 35 has his speed at 98, shot power at 95, deking at 94, acceleration at 93, agility at 93, heroics at 92. It has fighting at 50, injury resistance at 51, sprint speed at 52.

Speed and sprint measure different things. Speed is the long stride through the neutral zone, the glide that swallows a defenseman's gap and gets a man in on net. Sprint is the first three strides from a dead stop, the pop off the faceoff dot. A man 73 inches tall at 200 pounds who has skated at this level since 1990 keeps the long stride and loses the pop. The Book has the pop at 52 and the willingness to finish a hit at 50. They were built or missed in his 20s, and 35 doesn't move them.

The 33-grade gap between base and potential is the fall the Bureau expects. Fedorov's potential of 61 sits below the potential the Bureau gives Andreas Stahle76, a 20-year-old defenseman scratched from Toronto Maple Leafs's lineup this season. Stahle reads 96. Fedorov, who won the Hart Trophy in 1994 and has 62 goals this season, reads 61. The Bureau grades ceilings. It says a scratched rookie from the 2004 draft has a higher one than the man who was voted the league's best player by his peers last spring.

What the room has

Mighty Ducks of Anaheim is 25-18-11 with 75 points, second in the Pacific, on a three-game winning streak. Jean-Sebastien Giguere95 is at 97 in the crease, the highest grade on the roster. Vaclav Prospal77 is the next-best forward at 80. Erik Cole77 follows at 79, on a contract that expires in July. After those three the room drops to 77, where Niclas Havelid75 plays defenseman. The gap between Fedorov's 90 and the next skater's 80 is the gap between carrying a club and sharing one.

His goal pace went up. The Bureau tracks it at 83.3 goals per 82 today against 76.0 from last season. His assists fell from 55 to 28. He is scoring more goals and collecting fewer assists, and the total pace of 121 keeps him in the league's top 20. The minutes climbed from 21.1 to 22.9. A coach does not push a 35-year-old's ice time up without needing him on the ice.

The rest of the league tells the same story at different ages. Brett Hull is 40 and scoring at a pace of 126 in Detroit Red Wings. Mario Lemieux85 is 39 and at 140 in Pittsburgh Penguins. Peter Bondra81 is 37 at 122 in Washington Capitals. The league asks its aging stars to find a way to stay in the top 20 without the sprint grade that used to make it look easy.

Four years and a floor

$6.30M through age 39. Anaheim's payroll carries him at 18.0 percent. The Pacific has Dallas Stars ahead at 80 points. The Ducks sit second, five back of Dallas, with a goaltender at 97, a captain who scores a third of the team's goals, and a ceiling gap of 33 grades hanging over both.

Fedorov's morale reads 94, the same as his base grade. He plays 22.9 minutes a night. His speed reads 98. He scored 62 goals in 61 games at 35, a goal pace above what he managed last season at 34. The Bureau has the fall landing at 61. The record shows no sign of it starting.
