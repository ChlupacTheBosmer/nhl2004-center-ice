Sons of St. Albert: where Jarome Iginla learned to score 92

The Richard Trophy sits in a building where the team wins 18 and the contract dies in July

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterWednesday, 16 February 2005
ShareCopy link

He scored 92 goals last spring. He has 51 in 52 games this winter. The team around him has won 18 games in both years, give or take a handful, and his contract at $6.50M has 0 years left. Jarome Iginla91 turns 28 in July, the same month he could leave Calgary with nothing coming back.

The Calgary Flames Flames sit at 18-32-6, last in the Northwest, 46 points, 167 goals for. Iginla's 51 goals come out of those 167. He carries a pace of 136, eighth in the league. The Richard Trophy went to him last season on 140 points in 73 games. None of that moved the standings a single spot.

The road from St. Albert

Iginla was born in Edmonton and raised in the adjoining city of St. Albert, Alberta, by a single mother after his parents divorced when he was two. His father, originally from Nigeria, went to law school in another city. The man who drove him to hockey was his grandfather.

He played baseball until he was 17, catching the Canadian national junior team at catcher, and told Sports Illustrated once that he wanted to be a two-sport professional like Bo Jackson. He grew up watching Grant Fuhr in Edmonton and played goaltender for his first two years of organized hockey before switching to right wing. In 1992-93, at 15, he led the Alberta Midget Hockey League in scoring with 87 points for the St. Albert Midget Raiders.

The Kamloops Blazers took him to the Western Hockey League. He arrived at 16, scored 29 points in 48 games, and the Blazers won the league title and the Memorial Cup that spring. By 1994-95 he had 33 goals and 71 points, and the Blazers won again, with Iginla leading the tournament in scoring with five goals at the Memorial Cup.

The Dallas Stars drafted him 11th overall in June 1995. In December of that year, six months after they selected him, they traded him to Calgary for Joe Nieuwendyk, who was in a contract dispute. Iginla has worn the same sweater since. He came in sideways, a piece of someone else's trade, and the record shows him already on the roster when the book opens.

What the Book says

The Bureau has his base at 93 and his potential at 79. He is 27. The overall reads 85 today, which is 7 grades below base, dragged down by cold stretches and the general gravity of a man whose ceiling the Bureau believes he passed. His form reads +1 on the Index, so the number is not sliding further this week.

The attributes that make him the player he is: shot power 91, speed 90, balance 89, hero 88, accuracy 87, acceleration 86. The ones that keep him from 90-plus across the board: fighting 51, injury resistance 50, speed in breakouts 52. He is 73 inches, 200 pounds, a right shot. The grade sheet reads like a man who scores goals and does not drop gloves.

His morale sits at 87. He has no injuries on record this season. He has played 52 of Calgary's 58 games.

The room beside him

Craig Conroy82, a 76-overall center, has 16 goals and 54 assists in 58 games. Dean McAmmond82, a 76 at left wing, has 15 goals and 48 points in 40 games. Martin Gelinas80 is a 74 with 28 points. The best goaltender is Roman Turek86 at 80, a 50-game season in a building that has allowed 212 goals.

There is no second man on this roster the Bureau rates as a true top-six threat. The Flames have one elite player and a collection of 70s. Iginla plays 21.2 minutes a night and carries 136 pace on a team that scores 2.88 goals a game.

He does not sulk. He comes to the rink, does his workout, gets on the ice, shoots the puck in the corner the same way whether the scoreboard reads 4-0 up or 3-0 down. His 30 penalty minutes in 52 games says he does not go looking for trouble. The 87 morale is not the number of a man who has decided he is done. It is the number of a man who does not know yet what he is.

July

The contract has 0 years left. That means it expires this summer. The league's free agency period closes on July 28, and what the calendar has as the trade deadline is 31 days from today, March 19. Calgary sits at 46 points with no realistic path to a playoff spot.

The Bureau's potential of 79 tells a GM this man gets worse from here. The base of 93 says he is still one of the league's best pure goal-scorers right now. And the $6.50M says he is expensive for a rebuild. A team at 18 wins has a decision to make: keep a 27-year-old who will fall off the Book's page while his ceiling sits 14 grades below his base, or get something back while the 91 shot power and 90 speed still print a number worth trading.

Iginla came to Calgary in a package deal for a man in a contract dispute. He has been here for over nine years. He has a trophy on a shelf in a building where nobody has won a playoff series in the time he has been here. The 92-goal season was the best he has. The Bureau says nothing he does between now and the end of his career will top it.

He is scoring at 136 pace anyway. He turns 28 on July 1, the same month his name could go on the open market. St. Albert to Kamloops to a trade nobody at Dallas expected to haunt them. The big tree, which is what his surname means in Yoruba, is still standing in the same ground, and the ground is getting thinner around the roots.
