The Man Marc Crawford Passed On: Todd Bertuzzi at 30, 128 points, and a contract that ends in July

The Bureau published a potential of 58. He gave them 128 points on $2.60M. Vancouver is out, and he has 1 year left.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterSaturday, 23 April 2005
ShareCopy link

Todd Bertuzzi93 scored 56 goals and 128 points in 82 games this season. He played 21.1 minutes a night and took 96 penalty minutes. He earns $2.60M with 1 year left on his contract. The Bureau's potential grade for him, published sometime in the last decade and revised upward by no one, is 58.

That is 36 grades between what the room said he could be and what he is now. His base grade in the Book stands at 94. He sits at 93 overall today, two points above his base on the Development Index, which puts him in the upper tier of the Index's risers even though he is 30 years old and in his twelfth NHL season. The Bureau's Index rarely moves a man at this age.

What the grades say, and what they don't

Bertuzzi's stickhandling is a 99. His puck control is a 97. His passing is a 96. Accuracy and balance and composure are all 94s. These are the numbers of a player who can carry a line, break a structure, and make a defender wrong with his hands rather than his speed. His shot power sits somewhere behind that. His speed is a 53, the third-worst number on his card.

The weakest grade is injury, at 50. Fighting is 51. These are the two numbers the rest of the league knows. Bertuzzi is 75 inches and 225 pounds. He is the biggest man on the Vancouver Canucks roster who plays 20 or more minutes a night. Nobody checks him because nobody wants to. He hits back.

The ceiling. A 58-grade potential means the Bureau looked at this man, at whatever point they last looked before the grades locked, and decided he would top out as a third-line player. He scored 101 points in 63 games last season. He scored 128 in 82 this season. The 58 has not been a live number for years, and nobody has gone back to correct it.

Sudbury, Guelph, and the deadline

Bertuzzi grew up playing for two minor hockey teams in Sudbury, Ontario, in 1990-91. The Guelph Storm took him fifth overall in the 1991 OHL Priority Selection. Marc Crawford, then general manager of the Cornwall Royals, passed on him. Crawford has said it was because Bertuzzi was a big kid who had not grown into his body, and that there were maturity issues. Bertuzzi was 16, had just played minor hockey in northern Ontario, and Crawford went somewhere else.

At Guelph, Bertuzzi recorded 21 points in 42 games as a rookie, then 58 points in 60 games. He was suspended 15 games in that first season for kicking a Kitchener defenceman. He scored 54 goals in his third OHL season, a Guelph single-season record that beat Mike Prokopec's mark by 2 goals. He and Jeff O&#x27;Neill88 led the Storm to the best regular-season record in the league. Bertuzzi added 33 points in 14 playoff games before losing the OHL Finals to Detroit.

The New York Islanders picked him 23rd overall in 1993. He needed elbow surgery that August and missed their training camp. He went back to Guelph, scored 28 goals and 82 points, and still did not sign. Two years passed. The Islanders' general manager Don Maloney said he would take Bertuzzi again with the second overall pick in 1995 if no deal was agreed by July 7. At midnight on that date, Bertuzzi signed a four-year, $4.6 million contract. His agent had wanted a Lindros-type deal, five years and $6.7 million. He got four and $4.6.

Islanders head coach Mike Milbury said in September 1995 that Bertuzzi was the team's best performing forward. He scored on a wrap-around goal in his NHL debut, a 4-4 tie with Boston on October 7, 1995. He finished that rookie season with 18 goals and 39 points in 76 games.

The room in Vancouver

Bertuzzi has been with Vancouver for years the record does not cover. He is not new to the club. He is the best player on it. His base grade of 94 sits above Markus Naslund91's 89, above Dan Cloutier88's 85 and Ed Jovanovski88's 85, above Trevor Linden84's 83 and Brendan Morrison83's 82. He is the highest-paid man on the roster at $2.60M, with Morrison next at $2.30M and Cloutier at $1.00M.

Naslund is the other half of the top line. He scored 138 points this season, fifth in the league, on a $2.30M contract with 1 year left. They are 31 and 30, they have played together long enough that nobody in the building remembers when they did not, and the room built around them is a goaltender, two defensemen in their 20s, and a center who cost more than the winger beside him.

Bertuzzi's morale is 93. The room likes him. The numbers in the locker are the ones he puts on the board, and the ones he puts on the board are the reason $2.60M looks cheap to everyone except the man who might have to pay him next July.

The spring that ended in six

Vancouver finished 38-29-7, 99 points, the fourth seed in the Northwest. They drew Edmonton Oilers, 31-29-8, 98 points, the fifth seed. Edmonton won the series 4-2. The Oilers took Game 1 in overtime and Game 2 in regulation, went home and lost Game 3, won Game 4, lost Game 5 in overtime, and closed it out 3-1 in Game 6.

Bertuzzi had 2 goals and 9 assists in 6 playoff games. He played 24.2 minutes a night, up from 21.1 in the regular season. He took 4 penalty minutes. He was the best player on the ice in most of the games that Vancouver lost.

1 year, 31 years old, 96 days

Free agency closes on 2005-07-28, 96 days from now. Bertuzzi will be 30 until February 2. He will turn 31 in the middle of what would be the start of his free-agent summer. A man who scores 128 points at 30, with a 99 stickhandle and a 53 shot, commands a long-term deal from any club that believes the hands hold up past 33.

The Bureau's potential of 58 says no. The Bureau's base of 94 says yes. The Development Index has him at +2, still climbing at an age when most men's numbers are flat or sliding. His potential has been wrong for years. The only question left is whether anyone prices him on the ceiling the Bureau drew or the man who keeps scoring through it.

He scored 101 points in 63 games last season. He scored 128 in 82 this season. He has 1 year left at $2.60M, and the only number on his card that nobody believes is 58.
