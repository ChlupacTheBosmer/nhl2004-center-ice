Shot Power 94, Fighting 51: Steve McCarthy has 16 grades left and no armor to play with

The Blackhawks' 23rd overall pick from 1999 went from 14 points to 44 in a single season, and the grades say exactly why nobody is slowing him down and exactly what could.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterFriday, 15 April 2005
ShareCopy link

Steve McCarthy77 scored 14 points in 51 games last season at 15.2 minutes a night. This season: 44 points in 82 games at 29.9 minutes a night. He is 24 years old. He costs $0.90M. He has 16 grades between his Book base of 75 and his published potential of 91, and the Development Index has him up four.

The two grades

A defenseman with a shot power of 94 and a speed of 92 is not supposed to play on a club with 25 wins. Those are winger grades on a blue liner's frame, 72 inches and 197 pounds of a man who shoots the puck like Eric Daze87 and skates with the acceleration of a first-line center. His release grade of 94 sits alongside his balance of 82 and his passing of 86, which means the shot comes from structure, not luck. He finds the seam, he gets it off quick, he gets it off hard. The 37 assists are what those grades produce. The 7 goals are what a 94 shot power buys when the ice time doubles.

Fighting 51. Endurance 53. Faceoffs 55. A 197-pound defenseman with a fighting grade of 51 is a man who does not want to be separated from the puck by a body check in the neutral zone, and endurance of 53 means the legs go when the minutes push past what they are built for. The room in Chicago Blackhawks knows this. They have him at 29.9 minutes a night anyway.

The jump from 15.2 to 29.9 is the jump from a third-pairing job to the first minute on the ice. The jump from 14 points to 44 points is what happens when a man with that release grade gets twice the opportunity and the legs hold up.

Trail, British Columbia

McCarthy is from Trail, a town in the southeast corner of British Columbia, in the mountains, at the confluence of two rivers that do not care about hockey. So is Barret Jackman86, the defenseman on Toronto Maple Leafs who sits 9th on the Bureau's Development Index at +5, who plays 80 games a year and leads his club's blue line into a Cup defense. Jackman is 24, born 1999 draft class, 17th overall. McCarthy is 24, born 1999 draft class, 23rd overall.

Two defensemen from the same small town in the same draft year, six picks apart. One plays on the 133-point club in Toronto. One plays on the 78-point club in Chicago. The 6-grade gap in their base marks, 84 to 75, is the distance between a man who plays behind Mats Sundin91 and one who plays behind Alexei Zhamnov85 on a team that shot 232 goals for and 261 against.

"Trail produces tough kids," is what you would expect a man from Trail to say, and he is probably right. But toughness on the sheet is fighting 51 and endurance 53. You can be from a town that produces tough kids and still have a 51 fighting grade.

What $0.90M buys

Two years left on the contract. The Blackhawks' payroll has room. The Development Index has him up four on a base of 75, and the potential of 91 says the Bureau believes he is still mid-development. At 24, with a potential-91 ceiling and a base that sits 16 grades below it, McCarthy is a defenseman the rest of the league will try to take from Chicago in the next two summers.

The question the room asks is the one the grades answer badly: will the body hold? Endurance of 53 at 29.9 minutes a night means the legs are already past what the sheet says they can do. He has no injuries on record, which is the most important thing here, and Chicago Blackhawks has 25 wins and 78 points and is not in the postseason, which explains why. A 197-pound defenseman with a fighting grade of 51 has not been asked to absorb eight hits a game for three weeks in April, because there is no April run.

In April of a 25-win season, the body rests. In April of a 50-win season, it does not.

What the Index says and what the room says

The Bureau's Development Index puts him at +4, which means the current form reads four grades above the base of 75. That is the highest the Index has shown him this season. He played 51 games at 14 points in the 15.2-minute role last year and nobody called him a riser. This year, 82 games at 44 points in the 29.9-minute role, and the Index moves.

The room sees a man who shoots 94 hard, who cannot take a hit for 51, who plays 29.9 minutes on a team that has lost 36 games and will play no more after that. The room sees the 7 goals and knows six came on the power play, where a 94 shot power is the only grade that matters. The 37 assists mean the outlet is a gift, and 197 pounds on a blue line with a 51 fighting grade means the retrieval is a problem even so.

What the room would say about the $0.90M is something like this: it is the only number on the sheet that is wrong. Not wrong in the sense of cheap. Wrong in the sense that a base of 75 at 24 years old with a 91 ceiling and a release grade of 94 should not cost less than a million dollars a year. The Bureau's Contract Ledger has no answer for that gap. It only has the number, and the number is $0.90M, and it is there for two more years.

McCarthy has never been on an injured list. He has never won a trophy. He has never played a playoff game. He is 24 years old, from Trail, British Columbia, and he has 16 grades of room left on a body that the sheet says cannot take the hits that come with the minutes his game requires. The question about Steve McCarthy this April is what happens when the context changes and the body does not.
