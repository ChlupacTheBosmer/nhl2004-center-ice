Potential 56, Base 88: Tony Amonte is 32 grades past his ceiling at 34

The Bureau's ceiling on the 68th pick from 1988 sat at 56. He has been playing at 88 for two seasons, won the Masterton, and has 3 years and $5.90M a season left on a deal in Philadelphia.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterThursday, 7 April 2005
ShareCopy link

The Bureau's Development Index has Tony Amonte85 at 85 tonight, two grades below his base of 88. His published potential is 56. That is a gap of 32 grades, the widest between a man's base and his ceiling in this league, and Amonte turns 35 in August.

Glen Murray83 sits 27 grades past his potential. Robert Lang75, picked 133rd in 1990, sits 23 past his. Amonte sits 32, and the ceiling the Bureau put on him, 56, is a fourth-line number. He has spent two seasons scoring over 100 points.

Hingham, the trade, the long way to Philadelphia

He grew up in Hingham, Massachusetts, a town on the South Shore south of Boston. The New York Rangers drafted him 68th overall in the third round of 1988. He made his NHL debut in the 1991 playoffs and scored 84 goals over parts of three seasons in New York.

With seven games left in the 1993-94 season, the Rangers traded him to the Chicago Blackhawks Blackhawks. The Rangers won the Stanley Cup that June. He was 23 and had just been dealt by the club that won a Cup without him in the lineup.

He became a scoring star in Chicago and won gold with Team USA at the 1996 World Cup of Hockey. He opened the 2002-03 season with the Phoenix Coyotes Coyotes and was traded to the Philadelphia Flyers Flyers near the end of that season, and has been in Philadelphia since.

That is four clubs in a career that started with a third-round pick and a town nobody scouts. The Bureau looked at him at 18 and wrote 56. He has been arguing with that number ever since.

What the Book grades, and what it misses

His best attribute is deking, at 95. Passing, puck control and heroics are all 90. Accuracy sits at 89, acceleration at 88. The bottom of the sheet is fighting at 50, faceoffs at 53, speed burst at 53. He does not win draws, does not drop the gloves, and is not fast in a straight line.

He gets past a defender with the puck on his stick and then finds someone else who does it too. The Bureau measured something else when they wrote 56.

The numbers, both years

This season: 39 goals, 71 assists, 110 points in 82 games at 20.1 minutes a night. Last season: 56 goals, 62 assists, 118 points in 82 games at 21.1 minutes. The goals dropped by 17. The assists rose by 9. He is playing the same ice and making the same plays, and more of them are landing on a teammate's stick before they land in the net.

The Flyers scored 267 goals this season. He had 39 of them. He dressed for all 82 games in both seasons. He has no injuries on the record.

He won the Bill Masterton Memorial Trophy last season, the league's award for perseverance and sportsmanship. A man who has not missed a game in two years, who plays 20.1 minutes a night at 34 on a team that missed the playoffs, gets the perseverance award.

$5.90M, 3 years, and a roster full of 85s

Amonte carries $5.90M in salary with 3 years left. His morale is 97, the highest number on the page besides the 95 in deking.

Philadelphia finished 28-35-12 with 82 points, last in the Atlantic Division. Their top 7 forwards, by the Book, are Jeremy Roenick89 at 89, Simon Gagne86 at 86, and then Marco Sturm85, John LeClair85, Amonte and Michal Handzus85, all at 85, with Keith Primeau84 at 84. Four 85s in the top six, on a team that won 28 games.

The Bureau's potential column told the Flyers that Amonte would peak at 56. The Flyers gave him $5.90M to play top-six minutes at 34, and he scored 110 points. Nobody in the building is asking about the potential line. The contract runs 3 more years and the grade reads 85 today.

He came from a third round and a town on the South Shore. The team that drafted him won the Cup after trading him away, and he crossed three clubs to land in Philadelphia. He sits 32 grades past the number the Bureau set on him at 18. The Index reads -2 this year. He has 39 goals and 71 assists and a morale of 97 and a contract that still has 3 years to run. The page said 56 when he was 18.
