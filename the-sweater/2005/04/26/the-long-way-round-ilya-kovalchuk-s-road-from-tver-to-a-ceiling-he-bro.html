The Long Way Round: Ilya Kovalchuk's road from Tver to a ceiling he broke at 22

The first Russian drafted first overall wears 17 for Kharlamov. His base grade is 89, the Bureau gave him a potential of 84, and [[team:ATL]] has 84 points and no spring.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterTuesday, 26 April 2005
ShareCopy link

Ilya Kovalchuk92 played at the 1997 Quebec International Pee-Wee Hockey Tournament with Spartak Moscow, spent two seasons in the Vysshaya Liga with that same club, and then flew to Atlanta in the summer of 2001. He was the first Russian taken first overall in the draft's history. He chose number 17 because Valeri Kharlamov wore it, the Soviet superstar of the 1970s.

He is 22 now. The Bureau's Book has his base grade at 89. The Development Index adds 5. What the room sees is 90. His potential is 84. He walked past a ceiling the Bureau drew for him when he was 18, in a ballroom, the first kid from Russia to sit at the podium with the top name on the board.

The two grades that carry everything

Shot power: 99. Speed: 99. Acceleration 98, agility 97, deking 94. Those two numbers, 99 and 99, are the highest paired grades on any player in this league this season. Jaromir Jagr84 has 84 overall and 145 points for Washington Capitals. Kovalchuk scored 138 on 79 games, 20.0 minutes a night, a pace of 143.

The 63 goals are down from 72 last season. The 75 assists are up from 62. He played 3 fewer games and produced 4 more points. The league designates a shoulder on his record: the onset window falls between 2005-03-25 and 2005-03-30, the return date was 2005-03-30. He appeared in 79 of 82 games.

His fighting grade is 50. Injury resistance is 51. Speed on the penalty kill is 51. At 74 inches, 202 pounds, he does not go into a corner for a puck when he does not need to. The shoulder in his rookie year cost him 17 games and ended that season early. This March, another shoulder, three nights in the window.

The room

Atlanta Thrashers finished 31-37-6 with 84 points, fourth in the Southeast, and did not play in the spring. Kovalchuk and Dany Heatley91 scored 128 of the club's 274 goals between them. Heatley is 24, overall 88, the second pick from 2000, 65 goals and 131 points this season. The third-best forward on the roster is Sami Kapanen82 at 80. The goaltending runs through Byron Dafoe85 at 82, Milan Hnilicka81 at 78, and Pasi Nurminen77 at 78.

Kovalchuk plays 20.0 minutes a night for a team whose best defenseman on the record scores at the pace of a third-pairing player. His salary is $1.10M with 1 year left. The Thrashers carry no other man at that number who produces what he produces.

The contract year

One year left at $1.10M. Free agency closes in 93 days, what the calendar has as that date. He scored 138 points this season.

His morale is 87. The Development Index has him at +5, five points above the base grade. His base of 89 puts him behind Peter Forsberg89 at 95, Jarome Iginla92 at 94, Todd Bertuzzi89 at 93, and Ed Belfour at 93 among the men on the sheet.

At 22, a base grade of 89 against a potential of 84 means the Bureau projected a ceiling he passed at 20 or 21. Last season he scored 72 goals and 134 points. This season he scored 63 and 138. The goals fell, the points held, the assists rose by 13. He is 22 years old with a shot power of 99 and a speed of 99 and a fighting grade of 50, and his ceiling is already behind him.

What the number 17 means here

Kharlamov was a Soviet superstar of the 1970s. Kovalchuk chose the number as a kid in Tver. He grew up two seasons in the Vysshaya Liga, a second-division league, before he was 18, and then arrived in Atlanta with the first pick's name on the board.

The 22-year-old in the 17 sweater for Atlanta Thrashers scored 72 goals last season, the most in the league. This season he sits third in goals, behind Jarome Iginla's 76 and Markus Naslund87's 74. His 138 points tie Naslund for second overall behind Jagr's 145.

The contract expires in July. The club has 84 points and no playoff revenue. The Development Index has him 5 above his base, and the base is 5 above his potential. Next June the Bureau will have to say something about a man who passed his ceiling at 21. The Book has not answered that yet. The answer might be that they raise the potential. It might be that they do not. Kovalchuk turns 23 on April 15. He is on $1.10M and he will not be on $1.10M in October.
