David Legwand, at 25: Speed 99, Figh 50, and a PIM column nobody expected

The 2nd pick of 1998 leads all centers at 44 points and sits in the box 7 times more often than last season, on $8.37M with 1 year left

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterThursday, 15 December 2005
ShareCopy link

David Legwand90 has 24 goals, 20 assists and 44 points in 29 games for Nashville Predators. That is a pace of 124 points over 82, good for 2nd in the league behind Markus Naslund91 and 1st among all centers. His Speed grade is 99. His Fighting grade is 50. Forty-two penalty minutes.

Last season he had 17 penalty minutes in 82 games. This season he has 42 in 29. The rate is seven times what it was. His Fighting grade has not moved from 50, and nobody around him is calling it a change in who he is.

The road here starts in Detroit, at Grosse Pointe North High School. Then the Detroit Little Caesars Pee-Wee teams that went to Quebec in 1993 and 1994. Then the Plymouth Whalers of the OHL, where in the season before the draft he scored 54 goals and 51 assists for 105 points and won the Red Tilson Award as the most outstanding player in the league. Nashville's general manager David Poile traded the first and second-round picks to San Jose for the 2nd and 85th picks, and took Legwand second overall in 1998.

The mononucleosis came that fall at training camp. He went back to Plymouth for the 1998-99 OHL season. He was 18 and he missed the entire first stretch of what would have been his rookie year.

On December 23, 2000, against the Rangers, Legwand scored on a penalty shot in overtime, the first man in the league to do it. He was 20. That spring he played for Team USA at the World Championship because Nashville did not make the playoffs.

The numbers the Book tells and the numbers the box tells

The Bureau's Book gives him a base of 88, +3 on the Development Index, putting him at 89 today. Potential 85, which is three grades below what he already is. He has been better than what the Bureau thinks for two seasons running. Last year: 57 goals, 41 assists, 98 points in 82 games at 22.2 minutes a night. This year: 44 points in 29 at 22.4 minutes.

His strongest attributes are Speed 99, Agility 95, Acceleration 94, Hero 94, Deking 92, Puck Passing 92. His weakest are Fighting 50, Speed with Puck 55, Offensive Decision Making 60. The 50 in Fighting has been there long enough to stop being a surprise, the number on the card. And yet the box is where he has been spending more time than any teammate.

Nobody is dropping gloves at 42 penalty minutes. A 190-pound center with the best legs in the league, at 99 Speed, puts his body into lanes and his stick into scrambles. The 94 Hero grade spending itself where the referee sees it. The Bureau gave him a number for how much he competes and he is collecting the bill for it in two-minute stretches on the penalty kill's behalf.

The contract and the room

He is on $8.37M with 1 year left. Morale 94. The hip that took him out in April of last season lasted 5 days. That is the only injury on his record. He has been on Nashville's roster since before the record opened on December 1, 2004. He has never worn another sweater in this league.

Nashville Predators sits at 13-7-3 with 41 points, tied with St. Louis Blues for the top of the Central. Behind him in net is Tomas Vokoun92, whose overall the Bureau puts at 91. On his wings, Martin Erat86 carries an 85 and the Development Index has him +6. Scott Walker85 is the other top-side winger at 84. The center between them has 44 points and 42 minutes in the box.

A man who leads all centers in the league at a 124-point pace, who carries a 94 morale, who has never been traded and never asked to leave, is not going to walk into July as an unknown quantity. The $8.37M on his current deal is a price tag the market has to match or beat. And the market has to look at the Speed grade: 99. That has not moved. The base of 88 has not moved. Only the form line moved this edition, up 3.

The Bureau put his ceiling at 85. He is playing at 89. That gap, the one where the man is already past the number they set for him, is the number Nashville's front office cannot print in a press release. But it is the number every other general manager reads on the same Bureau page in July.

The linemates grin about the 42 penalty minutes. What the cap table remembers is the pace. The thing they built the team around is the Speed, and the Speed grade is still 99.
