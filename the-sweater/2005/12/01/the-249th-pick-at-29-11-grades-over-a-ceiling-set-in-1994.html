The 249th Pick, at 29: 11 Grades Over a Ceiling Set in 1994

The Bureau put Richard Zednik's ceiling at 76. He has been playing at 87 for two seasons and no one in Montreal seems surprised.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterThursday, 1 December 2005
ShareCopy link

Richard Zednik81 sits third in the league in scoring. Seventeen goals, sixteen assists, 33 points in 23 games, a pace of 118 over 82. He is the ninth-round pick of 1994, the 249th man taken in a draft that also produced Zdeno Chara and Adam Deadmarsh, and he is out-scoring the first-rounders.

The Bureau's Book grades him 83 today. His base grade is 87, and his Development Index reads 0. His potential, the ceiling the Bureau assigns him, is 76. That is 11 grades below where he is right now. He has been above his own ceiling since at least the 2002-03 season, when he put up 114 points in 82 games on 21.9 minutes a night. The Bureau has not revised it.

From Bystrica to 249th

Zedník was born in Bystrica, Slovakia, in January 1976. He crossed the ocean to play junior hockey for the Portland Winter Hawks of the Western Hockey League and scored 35 goals in his rookie year there, 1994-95. The Washington Capitals took him in the ninth round of the 1994 draft. Portland had kept him close to the Pacific Northwest, which meant the Capitals' affiliate was also in Portland, the Pirates of the American Hockey League, and his first full professional season was a Calder Cup finals run with a team that shared his junior city's name.

He made the Capitals' roster out of training camp in 1996 and scored his first career goal in the season opener on October 5 against Ed Belfour. After one goal in nine games the Capitals sent him back to Portland. His first full NHL season came in 1997-98: 65 games, 17 goals.

The trade that made him a Canadien came on March 13, 2001. Washington moved him and Jan Bulis76 and a first-round pick to Montreal Canadiens for Trevor Linden, Dainius Zubrus, and a second-rounder. Zedník has been in Montreal ever since. The record opened with him already there.

The elbow and what followed

On April 26, 2002, in a playoff game against Boston Bruins, Bruins defenceman Kyle McLaren elbowed Zedník in the face. The injury drew comparisons to the 1989 cut that nearly killed Buffalo goaltender Clint Malarchuk. That was the moment people outside Montreal learned his name. He returned and played on.

The record shows one injury since: a hip problem with an onset sometime between January 26 and January 30 of 2005, returned March 7. Nothing else is logged. The 62 games he played in 2003-04 is the lowest total the record carries for him, and the record does not say why.

What 89 looks like and what 50 looks like

The Book splits him in two. His speed, agility, and acceleration are all 89. Endurance reads 86, shot power 86, penalty killing 85. Those are the numbers of a player the Bureau respects as a hockey athlete.

Faceoffs, fighting, and injury resistance all read 50. That is the floor of the scale. A man with a 50 in faceoffs will not win draws in the defensive zone against a checking center. A 50 in fighting means he goes nowhere near it. The 50 in injury is the Bureau's projection, not a prediction, but the record carries one hip injury in three years, and Zedník told the world what happened to his face in 2002 without missing a full season after it.

He is a winger who wins nothing at the dot, never drops the gloves, and has a body the Bureau expects to keep failing him. He also has 89 in the three attributes that make a winger dangerous. The combination produces a 33-goal-23-assist season at 22.7 minutes a night on a Montreal Canadiens squad sitting 8-9-3 with 25 standings points.

The contract says everything

$7.33M. Four years left. Morale 97. The room around him includes Jose Theodore87 at 89 in goal and Mike Komisarek81 at 83 on the back end. None of this is urgent. There is no July to play for, no expiring deal to leverage, no trade rumor. He is the third-highest scorer in the league on a team that is not good, at an age where the Bureau expected him to be a 76, and he appears to be content.

The pace is holding. He has never scored fewer than 93 points over a full-length sample. The Bureau's 76 would mean a man who plays a top-six role and scores 20-something goals. The record says this man scores 50 and 60.

What the room says

A teammate in Montreal, asked about the 249th pick putting up the league's best goals-per-game rate on his line, would say the same thing everyone says about a man who has been in the building for five years and has not changed: he is here every day, he does not talk about it, and the ice is where he shows up. A coach, pressed on why a 50 in faceoffs does not bother him, would note that Zedník does not take draws. His minutes are in transition, on the forecheck, in space. The faceoff number describes a skill he never uses, not a hole he has to cover.

The Bureau revises the Book every edition. It has not revised Zedník's ceiling upward. The man is 29, has four years on a $7.33M deal, morale at 97, and sits 11 grades above the number the Bureau says is his best case. The 249th pick of 1994 has been telling them that since at least 2002-03, and the Book has not listened yet.
