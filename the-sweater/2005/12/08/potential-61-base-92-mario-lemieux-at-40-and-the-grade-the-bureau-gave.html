Potential 61, Base 92: Mario Lemieux at 40 and the Grade the Bureau Gave Up On

The first pick of 1984 is scoring at a 105-point pace on $9.90M with 4 years left. The Bureau's ceiling says he can never be this good again.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterThursday, 8 December 2005
ShareCopy link

Mario Lemieux88 is 40 years old. He earns $9.90M. His contract has 4 years on it. He is putting up 32 points in 25 games this season, a 105-point pace over 82, and the League Scouting Bureau's Development Index says he is 2 below his base grade of 92.

The Bureau's potential grade for him is 61, the ceiling it believes he will ever reach again. His base is 92. The gap is 31 grades. He has been playing above that ceiling for at least 3 full seasons of the record, and he does not look like a man coming down.

The road from Ville-Émard

Lemieux grew up in the Ville-Émard district of Montreal. His father Jean-Guy was an engineer, his mother Pierrette stayed home. He started playing hockey at 3 in the basement. Before there were real sticks, he and his older brothers Alain and Richard used wooden kitchen spoons and bottle caps. His father built a rink on the front lawn. When it was dark, the family packed snow onto the living room carpet.

He went to the Laval Voisins of the QMJHL, drafted at 15, and said out loud he would break the league's records. He did. 562 points in 200 games across 3 seasons. In the 1983-84 playoffs he scored 29 goals and 52 points in 14 games. The Voisins reached the Memorial Cup and lost all 3 games.

At the 1984 draft, the Pittsburgh Penguins picked him first overall. He did not shake general manager Eddie Johnston's hand. He did not put on the sweater. He told reporters Pittsburgh didn't want him badly enough. Johnston signed him two weeks later on a two-year deal worth $600,000 with a $150,000 signing bonus. The Penguins were averaging fewer than 7,000 fans a night and the Civic Arena held roughly three times that. There were rumors the team would leave.

What the Book says at 40

The Bureau's current grades tell a story the body tells too. His hands are where they always were: Lemieux's Puck Passing sits at 93, his Deke at 92, his Vision at 90, his Puck Control at 89. Speed 88, Shot Power 87. These are what make a first pick a first pick, and they have barely moved.

The ones that have moved are the ones a man cannot keep: Fighting 50, Sprint Speed 52, Toughness 55. He was never built to hit, and the sprint number tells you what 40 looks like on a first shift. He gets to 22.9 minutes a night because the coach needs what he still has.

His speed grade of 88 is still ahead of 4 of the 7 Pittsburgh forwards on the roster's top 8 by overall. He plays at a level the Bureau calls his potential, which is 61. He has been playing 24 grades above that ceiling for as long as the record has been open.

The contract

$9.90M a year. 4 years left. The cost per point at his current pace is $309,375, computed across the season's 32 points and his full salary. Pittsburgh carries more money on one man's production than any other club carries on its leading scorer.

The record opened on 2005-12-01 and Lemieux was already a Penguin, which is the only way this league records a man who has been somewhere longer than the archive. He has been in Pittsburgh since before anyone at this desk can remember. He told reporters at the 1984 draft that he would play for whoever took him. That was 21 years ago.

What the room says

Morale 98. He is the highest-paid man on the roster, he is the oldest man on the roster, and he is as happy as the Bureau's scale can measure. A teammate who has played alongside him this season says the man walks into the dressing room at 8:15 every morning, before the young guys, and is still there when the trainers pack up.

The room knows what 105-point pace looks like at 40. They watch it in practice. He does not skate as fast as he used to. Nobody on that bench skates as fast as he did at 22. But the pass arrives at the same moment, from the same angle, with the same weight, and the deke still takes a defender out of the play before the play has fully developed.

The gap that will not close

The Bureau revised him downward by 2 on the Index this edition. His base of 92 has been dropping. Season 0 of the record had him at 150 points in 82 games. Last season: 117 in 73. This season: 32 in 25, a pace of 105. The curve bends, but it bends from a place the Bureau does not think he can reach again.

Potential 61 means the Bureau believes the ceiling is 61. He is 85 today, on form minus 2. Even if the Index falls to zero on him, even if his base slides to the 70s, he is still above a number the Bureau says is the best he can be. He will spend his remaining 4 years playing above the grade the Bureau thinks he is capable of.

He will be 44 when the contract runs out. The next season the league plays will not have him in it if the Bureau is right. The next season the league plays will have him in it if the last 21 years are anything to go by.
