First Russian, Ceiling 86, Current 91: Ilya Kovalchuk's Number Was Set When He Was 18

He is 22, on $1.10M with zero years left, and Atlanta has no answer for a man the Bureau says can never be this good

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterThursday, 29 December 2005
ShareCopy link

Ilya Kovalchuk91 is 22 years old. The Bureau's Development Index has him at 91, which is his base grade of 89 plus 5 on form. His potential, the ceiling the Book assigned him, is 86. He is 5 grades above the number they gave him at 18.

The contract pays $1.10M. It has zero years left. He plays for a club with 29 points in 34 games, the worst record in the Southeast Division, the second-worst in the East. Atlanta scored 82 goals in 34 games this season, 2.41 a night. Kovalchuk scored 23 of them.

He was drafted first overall in 2001, the first Russian to go first in the league's history. Born in Tver in 1983, he played youth hockey with Spartak Moscow and spent two seasons in the Vysshaya Liga with the same club before crossing the Atlantic. He wears 17 for Valeri Kharlamov, the Soviet winger who won eight world championships and two Olympic golds in the 1970s.

What the Book says about him

His speed is 99. His acceleration is 99. His shot power is 99. His agility is 98. His endurance is 96. His fighting is 50. His checking is 51. His injury resistance is 52.

That bottom row is the reason the Bureau set 86 and stopped there. A man with a 99-shot and a 50-fight, the thinking went, could score 50 goals but would never survive a playoff series where a defenceman puts his stick through your ribs on the board. He never would have to.

He scored 134 points as a rookie. He scored 138 last season, on 79 games, and won the Lady Byng as the league's most sportsmanlike player. This season he sits at 41 points in 34 games, a 99-point pace, with 14 penalty minutes in 34 games. The Lady Byng was not a mistake. The 86 was.

The 2003-04 pace column is points per 82 games. He missed three games to a shoulder injury that spring, his only recorded absence since his rookie season, and came back on the same day he went down, which the record shows as a single-day shoulder.

What the room looks like

Atlanta is a team built around one winger and a 24-year-old right wing named Dany Heatley90, who also went first or second overall in his draft class and who sits 2 grades below Kovalchuk in the Book at 90. Behind them: Patrik Stefan79 at centre, 79, and Byron Dafoe84 in goal, 84. The Thrashers allow 97 goals in 34 games against 82 scored. The goal differential is minus-15.

A teammate's view, reconstructed from the way men talk about the player who carries them:

"He doesn't look at you when he takes off. He looks at the net. You see the ice open up and you're already skating because if you're not, the play is over and he already scored and you didn't even see it happen. You learn to skate blind behind him."

That is what a man with 99 acceleration does to the five players around him. They run routes toward a space that exists for about a half-second after he cuts. If they're there, it's an assist. If they're not, it's still a goal.

The contract year that isn't

Kovalchuk's deal pays $1.10M. That is the league's rookie-maximum range for a first overall pick signed in 2001. It expires this summer. He will be 23 in April.

The Bureau's Book has his potential at 86. His base grade is already 89. His current grade is 91. A man cannot be below his base on the Index if he is 3 above it. A man cannot be below his ceiling if he is 5 above it. The numbers are not a forecast anymore. They are a record of when the Bureau stopped watching.

He is the fastest player in the league on a team that scores 2.41 goals a night. He is on a contract that pays him less than Byron Dafoe makes in goal. He is 22, in his fourth season, and the record says he has been a 100-point scorer every year he was healthy enough to play 75 games.

The first Russian drafted first overall in the league's history is about to hit the market. The Bureau still thinks he can't be this good.
