The Contract Year: Vaclav Nedorost plays for July at the league's worst address

The biggest riser on the Bureau's Index is 23, on $1.00M with zero years left, and Florida cannot afford to keep him at that price

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterThursday, 22 December 2005
ShareCopy link

Vaclav Nedorost80 sits +12 on the Bureau's Development Index, the highest number on the risers' list, ahead of Marian Gaborik99's +11 and Igor Radulov76's +11. He is the reason a 23-year-old center on a team with 27 points has a grade of 83 today, which is 8 grades above where the Book had him at the start of the season.

The contract reads $1.00M with zero years remaining. Florida's payroll sits at $57.33M. Nedorost costs 1.7 percent of it.

He came from HC České Budějovice of the Czech Extraliga, the club that produced him before Colorado took him 14th overall in 2000. He spent 2001-02 splitting time between the Hershey Bears and the Avalanche, 25 NHL games, 2 goals and 2 assists. The next year brought 42 more games in Denver. On July 19, 2003, Colorado traded him and Eric Messier to Florida Panthers for Peter Worrell and a second-round pick. He has been in South Florida since.

The jump nobody predicted

Last season Nedorost played 69 games and produced 13 goals and 12 assists for 25 points on 13.4 minutes a night. That is a 29.7-point pace across a full 82-game schedule. A bottom-six center, useful, replaceable, worth his $1.00M.

This season he is at 22.4 minutes a night. The same 25 points, in 32 games. That is a 64.1-point pace. He has more than doubled his per-game production while taking on nine more minutes of ice each night.

His 25 assists in 32 games tell you where the production comes from. Nedorost has 17 assists to 8 goals at 22.4 minutes. Passing 95. Puck handling 94. Speed 99. Those numbers describe a man who carries the puck through the neutral zone and finds a teammate before the defense rotates. He scored 8 goals in 32 games.

What the grades say and what they cannot say

The Book has him at Speed 99, Passing 95, Agility 94, Puck Handling 94, Shot Power 94, Acceleration 92. The potential is 94, which is the ceiling the Bureau set for him as a prospect in 2000. The base is 75. The form has pushed him to 83 today.

The weak column is Fighting at 50, with Heroics at 59 and OD/BI at 61. His game lives in the open ice, where speed 99 and acceleration 92 stretch plays. In the corners and in front of the net, those numbers stop working.

Morale is 97. Florida has lost 17 games. He does not seem to care, or at least it does not show in the grade.

The room he plays in

Florida Panthers is 8-17-3 through 32 games. They are 27 points from 1st in the Southeast with 34 games left. The Panthers score 75 goals and allow 90. Their best player on the ice tonight would be David Aebischer85 at 86, or Olli Jokinen81 at 84, or Jay Bouwmeester79 at 82. Nedorost is 83 today, but that number is the form talking. His base is 75, which puts him below all three.

What the room would say about a man on an expiring deal who is the cheapest good player on the roster: he is in every shift because the minutes are there and nobody is arguing with him for them. The coach gives him 22.4 minutes because the second line on a team that scores 75 goals in 32 games cannot survive without the one player who can carry the puck the other way.

The leg injury in March cost him most of what remained of the 2004-05 season. He came back on April 24 and was ready for the fall. There is no second injury on the record.

July

Zero years left. He will be 24 before free agency opens. The Bureau's potential is 94. His base is 75 and his form is 83. If he finishes at a 64-point pace, he is asking for more than $1.00M in July, and Florida, which already carries $57.33M with a payroll that cannot get worse on its own record, would have to decide whether the speed and the passing are worth what a man at 83 will command on an open market.

Kerry Morassutti told The Tunnel this week about the shape of his roster and the need to spread money across more bodies. Nedorost at $1.00M is exactly the kind of number that makes the math work. At whatever a 23-year-old center with a 64-point pace will sign for in July, it stops working.

He will not drop the gloves. Fighting 50 means he knows what he is and he has made peace with it. Speed 99 is his argument and the contract year is his lever. Florida has 34 games to decide which one they trust more.
