First Overall, Zero Years: Rick DiPietro passes better than anyone in net and cannot yet be trusted

The 2000 first-pick has a Puck Passing grade of 99, a Consistency grade of 50, and a contract that ends this spring at $1.10M.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterSunday, 4 December 2005
ShareCopy link

Rick DiPietro86 passed the puck better than any goaltender in the league this season. The Bureau's Book puts his Puck Passing at 99, one above Martin Brodeur93's 95, and three above anyone else among the ten netminders whose grades sit above 90 overall. Speed 92, Agility 95, Puck Handling 93, Glove and Stick at 90 each. The body in front of the New York Islanders net is the most physically gifted in the league.

His Consistency grade is 50. So is his Style. So is his Toughness. Those are the floor. The Bureau draws a line at 50 and says: below this, a player does not exist in the record. DiPietro sits on it in all three categories that measure whether a goaltender can do what his hands permit him to do on a Thursday in November when nothing is working and the building is quiet.

That gap is why his first five seasons look the way they do, and why the contract conversation before July will be fought over those numbers by both sides.

The body

DiPietro was the first pick of 2000, taken out of Boston University after one season in Hockey East. He won the Beanpot, was named the tournament's top goaltender, took the Eberly Trophy, and left the school as co-MVP and Hockey East Rookie of the Year. The Islanders drafted him on that one year and the promise behind it. He did not play an NHL game until January 27, 2001, against Buffalo. He won 3 games that first season against 15 losses. He played 10 games the next season, one of them in the playoffs, then was called up permanently in 2003-04 and won 32.

This season he has started 21 of the Islanders' 24 games. He is 7-12-0 with no shutouts, 75 goals against in 1235 minutes, about 58.8 minutes a night. The team is 12-8-3, sitting fourth in the Atlantic division with 29 points, on a three-game winning streak. He has not been sharp. He has also not been benched.

The Book's current read is a man playing above himself. His base grade is 79. His form is +9 on the Development Index, putting his overall at 86. He sits fourth among the league's risers, behind Vaclav Nedorost84 at +12, Marian Gaborik99 at +11, and Igor Radulov80 at +11. The Index does not care whether the team wins or loses; it measures how a player performs against what the Book expects from his base. DiPietro is performing seven grades above what the Book expects, on a team that is under .500.

The head

The three grades at 50 tell you why he is 7-12 with a shutout total of zero. Consistency 50 means the Bureau expects a goaltender with his toolset to produce results that swing game to game in ways that cannot be predicted from talent. Style 50 means no reliable framework, no way of managing a game that holds when the puck is going in. Toughness 50 means the mental component of the position, the part that lets a goaltender stop the next shot after three bad ones, is at the floor.

His ceiling makes the gap worse. The Bureau's potential for DiPietro is 98, twelve grades above his base, fourteen above what he is playing at today. A potential of 98 is the ceiling of a Vezina-calibre goaltender in this league. Brodeur, the Vezina frontrunner two seasons ago, sits at 95 overall with a base of 95. DiPietro's ceiling is above Brodeur's floor. The Bureau thinks his body is built for that. The three 50s are what stand between him and it.

His morale sits at 84. He has no injuries on record. He is 24 years old, 6 feet 1, 185 pounds, shoots right. He was born in Lewiston, Maine, moved to Winthrop, Massachusetts at 10, played for a Beverly minor team in the 1994 Quebec International Pee-Wee tournament, went to Saint Sebastian's School, played one year at BU, and was drafted first overall at 19.

The clock

His contract carries $1.10M and 0 years left. It ends this spring. The League Scouting Bureau's Contract Ledger has him at that figure with no term beyond the current season. He will be a restricted free agent in July unless the Islanders act before then. The trade deadline falls on 2006-03-19, 105 days from now. Free agency closes on 2006-07-28.

At $1.10M, the Islanders hold a goaltender whose physical tools rank at the very top of the position and whose mental grades sit at the bottom. If he holds his current form through the spring, the Index puts him at 86, the ceiling at 98, and the market for a 24-year-old goaltender with that ceiling would be wide. If the +9 evaporates, which is what a Consistency of 50 invites, the team is left with a 79-grade netminder on a deal that expired in May.

The Islanders have seven players on injured reserve, the most in the league. Among them: Oleg Kvasha79 out with a foot until January 5, Mariusz Czerkawski76 out with an elbow until December 7, Radek Martinek72 out with an elbow until December 21, Stephane Robidas69 out with a hip until December 13, Shawn Heins67 out with a leg until December 18, Pierre Dagenais66 out with a wrist until February 7, and Justin Papineau72 out with a foot until December 12. The team is 12-8-3 without them and on a three-game win streak. DiPietro has been in net for most of those wins, and his form is +9, and his contract has no years on it.

The room

What the room would say: the body does the job, the head has not caught up. You dress him because the tools are real. You win some because the tools are real. You lose some because the head has not caught up. And you watch for July, when the question is whether those tools are yours or someone else's.

DiPietro is 7-12. He has not shut anyone out. The three 50s are on his card for anyone who looks. The 99 is also on his card. The Bureau has not raised his base. The Index has him up 9, and form terms are form terms: they mean the player is better than his card today and might not be tomorrow.

The Islanders are 12-8-3 in the Atlantic. Alexei Yashin83 leads the team at 33 points in 24 games, sitting fifth in the league's scoring. The offense is carrying them while their goaltender plays at 86 against a base of 79 and a ceiling the Bureau puts at 98. Whether the man in net gets paid what his ceiling says or what his record says is the question the front office answers in whatever comes next. Zero years on the contract, 105 days to the deadline. The 50s do not move fast.
