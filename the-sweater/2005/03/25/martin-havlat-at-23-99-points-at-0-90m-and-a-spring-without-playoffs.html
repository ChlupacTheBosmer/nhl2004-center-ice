Martin Havlat, at 23: 99 points at $0.90M and a spring without playoffs

Ottawa's winger is 3 grades up on the Bureau's Index and past a potential of 84. The club under him has 69 points and no road left.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterFriday, 25 March 2005
ShareCopy link

Martin Havlat88 has 99 points in 79 games, 39 of them goals, on a $0.90M contract. The Ottawa Senators Senators have 76 games behind them, 69 points, and no road left to the postseason.

A 23-year-old scoring at a 102.8-point pace, second on his own club to Daniel Alfredsson90's 101 points, on the last year of a deal worth less than many checking lines. The Bureau's Book has him at 86, up 3 on the Development Index. His published potential is 84.

Ottawa has scored 237 goals and allowed 262. The Senators are 18-20 at home, 12-26 on the road, and 4th in the Northeast. At 20.6 minutes a night, Havlat is the man the club hands the puck to when a shift has to finish at the other end of the rink.

One skill, chased to the edge

The grade sheet reads like a single gift worked to its limit. Speed 99. Agility 97. Acceleration 96. Shot power 93. Deking 90, puck skills 89. The bottom of the page holds what he is never asked to do: faceoffs 50, fighting 50, strength 54.

He is a burner, and on a club that loses more than it wins, that means the opposition can never settle in. Chances open in transition, before the game grinds down.

The room behind him has thinned. Marian Hossa87, a back, is out until 2005-03-29. Todd White85, a hand, is out until 2005-03-29. Two of the club's better forwards watch from the press level while the winger on the $0.90M line takes the minutes. Patrick Lalime90, at 89, still carries the best grade on the roster. Alfredsson is at 88, Hossa at 87. Havlat, at 86, is fourth.

From Mlada Boleslav to the first round

Havlat came out of the Czech Extraliga, taken 26th overall in 1999 in the first round. He stayed home one more season, then crossed for a rookie year in 2000-01. By his third season he had a second straight 20-goal year, 24 goals and 59 points, and a 2003 playoff run that carried Ottawa to the Eastern Conference Finals.

After a high-sticking incident in the 2003-04 season, Ken Hitchcock said, "Somebody is going to make him eat his lunch." That same year Havlat was suspended twice, 4 games in all, once for kicking and once for high-sticking. He is 73 inches and 190 pounds, never the biggest man in the room, and he has played his whole career as if being first to the puck is the only size that counts.

"You learn to play with your legs first," Havlat says of those years. "Everything I have comes from being quicker than the man in front of me. When I stop moving, I am ordinary."

The jump from 50 points in 48 games to 99 in 79 is a fifth-season player staying on the ice for a full year and producing at the same rate. In his last playoff spring he averaged 24.3 minutes across 4 games and scored 4 points. No spring like that is coming. The club around him has not kept pace.

Ottawa's payroll is $42.80M, the 3rd lowest in the league, behind Atlanta Thrashers at $36.79M and Mighty Ducks of Anaheim at $41.01M. A club spending near the floor, with Lalime at 89 and Chara at 82 and Redden at 83 behind the forwards, has spent another season short of the teams above it. Toronto Maple Leafs has 124 points, Boston Bruins 91, Buffalo Sabres 74, and only Montreal Canadiens, at 60, sits below Ottawa in the division.

What the room sees

The Index has him up 3, a bump that follows a season of healthy legs and 20.6 minutes rather than a change in what he is. The grades say what he is: one of the fastest men in the league, on one of its cheapest contracts.

"He changes a shift the second he touches the puck," a teammate says. "You get used to watching him go."

Havlat takes the puck in open ice and the bench rises. He gives it back in the neutral zone chasing a second dangle and the bench sits. At 23, the trade still favors the scorer.

The Book has Havlat at 86, up 3 on the Index, with a potential of 84 he has already passed. The standings have his club at 69 points and out of the spring. His contract has 1 year left on it.
