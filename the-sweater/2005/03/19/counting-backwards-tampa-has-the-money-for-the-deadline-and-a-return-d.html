Counting Backwards: Tampa has the money for the deadline and a return date that undoes it

The Lightning's coach said the numbers come out wrong. With $47.75M in payroll, 94 points without their first overall pick, and a return date of April 1, GM Jay Feaster has to decide tonight what a man at 21.9 minutes a night is worth in March.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterSaturday, 19 March 2005
ShareCopy link

Today is what the calendar has as the trade deadline, and Tampa Bay can buy. The Lightning carry $47.75M in payroll, 16,191 fans a night at $79 a ticket, and $32.28M in profit this season. Coach Michael Burt said it on The Tunnel this week: the money is there. "We can afford to buy. Most clubs can't."

The problem is who they'd be buying for.

Vincent Lecavalier90 has 105 points in 59 games this season, a 146 pace over 82, on 21.9 minutes a night. He has been in the press box since sometime between February 16 and February 19, when the league designated a leg. His return date is April 1. The deadline is today.

Burt told The Tunnel's Marcus Bell after a game on March 9: "It tells me he gets back for maybe three or four games before the deadline if that. Maybe. I've been counting backwards, and the number keeps coming out wrong."

The math has gotten no friendlier in the ten days since.

What the Book has at 24

The Bureau's Book puts Lecavalier's base grade at 90. His published potential is 86. He is 4 grades past the number the Bureau projected for him, at an age when most first overall picks are still finding their floor. The Development Index has him up 1, which means his current play reads slightly above his baseline even while he watches from the press box.

His best attributes are violent: HERO 98, SPEE 97, SHPW 96, ACCE 95, AGIL 94. His worst are FIGH 51, SPBI 52, INJU 53. He is a man who scores at 146 pace and gets a leg injury that keeps him out for six weeks.

Lecavalier was drafted first overall in 1998 by Tampa Bay Lightning, the pick Art Williams called "the Michael Jordan of hockey" on draft night. From Ile-Bizard on the North Shore of Montreal, through two years with Rimouski in the QMJHL where he won the Michel Bergeron Trophy and the RDS Cup as a rookie, to the number one selection at 18. He was given the captaincy and had it taken away before the 2001-02 season because management decided he was too young. Last season he put up 138 points in 82 games.

The room without him

Burt told Bell that the guys responded. Tampa Bay has 94 points without Lecavalier, good for second in the Southeast. The morale across the center group tells you a room that believes it is fine:

Everyone above Lecavalier's 93 is a man scoring fewer than 20 points. The room is happy. The team is winning without its best player. Burt said it himself: "The guys have responded."

The April 1 problem

Burt is blunt about the money: "If a piece falls into my lap that makes us harder to play against in a seven-game series, I can call ownership tonight and the money's there." The question is what a seven-game series looks like for a club that gets its 1st overall pick back right at the start of one.

Lecavalier logs 21.9 minutes a night. His HERO grade of 98 means he closes games. His SPEE of 97 means he changes the geometry of a shift just by existing on the ice. Tampa Bay is 94 points without him. The question is what 94 points becomes with him, in April, with his legs fresh from six weeks of not skating.

The contract gives Tampa time. $2.60M with 2 years left. He is not going anywhere in July. The ceiling the Bureau published at 86 means nothing when the base reads 90; the Index has him climbing, not stalling. At 24, on a deal that costs less than a mid-second-liner, this is a club about to get its best player back in the right month.

But the Lightning still have to decide, with hours left on the clock, whether to buy for a man who might dress two games in the regular season and disappear again, or whether the room that sits at 98 morale without him is the room they trust in a first-round series.

Burt told Bell: "I'm buying depth I already have. I'm buying for a run that starts in April with a healthy Vincent, or I'm buying for a run that might need me to win."

The return date makes the deadline feel like a formality, and someone still has to pick up the phone.
