Potential 98, Base 79: Mike Cammalleri has 19 grades left and a club that wins 21 games

The Index has him up six, his contract costs $1.10M, and he chose a college rink in Ann Arbor over a junior one in Toronto.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterFriday, 11 March 2005
ShareCopy link

Mike Cammalleri81 has 48 points in 47 games for the Los Angeles Kings Kings, the Bureau's Development Index has him up 6 on the base grade, and the Book still puts his ceiling at 98. He is 22 years old. His base sits at 79. That is a 19-grade gap between what he is and what the Bureau thinks he can be, and it is the widest ceiling gap on the Kings' roster by a distance.

He plays on a $1.10M contract with 1 year left. At 1.02 points per game, that costs $22,917 per point. The Kings, who are 21-32-7 and fourth in the Pacific, cannot afford what he is producing at the price they are paying.

The road that skipped junior

Cammalleri grew up in Richmond Hill, Ontario, in a house where his father is Catholic and Sicilian and his mother is Jewish, where his maternal grandparents survived the Holocaust in Poland and Czechoslovakia. His best sport as a kid was soccer. He played minor hockey for the Toronto Red Wings in the GTHL and suited up for them at the 1996 Quebec International Pee-Wee Hockey Tournament. The St. Michael's Majors drafted him in the OHL Priority Selection. He said no. He wanted to keep his American college eligibility, so he went to the Junior A Bramalea Blues in the OPJHL instead, won their rookie-of-the-year award at 15, and at 17 he graduated from The Country Day School in King City on an accelerated schedule and left for the University of Michigan.

He committed to Michigan at 15 on a full hockey scholarship. Four seasons there produced 29 goals in 2000-01, a CCHA first-team all-star nod, and a Frozen Four run that ended against Boston College. The Kings picked him 49th overall in 2001. He stayed in Ann Arbor anyway. On October 1, 2001, he played in the Cold War against Michigan State in front of 74,554 people, an outdoor game on a football field in East Lansing, and he nearly missed it with a hip-flexor injury. He figured in all three of Michigan's goals in the 3-3 tie.

He came to Los Angeles after four college seasons, at 21, and the first year on the Kings was quiet: 69 games, 11 goals, 9 assists, 20 points, 11.8 minutes a night. A fourth-line role. An elbow injury cost him two weeks around the start of this season.

What changed

This season he plays 20.5 minutes a night and has 16 goals and 32 assists in 47 games. The Index has him 6 above his base grade of 79. His morale sits at 93. He has not missed a game since the elbow cleared in mid-December.

A coach sat him for nine minutes more a night and the production followed. The per-attribute grades explain why: stickhandling at 89, passing at 89, puck control at 86, speed at 85. A 22-year-old with those hands and that much ice does not need 82 games to show what he is. Forty-seven was enough.

What the room sees

The Bureau has the potential at 98. His base is 79. The gap is 19 grades. For a man with 89 and 89 on his stickhand and his pass, 98 means the Bureau believes he can reach the top ten in this league. That number has not moved down this season. The Index reads 6, the highest positive form term on the Kings' roster, and it has been there since the last Bureau revision.

Zigmund Palffy81 leads the Kings with 104 points in 66 games, but he is out with an ankle until April 6. Alexander Frolov73 has 49 in 48. Cammalleri sits one point behind Frolov with one fewer game played. With Palffy gone, the production falls to Cammalleri and Frolov, both 22, both on cheap deals, carrying a team that has lost four straight and scored 215 goals in 68 games.

The contract year

He turns 23 in June. His deal expires in July. At 83.7 pace, the man with a potential of 98 will cost what a first-line winger costs, which is many times more than $1.10M. The Kings have not addressed it. The deadline is 8 days away and the club sits 21-32-7 with no realistic path to a playoff berth, which means the front office faces a choice between trading pieces around him or letting the contract ride into free agency.

The room would tell you this is not a man who thinks about July. Morale of 93 on a team that wins 21 games says the minutes matter more than the money right now. He is scoring 32 assists in 47 games, the playmaking number that a coach reads as the reason those nine extra minutes exist. The 16 goals are a career-high and he is not yet halfway through his first full season in this league.

The Bureau set a ceiling of 98 when he was a college sophomore in Ann Arbor playing in front of 74,554 people on a football field. Three seasons later he is at 79, the Index has him at 85 in form, and the Kings are the worst club in the Pacific with his contract dying in July. Nineteen grades remain.
