The Richard Trophy and the 54-Point Room: Jarome Iginla has 5 days

Potential 79, base 93, $6.50M expiring in July, and 31.3 percent of Calgary's goals

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterMonday, 14 March 2005
ShareCopy link

Jarome Iginla92 scored 31.3 percent of Calgary Flames's goals this season. The Flames are 22-40-6, last in the Northwest, 54 points in 70 games. His contract pays $6.50M and has 0 years left. What the calendar has as the trade deadline falls on Saturday.

The Bureau set his ceiling at 79. His base grade is 93. That puts him 14 grades past what the Book said he could be, the largest gap on a man his age that the desk has printed this season. He is 27 years old. He won the Maurice Richard Trophy last spring with 92 goals, the most any man scored in 2003-04, and this year he sits third in the league in scoring at 103 points in 64 games, a 132 pace over 82.

The people who found him are the reason he is here and the reason the ceiling looked so low. Iginla was born in Edmonton and raised in St. Albert, the city that butts up against it from the north. His father came from Nigeria, changed his first name from Adekunle to Elvis when he arrived in Canada, and went to law school. His mother, Susan, is from Oregon and worked as a massage therapist and a music teacher. The marriage ended when Jarome was two. He was raised by Susan and his grandparents.

He credits his grandfather. Without him, Iginla has said, there was no way to play at any high level. Susan was working. The kid needed someone to drive him.

He played baseball until he was 17. Catcher. He made the Canadian national junior team and went to amateur tournaments in Western Canada with his glove and his bat before anyone handed him a hockey stick at a level that mattered. He told Sports Illustrated once that he wanted to be a two-sport professional like Bo Jackson. He gave up the diamond the year before the 1995 draft.

Why the Bureau said 79

The grades that carry him now: shot power 91, speed 90, balance 89, hero 88, accuracy 87, acceleration 86. Those are all in the 86-to-91 range. The Book's per-attribute ceiling for the men it projects at 79 overall sits somewhere in the low 80s. The Bureau made its judgment on a 1995 first-round pick who had just won his second Memorial Cup with the Kamloops Blazers and who, at that moment, had 71 points in 65 WHL games at age 18.

He went 11th overall to the Dallas Stars. On December 20, 1995, Dallas traded him to Calgary for Joe Nieuwendyk, who was in a contract dispute with the Flames. Iginla was still a teenager. He has been a Flame ever since.

What the room looks like: Roman Turek85 is the best goaltender at 80 overall. Craig Conroy84 leads the team in assists at 64. Dean McAmmond83 is the second-highest point man at 61. There is no second man within 20 points of Iginla. The team scored 195 goals in 70 games and allowed 247.

The contract and the room that knows what he is

$6.50M with no years left means the Flames can trade him before Saturday or lose him for nothing in July. The team is in the bottom five in goals-for and in points. Morale reads 87, which means he is not angry. He is present. He plays 21.4 minutes a night, the same number as last season, on a club that allowed 247 goals in 70 games.

His weakest grades are the ones nobody can teach: injury 50, fighting 51, physical strength 52. He has no injuries on record this season. He takes 30 penalty minutes in 64 games. The man who won the Richard Trophy is not a bruiser and never has been. He is the fastest thing on the ice with a shot that reads 91.

The Index has him at +1 today. Cammalleri and Gaborik are at +6. He is simply good, consistently, and the Index gives him credit for exactly that. One grade above his base on a man whose base is 14 above his potential means the form term barely registers. The Bureau was wrong about him, and wrong about him early.

He told a reporter once that putting on the Blazers jersey was like putting on the Canadiens'. You have to perform. The same weight applies in a building that wins 22 games. He carries it without complaint because the men who raised him did not complain about driving kids to rinks in Alberta in January.

What Saturday changes

If Calgary keeps him, they commit $6.50M to a man on a 22-win club with a ceiling the Bureau says he already broke 14 grades ago. If they move him, they get a first-round pick and a prospect for the best goal-scorer in the league, and they admit that the rebuild is a rebuild and not a window.

No man on this team has 103 points. No man on this team has a base grade above 93. Turek, the highest-rated Flame, sits at 80 overall and 88 base. The gap by points between Iginla and Conroy is 21. The gap by base grade between Iginla and Turek is 5.

He is 27. The Richard Trophy sits on a shelf in a room that will be empty in July if ownership does not match what the market says he is worth. The deadline is Saturday. The room does not know yet which Jarome Iginla they will have on Sunday: the one the Bureau saw in 1995, or the one the Book sees now.
