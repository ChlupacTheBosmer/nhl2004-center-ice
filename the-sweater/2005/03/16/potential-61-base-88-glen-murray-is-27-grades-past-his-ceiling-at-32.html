Potential 61, Base 88: Glen Murray is 27 grades past his ceiling at 32

The Art Ross winner's ceiling sits 27 grades below his floor, his contract dies in July, and Boston has 30 points to catch Toronto before it does.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterWednesday, 16 March 2005
ShareCopy link

Glen Murray85's published potential is 61. His base grade on the Book is 88. The gap between those numbers is 27 grades, and the man filling it wears a captain's C in Boston, turned 32 in November, and won the Art Ross Trophy with 168 points last season.

No other player currently dressing in this league carries a ceiling gap that wide. The Bureau's ceiling page said Murray would peak as a middle-six winger. He just finished a 168-point year, the most in the league, and picked up the Lady Byng on top of it.

This season he has 53 points in 37 games. His pace is 117.5 points per 82, a drop of 50.5 from last year's pace, and the drop is almost entirely the 34 games he missed with an arm injury that kept him out until January 15. His contract costs $3.70M and has 0 years left. He can sign somewhere else in July.

Halifax to Los Angeles to Halifax

Murray grew up in Halifax, Nova Scotia. Boston took him 18th overall in the 1991 draft. He never wore the Bruins sweater as a teenager. Instead he landed in Los Angeles, where he spent five seasons putting up what the record calls moderate numbers before the Bruins traded for him on October 24, 2001, sending Jason Allison and Mikko Eloranta west.

In July 2002 Murray signed a two-year contract with Boston. He scored 92 points that first year and played in the 2003 All-Star Game. Then 168 last season. The Bureau watched a 61-point ceiling and watched him score 92, then 168, and never moved the number up.

His card tells the same story. Shot power 89. Stamina 88. Hero modifier 85. Speed 84. Passing 80. Puck control 80. The 50s sit at faceoffs, fighting, and toughness. A 225-pound right wing with a 50 in toughness is what the Bureau said he was, and the rest of his card says 88.

The room and the letter

Morale 95. He wears the C. At 32 he is two years older than Joe Thornton90, his best linemate at 85 overall, and five years older than Sergei Samsonov86, who sits one spot ahead of him on the team in points with 111 in 63 games. Murray's 117.5 pace trails Samsonov's 144 only because of the 34 games he missed. In the 37 he played he scored at a rate that would make the ceiling page look absurd.

The Development Index has him flat at 0. The Bureau sees no trend in either direction. He is what the Book says he is, 88, and that number is already the lie his ceiling tells.

Boston Bruins sits 2nd in the Northeast at 86 points, on a three-game win streak, with a goal differential of 237 for and 233 against. Toronto has 116. The gap between first and second in the division is 30 points with Boston carrying 4 games left on its schedule.

July

The trade deadline is 3 days away. Boston has a 2nd-place club, a captain with no years left on his deal, and a man who scored 168 points last year at an age when most 32-year-olds are watching from the press box.

If Boston keeps him, it renews a contract the ceiling page says he should not have earned. If it does not, a man with a potential grade of 61 and a base grade of 88 hits the open market on a day when every general manager in this league can read the same Book and decide whether they believe the number or the man.

Murray is the biggest gap between ceiling and floor that anyone is wearing tonight. He is also the oldest person on that list by a decade.
