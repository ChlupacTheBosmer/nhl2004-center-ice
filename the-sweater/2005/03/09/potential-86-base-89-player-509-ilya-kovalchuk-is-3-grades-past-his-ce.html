Potential 86, Base 89: [[player:509|Ilya Kovalchuk]] is 3 grades past his ceiling at 21

The Bureau set his ceiling when he was 18. It read 86. Three seasons later his base is 89, the Index has him 5 above that, and his contract costs $1.10M.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterWednesday, 9 March 2005
ShareCopy link

Ilya Kovalchuk90 turned 21 in April of 2004, halfway through the season that saw him score 72 goals and 134 points in 82 games. The League Scouting Bureau had his ceiling at 86. His base grade now reads 89. The Development Index has him 5 above that, putting his overall at 92 on a Tuesday in March when he should be finishing his fourth NHL season, not rewriting what a 21-year-old looks like in the Book.

Three grades between base and potential is not the number that makes you sit up. Three grades at 21 is.

Robert Lang81 is 34 and 23 grades past his ceiling. Sergei Fedorov86 is 35 and the Bureau sees a 33-grade fall coming. David Legwand86 is 24 and 3 past, the youngest ceiling-breaker anyone was writing about three days ago. Kovalchuk is younger than Legwand and in the same spot, and unlike the others he was not a late bloomer or a misread at 30. The Bureau graded him at 18. He left them in his third season.

Four nines, three fifties

The Book gives Kovalchuk 99 in four categories: Speed, Shot Power, Acceleration, Agility. His Deke is 96. His Heroic is 96, which means he will block a shot and get back up.

The other side of the page reads 50 in Fighting, 51 in Physical, 53 in Injury Resistance. He is 6 feet 2, 202 pounds, shoots right, plays left wing.

This is a player the Book describes as the fastest thing on a sheet of ice, attached to a body the Book does not expect to survive contact. His 113 points in 66 games this season put him 3rd in the league behind Markus Naslund91 and Jaromir Jagr90, at 20.3 minutes a night. He has 10 penalty minutes in the same span. He is not looking for trouble, and the Fighting grade of 50 says he would lose if he found it.

The Injury Resistance of 53 is the number a front office loses sleep over. He missed 17 games with a shoulder problem in his rookie season, 2001-02, scoring 29 goals and 51 points in 65 games before it ended his year. Two seasons later he was the first overall pick from 2001, the first Russian taken first overall in league history, and the Book still had him fragile.

Tver to the first pick

Kovalchuk was born in Tver, northwest of Moscow. He played in the 1997 Quebec International Pee-Wee tournament with Spartak Moscow's youth squad, spent two seasons in the Vysshaya Liga with Spartak, then crossed the Atlantic. He wears 17 in memory of Valeri Kharlamov, the Central Army club star from the 1970s whose combination of speed and shot power defined a generation of Russian wingers. Nobody in the Russian game thinks the comparison is far-fetched.

Atlanta Thrashers picked him first overall in 2001. He arrived in his 18th year and missed nearly a quarter of his rookie season. He scored 29 goals. By 2002-03 he had 38 and 67 points. By 2003-04, 72 goals and 134 points. The trajectory the Bureau set for him, climbing to 86 and plateauing, arrived and passed in the same window.

$1.10M with 1 year left

Kovalchuk is paid $1.10M and has 1 year left. Atlanta Thrashers carries $36.79M in total salary. He is the team's highest-graded player at 92. Dany Heatley88, the next-best man on the roster at 90, is the only teammate within 6 grades of him.

Atlanta is 26-28-4 with 72 points, 4th in the Southeast, on a four-game winning streak. Sami Kapanen80 is out with a hip until March 11. Patrik Stefan79 is out with a foot until March 14. The team's two best forwards by overall grade behind Kovalchuk and Heatley are both on the shelf, and Atlanta Thrashers has won four straight.

The trade deadline, what the calendar has as the trade deadline, is 10 days away.

Atlanta Thrashers has no reason to sell. Kovalchuk is 21, signed through next season, scoring at 140 pace, and the next team to try to rebuild around a player of his profile will do it with 6 more years of a first-overall pick's prime in front of them. When this contract dies, the Bureau will still have him at a base of 89 against a ceiling of 86 that is already fiction, and the market will not wait.

A 21-year-old on a $1.10M rookie deal is a gift to whoever drafted him. Kovalchuk's gift to Atlanta is this season and next. After that, the money is what the market says, and the market does not price 99 Speed and 99 Shot Power at $1.10M.

What the room says

A teammate watching Kovalchuk at practice sees the four 99s and the three 50s in the same sequence. He hits the first move, and the defender is two steps behind before the second move starts. He gets boarded along the wall, and he gets up, and he does not go back for it.

"He makes it look easy because it is easy for him," a Thrashers forward said, asking not to be named. "The thing that gets you is watching the hit land and watching him skate away. You want to tell him, this league is going to slow down. Then the next shift he does the same thing to the same guy and you feel stupid for worrying."

The morale grade is 95. He is not going anywhere he does not want to go, and right now he wants to be in Atlanta, scoring 113 points on a team that wins 26 games and plays in a building that has not decided whether it is a hockey town.

Kovalchuk turns 22 on April 15, six days before the season ends. The ceiling the Bureau set for him when he was a teenager in Tver is three grades behind. The Index has him 5 more above that, and the four-game win streak says the team is trying to hold on. Whether Atlanta can hold the player is a different problem, and it starts with $1.10M, a year left, and a market that will not be this generous after next season.
