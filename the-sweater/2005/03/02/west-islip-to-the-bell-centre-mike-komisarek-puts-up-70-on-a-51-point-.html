West Islip to the Bell Centre: Mike Komisarek puts up 70 on a 51-point club

The 7th overall pick from 2001 went from 3 points in 24 games to the third-highest pace among defensemen this season, and his contract dies in July

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterWednesday, 2 March 2005
ShareCopy link

Mike Komisarek82 played 24 games for the Montreal Canadiens last season and finished with 3 points at 11.8 minutes a night. This year he has 70 points in 65 games at 30.1 minutes, the third-highest 82-game pace of any defenseman with 63 or more games.

He went from a depth call who dressed 24 times to the most-used defenseman on a club that wins 16 games. The Canadiens are 16-37-5 with 51 points, last in the Northeast, 51 points behind Toronto Maple Leafs. They score 199 goals in 65 games, the fewest of any club in the league.

A Long Islander at 7th overall

He is 23. Born in West Islip, New York, he learned the game in the Suffolk PAL organization before two years of varsity hockey at St. Anthony's High School. He played for the New England Jr. Coyotes in the Eastern Junior Hockey League under coach Gary Dineen, then went to USA Hockey's National Team Development Program. Two seasons at the University of Michigan followed: 46 points and 145 penalty minutes in 80 games, a CCHA title, two trips to the NCAA tournament, and a First Team Division I All-American selection in 2002.

The Montreal Canadiens took him 7th overall in the 2001 draft. He signed on July 24, 2002, and played his first NHL game in the 2002-03 season. Two years at 24 games and 3 points each. The third year gave him 30 minutes a night and 70 points.

What the Book says

The League Scouting Bureau's Development Index has him at +6 this edition, sitting on a base grade of 77 for a current overall of 76. His potential reads 90, which places him 14 grades below his ceiling at age 23. Checking at 86, speed at 83, aggression at 82. Puck handling 82, slap shot 81, passing 79. Fighting at 51.

That is a defenseman who moves the puck, beats the other man to loose pucks, and throws his body into plays. The wins don't follow.

The room around him

The best player on the Montreal roster, by the Book, is Jose Theodore91 at 85. The next names are Dwayne Roloson86 at 82 and Richard Zednik85 at 79. Komisarek plays 30.1 minutes a night carrying the offensive load on a blue line whose other members include Andrei Markov81 at 75 and Ron Hainsey71 at 67.

Montreal lost 4 of its last 5 games heading into the end of February, including a 3-4 overtime loss to Minnesota Wild on 2005-02-27 and a 2-4 road loss to Washington Capitals on 2005-02-24. The club has lost 20 of 25 since January 15. Komisarek logged 30 or more minutes in all of them. A defenseman who plays that much on a team that cannot win sees more of the opposition's rush and spends more of his minutes chasing and exiting under pressure. The 59 assists on his line are built partly out of desperation.

The money

The Contract Ledger at Montreal lists eight defensemen. The highest-paid carries $4.10M with 2 years left. The lowest carries $0.30M. Komisarek sits at $1.10M with 1 year left, the same salary as Hainsey and Markov. He plays 30.1 minutes a night, more than either of them.

His pace of 88.3 on an 82-game basis puts him behind only two defensemen with at least 63 games this season: Nicklas Lidstrom at 95.0 in Detroit, a club that wins 27, and Sergei Gonchar at 92.1 in Washington, a club that wins 32.

What July looks like

What the calendar has as the trade deadline is 17 days away. A club that wins 16 games with a 23-year-old defenseman scoring at an 88.3 pace has two choices before July: trade him for something, or sign him to something. His contract has 1 year remaining. Free agency closes on 2005-07-28.

His morale reads 89. One name sits above 82 in the Book. Nobody else on the roster is going to put up 199 goals, and the coaching staff knows it, which is why he gets 30.1 minutes a night.

A kid from the Suffolk PAL who played varsity at St. Anthony's, then one season of junior in New England, then two at Michigan, then 24 games and 3 points as a 22-year-old, now scores 70 points at 30 minutes a night on the worst team in the Northeast. The Index has him at +6 and his ceiling at 90. The salary is $1.10M. The contract says one more July.
