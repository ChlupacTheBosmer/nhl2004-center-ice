The Long Way Round: Vaclav Nedorost's road from Ceske Budejovice to 13.4 minutes and a potential he hasn't touched

14th overall at 18, AHL by 19, 7 games last year, 25 points at 23. The Bureau's Index has him 6 grades up. The room has him at 13.4 minutes a night.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterTuesday, 17 May 2005
ShareCopy link

The piece

The Bureau's Development Index says Vaclav Nedorost80 is 6 grades above his base right now: 78 against a base of 75, with a potential the same Bureau put at 94. Nineteen grades sit between what he has been and what they think he could be. He has 25 points in 69 games, 13.4 minutes a night, and one year left on a $1.00M deal. That gap has been the story of his first four North American winters.

From the Czech Extraliga to the Hershey Bears

Nedorost was born in Ceske Budejovice and played for HC Ceske Budejovice in the Czech Extraliga before the Colorado Avalanche took him 14th overall in the 2000 draft. He came to North America for the 2001-02 season, splitting it between the Hershey Bears in the AHL and 25 games with Colorado Avalanche. He played 42 more for Colorado the next year, then was traded on July 19, 2003, along with Eric Messier, to Florida Panthers for Peter Worrell and a second-round pick.

He played 7 games for Florida in 2003-04, scoring one goal with no assists across 50 minutes of ice time for the whole season. The Panthers made the playoffs that year and lost in the first round in 6 games; Nedorost appeared in 5 of them with 3 points in 48 minutes, but the regular season had already told you what he was to them: a body, barely dressed, barely used.

What 69 games at 13.4 minutes looks like

This season he played 69 games, scored 13 goals and added 12 assists for 25 points, 14 penalty minutes, 13.4 minutes a night. The goals are real. The assists are thin, and the hands that produced only 12 helpers across 69 games do not look like the player whose Pass grade sits at 91 in the Book.

The leg injury ate part of it. The league designates it as a leg; the onset fell between March 9 and March 11; the return came on April 24. That is roughly 5 weeks gone from a 69-game season, and without it the pace would read differently.

Nedorost is 23, shoots left, 73 inches, 190 pounds. His strongest attributes in the Book are Speed at 93, Pass at 91, Puck Control at 90, Agility at 85, Shot Power at 85. His weakest are Fighting at 50, Checking at 67, Hero Ball at 60, Off-Dump-In at 61. A 93 Speed and a 67 Checking is a man the coach plays in space and avoids in traffic, which explains the minutes.

Nedorost would say the road was the point, even if nobody would have chosen it. A 15-year-old in Ceske Budejovice who left for Hershey at 19, played 25 games for Colorado, then sat on a Florida bench for 7 games while the team lost 11 in a row. You survive that. You do not pick it in a draft room at 18.

The room around him

Florida finished 25-36-16, 76 points, last in the Southeast division, 11 straight losses to close the season. The general manager Boris Van Ryswyk told The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk after the final game: "I'm not going to stand here and tell you I sleep great about losing 11 in a row. I don't."

Nedorost's morale is 100, on a club that finished with 25 wins and the longest losing streak in the league. A man with a base of 75, a potential of 94, and 1 year of team control who keeps his morale at 100 on a 25-win club reads his own situation differently than the record would suggest.

He plays center. The Panthers dressed Olli Jokinen81 (85 overall, 39 goals, 87 points) at center on the top line and Joe Nieuwendyk (82 overall, 27 goals, 64 points) elsewhere up the middle. Nedorost is the third center, 13.4 minutes, 25 points. The club is not going to trade Jokinen or let Nieuwendyk walk and hand the job to a man with a Checking grade of 67 and 25 points.

19 grades and a summer

The Bureau's Development Index has him at +6 this edition. That is the same number the desk has on Mike Komisarek81 and Pierre-Marc Bouchard79. Komisarek scored 82 points at 30.4 minutes a night. Bouchard has 29 points in 82 games at a similar clip. Nedorost's 25 points in 69 games is the lowest output of the three.

But his potential is 94. Komisarek's is 92. Bouchard's is unknown on the sheet the desk pulled. The gap between potential and base is 19 grades for Nedorost, 15 for Komisarek, 3 for Bouchard.

He has 1 year left on $1.00M. If the club does not extend him before free agency closes on July 28, he tests the market after next season with whatever the year ahead gives him. If he gets 18 minutes and 35 points, the $1.00M looks like a bargain. If the minutes hold and the points stall at 25, he is a third-line center on a rebuilding club who played the whole first contract year at 13.4 minutes and never got the room to find out what 94 looks like.

The leg is healed, the return date was April 24, and he finished the season. The Index is up 6, the potential is 94, the minutes are 13.4. Nineteen grades sit between those two numbers, and next season decides which one is the real him.
