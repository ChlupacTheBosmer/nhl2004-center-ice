AGIL 99, FIGH 50: Martin Havlat at 24, past the ceiling, into the market

103 points on $0.90M, speed grades at 99, a fighting grade at 50, and 70 days before the market finds out what he is worth

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterThursday, 19 May 2005
ShareCopy link

Martin Havlat88 scored 40 goals and 103 points this season for a Ottawa Senators club that won 28 games and went home in April. His contract pays him $0.90M and has 1 year left. Free agency closes on 2005-07-28, 70 days from now.

The Bureau's Book has his base grade at 87. The potential line reads 86. He is 24 years old and he is 1 grade past the ceiling they drew for him. The Development Index has him up 3 on the form term, putting his displayed overall at 88. Morale reads 92. There is no injury on record and no trophy.

Underneath the 88, his agility is 99, his speed is 99, his acceleration is 98. Those three sit at or near the top of the scale and they are the whole player. His shot power is 95, his deking 92, his puck control 91. The bottom of the same page says fighting 50, faceoffs 51, shot blocking 54. A 24-year-old whose skates are perfect and whose body is not, producing at a career year on a team that finished 28-39-9.

He came from Mlada Boleslav in the Czech Republic, and the Ottawa Senators took him 26th overall in 1999. He stayed home for one more season in the Czech Extraliga, then crossed over for the 2000-01 rookie year. He put up 24 goals and 59 points in his third season and helped Ottawa reach the Eastern Conference Finals in 2003. Then the 2003-04 season brought two suspensions totaling 4 games, one for kicking, one for high-sticking, and a sentence from Ken Hitchcock that Havlat heard from across the league: "Somebody is going to make him eat his lunch."

Nobody made him eat anything. He played 82 games this season, the first full schedule of his career, and set personal marks in goals, assists, and points. The 50 points in 48 games he put up in 2003-04 became 103 in 82. His minutes went from 21.5 a night to 20.5. He played fewer minutes and scored twice as many points.

Seventy days until free agency closes, and the question is what Ottawa does with a man at $0.90M and 103 points. The Bureau's Contract Ledger puts him at $8,737 per point, the fourth-best rate among the players on that list. Here is where that sits against the rest of the league's cost-per-point table:

Havlat is on the same shelf as Kovalchuk, Heatley, and Comrie. All three of those contracts are 1 year from expiring or already past, and all three produce at a rate the market cannot hold. Havlat is the cheapest of the four at $0.90M, and the youngest group in that table is his: 22, 24, 24, 24.

The room around him is not bad. Daniel Alfredsson89 scored 56 goals and 108 points with a grade of 90. Patrick Lalime88 is 90 in goal with 68 starts. Marian Hossa84 sits at 87 but played only 55 games. Zdeno Chara80 at 84 logged 58 assists from the blue line, 68 points total. The Senators had two men at 90, one at 88, one at 87, and a goaltender at 90. Twenty-eight wins, 77 points, the worst record in the Northeast.

Todd White80 managed 27 points in 25 games before going down. Wade Redden79 had 58 points at 85 and looked like the third defenseman on a contender. Mike Fisher94 put up 36 points in 82 games at 22 years old. The bottom six could not score enough to let the top four carry the night every time. OTT scored 254 goals and gave up 279. The fourth line could not hold a lead.

What 99 looks like on the ice

AGIL 99 and SPEE 99 together: the puck is on Havlat's blade, the defenseman turns his hips, and Havlat is already past him. The acceleration grade of 98 means the first two strides after the stick touches the puck are faster than almost anyone in this league. Shot power at 95 means the finish carries weight even from a bad angle. Deking at 92 means the move he sells with his shoulders works against goalies who have seen it 40 times already.

What matters is what happens when someone gets close enough to hit him. Fighting 50 means he does not win the shove. Faceoffs 51 means the center takes the draw and the play starts somewhere else. Shot blocking 54 means he is not a player who drops to one knee in his own zone when the penalty killer needs a body. At 190 pounds and 73 inches, he is not built to absorb contact, and the grades say so plainly. The 4 games he missed in 2003-04 for suspension were not from getting hit. They were from giving it.

Ottawa missed the playoffs by 30 points. Havlat played a full schedule for the first time, scored 103 points, and the club went 28-39-9. He did not need the Senators to be good. He needed them to be better.

Morale reads 92 on the Bureau's scale, the highest number on his sheet, and that is the only word the room gives you. At 24, with speed grades that will not get better and a body the grades say will not hold up to a physical league, the window is now. Not next year, not at 27, not when the faceoff grade climbs to 60. The legs are at 99 and the contract is $0.90M. The Ottawa crowd still thinks he is one of theirs.
