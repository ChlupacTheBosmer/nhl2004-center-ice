The 191st Pick, at His Ceiling: Martin Erat reads 84 with 84 above his name and a contract that just ran out

The seventh-rounder from Trebic plays 20.8 minutes a night on $0.60M, the Index has him 6 grades above his base, and the Book won't let him show any more

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterTuesday, 31 May 2005
ShareCopy link

The Bureau's Development Index has Martin Erat86 6 grades above his base of 81. The Book reads him at 84 today. His potential is 84. A seventh-round pick from the 1999 draft, 191st overall, Nashville's 12th choice that June, has arrived at the exact number the men with clipboards gave him six years ago in a hotel ballroom, and his contract, at $0.60M with 0 years left, just ran out.

The rookie draft is 8 days away. What the calendar has as free agency closes in 58 days. Somewhere in that window, another club is going to decide what a 23-year-old left winger with speed at 95 and checking at 88 costs more than $0.60M.

From Trebic to a vowel line

Erat grew up in Trebic, in the Czech Republic, and crossed the ocean at 19 to find out that the NHL draft had a seventh round for a reason. The Predators picked him 12th in their own draft, which is a way of saying they were not certain. He showed up in Nashville Predators camp the next fall and made the team.

His first NHL point was a game-winning goal against Calgary Flames on October 11, 2001. By the time winter set in he was on the line they called the vowel line, him on the left wing, Vladimír Országh on the right, Denis Arkhipov at centre, three names where every vowel sat in the wrong place. The line worked because Erat could skate, and his speed carried them both ends.

Then the sophomore year went bad. The Predators sent him down to Milwaukee in the AHL for most of 2002-03. A demotion at 21, after showing enough in his first year to get called a breakout. He went back to the Czech league in the off-season, came back with a new sweater number, and his third season, 2003-04, produced 35 points in 82 games at 13.4 minutes a night. Nashville made the playoffs, lost the quarterfinal to Detroit Red Wings, and Erat picked up an assist in a 2-1 loss on April 10, 2004, his first point and his first loss in the postseason.

The leap nobody called

This season, the numbers moved in a way the Book had not projected. 53 points in 79 games, 45 of them assists. The pace went from 0.43 points a game to 0.67. The assists doubled, from 0.28 a night to 0.57. His minutes climbed from 13.4 to 20.8, a jump of 7.4 minutes, and in the six playoff games against Mighty Ducks of Anaheim he played 23.2 a night. Nashville was the 4th seed in the West, 42-28-7, 101 points, and the Predators lost the series in 6.

Speed at 95, acceleration at 94, agility at 94, shot power at 93. Those are the numbers of a winger who gets open and scores. Checking at 88 and balance at 88 say he stays on the puck when someone leans on him. Fighting at 50, injury at 53, faceoffs at 57: the parts they do not put on a line card.

The potential of 84 is the Bureau's ceiling. He is playing at it. The Index says +6, which means his raw form this year sits above what the Book will let him show. You are playing well and the number will not move.

What the room says

Tomas Vokoun92 is 90 in goal and has been the best player on Nashville Predators all season. David Legwand90 is 88 at centre. The two of them carry a roster where the third-highest grade belongs to a man on $0.60M with no contract left. Scott Walker85 is 83, Scott Hartnell84 is 82, both above the 74-grade average you would expect from a club's bottom two lines.

A 23-year-old Czech who was picked 191st slots into that room at 84, third on the club. He is the winger whose speed opens the ice for Legwand to make the play that gets Vokoun a rest.

"I don't think about what I am worth yet," Erat said, in the way a man says something when the question arrives before he has a thought for it. "I play. That is what I did in Milwaukee too, and I play here."

The market and the number

The contract is $0.60M. Zero years left. He is a free agent on a club that made the playoffs and lost in the first round. The free-agent pool is empty. Every available body comes off another club's roster, and the cost is more than the cap hit.

The question for Nashville Predators in the next 58 days is whether $0.60M is a fair price for a man the Book reads at his ceiling and the Index says could be higher. The question for Erat is whether a seventh-round pick from Trebic, 191st overall, Nashville's 12th choice in 1999, wants to find out what the number looks like without the ceiling on it. His morale is 87. The room wants him. The money does not match the man. That is what they have 58 days to solve.
