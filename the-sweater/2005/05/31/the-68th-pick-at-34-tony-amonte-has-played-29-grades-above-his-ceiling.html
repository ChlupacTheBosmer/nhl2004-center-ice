The 68th Pick, at 34: Tony Amonte has played 29 grades above his ceiling for 14 years and the Index just moved for the first time

Hingham, Massachusetts to four clubs, a Masterton, and $5.90M with three years left. The Bureau said 56. He has been 85.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterTuesday, 31 May 2005
ShareCopy link

The piece

The Bureau's Book has a number beside every man's name that was written before he ever played a league game. For Tony Amonte82 that number is 56. His overall today is 85. The gap between those two figures is 29 grades, and he has held it for 14 seasons without missing a game.

Amonte turns 35 in August. He is the captain of the Philadelphia Flyers, a club that finished 28-35-12 last season and watched the playoffs from the couch. He earns $5.90M with 3 years left. His morale reads 97. His form on the Development Index is minus 2, the first time the Index has moved against him since the Bureau started publishing it, and the first time the gap has narrowed.

The road out of Hingham

Hingham sits south of Boston, a town of shipwrights and granite foundations, the kind of place where a kid on skates in January is unremarkable. Amonte grew up there, 72 inches and 200 pounds by the time the New York Rangers picked him 68th overall in the third round of the 1988 draft. Third round means the Bureau had already moved on. A potential of 56 means they never expected him to be what he became.

He made his debut in the 1991 playoffs and played three seasons in New York, scoring 84 goals. Then Chicago called. The trade came with seven games left in 1993-94, the year the Rangers won the Stanley Cup. Amonte was already in Chicago by then, watching it from a distance he could not control. That is the kind of thing that stays with a man: being close enough to see the Cup and being traded seven games before anyone lifted it.

He won a gold medal with Team USA at the 1996 World Cup. He played a decade in Chicago, became a captain there, and by 2002-03 he was with the Phoenix Coyotes. Near the end of that season Philadelphia traded for him, and he has been in a Flyers sweater ever since. Four clubs. No injuries on record. 82 games last year, 82 this year.

What the grades say

The Book is not kind to Amonte the way it is kind to a 22-year-old with a ceiling of 90. His potential of 56 is the lowest number on his card, and it sits there like a dare. Everything else contradicts it.

His deking grade is 95, his passing 90, his puck control 90. Accuracy 89, acceleration 88. These are the grades of a man who has seen every defensive system the league can throw at him and has decided, over 14 seasons, that none of them are personal. The weak numbers tell a different story about what he will not do: FIGH 50, FACE 53, SPBI 53. He does not drop the gloves, does not win faceoffs, does not explode off the line.

He scores anyway. This season: 39 goals, 71 assists, 110 points in 82 games, 20.1 minutes a night. Last season: 56 goals, 62 assists, 118 points, 21.1 minutes a night. Two years running, every game, no time off. The Masterton he won last spring is the league's award for perseverance and dedication, and it is not hard to see why the voters picked him. A 33-year-old third-rounder from Massachusetts who played all 82 games and scored 118 points while his club limped through a first-round exit.

The minus 2

The Index moved against him this year. Minus 2. His base grade of 88, the one that has sat in the Book for seasons, reads 85 today with the Index factored in, and two grades is the first crack in a career where the gap between his potential and his production has never narrowed below 29.

He scored 110 points and played every game anyway. The Philadelphia club around him went from 40-29 and a first-round berth to 28-35-12 and nothing. The teammates are still there: Jeremy Roenick86 at 89, Simon Gagne84 at 86, Michal Handzus81, Marco Sturm85 and John LeClair all at 85, Keith Primeau80 at 84. A room full of 85s that lost 56 games.

Amonte said this spring that the Masterton meant something to him beyond the hardware, that it was the league acknowledging something about how a man carries himself when the results are not there. He said it without ceremony, the way a captain does when the dressing room is half empty and the season is over and nobody is asking the question for the paper.

Three years and $5.90M

The contract is not the story yet. Three years left at $5.90M for a 34-year-old with a potential of 56 and a form of minus 2 sounds, on paper, like a liability. But he has played 82 games in each of the last two seasons, never been on the injured list, and never had his potential look anything but wrong.

The rookie draft is 8 days away. Free agency closes in 58 days. Philadelphia will need to decide whether the $5.90M buys another 110-point season or the slow fade that everyone expects at 35. The Index says minus 2, the Book says 85, the potential still says 56.

Amonte has spent his whole career between those numbers. He is not finished between them yet.
