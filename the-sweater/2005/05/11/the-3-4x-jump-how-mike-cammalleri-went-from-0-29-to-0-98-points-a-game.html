The 3.4x jump: how Mike Cammalleri went from 0.29 to 0.98 points a game in one season

The Bureau's pace numbers are precise. The reasons are not. A 22-year-old on a 25-win club doubled his minutes and tripled his output, and the question is how much of that was talent and how much was opportunity.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterWednesday, 11 May 2005
ShareCopy link

The jump from 20 points in 69 games to 60 points in 61 games is not a hot streak. The Bureau's pace numbers read 0.29 points per game last season and 0.98 this season. That is a multiplier of 3.4. Mike Cammalleri83 is 22 years old. He played 11.8 minutes a night last season. He played 20.8 this season. His minutes went up 76%. His points went up 200%. His per-minute rate went up roughly 70%. He got better, and the ice time helped.

What kind of player goes from 0.29 to 0.98 on a club with 25 wins, and what does that mean for the price tag?

The pace numbers, read carefully

Mike Cammalleri is one of the Bureau's top risers this season at +6 on the Development Index, tied with Marian Gaborik99, Mike Komisarek82, and Vaclav Nedorost83. His base grade is 79. His current reading is 83. His potential is 98, which means 19 grades of room between where he is and where the Bureau thinks he can go. His morale is 98.

Over an 82-game season at his new pace, that is an 80-point player. At his old pace, it was a 24-point player. That is the difference between a fourth-liner and a second-line producer.

Last season he played 11.8 minutes a night. This season 20.8. If you divide points by minutes, the extra ice time did not simply dilute him. He was more effective per shift at 20.8 minutes than at 11.8.

The usage shift

The minutes jump from 11.8 to 20.8 tells you the coach moved him. On a Los Angeles Kings club that won 25 games, the top-six forward groups were not protected by a defensive system because no such system existed. A 25-win team does not game-plan against fourth-line players, which means the opposing coaches did not put their checking line on him. He had open ice to use his 93 deke and his 93 passing grade.

The Kings' top scorers this season: Zigmund Palffy83 with 100 points in 63 games, Jason Allison82 with 86 in 67, Adam Deadmarsh83 with 64 in 50, and Cammalleri with 60 in 61. Robert Svehla led defensemen with 57. Cammalleri was the fourth-forward on a team with four forwards above 60 points. The club scored 251 goals. Cammalleri's 60 points represent roughly a quarter of the team's offense. That is a big share on a bad team. On a good team, the same 60 points would represent roughly a sixth or a seventh, and nobody would notice.

The question is which one you are pricing. A 25-win club with no defensive structure and an aging roster gave him 20.8 minutes and open ice. If a contender puts him on the second line against top pairing defensemen, the pace might not hold. His shooting-under-pressure grade is 55. His puck-control-under-pressure grade is 60. Those are not grades that survive a structured opponent who sends a physical winger to body-check a 180-pound man in the neutral zone.

The price

His deal pays $1.10M with 1 year left. The Bureau's own arithmetic puts him at $18.3K per point. Free agency closes July 28, 78 days from now. The Kings have to decide whether 60 points on a 25-win club is worth raising off $1.10M, or whether they would rather let the market sort it out.

The risk is straightforward: the pace numbers are inflated by the context of the team he played on. The upside is also straightforward: he is 22, he has 19 grades of room according to the Bureau, his Index is at +6, and his morale is 98. He tripled his pace in his second professional season and the development curve has not bent yet.

He also has a fighting grade of 50, which is a floor, and floors are harder to raise than ceilings. The Bureau believes he can be a 98. Whether he can be a 98 at 180 pounds with a fighting grade of 50 in a league that punishes small men is the question. The next 19 grades will answer it. He does not need to be a big man. He needs to be a man who does not get separated from the puck when the game is tied in February and the other team sends a hit because they know he will not send one back.

He scored 60 points because the ice was open and nobody was checking him. The next step is scoring 60 points when somebody is. The 78 days until free agency close will tell the Kings whether that next step is coming on their ice or someone else's.

What the numbers cannot tell you

Cammalleri was born in Richmond Hill, Ontario, and his best sport was soccer. He played for the Toronto Red Wings in the GTHL. He declined the OHL at 15 to keep his college eligibility, went to Junior A, scored 103 points in 41 games, and committed to the University of Michigan at 15. He played in an outdoor game at 19 in front of 74,554 people before he played a professional shift. The road to 0.98 points a game on a 25-win club ran through a Junior A rink in Bramalea and a college scholarship and a hip-flexor strain at Spartan Stadium.

He is not a lottery pick. He was the 49th player chosen in 2001. He scored 20 points in his first season and 60 in his second. The Bureau gave him a base of 79, which is a good grade. The ceiling of 98 is a great one. The 19 grades between them are the whole argument, and he has 78 days to decide where they get closed.
