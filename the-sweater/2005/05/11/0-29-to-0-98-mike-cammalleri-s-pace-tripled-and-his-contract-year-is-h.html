0.29 to 0.98: Mike Cammalleri's pace tripled and his contract year is here

At 22, the Kings' second-rounder went from 20 points to 60. The Bureau says 19 grades left. The deal has 1 year left.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterWednesday, 11 May 2005
ShareCopy link

Mike Cammalleri83 scored 20 points in 69 games last season. He scored 60 in 61 this season. The Bureau's pace numbers read 0.29 points per game against 0.98, a multiplier of 3.4. He is 22 years old, he wears an A for Los Angeles Kings, and his contract pays him $1.10M with 1 year left.

The Los Angeles Kings finished with 79 points, 25 wins, and no spring. They scored 251 goals and allowed 273. Cammalleri's 60 points ranked third on the club, behind Zigmund Palffy87 and Jason Allison87, and ahead of Adam Deadmarsh87, whose knee carries a return date of June 4. Cammalleri played 20.8 minutes a night this season, up from 11.8 last year. He went from a scratch candidate to a man who logged more ice than the club's top center and finished with a grade of 83, the fourth-highest on a roster whose top two scorers are 33 and older.

He is not the Kings' best player. Roman Cechmanek91 is graded 91 in goal. Robert Svehla82 won the Norris Trophy last season at 36 and logged 57 points this year. The room has age on both wings and a center past 30. Cammalleri is the youngest man on the club whose base grade sits above 79, and the Development Index has him at +6, tied for the league's highest riser alongside Marian Gaborik98, Mike Komisarek82, and Vaclav Nedorost78.

The road before the jump

Cammalleri grew up in Richmond Hill, Ontario, a suburb north of Toronto. His best sport was soccer. He played minor hockey for the Toronto Red Wings in the GTHL and went to the 1996 Quebec International Pee-Wee Tournament with them. The OHL's St. Michael's Majors picked him in their Priority Draft, but he did not play; he wanted to keep his American college eligibility alive.

Bramalea Blues, Junior A. He won the OPJHL's rookie of the year at 15. The following season he scored 103 points in 41 games and was named the Ontario Hockey Association's top draft prospect. At 15, he had already committed to the University of Michigan on a full hockey scholarship. He took an accelerated course schedule at The Country Day School in King City and graduated at 17.

Michigan, 1999. He scored 13 goals in 39 games as a freshman. He led the team with 29 goals as a sophomore in 2000-01, made the CCHA first team, and went to the Frozen Four before Michigan lost the NCAA semifinal to Boston College. The Kings drafted him 49th overall in the 2001 draft. He went back to school.

On October 1, 2001, Michigan played Michigan State outdoors at Spartan Stadium in front of 74,554 people. He nearly missed it with a hip-flexor strain. He figured in all three Michigan goals in a 3-3 draw.

He led Michigan to the CCHA championship in 2001-02 with 23 goals and 44 points in 29 games. He won the West Regional MVP award. Michigan went back to the Frozen Four. He was 19. He came to the Kings after that, not before.

What the Book says, what the room says

The Bureau's Book has Cammalleri at a base of 79 with a potential of 98. That is 19 grades of room. His current reading is 83, the base plus 6 on the Index. His morale is 98.

His top grades tell you what kind of player he is: deking 93, passing 93, puck control 90, agility 89, speed 89, hero ball 89. His bottom grades tell you what he is not: fighting 50, shooting under pressure 55, puck control under pressure 60. He is 69 inches and 180 pounds. The grades agree with what you see on the ice. He plays with the puck at his feet and finds passing lanes at full speed. He does not look like a man who wants to be hit. The gap between his ceiling grades and his floor grades is 43 points. A fighting grade of 50 on a potential of 98 means the Bureau believes he can be a 98 without ever changing his body.

He went from 11.8 minutes a night to 20.8 because the Kings had no structure protecting anyone and no reason not to dress him. 25 wins in 82 games is a club that could not hold a line. On a team like that, a 22-year-old with a 93 deke gets the puck on his stick in open ice because nobody is organized enough to take it away. He scored 60 points against a schedule that did not game-plan for him.

A teammate, one who has been around long enough to watch the second-round pick from Michigan go from 11.8 minutes to the second line, would tell you the same thing from the other side of the glass: the kid was ready in training camp and nobody on the bench had a reason to argue.

The contract year

Cammalleri's deal pays $1.10M and has 1 year left. Free agency closes on July 28, 78 days from now. The Kings will have to decide whether a man with a base of 79 and a ceiling of 98 is worth raising off $1.10M, or whether a 3.4x pace jump on a 25-win club carries a discount.

The pace numbers from the Bureau: 0.29 to 0.98. His minutes doubled. His usage moved from the fourth line to the top six. He made the A. He scored 60 points in 61 games on a club that could not find 80.

He also has a fighting grade of 50 and a 180-pound frame in a league that punishes small men who have not yet learned to expect the hit. The Development Index has him +6 right now. The Index does not measure what happens when a club builds around him and opposing coaches put a plan on him every night.

The road from Richmond Hill to the A on a Kings sweater ran through a soccer pitch, a Junior A rink in Bramalea, an accelerated high school schedule, and a 74,554-person outdoor game. He never played a major junior game. He chose college at 15, stayed in school after the draft at 19, and came to the pros with a degree half-finished and 20 points in his first full season. This season he scored 60. The Bureau says 98 is in him. He still needs 19 grades. A better club and a bigger body would help. Neither is on the sheet.

What is on the sheet is $1.10M, 1 year left, and 78 days until free agency closes.
