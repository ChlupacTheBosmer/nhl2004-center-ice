The 49th Pick, at 22: Mike Cammalleri stayed in school after the draft and it cost him two years or gave him everything

He turned down the OHL at 15, committed to Michigan at 15, played the Cold War at 19, and scored 60 points at 22. The Kings picked him 49th overall in 2001 and he went back to college anyway.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterWednesday, 11 May 2005
ShareCopy link

Mike Cammalleri87 was 19 years old and had just led the University of Michigan to a CCHA championship when the Los Angeles Kings took him 49th overall in the 2001 draft. He went back to school. The Wolverines were young and he wore an A. He scored 23 goals in 29 games that year and won the West Regional MVP. Then he came to the pros and scored 20 points in 69 games.

The Kings did not need to win that night. A rebuilding club with 79 points had no path to a first-round pick. Taking a college senior with a potential ceiling in the second round was the safe read. Safe meant waiting. Cammalleri had a degree in progress and a scholarship that mattered to his family. His maternal grandparents came from Poland and Czechoslovakia and survived the Holocaust. His mother is Jewish, his father is Sicilian. He is 69 inches and 180 pounds and he decided at 15 that he was going to college before he was going to the NHL. Nobody talked him out of it. The draft was 49th overall. He was not a man the organization was going to strong-arm.

He was right about the college path, in the end. At 22, he scored 60 points in 61 games, played 20.8 minutes a night, and wore an A for a club that won 25 games. The Bureau's pace numbers read 3.4 times his 2003-04 output. He is the league's co-leader in form on the Development Index at +6. His morale and potential are both 98. His base is 79. He has 19 grades left and 1 year on a $1.10M contract.

What the road cost

Cammalleri grew up in Richmond Hill, Ontario. Soccer was his best sport. He played minor hockey for the Toronto Red Wings in the GTHL, and when the St. Michael's Majors picked him in the OHL Priority Draft he declined. He went to the Bramalea Blues in the OPJHL instead, won the league's rookie of the year at 15, and scored 103 points in 41 games the next season. The Ontario Hockey Association named him its top prospect. The University of Michigan gave him a full scholarship; he had committed at 15. He took an accelerated schedule at The Country Day School and graduated at 17.

At Michigan, he was a 13-goal freshman. He led the team with 29 goals as a sophomore in 2000-01, made the CCHA first team, and lost the NCAA semifinal to Boston College in the Frozen Four. That is when the Kings picked him. He stayed.

The Cold War game on October 1, 2001, between Michigan and Michigan State at Spartan Stadium drew 74,554 people, a world record at the time for a hockey crowd. Cammalleri nearly did not play because of a hip-flexor strain. He scored or assisted on all three Michigan goals in a 3-3 tie. He was 19. He had never played a professional shift.

He came to the Kings for 2003-04 and played 69 games in 11.8 minutes a night, 11 goals, 9 assists, 20 points. He played a fourth-line role on a club that missed the spring again. He was 21. He was not ready, or rather he was ready in the ways that matter for a college player (he could read a play, he could handle the puck, he could pass it) but not in the ways that matter for a 180-pound man at 5 feet 9 inches who had spent four years in a conference where defensemen did not hit first and ask later.

What the road bought

This season, he is the Kings' alternate captain. He is 22. The team around him is old: Zigmund Palffy87 is 33, Jason Allison86 is 33, Robert Svehla is 36. Adam Deadmarsh87 has a knee with a June 4 return date. Lubomir Visnovsky80 has a concussion. The Kings finished 25-37-11 with 79 points and missed the spring for the second straight year. They have a goaltender graded 91 in Roman Cechmanek90 and a Norris Trophy winner in Svehla, and they are not close to being good enough.

Cammalleri is the youngest player on that roster with a base grade above 79. His grades are lopsided: deking 93, passing 93, puck control 90, agility 89, speed 89. Fighting 50. Shooting under pressure 55. Puck control under pressure 60. He can play. He cannot survive. The gap between his best attribute and his worst is 43 grades. The Bureau's potential of 98 says the deking and the hands are worth more than the fighting and the grit. That might be true. It might also be the kind of thing you believe when you watch a college player at 22 and forget what February looks like when the games are tight and the other team sends a 215-pound winger to body-check a man whose fighting grade is 50.

He is on a $1.10M deal with 1 year left. Free agency closes July 28. The Kings will have to decide whether 60 points on a 25-win club is a price or a discount.

The 49th pick is the kind of position where the Bureau cannot promise you anything. The 2001 draft went first to Ilya Kovalchuk91, who has 138 points at 22 and a base of 89. The seventh pick was Mike Komisarek81, who went from 3 points to 82 in a single season at 23. Cammalleri is between them, a man who chose the long way and got here in time to be the best 22-year-old on a club nobody watches in April. The road bought him a career. It also cost him two years of physical development in a major junior locker room where they would have thrown him into a board and made him learn to get back up. He has a fighting grade of 50 and a potential of 98. The next year will show whether those can coexist.

He plays for July.
