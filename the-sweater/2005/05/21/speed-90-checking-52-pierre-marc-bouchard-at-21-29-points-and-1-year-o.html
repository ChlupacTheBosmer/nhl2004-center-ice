Speed 90, Checking 52: Pierre-Marc Bouchard at 21, 29 points, and 1 year of control

The 8th pick of 2002 plays 11.6 minutes on a 79-point club. The Index has him up 6. The Bureau says a potential of 88 is in there somewhere.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterSaturday, 21 May 2005
ShareCopy link

Pierre-Marc Bouchard79 was 18 years old in his first full professional season. He played 50 games and put up 20 points. The Minnesota Wild had drafted him 8th overall in 2002 and handed him the jersey before most kids his age finished high school. Three springs later, he has 29 points in 82 games, 11.6 minutes a night, and a contract that expires in July.

The Bureau's Development Index has him up 6 on the base grade. The Book says 77 today, against a base of 74 and a potential of 88. The gap between those last two numbers is what the whole argument is about.

The grades that don't match

Bouchard's offensive attributes cluster in a way that makes the physical grades look like a clerical error. Speed 90. Deke 88. Pass 88. Puck 88. Acceleration 87, Agility 87. Then: Checking 52. Toughness 50. Fighting 50.

The room says morale 95. A 21-year-old on a 22-38-9 club that missed the spring by 14 points, with 11.6 minutes a night and a third-line role, is not unhappy. Ask anyone why and the answer is the same: he knows his speed plays at 90 and he knows what happens when someone puts a shoulder on him in the corner.

The 38-grade spread between his best attribute and his worst explains his ice time. Coaches at Minnesota Wild have used him in 11.6 minutes a night because that is what you get when you dress a center who cannot win a puck battle to save his life but can outrun every forward on the other side. The minutes go to Wes Walz82 at 84 overall, to Saku Koivu84 at 85, because those men can play 20 and do the things Bouchard's body cannot do yet.

The road from Sherbrooke

He grew up on the South Shore of Montreal, the kind of hockey geography where every kid plays the Pee-Wee tournament before he decides whether he's serious. Bouchard played the 1998 Quebec International Pee-Wee Tournament with the College-Français Rive-Sud team. He was 14. Four years later he was wearing 96 in Minnesota.

The background sheet is short. A Pee-Wee tournament in 1998. A pro debut at 18 in 2002-03 with 7 goals and 20 points in 50 games. Then 36 games and 10 points last season. Then this season: 82 games, 14 goals, 15 assists, 29 points, 13 penalty minutes. He has never been injured on record. He has never missed a training camp the record can see.

That 2003-04 line of 36 games is the one that stings. He went from 50 games and 20 points at 18 to 36 games and 10 points at 19. The record carries no injury. The record carries no explanation. What it carries is a player who came back at 21 and played every game on a team that won 22.

The contract year

$1.20M, 1 year left. He turns 22 on the 27th of April, which means he will be 21 in this contract's final game if Minnesota plays one, and 22 by the time free agency closes on the 28th of July.

The draft is 18 days away. The rookie draft on June 8 means Minnesota's front office is thinking about next year. The question for them is whether they re-sign a center with Speed 90 and Checking 52 before or after the draft. The question for Bouchard is whether his agent has anything to compare his $1.20M to.

Vaclav Nedorost80 plays for Florida Panthers on a similar deal, $1.20M, 1 year left, 14th overall in 2000, 25 points at 23. The difference is Nedorost is on an 11-loss team. Bouchard is on a 79-point team. Neither will be in the spring.

What the Index says, what the room says

The Development Index has him +6 on the base. That means the Bureau is seeing a player performing above what the Book's base grade would suggest. His 29 points are 19 more than last season. His 82 games are 46 more. The Index is reacting to volume and availability, not necessarily to a sudden leap in the underlying game.

His potential is 88. His base is 74. The Bureau thinks there are 14 grades of hockey in him that the Book hasn't seen yet. A player with Speed 90 and 14 grades in reserve is a terrifying thought for whoever draws him in the playoffs. A player with Checking 52 and 14 grades in reserve is also a terrifying thought, depending on which end of the ice he's standing on.

The room says morale 95. The room says 11.6 minutes a night. The room says Saku Koivu takes the 20-minute assignments and Bouchard takes what's left. The room says a 21-year-old who was the 8th player chosen in a draft is playing the fourth-center role on a club that finished 4th in the Northwest with 79 points.

What comes next

The draft is 18 days away. Free agency closes 68 days away. Bouchard plays for July.

If he finds 15 minutes and keeps the 29 points, he has a case. If the 14 grades show up in the checking and toughness columns, he has a different case. If the Index's +6 is a blip on a 79-point team and next year brings 10 points in 50 games, the front office lets him walk at $1.20M and no one in Sherbrooke loses sleep.

What the Bureau printed on this man's page is a potential of 88. What the Book prints today is 74. What the Development Index shows is a player six grades above his base right now, which means the Bureau thinks he is closer to 88 than the base number suggests, but he hasn't arrived.

Speed 90, Checking 52. Twenty-one years old. One year of control left. The 8th pick of 2002 plays 11.6 minutes on a team that missed the spring by 14 points. The draft is 18 days away. The phone will ring in July.
