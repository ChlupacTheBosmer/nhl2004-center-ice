26 Past the Ceiling, 26 Wins Behind Him: Geoff Sanderson at 33

The Bureau projected 61. He is playing at 87. Neither number explains why he is still here.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterMonday, 23 May 2005
ShareCopy link

The Bureau's potential for Geoff Sanderson84 is 61. His base grade is 87. He is 33 years old, he has 14 seasons in the league, and he scored 64 of Columbus Blue Jackets's 222 goals this season. That is 28.8 percent of everything his club put in the net. The club won 26 games.

The Bureau grades a player's ceiling, the top he can reach given his tools and his body and his mind. The 61 was written when Sanderson was a teenager and his frame was still filling out. The 87 took 14 seasons to reach, on a franchise that has never won a playoff series with him on it, from a town in the Northwest Territories where the skating rink freezes over in September and thaws in May.

The road nobody asked him to walk

Sanderson was born in Hay River on February 1, 1972. Hartford picked him 36th overall in 1990, second round. He played his first NHL game at 19, six games in the last stretch of 1990-91. The next year he played 64 games and stayed. The Whalers missed the playoffs every season he was there. Five full years, 30 goals or more in four of them, and the post-season was a word that happened to other teams.

The franchise moved to Carolina. He stayed for half a season in 1997-98, then was traded to Vancouver, then traded again to Buffalo. Two clubs in one year. He was on the Sabres in 1999 when they went to the Cup Final. He was third line. Dallas won it on the Hull goal in triple overtime, the one that everybody still argues about. Sanderson watched from the end of a line that did not matter in the games that decided anything.

Columbus Blue Jackets took him in the 2000 Expansion Draft. He scored 30-plus in his first year in Columbus and 30-plus again in 2002-03. He went back to Vancouver at the deadline in 2003-04, played in a first-round loss to Calgary, and came back to Columbus. He has been here ever since.

Six clubs, one expansion pick, one Cup Final appearance as a passenger, fourteen years, no trophy case.

What the Book says

The current grades: Speed 93, Accuracy 92, Shot Power 91, Puck Control 92, Acceleration 87. Fighting 50. Injuries 52. A winger who can fly and shoot and protect the puck, and who cannot be hit without being hurt himself.

He plays 21.7 minutes a night on a team with 81 points. The team needs him there. Sanderson is the only player on the roster who can carry a line from one end to the other without the sequence breaking. His best teammates this season: Pascal Leclaire91 at 88 in goal, Marc Denis87 at 86, Todd Marchant82 at 84 up the middle, Dimitri Kalinin82 at 80 on the back end. Nobody else on the forward group above 80 except David Vyborny78 at 80 on the right wing. This is a team that needs him for everything.

His overall today is 85. The base is 87. The Index has him at plus 1, one grade above his base. For a man who scored 121 points on 82 games and 64 goals, the form is steady. The Development Index looks at the last few weeks. One grade up on a 33-year-old who scored 28.8 percent of his team's goals. He is still producing at a rate the Book did not think he could hold this long.

Morale 92. On a 26-win team. At 33, on a contract with 2 years left. He is the captain, and the room is his.

What the money says

$6.34M this year. Two years left. $52,368 per point at 121 points. A 33-year-old winger making top-six money on a team that scored 222 goals all year, 64 of them from one man.

The Bureau's potential of 61 was set long ago, probably when he was 18 and 19 and still growing into a frame that would end up at 6 feet 2, 190 pounds. The potential says what the tools could build if everything went right. Everything went wrong for 10 years first. The Whalers were the Whalers. Carolina was a new building and a new name and no depth. Vancouver traded him inside a month. Buffalo used him as the fourth option on a Cup run. Expansion gave him ice time and nothing else.

61 could not see a man outrunning defenders at 93 speed in a league that gets faster every year, still showing up to find 64 goals at 33 on a club that should have been rebuilding for the third straight draft.

The room at the end of it

CBJ scored 222 goals. He scored 64. His next-best teammate on the scoring list is not on the sheet's top 30 in the league. The Blue Jackets have Leclaire, a 22-year-old goaltender at 88 with 49 starts and a +4 on the Index. They have Vyborny at 80 on the right wing. They have nothing else that grades above 84 except the captain.

He cannot be hit. Fighting 50. Injuries 52. Speed 93. A winger who survives by being gone before the shoulder arrives. At 33, that is a full decade of not being where the hit is, and his body has held. In 14 seasons of league hockey, the sheet does not carry a single trip to the injured list.

2 years left on the contract. He will be 35. The potential is 61, 26 grades below what he is. The room says he is the captain. The team says he scores 28.8 percent of their goals. The Book says 85 tonight, 87 on a good week, and a ceiling of 61 that nobody has believed for a long time.

Hay River, Northwest Territories. The rink is small and the season is short. He left at 18 and he has been playing since. Fourteen years, six clubs, one expansion pick, one Cup Final as a passenger, one potential number the Bureau wrote down when he was a teenager, and 64 goals at 33 on a team that won 26 games.
