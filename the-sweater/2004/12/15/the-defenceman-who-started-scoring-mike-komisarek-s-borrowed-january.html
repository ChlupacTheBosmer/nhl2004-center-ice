The Defenceman Who Started Scoring: Mike Komisarek's borrowed January

Montreal gives up 125 goals in 29 games. Its 22-year-old from West Islip, drafted seventh in 2001, is up six on the Index with 35 points, and it is the only warmth in the room.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterWednesday, 15 December 2004
ShareCopy link

Mike Komisarek82 has 35 points in 29 games from the blue line. He is 22, he was drafted seventh overall in 2001, and he was born in West Islip, New York, which is to say he arrived in Montreal the way half the league's defencemen arrive: to take care of the other team's best player, not to put points on a sheet.

He has 31 assists. The Book has him at 76 overall, and the Development Index has him up six this month, one of the biggest risers in the league. The room that contains him is Montreal, 9-17-1, 23 points, three straight losses, 125 goals against in 29 games.

The points are the one thing that is going right in that room.

The quietest stat line in the East

The Bureau's Index catches form the way the standings catch luck, and Komisarek's +6 is the sort of movement that usually belongs to a winger who has found a line. A defenseman whose reputation was built on the penalty kill and the wall does not suddenly carry 31 assists unless he is seeing the ice differently, or the ice is simply more open because his club is behind so often.

Either way the numbers are real. The man skating them is not.

He is a Long Island kid who came out of the 2001 draft as the seventh name called, and Montreal put him straight into the role he had played his whole life. You notice him on the kill first, a veteran in that room would say, and the points are a gift on top of the work. The room has lost three straight now, and the two men it leans on most are out: Andrei Markov76 with an elbow, returning December 19, and Yanic Perreault76 with a back that does not come back until February 3.

The club's answer has been to lean harder on the 22-year-old who arrived to be a wall. So far the Index says he is giving them a wall and a point a game.

Among the blue-liners the Index has moving up, Komisarek's production stands apart. Klesla and Jackman are at 19 and 16 points in the same neighbourhood of games. Komisarek has nearly doubled them, on a team that has scored 100 goals in 29 games and is looking for offence anywhere it can find it.

The Book at 76 does not flatter him, but the direction does. The Index has him +6 on the month, and in Montreal that is a headline all its own.

What the room says

He is quieter than the stat line, is what they would tell you. He goes about the penalty kill the way he went about it in October, before any of the assists arrived. The points have not changed the manner; the manner is the reason the points arrived.

A young defenceman who starts scoring usually starts picking his spots. The ones who stay in the league are the ones who do not. The room in Montreal, which is young and thin and losing, treats his 35 points the way a man treats a good coat in a bad winter: you wear it, and you do not talk about it.

There is a version of this season where Montreal is even worse than 23 points, because an elbow and a back sent two of its best men to the injured list and the schedule kept coming. He has been the reason it is not worse.

He was drafted seventh overall to be a mirror for the other team's biggest winger. He is 22, up six on the Index, with 35 points in 29 games. The mirror is still there. It just has a point total now.
