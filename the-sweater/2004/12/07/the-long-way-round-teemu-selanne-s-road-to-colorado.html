The Long Way Round: Teemu Selanne's road to Colorado

He taught kindergarten in Espoo, served in the Finnish army, and signed an offer sheet Calgary couldn't hold. At 34, he's on the best team in the league with one year left.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterTuesday, 7 December 2004
ShareCopy link

Teemu Selanne81 is 34 years old. He has 28 goals in 26 games for Colorado Avalanche this season, a pace that reads 142 points over 82. The Avs are 21-4-0, the best record in the league by 3 points, with a goal differential of +59. Selanne is on a one-year deal worth $6.50M with 0 years left on it. He becomes a free agent in July.

He has never won a Stanley Cup.

The boy from Espoo

Selanne was born on July 3, 1970, in Helsinki. His family moved to Espoo when he was around 5 years old. He had a twin, Paavo, and an older brother, Panu. The boys played hockey and football together on whatever patch of ground was flat enough. Teemu was small. His mother Liisa and father Ilmari divorced in the late 1980s.

He played three sports as a youth: hockey, bandy, football. Paavo eventually left them all for field hockey. Teemu stayed with the one he was worst at for the longest time. He joined the junior squad at Jokerit, the club in his own city.

Between junior hockey and the NHL, he did two things that would sound like filler in any other player's story. He spent two years in business school, then served a mandatory year in the Finnish Defence Forces. While playing for Jokerit's senior team, he taught kindergarten for three years. He visited children at Helsinki's children's hospital and, with a colleague, co-founded the Godfathers' Foundation, which raises money for sick kids.

He was not in a hurry to leave Finland. He arrived in the NHL at 22, drafted four years earlier.

1988 to 1992

The Winnipeg Jets took Selanne 10th overall in the 1988 entry draft. He attended their training camp, went back home for military service, and stayed. The Jets let their European picks develop in their native countries, and Selanne was content. In the Finnish second division in 1988-89, he scored 69 points in 35 games. In the SM-liiga the year after, a leg injury cost him most of the season, 12 points in 11 games. He returned in 1990-91 for 33 goals and 58 points in 42 games, and the Raimo Kilpiö trophy as the league's most gentlemanly player. In 1991-92 he scored 39 goals, won the scoring title, and buried the winning goal as Jokerit took the Kanada-malja.

Then the Calgary Flames tried to steal him. They extended a three-year offer sheet worth $2.7 million, $1.5 million more than Winnipeg had been offering. Winnipeg matched it, grudgingly. Selanne made his NHL debut on October 6, 1992, against the Detroit Red Wings, and recorded two assists in a 4-1 win. He was 22.

The room in Denver

The Colorado room is built around skill. Peter Forsberg89 is the best player on it, an overall of 89 with 55 points in 26 games. Joe Sakic84 is behind him at 84. Paul Kariya84 and Milan Hejduk83 round out the forward group at 84 and 83. No one on that roster is asking Selanne to drop the gloves or stand in the slot and eat a check. His weakest attributes are fighting (50), toughness (50) and injury resistance (50). His strongest are deking (90), puck handling (89) and speed (88). The room knows what he is. They give him the ice and let him go.

He has 45 points in 26 games, a 142 pace. His ice time is 17.0 minutes a night, down from 18.4 last season when he played 82 games and scored 140 points. The Development Index has him one point below his base of 89, which puts his overall at 81 today. His morale reads 100.

The contract and the clock

One year. $6.50M. After June 30 he walks unless someone writes him a new one. At 34, with a base grade that reads 89 but a form line dragging it to 81, with a physical game built entirely on his hands and his feet and nothing above a 50 in the categories that wear men down, the market for him is narrow. He could go home. He could take a lesser number somewhere and chase a fourth line role with a cup window still open. Or he could play for July, in the best regular-season record in the league, and find out what happens in April.

Colorado played 24 playoff games last season and won 13 of them. Toronto took the Cup. The Avs were one series away from the final, and Selanne scored 14 goals and 30 points in those 24 games. He was the best player on the ice in several of them and it was not close.

The small Finnish kid

The man who taught kindergarten in Espoo and buried the winner for Jokerit in the Kanada-malja is sitting on a one-year deal, playing 17 minutes a night, scoring at a 142 pace, on a team that has lost 4 games all season. His deking grade is a 90. His toughness is a 50. He will never change that. The question the room has been living since October 1 is whether 28 goals in 26 games from a 34-year-old who cannot fight, cannot take a hit, and cannot be relied on to stay healthy is enough to win one more series than he did last spring.

He plays in July. What that means depends on who is still standing in April.
