A Fifty-Faceoff Captain: Tony Amonte and the geometry of heart

The Masterton went to the player whose Book reads 89 deke, 50 faceoff, 50 fight. Three seasons later, he wears the C in Philadelphia and is scoring for a team that cannot win.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterTuesday, 28 December 2004
ShareCopy link

The Book gives Tony Amonte85 a faceoff rating of 50 and his deke an 89. The Bill Masterton Memorial Trophy ended up in his hands last June because of what that gap says about the kind of player he is and the kind of captain he makes.

He is 34. He was born in Hingham, Massachusetts, 200 pounds, left-handed, drafted 68th overall in 1988 by the New York Rangers. He scored 84 goals in three seasons before the Rangers traded him to Chicago with 7 games left in 1993-94, the year New York won the Cup. He went to Phoenix in 2002-03, then to Philadelphia near the end of that season. Two trades, three clubs, no injuries on record, no suspension. Just a man who kept skating and kept getting shipped somewhere new.

The Masterton is for persistence, sportsmanship, dedication. It went to Amonte because the room saw him every day: 21.1 minutes a night last season, 118 points, 92 penalty minutes from a man whose fighting grade is 50. He does not fight and he does not win faceoffs, but he dekes, he passes, and he gets to the net at the right moment. Three attribute grades that should not produce a 118-point season.

What the room says when the record cannot

Philadelphia is 11-16-3, fourth in the Atlantic, 31 points on the board. They have scored 124 and given up 143. Over the last ten games they went 3-7, including a 1-7 home loss to Toronto and a 4-5 loss to New Jersey. The goals against came in waves: 7 from Toronto, 6 from Florida, 6 from San Jose, 5 from the Rangers, 5 from New Jersey. A leaky club.

Amonte has 21 goals and 34 assists in 34 games. That is 55 points. He had 118 in 82 last season. The rate is up, and the team around him is worse. The Bureau's Development Index has him at -2. His base grade is 88; today the Book reads 79. The Index sees the team collapsing around him and marks him down as if the goals against are on his stick.

He does not look like a man below his base. He looks like the only player on the roster producing at his true level. Jeremy Roenick89 is 83, Simon Gagne86 80, Keith Primeau84 80. Amonte's base is 88, which would put him first on this club if the Index let him sit at his real number.

His contract runs three more years at $5.90M. He is not going anywhere. The team that drafted him 68th overall is not the team that will pay off this deal, and neither is the team that gave him the Masterton. He will finish it in Philadelphia, scoring 50-plus points in a season nobody watches, on a team that finishes below .500 and forgets him by August.

The potential was always wrong

The Bureau grades his potential at 54. His base grade is 88. He has exceeded his ceiling by 34 points on a 50-to-99 scale, which is the kind of gap that makes the metric look stupid. A 54 potential means the Bureau, at some point, looked at this 16-year-old from Hingham and saw a third-liner. He scored 84 goals in three seasons as a Ranger. He won the World Cup in 1996. The potential number says he should not be here. He is here, and he is wearing the C.

The Masterton is the Bureau's way of measuring the gap between what you were supposed to be and what you showed up to do, across 14 years.

His morale sits at 89. He is not angry, not sulking, not demanding a trade. He plays 20.4 minutes a night and goes home. His faceoff is 50 because he does not care about faceoffs. His toughness is 58 because he has never cared about that either. The grades that make him a hockey player are the ones the Masterton does not measure: deking 89, passing 84, hands 84, accuracy 83, acceleration 82, speed 81. Those are the numbers of a man who reads the ice before the D do and puts the puck where it needs to be while the other team is still guessing.

December, for a man who has seen this before

Philadelphia's December reads like a horror film for a scoring winger. The last ten: 4 goals against from Ottawa, 5 from Atlanta, 7 from Toronto, 6 from Florida, 6 from San Jose. The wins came against Dallas (5-1), Ottawa (4-2), and Buffalo (5-4). The rest of the time he was on the ice while the other team scored five or six or seven times, and there is no deke that stops that.

He is not the type to stand in front of his locker after a 1-7 loss and promise something. The background sheet tells you he played for Team USA in the 1996 World Cup and won gold. He has worn a jersey that mattered more than this one, in a building that mattered more than this one, and come home. He knows how to be the best player on a bad team. He did it in Chicago for seven years after the trade. He is doing it now.

The room around him would say: the C is on the right guy because he shows up at 20.4 minutes a night at 34 and nobody in that building produces more than he does. Faceoff grade 50, deke 89. The trophy is in the gap between them.
