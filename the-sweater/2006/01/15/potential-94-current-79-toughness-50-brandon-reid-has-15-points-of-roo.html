Potential 94, Current 79, Toughness 50: Brandon Reid Has 15 Points of Room in a Body That Won't Stretch

The 208th pick of 2000 is 6 foot 8, weighs 165 pounds, skates like nobody in this league, and is playing the best hockey of his life in a contract year nobody can answer for by July.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterSunday, 15 January 2006
ShareCopy link

The piece

The Bureau grades speed on a 50-to-99 scale. Two men in this league sit at 99 on the acceleration grade. One is Marian Gaborik99, the 23-year-old whose form has him 18 points above base and wearing a Toronto sweater that looks like it was built for him. The other is Brandon Reid79, who is 6 foot 8, weighs 165 pounds, and cannot be hit.

Not will not be hit. Cannot. The Book has his Toughness at 50, his Fighting at 50, his Checking at 50. The floor of every physical attribute the Bureau measures. He was the 208th pick of the 2000 draft, a seventh-round selection out of Kirkland, Quebec, and the men who drafted him got Speed 99, Acceleration 99, and Agility 98 in return. They also got 50, 50, 50 in everything that keeps a man healthy through an 82-game season.

Brandon Reid is 24. His contract reads $0.60M with zero years left. He is 46 points from 43 games, a pace of 1.07 a night, and the only year he has spent entirely in this league produced 58 points in 73 games. He is running ahead of his best full season on fewer games and more ice time. The Bureau's Development Index has him at +4 this winter.

The ceiling and the frame

The potential grade is 94. The current overall is 79. Fifteen points of room between what they think he can be and what the record says he is today. The Bureau wrote 94 the summer he was 19, when he was a skinny French-Canadian kid with hands and a stride, and it has not moved. The current grade has moved: 77 at base, 79 on the Index right now.

A 94 potential in this league puts you in the room with men wearing letters. It says you are the best version of yourself in your prime. Brandon Reid is not the best version of himself. He is the best version of himself that a 165-pound frame allows.

He plays 20.4 minutes a night. That is second-line time on a team that has Keith Tkachuk90 at left wing, Doug Weight85 at center, and Chris Pronger86 running the back end. Those are three men with overall grades of 90, 85, and 86. Reid plays with them and against them in practice every day, and the coaches put him out there because when he has the puck in open ice, nobody in this league closes the gap the way his stride does.

The road that left nothing behind

The record opens on December 1, 2004, and Reid was already a Vancouver Eagle at that point. He came to St. Louis on March 4, 2005, in a mid-season move the record does not describe in detail. He was 23. He had played 76 games that year, put up 70 points, and been hurt once, an abdomen issue that kept him out three weeks in January and February.

No background sheet, no junior team, no college, no tournament. The desk has a birthplace in Kirkland, Quebec, a draft line that reads 2000, 208th overall, and the seasons he has put up since he turned pro. A seventh-round pick from a town most scouts drive through.

What the record shows is a man whose production jumped when his minutes did. In his first full year, 19.1 minutes a night produced 70 points in 76 games. Last season in St. Louis, 14.3 minutes a night produced 58 in 73. This year, back to 20.4 minutes, the pace is higher than either. More ice, more points.

What July costs

$0.60M. Zero years. He is 24. The trade deadline is 38 days away. Free agency closes on July 28. At his current pace, St. Louis is paying $13,043 per point for a man the Bureau grades at 94 potential. No other contract in this building comes close to that number.

St. Louis sits at 57 points through 43 games, 12-9 at home, 14-8 on the road, in third place in the Central. They are not rebuilding. They are not selling. They have a Norris Trophy-winning defenseman in Chris Pronger and a 90-overall winger in Keith Tkachuk. They have a 24-year-old seventh-round pick on a minimum-salary contract with no years left, and they have not extended him. The deadline is 38 days away.

A club can trade him now and get something back. A club can re-sign him and pay what a leading scorer on a playoff team costs. Or a club can let him walk and hope the next kid from Kirkland with Speed 99 lands in their round seven.

The room says what rooms always say about a man this fast and this light: they ride him because they can, they put him out there because the other option is slower, and they do not talk about February when he's on the ice because February is a problem for July.

What the Index sees

+4 on the Development Index. His base is 77, his overall today is 79. He is 15 points from his ceiling and 4 points above his own base. He is not on a hot streak the Bureau flags, not a heater, not a snowflake. He is playing at a level four points better than the Book's resting number says he plays, which is what a player in a contract year does when he knows what July costs.

Toughness 50. Fighting 50. Checking 50. These are not the numbers of a man who will stay at 20.4 minutes a night for eight more seasons. His ceiling was set when he was 19, his current grade still sits 15 points below it, and a 165-pound body does not get more forgiving at 30.

The room in St. Louis has a 40-year-old in Mario Lemieux86 scoring 60 points and a 33-year-old in Pavol Demitra83 with an abdomen injury that keeps him out until February 6. They have minutes to fill. They put Reid on the ice because when he's on it, the other team has to cover a man who can outrun their defensemen to the far point, and nobody in that building has an answer for it.

Nobody has an answer for it in July either. $0.60M buys Speed 99 right now. It will not buy it next year from a man who knows what he is worth.
