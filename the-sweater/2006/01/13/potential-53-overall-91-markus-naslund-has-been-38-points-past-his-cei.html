Potential 53, Overall 91: Markus Naslund Has Been 38 Points Past His Ceiling Since He Was 17

The Hart Trophy winner leads the league in scoring at 32. The Bureau set his ceiling in his draft year. It has never moved, and neither has his game.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterFriday, 13 January 2006
ShareCopy link

Markus Naslund91 leads this league in points. 30 goals, 33 assists, 63 in 43 games, a pace of 120 over 82. He is 32 years old. Last season he won the Hart Trophy with 138 points. His ceiling grade on the Bureau's Book is 53.

The gap between his current overall of 91 and the grade the Bureau says he can never exceed is 38 points, the widest between ceiling and current of any player in this league. The number was set in his draft year, 1991, when he was 17 and still playing for Modo Hockey in Sweden. The Bureau has printed the same grade every edition since.

The outdoor rink in Örnsköldsvik

Naslund grew up on the Swedish coast, in a city that has produced Peter Forsberg95, Henrik Sedin72, and Daniel Sedin. He played most of his organized hockey on an outdoor rink in his neighbourhood. The player he watched growing up was Håkan Loob, the Swedish forward who became an Elitserien and NHL star.

He and Forsberg were born 10 days apart in the summer of 1973. They met on opposing youth teams in Ångermanland, joined the same regional all-star squad at 14, won a national under-16 championship at TV-pucken in 1988 (Naslund took the Sven Tumba Award as the tournament's best forward), attended the same high school, and took the same summer job at an electrical company that employed both their mothers and Forsberg's father. They skated on the same line for Modo's junior team, helping the club to back-to-back junior titles, and then in the Elitserien, where Naslund recorded 10 goals and 19 points at 17, a league record for his age that stood until Robert Nilsson scored 21 in 2002-03.

The Modo equipment manager gave him jersey number 19. He has worn it ever since, at Modo, with the Swedish national team, and now with the Vancouver Canucks Canucks.

Craig Patrick, Pittsburgh's general manager, drafted him 16th overall in 1991 and called him the best goal-scorer in the class behind Eric Lindros. Patrick compared him to Tomas Sandström but thought Naslund had a more natural shot than Jaromir Jagr84, the Penguins' Czech winger who had just finished his rookie year in Pittsburgh. Naslund stayed in Sweden two more seasons, posting 39 points both years, then let his Modo contract expire on April 30, 1993 without ever signing in Pittsburgh.

He has been in Vancouver for as long as this league's record can reach. He is the captain and wears 19.

What the Book prints

The ceiling grade of 53 sits beside an overall of 91 and a set of per-attribute grades that look nothing like a 53. His deke is 98. His puck control is 98. His passing is 96, his accuracy 96, his shooting percentage 94, his hero grade 96. His speed is 92, his agility 92, his endurance 86, his faceoff 76. His fighting grade is 50. His injury resistance is 51. Those two are the only numbers on his card that a ceiling of 53 could plausibly describe.

Everything the Book measures about what a forward does with the puck and around the net, he does at a level the Bureau considers close to the top of the scale. Everything it measures about toughness and durability, he does at the bottom. The ceiling grade treats those two weaknesses as if they cancel everything else out.

The Development Index has him at 0 form this winter. Neither rising, nor falling. He is having the season the Book says he is having, which is a Hart Trophy year, which is a 120-point pace, which is a ceiling of 53.

His career in this league reads: 133 points in 82 games in the season before last, 16 in 7 playoff games. 138 points in 82 last season, 13 in 6 playoff games, the Hart. 63 in 43 this winter. The ceiling is 53.

What the room says

Vancouver Canucks is 16-15-5, second in the Northwest Division, 6 points behind Colorado Avalanche. They have scored 110 goals and allowed 111. That is a hockey team that could win on any given night and lose on the next one, and Naslund is the reason they are competitive at all.

Todd Bertuzzi94 plays right wing on his line and has 34 goals, more than anyone else on the roster, and 58 points. Alexei Zhamnov84 centers at 85 overall. Dan Cloutier90 is the goaltender at 90. Ed Jovanovski88 and Mattias Ohlund83 anchor the blue line at 88 and 84. Trevor Linden84, the former captain, still dresses at 84.

Last season Vancouver finished 38-29 and won 1 playoff game in 6 before the run ended. They had 277 goals for and 256 against, and Naslund had 138 of the 277. This winter the numbers are tighter: 110 for, 111 against, and he has 30 of the 110. Bertuzzi has 34. The two of them are the engine of a team that has not moved much from the league's middle since the record opened.

Naslund earns $4.40M with 1 year left on his contract. He will be 33 when that deal expires. The Bureau's Book says he can never be better than 53. The last three seasons say he is one of the top three point producers in the league, alongside Jaromir Jagr last year and Ilya Kovalchuk89 this one, and has been for a decade. The number will still be 53. The points will still be coming.

In Örnsköldsvik they put a kid on an outdoor rink and the equipment manager gave him a number to wear. He has been past every grade they printed for him since before he was legal to drive. The Book keeps printing it anyway. He keeps scoring.
