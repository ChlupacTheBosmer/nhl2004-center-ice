Potential 57, Current 76: The Bureau Told Tuomo Pihlman Where to Stop Before He Started

The 2004 ninth pick won the Calder at 20 and is at 42 points this winter. The ceiling grade was set before his first shift, and he walked past it before his 22nd birthday.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterThursday, 5 January 2006
ShareCopy link

The Bureau set Tuomo Pihlman78's potential at 57. He is 21. His overall is 76. The gap is 19 grades, and the model has no way to close it.

The Development Index has him at +4. The form is climbing. The ceiling is not moving. He plays 23.4 minutes a night in Minnesota for a team in the middle of a season nobody is writing about, and the number that says where his road stops is 19 grades below where he already stands.

What the model read

The potential came from three numbers. Speed 52. Faceoff 53. Fighting 51. In the Bureau's model, a winger who cannot skate, cannot win a draw, and will not fight has a ceiling in the low 50s. You develop a shot. You teach a man to check. You cannot make him fast who is not fast.

The rest of the Book tells you what he built on the other side. Shot power 85. Toughness 85. Balance 84. Checking 83. Agility 81. He is 6 feet 1, 204 pounds, and he shoots the puck 85 from the circle while holding his position against a defenseman who is trying to push him off it. The Bureau looked at the speed grade, the faceoff grade, and the fighting grade, and it said 57. The combination is producing 42 points in 36 games.

The model was built on wingers who need speed to get open before the shot does any work. Pihlman gets open because 204 pounds of him is standing in the slot and the defenseman has to dislodge him first. Toughness 85 and balance 84 are not speed. They are a different way to get the puck to an 85 shot, and the model does not have a lane for that path.

From 12.7 to 23.4

He played 73 games as a 20-year-old last season on 12.7 minutes a night. 23 goals, 11 assists, 34 points, 34 penalty minutes. He won the Calder Memorial Trophy as the league's best rookie. The minutes told you his coach had not figured him out yet. The trophy told you the rest of the league had.

This season he plays 23.4 minutes. 15 goals, 27 assists, 42 points in 36 games, a pace of 96 over 82. He sits 27th on the league's scoring table, behind Markus Naslund91 and ahead of Jamie Langenbrunner86. His overall is 76, which is the lowest on the top-30 list. The next lowest is Eric Meloche78 at 75, and Meloche is 29 with a Speed grade nobody in the room would call a liability.

Pihlman is the youngest man in the top 30. He is also the one the Bureau says can never be better than he is right now.

Spacek, the first overall, has 4 points in 14 games and sits out nights. The class is wide at the bottom and narrow at the top, and the ninth man up the podium has the only line that looks like a professional season.

The room

Minnesota Wild is 10-15-7, 43 points, fourth in the Northwest, two points behind Calgary Flames with one game in hand. Saku Koivu86, 84 overall, 31 years old, centers the top line. Manny Fernandez92 guards the net at 90. Richard Park79, 29, has 47 points at a 96 pace, the same pace as Pihlman. Pierre-Marc Bouchard81, 21, is up 9 on the Index and out with an abdomen injury until January 24.

The line that works is Koivu between Pihlman and Park. Pihlman shoots from the circle and defenders step up, which opens the middle where Koivu collects the second puck. The 85 shot power does work that speed 52 cannot. Toughness 85 keeps him standing when a defenseman finishes a check that would separate most wingers from the puck. The line scores because two men can put it in the net and one man can find them, and nobody on that line needs to be the fastest player on the ice.

Minnesota scores 112 goals in 40 games and gives up 111. It is a club in the middle of a season that is working. Pihlman's 23.4 minutes are the most he has played in any season, and the number has gone up every year: 12.7 last winter, 23.4 this one. Nobody has suggested he should play fewer.

The contract

$1.05M. 2 years left. He turns 22 in April.

A winger at a 96 pace does not play for $1.05M for 2 more seasons. The $1.05M was set when he was 20 and 12.7 minutes a night and a 34-point rookie season were the entire record. When the deal runs out, whoever wants Pihlman writes a check for what the record says he is, and the record says 96.

Minnesota Wild can trade him. Minnesota Wild can sign him to a bridge at a number that acknowledges what he has become. Minnesota Wild can let him walk. The Bureau's potential of 57 will be in the file when the decision gets made. The pace of 96 will be in the record. Those two numbers sit in the same document and disagree, and the general manager has to decide which one the money follows.

What the number means

The Bureau does not raise a ceiling for a young player whose overall is already past it. The potential of 57 was written when he was 20, based on the speed grade and the faceoff grade and the fighting grade. Those numbers have not changed. They will not change. A man whose Speed reads 52 today is not going to read 70 at 24.

The overall is 76. The form is +4 and climbing. He is 19 grades past the ceiling, and the model has no mechanism to adjust the ceiling for a player who found a path the model did not chart. The potential of 57 was written before his first shift. He walked past it before his 22nd birthday, and the number stays where it is for as long as the file is open.

Pihlman is 21. He plays 23.4 minutes on the first line in Minnesota. He is 6 feet 1, 204 pounds, and he shoots the puck from places where a man with speed 52 should not be open, and 85 on the shot power says he is.
