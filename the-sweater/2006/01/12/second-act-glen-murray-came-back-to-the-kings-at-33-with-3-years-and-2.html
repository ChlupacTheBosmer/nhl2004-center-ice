Second Act: Glen Murray came back to the Kings at 33 with 3 years and $21.46M nobody can spend

He won the Art Ross. Now he's the best skater on a 9-19-8 team, and the trade that sent him to Los Angeles reunited him with the man who went to Boston in his place five years ago.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterThursday, 12 January 2006
ShareCopy link

On January 5, Glen Murray86 was traded from Boston Bruins to Los Angeles Kings. Five years earlier, almost to the month, he had been traded the other way. In October 2001, the Kings sent him to Boston with Jozef Stümpel for Jason Allison84 and Mikko Eloranta. Now he is in Los Angeles and Allison is his teammate.

Murray is 33. He is on $7.15M with 3 years left, which means Los Angeles Kings owes him $21.46M over a window in which the club has 9 wins, 19 losses, 8 overtime losses, and 38 points. He came from Boston, a team with 52 points and a real season. He arrived in a building that is 4 games behind the Pacific's third place and 24 behind Dallas.

What the Book says

The Bureau's Development Index has him at 0. His base grade is 88, his current grade is 82, and his potential is 61. That 61 was set when he was a prospect. He has scored 168 points in a single season, won the Art Ross Trophy and the Lady Byng. The Book has not caught up.

The attributes that carry him are Glen Murray's body and his shot: ENDU 94, SHPW 91, HERO 91. Speed 86, acceleration 83, aggression 83. The attributes that hold him down are the ones a man cannot train: FIGHTING 50, INJURY RESISTANCE 50, SPEED UNDER PRESSURE (breakaway) 51. He will not drop the gloves and he will not stay healthy on a schedule. The Bureau saw both of those before his first shift in this league.

What the numbers say now

This season: 28 goals, 29 assists, 57 points in 42 games at 22.1 minutes a night. That is a 111-point pace, which ties him with Todd Bertuzzi94 and Mario Lemieux86 for fourth in the league. He is 33 years old and scoring at a rate that would make a 24-year-old's contract look like a bargain. The cost per point, on his salary against his current production, is $125,503. That number will get worse if his production drops and cannot get better without a cap reduction that does not exist.

Last season he played 48 games for Boston and put up 64 points. The season before that, his peak: 82 games, 68 goals, 100 assists, 168 points, the Art Ross, the Lady Byng. He was 30. The Bureau's ceiling had him at 61. He was 168.

His background sheet from before the summer of 2004 tells the rest. Born in Halifax, Nova Scotia, 1972. Drafted 18th overall by Boston in 1991. He played for the Kings for five years and had "moderate success" in his own words from the public record. He went back to Boston in 2001. In 2002-03 he scored 92 points and made the All-Star Game.

The room

His morale is 100. A man traded from a 52-point team to a 38-point team, three years into $7.15M, at 33, with a fight grade of 50 and a breakaway speed grade of 51, is reportedly content. The sweater in Los Angeles is not new; he wore it for five years already. He knows the rink, the city, the locker room. The trade was a return, not a demotion, whatever the standings say.

His best teammate on paper is Roman Cechmanek92, the goaltender, at 88. The best skater beside him is Adam Deadmarsh87 at 83. Mike Cammalleri87 is also 83, though Cammalleri is out with a foot injury until 2006-03-15. Zigmund Palffy87 rounds out the top group at 83, and Jason Allison, the man Murray was traded for in 2001, is at 82.

That is a roster built around one 33-year-old wing who will not fight anybody and a 34-year-old Slovak who is himself an aging asset. Los Angeles Kings has 9 wins. The Pacific is 43 points deep to third place. Murray has 3 years of this ahead of him unless something moves, and the something that moves is him, which costs cap space the Kings do not have.

The problem for July

The Kings' revenue will not support $7.15M for a man scoring 57 points on a 9-19-8 team, because the revenue is a function of the wins. LA drew fewer fans than any team in its division except San Jose. A 9-win club cannot fill a building that pays for a player at $7.15M, which means the money has to come from somewhere else: a ticket hike, a trade that dumps salary, or the three years simply run their course while the club finishes last.

Murray's morale is 100. He has scored 168 points in this league. He is playing 22.1 minutes a night in Los Angeles in January 2006 and the Bureau's Development Index has him going nowhere. The 61 is still there, printed in the Book. He blew past it a long time ago.
