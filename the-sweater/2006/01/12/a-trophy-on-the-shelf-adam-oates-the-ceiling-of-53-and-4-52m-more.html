A Trophy on the Shelf: Adam Oates, the Ceiling of 53, and $4.52M More

He went undrafted at 19, worked a gas station, played box lacrosse, and is now the oldest man in this league on three years of a contract nobody should have given him.

Tomáš VránaProfile writer · The SweaterThursday, 12 January 2006
ShareCopy link

The Bureau's Book gives Adam Oates77 a potential of 53. He is 43 years old, plays centre for Colorado Avalanche, and has a base grade of 80 that the Development Index has shaved two points off for the current winter. The number 53 was set a long time ago, by men who looked at a 19-year-old from Weston, Ontario, working the pump at a gas station, and decided he would never be good enough for this league. He has been ignoring that number for twenty years.

Oates plays 19.9 minutes a night. He has 4 goals and 22 points in 42 games. His faceoff grade is 89, his passing grade is 89, his endurance is 90. His checking, fighting and toughness are all 50, the floor of the scale, the marks of a man who never hit anyone and never will. His HERO grade is 95. That is the grade for a man who keeps showing up.

He is on $4.52M with three years left. He will be 44 in August. He will be 45 when this contract ends.

The road before the record opened

Oates favoured box lacrosse over hockey as a boy. He scored 181 points in 19 games for the Etobicoke Eclipse of the OLA Junior A Lacrosse League in 1981, won the league's scoring title, and once put up 19 assists and 29 points in a single game. He left lacrosse in 1984 to chase hockey. He had dropped out of high school. At 19 he was pumping gas.

He went undrafted. Scouts said he was too slow. He returned to high school to finish his diploma, then walked onto the team at Rensselaer Polytechnic Institute. An assistant coach named Paul Allen found him during a junior A game while scouting a different player. Oates spent three years at RPI, set school records in points and assists, won the 1985 NCAA championship, and signed a four-year, $1.1 million deal with Detroit that made him the highest-paid rookie in the league. He scored on his first shift in his first NHL game, October 10, 1985, against the Minnesota North Stars.

None of that is in the Bureau's Book. The Book says his ceiling is 53.

His playoff line in that first recorded season: 5 goals and 21 points in 24 games. He won the Bill Masterton last spring, the trophy for dedication and perseverance, the one they give to men who should not still be here. Morale 100. No injuries on record. Not one trip to the injured list in the three seasons this desk has tracked.

The 71-point season at age 41 produced the Masterton. The 22-point season at 43 produced nothing. His points-per-game this year is 0.52. Last year it was 0.87. The decline is visible in every column, and it does not matter, because the grade he holds today is 77 and the ceiling they set for him was 53.

The room

Colorado has Peter Forsberg95 at 95, Paul Kariya89 at 89, Milan Hejduk88 at 88. Oates skews younger on the sheet than every one of them. He is the oldest man in the building by a decade. The team is 19-12-7, first in the Northwest, and he is their centre in a role that does not show up in the goal column. His 89 faceoff grade means he wins draws. The puck moves when it gets to him, passing grade 89. His 90 endurance means he can do it in the third period at 43.

A man with checking of 50 and toughness of 50 does not play by putting his body on anyone. He plays by not being where the hit is. Twenty years of not being there. No injuries. Four penalty minutes in 42 games this season.

The $4.52M buys a centre who wins faceoffs, moves the puck, never takes a penalty, never misses a game, and scores 22 points in a season. Colorado is first in its division with that contract on its books. The question is what happens when a 44-year-old centre with a potential of 53 is still on this salary and the points keep falling.

He will be 44 in August. He will be 45 before this deal is done. The Bureau has him at 77 today, below his base of 80, and the Index says he is still sliding. Two points below base at 43 is a slow drift, not a cliff. He has never been on a cliff. He has never been on the injured list. He has been here the whole time, on a number that was supposed to be his ceiling, and the number is wrong, and it has been wrong for twenty years.

Oates has said he was a punk as a kid. He has said he liked lacrosse more than hockey. He was a gas station attendant at 19 and the highest-paid rookie in the league at 23 and a Masterton winner at 41 and a 43-year-old centre at 19.9 minutes a night in Denver right now. The Bureau gave him 53. He is at 77. The gap between those two numbers is twenty years of a man doing something everyone said he could not do, for money he should not have been given and minutes he does not need to justify.
