Nine Trades in One Day: The 18 Clubs That Repositioned 40 Days Before the Deadline

Detroit holds 2 picks total. Vancouver pays $0.97M a point and bought anyway. New Jersey sits 2nd in the Atlantic and added depth.

E.J. "Ears" CallahanInsider · The Hot StoveFriday, 13 January 2006
ShareCopy link

Nine confirmed trades between January 9 and January 10 pulled 18 of 30 clubs into the market, the busiest single window this season, 40 days before what the calendar has as the trade deadline.

Identity drove this. Cheap, profitable clubs bought. Expensive, low-standing clubs sold futures. Others grabbed injury insurance nobody asked for.

The buyers at the cheapest rates

Vancouver Canucks sent Yuri Butsayev to Chicago Blackhawks for Alexei Zhamnov and a pick. Vancouver holds $49.50M in payroll, sits at $0.97M per standings point (second cheapest in the league), and posted a 98.7 club morale average, the highest of any room in this league. They added a veteran to a 51-point team with 4 picks and no first-rounder. This is a team that believes its window is now, not next June.

New Jersey Devils, 2nd in the Atlantic at 61 points, acquired Wayne Primeau from Philadelphia Flyers in what the transaction log shows as a same-day sign-and-trade. Primeau signed a $401,120 deal in Philadelphia on January 9 and was in New Jersey by the afternoon. NJ pays $0.95M per point, holds 5 picks, and won 3 straight. They added a bottom-six forward. Small move, clear signal: they are not satisfied.

Pittsburgh Penguins, 3rd in the Atlantic at 54 points and $0.92M per point, took Ross Lupaschuk72 from Mighty Ducks of Anaheim in exchange for a player whose name the log obscures. Lupaschuk carries a hip designation with a January 31 return date. Pittsburgh bought a defenseman who cannot play for another 18 days. Deadline insurance on the blue line, not a rental gamble.

The all-in club

Detroit Red Wings sent Niclas Havelid and a pick to Washington Capitals for Peter Bondra. Detroit now holds 2 total picks across 5 draft rounds, the fewest of any club. Their payroll sits at $66.91M on 47 standings points ($1.42M per point). They are spending like a contender while carrying the asset base of a team that has already emptied the vault. The 43-16-3 record says it is working. The pick count says there is no second move if it stops.

The sellers

Chicago Blackhawks now holds 6 picks, 2 of them first-rounders, and sits last in the Central at 35 points on a 3-game slide. They traded their veteran center and their pick. Washington Capitals parted with Bondra and a pick, keeps 6 picks, and holds 37 points. Both clubs are building. Neither is pretending otherwise.

Florida Panthers sent Sami Ronnqvist to Montreal Canadiens. Florida is 11-24-4, last in the Southeast at 34 points, $1.59M per point, and on a 6-game losing streak. Every month they keep a veteran is a month they pay $1.59M a point for the privilege.

The rest

The remaining four trades moved a goaltender, a depth defender, and two players whose names the transaction log filed under a placeholder. TAC sent a player to San Jose Sharks and received Boris Mironov back. Colorado Avalanche acquired Philippe Sauve from Nashville Predators. Edmonton Oilers got Pasi Mantyla from Dallas Stars. Boston Bruins sent a player and a pick to Carolina Hurricanes for Ben Clymer.

On the same day, St. Louis Blues signed Keith Tkachuk88 to a $315,430 deal. He appears on the injured list with a hip designation, onset between January 12 and January 13, return February 12. Three days from signing to IR. The sign-and-injure pattern has not stopped; it just lost its novelty.

The deadline is 40 days out. Nine clubs moved this week. The market has not thinned. It is early.
