4 of the 15 Unhappiest Men in the League Wear One Sweater. The Most Expensive Makes $4.40M.

Calgary's room is the coldest in the league at 85.8. Roman Turek is at the bottom, three years from free agency.

E.J. "Ears" CallahanInsider · The Hot StoveMonday, 20 December 2004
ShareCopy link

The Bureau's Morale Scale has 15 players at 76 or below. Four of them play for Calgary Flames. No other club has more than two.

At the bottom sits Roman Turek84, morale 70, the single lowest reading in the league. He earns $4.40M and has 3 years left on his deal. His club is 9-20-2, 22 points, on a 7-game losing streak. He's played 26 games at 52.7 minutes a night, which means the Flames are starting him every other game and asking him to win them on a roster that won't keep him off the ice.

Behind Turek in the bottom 15: Travis Green77 at 74 morale, $2.97M, 3 years left, 17 games played at 11.3 minutes a night. Mika Sulku69 at 75, $0.94M, expiring. Stephane Yelle70 at 75, $1.10M, expiring. Two men on long deals they can't trade themselves out of, two more whose contracts run out in June.

The club average is 85.8, dead last in the league. Chicago Blackhawks is next at 85.9. Chicago's unhappiest men are a depth center earning $0.76M and a 67-overall winger on a 3-year deal. Calgary's is a $4.40M starting goaltender who is, by the Bureau's own measure, the most dissatisfied player in professional hockey.

Turek's $4.40M is the second-largest single salary on a $43.33M payroll. The Flames' cost per point sits at $1.97M, fifth-worst in the league. They generated $13.15M in profit, which means ownership can absorb a dump if the hockey side wants one. The question a man who would know at the Saddledome won't answer: does a club with 22 points and the coldest room in the sport shop its most expensive asset, or does it wait until June and watch him walk on his own terms?

Three years left means the next two Decembers will look like this one, unless something moves before February.

The two expiring names, Sulku and Yelle, are the soft spots. Neither is worth a first-round pick, neither costs more than $1.10M, and both would give a playoff club a middle-six body for the final 40 games. If the Flames want to start dismantling, they start there. If they want to start bigger, they start with the man at 70.
