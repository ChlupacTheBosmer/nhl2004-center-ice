RUMOR METER: Fire Sale Brewing in Atlanta? 🔥🔥

The league's worst team has two 20-something superstars on expiring pennies. The phone is about to ring off the hook.

E.J. "Ears" CallahanInsider · The Hot StoveSaturday, 4 December 2004
ShareCopy link

Here's the arithmetic nobody in the Atlanta Thrashers front office wants to do in public: you are 5-16-1, last in the league by a full division, your club-average morale is the lowest of all thirty buildings at 84.6, and your two best players are both on one-year deals at $1.10 million. Every contender in this league is salivating. Every contender should be on the phone.

Ilya Kovalchuk86, 21 years old, 39 points in 23 games. Dany Heatley85, 23, 34 points in 20 games before going quiet. They make the combined salary of a fourth-liner on most clubs. The Atlanta Thrashers record says this team is not going anywhere this season. The contract sheet says these two are not coming back at these numbers. That is the definition of a sell window, and it opened the moment Atlanta Thrashers lost its sixteenth game.

A man who would know says the phone has rung. He won't say who called. The Meadowlands are watching. The same sources note that Byron Dafoe79 at 70 morale and Magnus Arvedson75 at 70 morale — the two lowest figures on the entire roster outside of Minnesota's goalie situation — are being shopped as depth pieces to sweeten a package. Nobody's moving Dafoe for a first-rounder, but he and his $2.40 million in remaining cap space are a throw-in that helps a contender's math.

The question is not whether Atlanta Thrashers moves one of them. The question is whether they move both, or whether they try to keep the Kovalchuk-Heatley window alive for one more season of misery. Given that the Thrashers hold five picks and one first in the draft capital ledger, and that their payroll is the second-lowest in the league at $36.79 million, there's no payroll pressure forcing their hand. They could tank, rebuild, and re-sign nobody. Or they could flip two 20-something goal-scorers for a fortune in prospects and picks before they leave for nothing.

The buyers are staring. Colorado Avalanche is missing Joe Sakic until December 17 and Paul Kariya83 until January 23, and at $71.56 million in payroll they have the money to absorb either salary without blinking. New Jersey Devils just made a deal with Calgary Flames — a four-player swap that was filed as news — and with Patrik Elias88 out until December 12, their top-six is thinner than their 13-2-3 record suggests. Vancouver Canucks has Todd Bertuzzi93 putting up 43 points and the lowest payroll in the league at $34.94 million, which means the money is there if Brian Burke wants to swing big.

One fire on this because nothing has been filed. Two fires because the pieces are on the board, the timeline is short, and the Atlanta Thrashers record says patience is over. Three fires when the first call gets logged.
