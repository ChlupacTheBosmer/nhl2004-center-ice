Contract-Year Watch: The $1.00M-per-Point Team Has Three Expiring Deals and No Margin for Error

New Jersey carries the league's best cost per point. The three players driving it are all unsigned past June.

E.J. "Ears" CallahanInsider · The Hot StoveWednesday, 22 December 2004
ShareCopy link

New Jersey Devils pays $1.00M for every point it earns this season, the lowest figure in the league. The club sits 4th in the Atlantic at 45 points, third in revenue at $55.30M, and second in profit at $31.09M. Attendance is 17,762 a night, the best gate in hockey.

All three names driving that per-point figure are at or near the end of their deals.

Jamie Langenbrunner87 has 45 points in 23 games on $2.10M. His morale is 97. His Book grade is 86. He is out with an ankle, back December 30, and he turns 30 in July. The Devils will need a new contract to keep him, and the market for a 29-year-old right wing scoring two points a night does not exist at $2.10M.

Patrik Elias85 has 6 games played, 9 points, and $4.90M on a deal that expires in six months. His overall is 84, his morale 89. The contract-year table lists him at 6 GP. That is the lowest games-played figure of any player with a contract year remaining in the league's top 30 by overall. Either the ankle is not the only thing sitting him, or the Devils are managing a 28-year-old forward who scored at a near-90-point pace last season and cannot stay in the lineup.

Ari Ahonen85 rounds it out: 84 overall, $0.80M, 6 starts, morale 94. A backup on an expiring rookie deal is the least urgent of the three, but the depth behind Martin Brodeur92 disappears if New Jersey does not re-sign him.

Langenbrunner told The Tunnel's Marcus Bell this week what the lineup looks like from his side: "When your best player puts three up, that's, that's the cushion you need on a night like mine. I'm happy for him. You have to be." That was about Fedorov in Anaheim, not about himself, but it is the same sentence a Devils fan reads when they see Elias at 6 games.

NJ's payroll is $44.98M, 21st in the league. New York Rangers pays $77.49M for 48 points at $1.61M each. The Devils do not have the depth to absorb a Langenbrunner raise and an Elias raise in the same summer, and the per-point figure reflects cheap production from expensive-to-replace players.
