RUMOR METER: We Called the Streak Dead at 57. It's at 65. Every Man Signed This Week Is Hurt. 🔥🔥

Shawn Thornton's hand shows up on the IR. The desk was wrong. Then seven more clubs signed seven more men, and every one of them landed too.

E.J. "Ears" CallahanInsider · The Hot StoveSaturday, 12 February 2005
ShareCopy link

On Saturday, this desk reported the signing-injury streak over at 57. Shawn Thornton68 signed with Chicago Blackhawks on February 4 or 5, and when five of his six window-mates went to IR and he did not, the desk scored it as a survivor.

The record says otherwise. Thornton's hand now sits on the injured list with an onset between February 7 and February 9. He missed the previous edition by hours. The streak didn't end at 57. It ran to 58.

Between February 6 and February 8, Columbus Blue Jackets, Edmonton Oilers, Phoenix Coyotes, Detroit Red Wings (twice), Atlanta Thrashers, and Carolina Hurricanes signed seven players. Every one of them is on the injured list with an onset between February 9 and February 12. The run stands at 65 consecutive signings in this pattern that have landed on IR.

The most expensive contract on that list is Smehlik at $505,830. These are depth guys, fourth-liners, bottom-pairing defensemen. The kind of move a GM makes on a Tuesday because a scout called. Every club that made one this week now has an empty roster spot and a player in a hospital gown.

Carolina's problem

Carolina Hurricanes signed Glen Wesley on February 7 at $417,460. The 39-year-old defenseman had 70 overall. His neck injury keeps him out until February 14, so at best he contributes one week before what the calendar has as the trade deadline, 35 days from today. Carolina Hurricanes sits at 72 points, second in the Southeast, and needs bodies that hold up. Wesley will not be one for most of the window that matters.

The desk owns the miss

The February 9 piece read "Shawn Thornton Survives." He didn't survive. He showed up two days late. The injury window is broad enough that a signing can look clean on publication day and land on the list before the next edition. The desk will stop calling the streak dead until it actually stops.

The next signing window will decide whether 65 is the ceiling or just another number on the way up. If any club signs a player between now and the trade deadline and he plays a game before March 19 without an IR designation, the run breaks. The desk's money is on it not breaking.
