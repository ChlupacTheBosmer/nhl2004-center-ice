CONFIRMED: The Most Profitable Club in Hockey Takes a Multi-Year Bet on Saku Koivu

A 6-player trade sends Koivu to Minnesota. Minnesota holds 52 points. This is not a February move.

E.J. "Ears" CallahanInsider · The Hot StoveSaturday, 19 February 2005
ShareCopy link

Minnesota Wild and Montreal Canadiens filed a trade on February 14. The league record carries it as news. Minnesota receives Saku Koivu, Donald Audette and Patrick Traverse. Montreal gets Michael Nylander and Kyle Wanvig. The league's trade detection reads 6 players, zero picks.

Koivu is 51 games in with 25 goals and 45 assists for 70 points. His contract carries 3 more seasons. Minnesota's payroll, per the business table, sits at $37.91M. That number is 4th-lowest in the league. Minnesota's profit sits at $37.47M. That number is the highest in the league.

Minnesota has 52 points and trails 3rd-place Colorado Avalanche in the Northwest. No playoff run this season. This is no rental, no salary dump. It's a club with more money than it knows what to do with, planting a flag on a 3-year contract and calling center settled.

Audette came in with 2 years left on his deal. He has 36 points in 54 games. Traverse is the odd man in: 1 year left, morale 100. He is one of the happiest men in hockey and he is now in a room that averages 88.7 on the Morale Scale, the 6th-coldest in the league.

On the Montreal side, this is arithmetic. Koivu's 3-year deal walked out the door. Audette's walked out. What came back is Michael Nylander on an expiring deal with 60 points in 59 games, and Kyle Wanvig, also expiring. Montreal Canadiens runs a $45.26M payroll with 48 points and the Northeast's last place. They cleared money, picked up a rental center, and can pretend next season's cap hit won't exist.

Why Minnesota does this now

The trade deadline, what the calendar has as March 19, is 28 days out. Minnesota will not be a buyer then. They will not be a seller. The front office looked at a 70-point center with 3 years of control and decided the window to build around him opens now, not when his deal runs out.

The business table shows Minnesota earning $66.55M in revenue against a $37.91M payroll, the widest margin in hockey. They generated $37.47M in profit last fiscal cycle. A long-term commitment to a single center does not crack this. It bends it. Minnesota has run the cheapest roster among the league's profitable clubs all season. This move says they intend to spend now, not later.

Minnesota's goaltending shelf is still burning. Manny Fernandez88 sits at 72 morale on an expiring deal. The center position now has a name. The crease does not.
