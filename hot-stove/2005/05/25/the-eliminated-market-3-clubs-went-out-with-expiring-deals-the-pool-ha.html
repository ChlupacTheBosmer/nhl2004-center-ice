The Eliminated Market: 3 Clubs Went Out With Expiring Deals. The Pool Has 0 Names.

The draft is 14 days away, no player is unsigned, and 6 names at 86 or better are about to become somebody else's problem.

E.J. "Ears" CallahanInsider · The Hot StoveWednesday, 25 May 2005
ShareCopy link

The free-agent pool is empty. The league carries zero unsigned players. The draft is 14 days out. And three clubs that finished their seasons this spring are staring at six top-30 contract-year players they either pay or trade.

Phoenix Coyotes pulled the biggest result of the spring, taking the top-seeded Colorado Avalanche to seven games in Round 1, then bowing to Dallas Stars in Round 2. Three players on their expiring list sit in the Bureau's top 30: Sean Burke82 at $4.20M, Mike Sillinger81 at $1.80M, and Shane Doan79 at $2.40M. Burke is 38 and played 63 games. Doan is 28 and logged all 82 with 62 points. Sillinger sits at 97 morale, the highest of the three, and carried 68 points in 62 games.

Phoenix has no pool to raid, so a replacement for any of those three costs either a re-sign or a trade partner. The draft is two weeks away. If the club does not move a piece before July, it gets a rookie lottery ticket where a 94-point-per-game winger used to sit.

New Jersey Devils is the other one worth watching. They carried $0.40M per point this season, the best value in hockey on a $45.15M payroll that returned $45.96M in profit. They made the second seed and got bounced in Round 1 by Pittsburgh Penguins. Two players in the top 30 expiring class wear their colors: Patrik Elias88 ($4.90M, 86 overall, 36 points in 37 games because injuries ate his schedule) and Jamie Langenbrunner88 ($2.10M, 86 overall, 88 points in 69). Both sit at 94 morale. The Devils' front office built the league's most efficient roster. Now it faces the choice every efficient roster has to make at some point: do we pay up for a 29-year-old who played 37 games, or do we shop him for picks while he still has a year of trade value?

Vancouver Canucks is the wildcard. Todd Bertuzzi put up 128 points at $2.60M. The Canucks carry the lowest payroll in hockey at $35.16M and turned $40.84M in profit. They went out in Round 1 to Edmonton Oilers in six. Bertuzzi's morale sits at 93. If Vancouver wants to build around him, the money is there. If not, he's the best player on the market for a club that can absorb $2.60M for a 93-overall winger. There is no depth chart of suitors because there is no pool.

Clubs that cannot re-sign will lean on the draft board harder than they planned. A team losing a 94-morale center or a 93-overall RW without a replacement signing walks into June with a hole no pick fixes in one round. The league sources I talk to around the Meadowlands and the other back rooms keep saying the same thing: zero unsigned names turns every expiring deal into a decision with a deadline. You pay at your number, you trade for picks, or you watch the player walk and get nothing back.
