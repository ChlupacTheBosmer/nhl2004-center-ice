RUMOR METER: Atlanta's 2 Stars Hit the Market Next July. Neither Is Happy. 🔥🔥

Kovalchuk and Heatley are the top 2 scorers among expiring players. The room sits at 86.6. The draft is 28 days out.

E.J. "Ears" CallahanInsider · The Hot StoveWednesday, 11 May 2005
ShareCopy link

Ilya Kovalchuk90 put up 138 points on $1.10M. Dany Heatley88 put up 131 on the same money. They are the top 2 scorers in the entire contract-year class. Their club sits 2nd from the bottom on the Morale Scale at 86.6. Both deals expire after the season.

That is the Atlanta problem, and nobody from this desk has written about the room since mid-April. The draft is 28 days away. The free-agent window closes July 28. In between, somebody in the front office has to decide whether to build around these two or sell them while they are still worth something.

What the morale numbers say that the points don't

Kovalchuk and Heatley both sit at 87 morale. For a 22-year-old with a 90 overall rating and a $1.10M salary, that is a man looking for an exit. For a 24-year-old with 131 points and a 88 overall, the same.

Below them, the floor drops out. Byron Dafoe82 is at 72 morale, tied for the lowest in the league. His $2.40M deal expires this summer. Magnus Arvedson77 sits at 74 on $2.49M, also expiring. Two veterans in the league's bottom 15, two young superstars slightly above them, and the whole locker room averaging 86.6. Only Edmonton is lower, and Edmonton at least had a Cup run to talk about.

Atlanta finished 31-37-6, 84 points, 5th in the Southeast. They missed the playoffs and hold 5 picks and 1 first-round selection in the June 8 draft.

What the payroll says

The Thrashers carry $36.79M in player salaries, the lowest in the league. They turned $25.99M in profit on a $70.29M revenue line. Their 14,430 attendance figure is the 3rd lowest. The $0.44M per point is among the cheapest in the league, which sounds like efficiency until you realize the points are only 84.

A league source who would know says the front office has looked at those numbers and done the math: two players worth 2 first-round picks and 2 top prospects each, sitting on expiring deals in a locker room nobody likes. The question is timing. Do you call around before June 8, when the draft is the only thing on the calendar and every general manager is on the phone? Or do you wait for training camp, when desperation sets in on a different side of the trade?

A man who would know puts it simpler: you do not start a rebuild with 2 superstars on one-year deals. You start it by trading them.

The Atlanta Thrashers brass has not returned calls this week.
