No Expirings, No Free Agents, No Way Out: The 2 Clubs Trapped by Their Own Payrolls

New York and Detroit carry the league's biggest numbers for the worst cost-per-point. Twenty days to the draft, and nothing about the math moves without a trade.

E.J. "Ears" CallahanInsider · The Hot StoveThursday, 19 May 2005
ShareCopy link

The league's two largest payrolls belong to clubs that finished with the two worst cost-per-point figures in the 30-team field. New York Rangers carries $78.14M for 84 points, $0.93M a point. Detroit Red Wings carries $80.89M for 96 points, $0.84M a point. Neither club has a player among the Bureau's top 30 expiring contracts.

That's the trap. You spend to the top of the league, you get the worst return per point, and nobody walks out the door in July. The usual summer fix, a cap dump landing on a contender, does not exist when all the big money is locked in for two or three more years.

The free-agent pool is empty. The Bureau's contract ledger lists zero unsigned players. Washington GM Shawn Holliday said it plainly on The Tunnel after his own club's elimination on 2005-05-18: "if I want a difference-maker, I'm buying him off another club's roster." Any club that wants to get better is trading, because the market outside the league has nothing to sell.

The Rangers missed the postseason on a tiebreaker. Three clubs finished at 84 points in the East: New York Islanders, Atlanta Thrashers and New York. The Rangers' 28 wins trailed both. Their room averages 88.2 morale, 5th lowest in the league. Attendance sat at 14,713 a night against ticket prices of $80. Profit is not recorded for the club.

Detroit's case is sharper. $80.89M, 96 points, a 4-0 sweep by Dallas Stars in the second round. The club holds 6 picks across 5 rounds, 2 of them firsts, including Toronto Maple Leafs's top selection from a franchise that just won Game 7 of the conference final. That pick belongs to the team with the league's best cost-per-point at $0.45M.

Detroit could package picks for a player whose contract is expiring elsewhere, which helps the roster and makes the payroll worse. Or they could trade the picks for future assets and ride out contracts that don't expire for two years, getting thinner and leaving the $0.84M a point to stand.

New York holds 6 picks, 1 first, and 2 selections originally belonging to Phoenix Coyotes in rounds 2 and 3. Phoenix, with three top-30 expirings walking after knocking off the Presidents' Trophy winners, might want those picks back. A trade there gives Phoenix draft capital and gives New York a player they still have to pay, and the denominator doesn't move until a September puck drops.

Twenty days to the draft. Neither club appears on the transaction log this week. The league's two biggest spenders, and the only path is through another club's roster.
