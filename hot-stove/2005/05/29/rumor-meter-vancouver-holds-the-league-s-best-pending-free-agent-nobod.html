RUMOR METER: Vancouver Holds the League's Best Pending Free Agent. Nobody in the Room Wants to Pay His Price. 🔥🔥

Todd Bertuzzi scored 128 points at $2.60M. He has one year left, the club owns 4 picks, and the draft is 10 days away.

E.J. "Ears" CallahanInsider · The Hot StoveSunday, 29 May 2005
ShareCopy link

The piece

Todd Bertuzzi89 put up 128 points in 82 games at age 30 for a club that carries the league's lowest payroll. His salary is $2.60M. His contract has one year left. A man who would know says the phone has been ringing since Vancouver went out in Round 1.

Vancouver Canucks lost 4-2 to Edmonton Oilers in the first round and are now in a position almost no other club faces: their best player is about to walk, they own four draft picks total, and their payroll is $35.16M. That is the cheapest roster in hockey. It is also, at $0.36M per point, the best value in hockey.

Vancouver's $0.36M per-point mark is built on a small payroll that stretches far because the contracts are young and cheap. Bertuzzi at $2.60M is the engine underneath it. If he signs elsewhere in July for what a 93-rated 128-point winger fetches, that payroll climbs and the value drops. League sources say the front office knows this and is listening.

The draft is 10 days away. The free-agent pool is empty: zero unsigned players, nothing to sign, no lever outside of a trade. WSH GM Shawn Holliday said it plainly to The Tunnel on May 17: "There's nobody unsigned. So if I want a difference-maker, I'm buying him off another club's roster." That's everyone's problem now, and it's Vancouver's opportunity.

Who calls? Every club that lost a first round or earlier and needs top-six scoring is a candidate. Colorado Avalanche went 43 games and still fell to a 93-seed in Round 1. New Jersey Devils carried 114 points and lasted six games against Pittsburgh. Both are out with picks and room to spend.

What the trade costs is where the flame sits at two. Vancouver owns 4 picks and 1 first-rounder, so a club wanting Bertuzzi needs to clear a top-four piece and a package that lets Vancouver restock the cupboard for a second straight rebuild year. No club in the league is short on needs after watching 14 others get eliminated.

The counter-argument: Bertuzzi turns 31 next season. His morale is 93, high enough that he's not agitating. The club made the playoffs as the 4 seed. They could re-sign him, push payroll to $40M, and try to be the best $40M team in the league again. League sources say that's the plan the coach prefers. The GM's office has other files open.

What happens before June 8 decides whether this is a playoff team that got outgunned or a fire sale that starts at the draft table. I have Vancouver moving Bertuzzi before the first round of the draft. Two flames because I have sources telling me he's available, not because the deal is filed.

Bertuzzi sits at 93 in the Book. With 0 names in the pool, the market has 1 name that matters.
