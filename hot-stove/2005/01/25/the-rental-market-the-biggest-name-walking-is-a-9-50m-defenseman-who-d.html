The Rental Market: The Biggest Name Walking Is a $9.50M Defenseman Who Doesn't Seem to Want to Walk

St. Louis spends $1.45M per point and profits $13.87M. Their biggest rentable contract belongs to a defenseman at 98 morale.

E.J. "Ears" CallahanInsider · The Hot StoveTuesday, 25 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Chris Pronger86's $9.50M contract expires in July. He's 30, graded 86 by the Bureau, and plays for a team with 39 points through 47 games. That's the single biggest rentable salary on the board, and the St. Louis Blues have shown no signal they intend to move him.

The Blues spend $1.45M per point, second-worst in the league behind only Philadelphia at $1.48M. They committed $56.63M in salary and produced 39 points. Their profit is $13.87M on $53.83M in revenue, so nobody in the front office is sweating the ledger. The club's average morale, 94.4, ranks 4th among all 30 clubs.

Pronger sits at 98 morale, played 47 of 47 games, and has 29 points from the blue line. A defenseman at that morale who doesn't miss time isn't asking to leave, and his room isn't going to push him out.

Three Blues with 85 or higher overall grades are on expiring deals: Pronger at $9.50M, Brent Johnson85 at $0.60M, and Chris Osgood85 at $3.80M. Johnson started 37 games at 98 morale. Osgood started 14 at 83. The goaltending situation is a decision waiting to happen regardless of Pronger.

Jocelyn Thibault86 at $2.80M and 94 morale is the fourth. Four 85-overall men walking from a 39-point club makes Chicago the deepest single team on the rental board.

If a buyer moves, the Detroit Red Wings hold 6 picks, 2 firsts, and the Toronto first-round selection. They sit at 58 points on a 4-game winning streak with $80.89M committed, the highest payroll in the league. They hold the assets to move for any name on that table.

The trade deadline, what the calendar has as the trade deadline, falls on March 19. Fifty-three days.
