Pick Stockpilers: Detroit Holds 6 Picks Including a Toronto First, and Nobody Is Explaining Why

The [[team:DET]] own the most draft capital in the league. The [[team:NYR]] aren't far behind. Both are in playoff races with 59 days to the deadline.

E.J. "Ears" CallahanInsider · The Hot StoveWednesday, 19 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Detroit Red Wings hold 6 draft picks in 2005, including 2 first-rounders. One of those firsts belongs to the Toronto Maple Leafs Maple Leafs by original ownership, a fact that stops a reader cold on a Tuesday afternoon.

Detroit sits 20-17-4, 54 points, second in the Central Division, 7 points back of Nashville Predators. Toronto sits 34-8-2, 76 points, 27 clear of second-place Buffalo Sabres. The league's best team gave a first-rounder to the second-place team in a different division, and what it got back is not in the transactions filed this week.

The New York Rangers hold 6 picks as well, including Phoenix Coyotes's original second and third. Phoenix are 17-23-4, 40 points, fourth in the Pacific. Those two picks could land in the top third of their rounds. New York are 21-17-5, 57 points, one point back of New Jersey Devils in the Atlantic race.

Two clubs with the deepest pick inventory in the league are both currently in the playoff hunt. Detroit spend $80.89M at $1.50M per point. New York spend $77.49M at $1.36M per point. Neither club can afford to lose players and still keep those contracts.

The expiring class tells you which way the trade window swings

Detroit have Brett Hull81, 40 years old, $4.20M, 76 points in 46 games, unsigned past June. Mathieu Schneider80, 35, $3.50M, 82 overall, also on an expiring deal. Two rentable veterans sitting on a 54-point club. Steve Yzerman83 returns from a shoulder on January 25 and adds urgency to the question: if the roster can't crack NSH, what does a GM do with a 40-year-old who puts up 76 points and costs $4.20M?

New York have Mariusz Czerkawski74, $2.60M, 76 overall, 76 morale, 11.1 minutes a night across 33 games, 1 year left. The Rangers also carry the most points-per-dollar pressure of any club that isn't already out of it: 57 points from $77.49M means every roster spot is expensive.

Fifty-nine days to the trade deadline. Are the picks the ammunition, or are they what's left after the ammunition is spent?

Detroit have 2 firsts. One is a lottery-odds asset in a draft where the Toronto original could be anywhere from 15th to 30th depending on how the season finishes for the Maple Leafs, who are 34-8-2 and would need a collapse of historic proportions to fall that far. The other is Detroit's own, and at their current record it lands between 18th and 25th. Two late firsts, three mid-round picks, one extra from Toronto's back end. That is a package a rebuilding club would trade two veterans to acquire.

Detroit aren't rebuilding. They're 54 points and chasing Nashville. So what does a contender need with the most inventory in the room?

League sources say they're not the ones who'll use the picks. They'll sell them in March to a club with a bad record and a young roster, and spend the money on a rental with a price the Rangers or another buyer can't match.
