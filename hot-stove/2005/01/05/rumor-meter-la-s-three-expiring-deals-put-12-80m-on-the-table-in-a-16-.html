RUMOR METER: LA's Three Expiring Deals Put $12.80M on the Table in a 16-Point Race 🔥🔥

Jason Allison, Adam Deadmarsh and Roman Cechmanek are all unsigned past June. One is the happiest player in the league. One hasn't played since November.

E.J. "Ears" CallahanInsider · The Hot StoveWednesday, 5 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Three contracts, $12.80M in combined salary, all expiring June 30. All three are in a Los Angeles locker room currently winning 4 straight at 39 points, while Dallas Stars holds 55 at the top of the Pacific.

Jason Allison87 carries the largest number of the three at $6.70M. He's 29, rated 83, with 51 points in 33 games. His morale reads 100, tied for the highest in the league with Jozef Stumpel79 and six others. A man at 100 morale on a $6.70M expiring deal doesn't walk unless the money is wrong. And at 1.55 points per game, the money might not be wrong at all. It's just wrong three times over.

Adam Deadmarsh87 is the one with a clock on him. 29 years old, 83 overall, $2.80M expiring. He's played 12 games, scored 11 points, and has been on injured reserve with a groin since sometime in November. The league's return date is January 21. If Los Angeles wants to get anything back for a man they won't re-sign at this number, the conversation has to happen inside the next two weeks. After January 21 he's in the lineup, his stock resets, and the window narrows to a single deadline date.

League sources say the club is open to calls on Deadmarsh. No deal believed done. The asking price, if it exists, probably starts at a second-round pick given 12 games of recent tape.

Roman Cechmanek91 is the third man. $3.30M, 33 years old, 87 overall, 31 starts. His morale sits at 86 in a room that averages 96.6. That gap between a man's mood and his team's mood is the kind of thing league sources notice. A 33-year-old goalie with one year left, below-room morale, and a payroll slot the size of his contract is a trade candidate in any market, in any month. He hasn't surfaced in the chatter yet. That doesn't mean he won't.

The problem for Los Angeles Kings is simultaneity. They can't re-sign all three. At 39 points and 12 wins, they're good enough to want Allison's production but not good enough to justify $12.80M in expiring talent if the ceiling is a first-round exit. The front office has to pick a lane: re-sign Allison, trade Deadmarsh, find a backup for Cechmanek who costs half his number. Or sell two of the three and reset. Nobody in the building is talking. League sources say the phone has rung twice on Deadmarsh since New Year's Eve.

Allison's 100 morale is the number that matters here. A player at 100 on an expiring deal either stays because the offer is right or leaves because it isn't. There's no middle path where he signs a one-year prove-it and comes back angry. If Los Angeles wants him in October, they need to be ready to pay before March 1, and that commitment on $6.70M makes trading Deadmarsh and Cechmanek a payroll necessity, not just a strategy question.
