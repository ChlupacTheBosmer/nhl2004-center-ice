Pihlman: The Bureau Says 57. I'm On the Ice at 78.

The 21-year-old Calder winner, told his ceiling sits below his current grade, says toughness at 87 doesn't peak at 21 and that his yardstick is right there in the room, not on a Book page.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelSunday, 8 January 2006

4:01 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

The Bureau's Development Index has Tuomo Pihlman78 four points above his base grade at 78 overall, but the same Book gives the 21-year-old left winger a potential of 57, below the base he already carries. Pihlman leads Minnesota Wild in scoring at 43 points in 38 games on a 93 pace, averaging 23.3 minutes a night after a Calder-winning rookie season of 34 points in 73 games at 12.7 a shift. Marcus Bell sat down with him in the studio.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here in the studio with Tuomo Pihlman of Minnesota. Tuomo, you've got 43 points in 38 games, you're logging 23.3 a night, you won the Calder last spring at 20 years old, and the Bureau's Index has you four above your base right now. But the same Book puts your potential at 57. That's below your base of 76, below where you are on the ice today. How do you read that?

Tuomo Pihlman

You know, I saw the number when the Book dropped. 57. And obviously I wasn't... I wasn't over the moon about it. But look, I'm on the ice every night, I've got 43 points, and I don't feel like I'm on the back nine. So maybe they look at the speed at 52, the faceoffs at 55, and they think, well, those don't grow. Fair. I get the logic. But 87 toughness? 87 on the shot? At 21? That doesn't peak, man. That gets better with road.

Marcus Bell

The Bureau doesn't put a 57 on many first-round picks, though. You went 9th overall in 2004. That's a high number attached to a low ceiling.

Tuomo Pihlman

No, and I'm not going to sit here and tell you it's flattering. It's not. But the draft thing, that's two years ago now. I'm not leaning on that. I'm leaning on what I do tonight.

Marcus Bell

Let's talk about tonight, then, or the season around it. Last year, 12.7 minutes a night. This year, 23.3. That's a different player.

Tuomo Pihlman

Same player. Different job. That's the thing. I didn't get faster, I didn't get stronger, I got a bigger role. The bench sat me down before camp and said you're on the top line with Saku Koivu. You want you driving the net on the power play, back-checking when we lose it. And then the part that... that stuck. They said we want you out there when it's on the line. And once you hear that, you step up. You just do. You don't get a choice, really.

Marcus Bell

So was that a vote of confidence or a job description?

Tuomo Pihlman

It was a job description with a confidence vote taped to it. Obviously. That's a cliché. I know it's a cliché. But it's true. You don't get one without the other. And before that, I was, I mean, I was on the third line, maybe a shift on the second, I'm trying not to make a mistake. Now I'm trying to make the right one. That's a completely different guy on the ice.

Marcus Bell

And Saku's in that slot. An 86 overall. Did it take long to click with him?

Tuomo Pihlman

Sak doesn't talk much. You know that? He's just... the same guy every single day. You come off a bad shift, he goes, again. You come off a good shift, he goes, again. And he's right there, 86, treating every practice like he's 78 trying to get there. And I'm 78, looking at 86. So he's the yardstick. Not some number on a Bureau sheet. Right there, every practice, in my face.

Marcus Bell

Your toughness reads 87. You're 73 inches, 204 pounds, you shoot left, and the Book says you're one of the hardest men in that room to move off the puck. Where does that come from? Who put that in a kid?

Tuomo Pihlman

My old man. That's the short answer and the honest one. He wasn't a hockey player. But he coached me from, you know, when I was little. And the first thing he taught me wasn't the shot, wasn't the stickhandling. He said, go to the front of the net. Stand there. Take the hit. Get the rebound. And I did it. I did it at seven, I did it at nine. And I didn't think about why. And somewhere around 12, 13, you start doing it because you want it, not because he's screaming at you from the boards. And once that switch flips, you're fine. You're fine for good.

Marcus Bell

Your English is native though. I mean, most guys coming out of Finland need a full season before they sound like you do.

Tuomo Pihlman

I grew up here, basically. My family moved when I was small. So hockey came out in English from the start. My Finnish is... okay. I can talk to my grandma, I can get through a phone call. But when I'm mad at a teammate for blowing a coverage, it's English. And Sak and I, we mostly do English in the room. He says maybe two words of Finnish a day and both of them are just my first name, said once, flat.

Marcus Bell

I want to bring up something the Book puts under your weakest grades. Fighting at 51. You're the toughest man in that room at 87, and you don't drop the gloves.

Tuomo Pihlman

No. Obviously no. That's not what I'm about. Toughness is winning the puck battle, blocking the shot, finishing your check. Those are different things and I know they're different. And fighting at 51 is just... it's accurate. I'm not going to pretend I'm going to be someone I'm not because the grade is low and someone thinks I should fix it. The gloves stay up. I'll screen the goalie all night.

Marcus Bell

Last one on the potential. 57. You said it stung. Does it make you want to prove them wrong?

Tuomo Pihlman

I don't... I don't want to prove them wrong, because they might not be wrong. Look, maybe I don't get faster. Maybe 52 on the speed is where it stays. Those weak grades, I know they're there. But maybe I get to 80 overall without getting faster. Maybe I just get smarter, tougher, and my shot gets a little better. And then 57 doesn't mean what they think it means. I just need to keep playing.

Marcus Bell

Tuomo, great to see you. Thank you very much for sitting down with me.

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell, from the studio. Back to you.
