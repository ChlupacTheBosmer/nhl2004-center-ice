Naslund On His Ceiling: "I Just Put On My Skates"

The captain of the Canucks, graded 38 points above what the Bureau says he can do, sat down in the studio to talk about a quieter season, a younger room, and one year left on the contract.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelSunday, 15 January 2006

4:44 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Markus Naslund91 came into this season as the reigning Hart Trophy winner and the best scorer in the league, a hundred and thirty-eight points in eighty-two games last year. He has sixty-five points in forty-four games so far, on pace for one hundred and twenty-one. The Sweater put up a profile this week that asked a pointed question: the League Scouting Bureau gives him a potential of 53, and he has been playing at ninety-one for most of a decade. He sits down with Marcus Bell in the studio to answer it.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here from the studio. Markus Naslund, captain of the Vancouver Canucks, thank you for making the time. I want to open with something that has been passed around this league all week. The Sweater ran a profile on you. The title reads: Potential 53, Overall 91. Markus Naslund has been 38 points past his ceiling since he was seventeen. The Bureau's Development Index puts your potential at fifty-three. You are at ninety-one. That is a gap the system says should not exist. What do you make of that?

Markus Naslund

I make of it nothing, honestly. The number is what they write. I read it, I think, okay, and then I go to the rink. Fifty-three. I scored nineteen points in Sweden when I was seventeen. I think that was more than fifty-three would give you. I do not know. I just try to get the puck and make a play. The rest is for other people to worry about.

Marcus Bell

That is a record points total for a seventeen-year-old in the Elitserien, by the way. Nineteen points. The Bureau would have you at fifty-three and you were out-producing most of the adults in the league before you crossed the ocean. Let me put this season on the table. You have sixty-five points in forty-four games. Last year you had a hundred and thirty-eight in eighty-two. You are on pace for one hundred and twenty-one. That would still be top five in this league. But it is not a hundred and thirty-eight. What changed?

Markus Naslund

It is not a hundred and thirty-eight, no. And I am not making excuses. I think I play good hockey. But the team, we are putting young guys in and the lines are moving around. And I am thirty-two. My legs, they are not what they were two years ago. That is just true. But sixty-five in forty-four, I can live with that. I cannot live with where we are sitting. That is the part that stays with you after the game.

Marcus Bell

And your ice time is up, though. Twenty-two and a half minutes a night, up from twenty and a tenth last season. The coach is putting you out there more and the points per game are going down. Does that feel different? Like the job itself is shifting under you?

Markus Naslund

It is different. For sure. Last year I take the faceoff, I go on the power play, I go short-handed, I do everything. This year maybe I go short-handed more because the young guys are not ready for that yet. He puts me out when we need a goal and I try to be there. I do not mind the minutes. I mind when I cannot get there as fast as I want. But that is just, you know, getting older. Everybody gets there.

Marcus Bell

Todd Bertuzzi is sitting right behind you. Sixty-one points in forty-four games. And the Book grades him at ninety-four. Three points above you. He is thirty years old. You have shared a line with him for a long time. How does it feel to skate next to a man the Book rates higher than you?

Markus Naslund

You ask me this and I give you the same answer every time. It does not matter. Todd is the hardest player to play against in this league. I do not say that to be nice. He gets the puck and the other team has to change everything they are doing. If he has sixty-five and I have sixty-one, I am passing him the puck myself. I mean that. He is a good player and he is a friend. I want him to be as good as he can.

Marcus Bell

You are the captain of this room. The youngest man on the dress is Drury. You have Trevor Linden in there, you have Mattias Ohlund, Ed Jovanovski. What is your job in that room, now, that it was not five years ago?

Markus Naslund

Five years ago I just played. I was the best player on the team and nobody needed me to say anything. Now I do the meetings, I talk to the officials so maybe a young guy does not have to. I try to come in early and I try to leave late and I hope they see that. I do not give speeches. One speech a year, that is enough. I think the young guys watch what you do more than they listen to what you say. That is fine.

Marcus Bell

You have one year left on the contract. Four point four million. Do you think about what this summer looks like?

Markus Naslund

Of course. I am not stupid. I think about it. But I cannot play well if I think about it. You think about next year, you play careful. And careful is not how you score goals. So I try to finish this season and then we see. I want to play. I love to play. But I will not beg anyone for a contract. If they want me, I am here. If not, I go home.

Marcus Bell

Peter Forsberg. Your friend since the two of you were boys in Örnsköldsvik. Same school, same summer job at the electrical company. He is in Denver, thirty-two, fifty-five points, twenty-six goals. Did you talk to him before this season?

Markus Naslund

We talk. He calls, I call. We talk about hockey for maybe two minutes and then we talk about the town. The electrical job, my mother worked there and his father worked there. We were eighteen and we carried wire around for two months in the summer. And now we are both playing hockey for a living and we talk about the wire. It is funny. He is playing good in Denver. The numbers are there. He is doing well.

Marcus Bell

Henrik and Daniel Sedin. From Örnsköldsvik. They are headed to your room at Vancouver. What do you know about them?

Markus Naslund

I know the family. Henrik is very skilled, you know? He sees things before the play happens. Daniel is bigger, he is stronger, maybe he is more willing to go to the net. They will be good players. Örnsköldsvik is a small place and it keeps giving us players. Peter, me, the Sedin twins. I think it is the cold. You skate outside when you are eight and you learn to be good because if you are not good you freeze. That is the whole secret.

Marcus Bell

Last one. You are thirty-two. The room is younger than it has been in a while. One year on the deal. Where does this road go?

Markus Naslund

I do not know. I try not to look that far. If I look two years ahead I play different and I do not want that. I just want this season. If the club wants me back I come back. If not, I go home. Örnsköldsvik is nice. But not yet. I am not done yet.

Marcus Bell

Markus Naslund, captain of the Vancouver Canucks. Markus, thank you very much for your time. Back to you.
