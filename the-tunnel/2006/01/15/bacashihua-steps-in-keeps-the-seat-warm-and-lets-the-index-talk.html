Bacashihua steps in, keeps the seat warm, and lets the Index talk

The 23-year-old took his first start of the season and spoke after about staying ready in a room full of veterans while the Stars run away with the Pacific.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelSunday, 15 January 2006

2:15 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Jason Bacashihua97 got his first start of the season this week for Dallas Stars, finishing the night with no decision. The 23-year-old has a 97 overall in the Book, matching Marty Turco at the other end of the bench, and the Bureau's Development Index has him up 16 from his base grade, the kind of swing that makes a backup's phone buzz.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk down in the tunnel here with Jason Bacashihua. Jason, you got your first start of this season this week, first one since last year. What's that like coming back in?

Jason Bacashihua

Yeah, it's good, you know. You get out there and it feels like you never left. The ice is the same, the guys in front of you, the same guys you've been skating with every day. So it's not, I mean it's not like a whole new thing. It's good to be back in there.

Jan Kowalczyk

You took no decision that night. The team didn't come away with the two. Does that stick with you more than it would if you were playing every night?

Jason Bacashihua

Well, you always want to, you know, you want to give the team a chance and, uh, yeah, the one that went in, you're thinking about that shift for the next couple of days. But you don't get to worry about it because you're back in the building tomorrow doing the same stuff. So you move on.

Jan Kowalczyk

You've got Marty Turco at the other end of the bench. You two share the same grade in the Book, 97. Does that come up in the room between you?

Jason Bacashihua

No, man, Turc and I, we, you know, we just want the team to win. He's played a ton, he's, what, got like a hundred wins or whatever, I don't even know the number. But he's been great for me. He's the guy I talk to every day. The grade thing is, you know, that's for people outside the building to worry about.

Jan Kowalczyk

Well, the Bureau's Development Index has you up 16 from your base grade. That's a big move. What's changed?

Jason Bacashihua

Uh, I mean, I don't know, man. I feel the same. I've just been, you know, the last couple years I've been in here, I've been watching, I've been learning from, what, Marty, from the coaches, and you just, you absorb stuff. You're 23, you're not, you're not stupid, you know what's going on. So you just, you keep going to work and maybe that's what, that's what shows up on the, on whatever index people use.

Jan Kowalczyk

Dallas is alone at the top of the Pacific, 62 points, three up on the next team. How does a backup feel that? You're in the middle of a run most guys on other teams would kill for.

Jason Bacashihua

It's great. You know, you're watching Modano out there every night and, uh, Morrow and Lehtinen, they just keep going. The room's, you know, it's a veteran room but the energy is good. Everybody knows what's at stake. You sit on the bench and you feel it. So yeah, you want to be out there every night with those guys.

Jan Kowalczyk

One year left on your deal, $0.90M. Does that sit in the back of your mind when you're sitting on the bench?

Jason Bacashihua

You know what, it doesn't. Not right now. I mean, when you're in the middle of something like this, the points don't matter. If I'm playing good hockey and the team's winning, all that stuff takes care of itself. So I'm just trying to, you know, be ready when they need me.

Jan Kowalczyk

Jason Bacashihua, thank you. Back to you upstairs.
