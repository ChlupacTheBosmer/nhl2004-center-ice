Pat Quinn won't name his starter in Washington, and likes that it's a hard call

The Maple Leafs' bench boss on the 31-8-2 room that won't get too high, the three-way split in net, and why a back-to-back in the East changes everything about who suits up.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelSunday, 15 January 2006

3:44 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Pat Quinn's Toronto Maple Leafs beat Calgary Flames 5-2 at home on 2006-01-14, their fourth straight win, and the Maple Leafs sit first in the league at 31-8-2 and 72 points. Rinkside reporter Marcus Bell caught the coach in the corridor afterward for a word on the room's mood, the goaltending, and the back-to-back ahead.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here outside the dressing room, after a 5-2 win over Calgary. Four straight, Pat, 31 and 8 and 2, 72 points, ten clear of the next team. What's that room like on a night like this?

Pat Quinn

You know, it's a good feeling. I think the guys enjoyed tonight, especially that second and third. But the room is, it's workmanlike. We've been here in this stretch before, where you string some together, and I like that nobody's riding too high. That's the compliment I'd pay them. They know Saturday's coming.

Marcus Bell

You outshot them 26 to 21. Was there a moment tonight where you felt it turn?

Pat Quinn

The first period we managed the puck well enough, and then, you know, the second period the compete level just, it goes up a notch. Calgary's a team that'll hang around and bite you if you let them. So we talked about some things in the intermission. Small things. Puck support on the far side, the first stick on the puck. And the guys responded. That's what you want. You want to talk about the details and have them show up. Whether they always do is another matter.

Marcus Bell

You've dressed the same groups this week, no changes. Does that help the feeling, when guys know exactly where they sit?

Pat Quinn

Yeah, obviously. I think we've found a structure that suits us. When you roll the same lines, they get a rhythm. Sundin and Chlup, those two know each other's game now, they read each other on the half-wall. Nash is getting comfortable up there with them. You can see it. The first line is pretty consistent and the numbers bear that out. The second and third lines, they're doing their job. That's what keeps the mood right, you know. Everybody's got a role and nobody's confused about it.

Marcus Bell

Let me talk about the net. That's the thing everybody's watching. You've split it three ways. Luongo at 51 percent, Ouellet at 27, Tellqvist at 22. That's unusual for a team at the top.

Pat Quinn

Unusual to some people. Look, Roberto Luongo has been our number one. His numbers say that. Seventeen wins, four shutouts. That's a top goaltender. But you play 82 games. You can't ask one man to carry that alone and expect him at his best in April. So Maxime Ouellet gets his starts. Mikael Tellqvist gets his starts. They've all earned their time. I don't want to lose that trust for a short-term advantage.

Marcus Bell

Tellqvist is nine and one. That's a hell of a record. Does that make the call harder?

Pat Quinn

You know, it makes it harder in the sense that I don't want to take the wind out of anyone. Every man who's dressed and played this year has earned it. But the playoff stretch, we're going to need Robby when it matters most. So I manage the load and I keep everybody sharp. That's my job. That's what they're paying me for, whether I deserve it or not is a different question.

Marcus Bell

Washington Saturday, Montreal Sunday. Back-to-back on the road. Can you tell me who starts in D.C.?

Pat Quinn

You want me to do my job on national television. No, I won't. But I'll tell you this. A back-to-back in the East, that shapes the crease. There's a good chance the guy who plays Saturday doesn't play Sunday. That's the simple version. I'll tell the men when I tell the men.

Marcus Bell

So the schedule drives it more than who's hot?

Pat Quinn

It's all of it. You can't ignore who's hot. You can't ignore who's fresh. You can't ignore who's been the guy when it counted. And I've got three guys who can win tonight. That's a good problem. I'll take that. You've seen teams in this league who are desperate for a body in net and they'll tell you about it every single day.

Marcus Bell

Four in six after tonight. WSH, MTL, then New York Islanders on the 19th. That's the East road. How do you manage a young group through that kind of travel?

Pat Quinn

Four in six. Yeah. The travel in the East, you know what it is. Late arrivals, early mornings. And we've got young guys, they're learning the schedule as much as they're learning the game. So practice is lighter on those days. You get your legs back in the morning skate, you protect the energy. It's about managing the load. You don't want to be, you know, grinding them on a travel day. That's how you get beat three games later.

Marcus Bell

Last one. You're ten ahead of New York and New Jersey. Does that number show up in the room at all?

Pat Quinn

The gap. No. And I wouldn't let it. That's a line you hear every year, every single year, someone's up by ten and it's over. It's never over. We're not the team that runs away with it. By Monday morning, it's Washington and Kolzig and we've got to earn it again. The gap is a nice thing on a Sunday. That's all it is.

Marcus Bell

Pat Quinn, thank you as always. Back to you.
