Jimmy Benton built the bus before the barn

The Boston general manager explains why his team is 16-5 on the road and 8-14 at home, and says the rooms that move the needle are the ones he has not built yet.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelSunday, 15 January 2006

3:59 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Boston sits 20-15-4, 52 points, second in the Northeast division, but the split tells a story the standings hide: 16-5 away, 8-14 at home. Boston Bruins carries a travel budget at level 5, the only facility line above par, with equipment and workout both at zero and practice at one. The club's per-point cost is $1.02M, the fifth-best in the league, and the profit sits at $20.45M.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Jimmy Benton, thank you for coming in. Marcus Bell here from The Tunnel. Let me start with the split, because it's hard to miss. Sixteen and five on the road, eight and fourteen at home. Most coaches would say that's a mental thing. You're running the building, so I'll ask you: what's different when you're in your own rink?

Jimmy Benton

It's a building thing, yeah. I won't pretend it's something else. We put the money into travel first. That was a choice I made, and it's showing up in the numbers, sixteen and five on the road, you look at that and you think we've got the right idea. But practice, we're at one. Equipment's zero. Workout's zero. Those are the rooms you need when you're at home. The travel budget helps you sleep on a Tuesday night in St. Louis. It doesn't help you skate on a Thursday afternoon in Boston.

Marcus Bell

So you'd say the home record is the price of that choice.

Jimmy Benton

I'd say it's part of the price. There's other things, injuries, a stretch where we lost three straight. But you look at the facilities ledger and it's plain. Travel at five, everything else at the floor. I don't think anyone in this league would've spent it the other way and gotten sixty points instead of fifty-two. But eight and fourteen at home, that's not the whole story, that's a piece of it I can put my finger on.

Marcus Bell

The record filed a locker room upgrade for you between January 5th and the 8th. That's the most recent thing any club on this list has done. Does it change anything for your room?

Jimmy Benton

It's a step. I'll say that much. I'm not going to stand here and tell you that a new locker room wins you a game on a Saturday. It's about the culture, the way a guy walks in. But I'll be honest with you, the things that cost you a game are practice ice and strength and conditioning. Those are where we're thin. The locker room is... it's the thing you can point to and say you did something. I needed to do something visible while I figure out the rest.

Marcus Bell

Let's talk about the money, because you have it. Fifty-two-ninety-six on payroll, $20.45M in profit, tickets at $79. You're efficient. You're profitable. Where does the next dollar go when you get a chance to spend it?

Jimmy Benton

Practice. Practice first. I've said it internally, I'll say it here. We've got five guys out right now, Samsonov's got that ankle, Laperriere's shoulder, O'Donnell's neck, Zamuner's back. When I've got a bunch of guys coming back and they need to get their legs under them, I've got one sheet of ice that's at a one. That's the problem. I can't develop a young guy and rehab three veterans on the same schedule at the same quality. That's what the practice line being at one means for a man in my chair.

Marcus Bell

With the trade deadline what the calendar has at 38 days out, are you a buyer?

Jimmy Benton

We're looking. I'll leave it there. What I'll tell you is this: when I look at the roster and I ask myself what would fix that eight and fourteen at home, the answer isn't one player. It's ice. It's the rooms. A player I put on a level-two practice rink at home is still going to be at a disadvantage to a player on a level-five rink. So do I buy a body, or do I build the thing that makes my bodies better? Right now my answer is both, but the building comes first.

Marcus Bell

You're at $1.02M per point. Only four clubs in the league are getting more for their dollar. Does that number give you latitude, or does it mean you're already stretched?

Jimmy Benton

It means I've got a good front office and a good coach getting value out of guys who aren't getting paid like superstars. Does that give me latitude to upgrade the rooms? Not automatically. The money's there, the profit is there, but the payroll is where it is for a reason. I'm not going to tear up a contract to put in a weight room. Those decisions get made over years, not over a trade deadline.

Marcus Bell

One more on the roster. You've got five injuries, some of those are veterans coming back in a week or two. Does that change what you're willing to trade at the deadline, or does it mean you wait and see who comes back healthy?

Jimmy Benton

Wait and see, mostly. Samsonov back the 24th, O'Donnell the 19th. If those two come back and look like themselves, we're deeper than we've looked all month. I'd be making a decision at the deadline with a different roster than the one I'm looking at right now. And I don't like trading with incomplete information. The boys know that. The coach knows that.

Marcus Bell

Jimmy Benton, general manager of Boston, thank you for your time.

Jimmy Benton

Appreciate it, Marcus.
