Giguere felt the third, then felt nothing at all

After a 2-1 win on 20 shots against, Jean-Sebastien Giguere talks about the period that mattered, the one he did not, and where 12-18-5 leaves the room.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelFriday, 13 January 2006

2:17 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

The Jean-Sebastien Giguere91 record reads one more win, no shutout, twenty shots against and one past him, the same line he has been riding all season: the game is there, the room gives him a lead to protect, and then the third period arrives. Mighty Ducks of Anaheim sit at 45 points, 12-18-5, second in the Pacific, 18 points behind Dallas Stars. Giguere spoke to Jan Kowalczyk in the corridor after the game.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here with Jean-Sebastien Giguere. Two-one over San Jose, twenty shots against, one in the back. Talk to me about that third period. Twenty shots means they were pushing. How did you feel in there?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

No, no, you know, the third was the good one. The first period, they get five, six shots quick and some of them, I don't know, the angle, the screen, I have to work. The third, the guys collapse. Havelid and Ward, they, they get bodies in front of the net and nothing comes clean. So the third is, it's good. I'm, I'm okay. The first one is the one that worries me.

Jan Kowalczyk

You were 12-17-5 before tonight. You had that stretch in December where you told Marcus you could win ugly. Did this one feel ugly to you?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

Yeah, yeah, it's ugly. Eighteen shots for us. Eighteen. You can't ask the goalie to win with eighteen. You can't. But we get two. Havelid, from the point, I don't even see it, I just see the red light. And Fedorov, the second one, that's, you know, that's just, that's Sergei. He goes to the net and he scores and now I have a cushion and I can breathe.

Jan Kowalczyk

You said to Marcus after the Philadelphia game in January that you can't fix the other end. You just kept saying his name, Fedorov, every time. Tonight he gives you that. Does it change what you feel in there?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

Look, I say it every night, for sure. Every night I'm looking at him, thinking, 'Sergei, do something.' And most nights he does something. And I'm grateful. But one goal, it's not the fix. I need eighteen, I need twenty shots from us. I need the guys to get the puck in and hold it. One goal from Sergei, I'm still protecting a one. I'm still, you know, every shot is a tie. That's the, that's the nervous part.

Jan Kowalczyk

So the schedule. Forty-three games down. You're eighteen points behind Dallas. What does the room look like right now when the coach talks?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

The coach talks about the next game. Always. I don't hear him say 'Dallas' or 'the standings.' He says, 'here's the next guy, here's what we do.' And the guys, the guys listen, mostly. We're eleven at home. Eleven wins at home. That's, that's not enough. The road is where it, where it, where we're fine. The home building, we lose too many. So if we get the home, we get the points.

Jan Kowalczyk

You got the win tonight. Does it help the room sleep tonight?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

Yeah. One night. We sleep one night. Tomorrow we watch video. We talk about the first period, the six shots, the ones I can't get the angle on. That's the work. The win is, the win is tonight. The work is tomorrow.

Jan Kowalczyk

Jean-Sebastien Giguere, thank you. Back to you.
