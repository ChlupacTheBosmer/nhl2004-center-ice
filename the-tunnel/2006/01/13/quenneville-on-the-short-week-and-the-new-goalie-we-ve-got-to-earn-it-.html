Quenneville on the short week and the new goalie: 'We've got to earn it every night'

The Avalanche coach talks Storr's shutout, the Sauve acquisition, and what a two-game week does to a team riding eight straight.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelFriday, 13 January 2006

4:07 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Colorado Avalanche head coach Joel Quenneville spoke with The Tunnel after a 2-0 road win at Carolina on January 12, the club's 8th straight. COL is 20-12-7 through 44 games, sits 5th in the Northwest at 57 points, and has just two games left in the week before January 20. The club acquired Philippe Sauve from Nashville in a two-player trade confirmed this week.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here in the tunnel at Raleigh with Joel Quenneville. Coach, 2 and 0, your guys hold Carolina to nothing. Jamie Storr, the first shutout. How did that one feel, sitting in the bench?

Joel Quenneville

Yeah, look, we didn't give them a lot to work with, and that's, you know, that's what you want. Twenty-two shots for them, we were good in that area, the neutral zone, the back end, the details. Jamie was sharp when he had to be. He made the saves that mattered in the second period. You take those nights. They don't come easy.

Jan Kowalczyk

Eight in a row. You look at that stretch, what's changed from the team that was, uh, 12 and 12 at the start of November?

Joel Quenneville

Well, I think the thing that changed was the structure held. When you play with a system and guys buy into it, you see it. We're not, we're not making mistakes in our own end the way we were early. The compete level, obviously, it goes up when you're winning. That's natural. But the habits are in place now. Guys know where they're supposed to be, they know the read. Peter's playing at a, you know, at a level that's hard to describe.

Jan Kowalczyk

Peter Forsberg's got 52 points in 44 games. You've got Paul Kariya with 37 assists. Those two together are putting up numbers that nobody's touching. Is there something specific in the way you've put those two together, or is it simply, they see the ice?

Joel Quenneville

You know, the short answer is they see the ice. The longer answer is we try to, we try to give them room. We've got guys around them who don't hold the puck too long, who make the play, and that lets those two do what they do best, which is, which is make the other guys better. Paul's got 50 points, 37 of them assists, that tells you everything. He's passing first. And Peter's, Peter's got vision that, you know, you see it in practice every day and it still surprises me.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let me ask about the transaction this week. You got Philippe Sauve from Nashville. Two players each way. What does that move do for you, because Jamie Storr is playing 70 percent of your games, Trevor Kidd is backing him up, and you just added a third option?

Joel Quenneville

Yeah. Look, the front office made that call, and I'll tell you how I see it. Depth. We're in a, we're in a stretch where you've got guys coming back from injury, you've got guys who could go down tomorrow. You want bodies you can trust behind the top two. It's not a statement about Jamie or Trevor. Both of those guys have been, have been very good. It's about, you know, about having a roster that can absorb something if it happens.

Jan Kowalczyk

How did the room take it? You've got a dressing room that's riding eight wins, the highest morale, and then you bring in another guy on a two-way deal. Did that register?

Joel Quenneville

It registered. You know, Philippe came in, he's a professional, he skated, he said hello, he's a quiet guy. We don't make a, we don't make a big production of it. You bring in a third goalie, you make it clear to the other two that nothing's changed, their roles are set. And those two guys, they're grown men, they get it. So, yeah, it's a body. We're not, we're not turning anything upside down.

Jan Kowalczyk

Now let's talk about the calendar. You've got two games left this week. Detroit on Monday at home, Dallas on Friday at home. That's it. Four days off, then three. Does a schedule that thin bother a team on a run?

Joel Quenneville

It does. It does, you know, the honest answer is it does. You're playing, you're in a rhythm, you're winning, and then you go three days. What we've done is we've kept the practices tight. Short, sharp, the details. We don't, we don't want a guy coming in to practice on a Tuesday and thinking, well, we don't play until Monday. No, every shift counts in practice or it doesn't stick in the game. But yeah, it's, it's not ideal. You want to play, you want to keep the momentum going.

Jan Kowalczyk

Detroit's coming in, they're on a one-game losing streak but they're a tough group, Rhett Warrener's back from that elbow. You watched tape on them this week?

Joel Quenneville

Obviously. They're, they're a good team, they've got size, they've got experience, and Rhett's back, that helps them. They've got, Kris Draper, Maltby, they'll make you pay for, for the little things. So we've been working on those little things. The puck management, the entries. You don't want to give them, give them short shifts and easy pucks. They'll take it to you.

Jan Kowalczyk

Coach, last one. You've got the best morale in the league right now, everybody in that room's smiling. Does that bother you at all, the complacency risk with a schedule this light?

Joel Quenneville

Look, you know, I think when you're winning, everyone's smiling. That's just, that's how it works. Does it bother me? No. Because I'm in that room every day. I know what the competition looks like. I know who's taking their rep, who's not. And those guys know what we're trying to do. We've been 20 and 12, but there's a difference between that and, between what we want to be in April. Nobody in there's thinking about the standings right now. They're thinking about Detroit.

Jan Kowalczyk

Joel Quenneville, thanks for your time. Back to you.
