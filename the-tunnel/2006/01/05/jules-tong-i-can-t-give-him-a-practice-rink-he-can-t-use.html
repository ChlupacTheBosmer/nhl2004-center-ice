Jules Tong: 'I can't give him a practice rink he can't use'

The Sharks GM walks through a payroll that returns $2.07M per point, a practice facility stuck at zero, and why he won't name the kid he's building around.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelThursday, 5 January 2006

4:07 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

San Jose Sharks sit 32 points back of the playoff line on an 11-game losing streak, and their general manager Jules Tong told The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk that the money and the buildings are the problem, not the man behind the bench. San Jose carries a payroll of $66.24M that returns $2.07M per point, the worst figure in the league. Tong addressed the gap between what he has paid for and what shows up on the scoresheet.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here, studio, and with me is Jules Tong, general manager of the San Jose Sharks® Sharks. Jules, eleven straight. Thirty-two points. You're the worst cost-per-point team in this league at $2.07M. Where does that sit with you on a Tuesday morning?

Jules Tong

It sits where it sits. I look at it every day, I don't pretend it's not there. Eleven is eleven. I won't dress it up for you, Jan.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're spending $66.24M. That's ninth in the league on payroll. You're returning thirty-two points. That's dead last. What's the disconnect?

Jules Tong

The disconnect is... look, I can tell you what I've bought and you can tell me what shows up on the ice. I've got an equipment room that's the best in this league. Level 5. That's not a problem area. But our practice rink is at zero. Our workout room is at one. I'm paying for a front end that the guys can't actually train in. That's the gap.

Jan Kowalczyk

So when I ask, does your coach have what he needs? You just told me no.

Jules Tong

I didn't say that. He's got a roster. He's got the players I put in front of him. I'm not going to stand in your chair and talk about his lines, that's not my job. But I'll tell you this: I walk into a practice facility that is the worst in this league and I look at the man's face and I know what he's thinking. He's thinking, 'How am I supposed to work with these guys in here?' And I can't answer him. I can write a check, it doesn't show up tomorrow.

Jan Kowalczyk

You've made upgrades. I can see travel went up, equipment went up. What happened between then and now?

Jules Tong

We made moves last summer during the off-season. Travel, practice, some of it got done. Practice came up but it came up from... look, we're still at zero. We moved it and we're still at zero. That tells you where we started. Equipment got to five, that took time and that took money I pulled from somewhere else. But somewhere else was practice. That's the honest answer.

Jan Kowalczyk

There's a kid in that room you're building around. Every GM has the one name, the one he's circling the wagon for. Who is he?

Jules Tong

You're going to make me say it in front of a camera. No. I won't do that to a young man. Not now, not with us on eleven straight and every word he says after gets turned into something else. He knows who he is. The coaching staff knows who he is. I know who he is. That's enough for now.

Jan Kowalczyk

But are you banking on him? Is that the plan?

Jules Tong

The plan is a roster that can win forty games. That kid is part of it. Every rebuild I've been around, and I've been around a few, there's a window where you hand the keys to a young guy before he's ready and he either drives it or he doesn't. We're in that window. I'm not going to rush it by putting his name on your broadcast.

Jan Kowalczyk

The deadline is seventy-three days away. You're eleven games back of the eighth spot at best, more like thirteen. Are you selling? Are you buying? What does Jules Tong do in March?

Jules Tong

I do what the math tells me. And the math says... the math says I'm spending $2.07M for a point and I need to figure out which contracts on that $66.24M aren't helping me get to where that kid is going to be ready. That's a deadline question. That's also a summer question. I'm not going to give you a yes or no on March. But I'll tell you this, Jan, I'm not paying $66.24M to lose eleven in a row and call it development.

Jan Kowalczyk

Your gate is 13,181 a night at eighty dollars. That's the second-lowest attendance figure in this league. What do you say to a season-ticket holder who paid for this?

Jules Tong

I say I know. I say I'm fixing the room they watch from and the room the guys train in, both of them, and it takes time and it costs money. I can't wave a wand. I've got a profit of $9.19M on $48.30M of revenue, which tells me the building's not broken financially. It tells me the product on the ice is what's keeping people home. And I can fix the product. It takes longer than people want it to take.

Jan Kowalczyk

One last one. You're not the coach. You said so yourself. But the phone rings at two in the morning on the way to an eleventh loss. What does that conversation sound like?

Jules Tong

It doesn't ring at two in the morning. I'm not that kind of GM. I hire good men and I let them work. What I do is... I look at the practice schedule and the travel budget and the workout room and I say, 'What else can I give you that isn't a player?' And right now the answer is a floor. A floor to practice on. That's what he needs from me. I'm working on it.

Jan Kowalczyk

Jules Tong, general manager of San Jose. Thank you for your time.

Jules Tong

Thank you, Jan.
