Gaborik had nothing and won, and that is the schedule talking

The quiet right wing says the line is closer than the box score shows, and a year after his 37-goal loss in this building, he says winning without the points feels different now.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelThursday, 12 January 2006

2:15 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Marian Gaborik99 and the Toronto Maple Leafs Maple Leafs beat Boston 4-2 on the road on 2006-01-11, with the Bruins outshooting Toronto 44 to 28. Gaborik finished with zero points on the night. Toronto sits at 68 points, 29-8-2, on a two-game win.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here with Marian Gaborik in the tunnel after a 4-2 road win. Marian, forty-four shots against, twenty-eight for you, and you go home with two points in the standings. What is the thing that lets a team win a game like that?

Marian Gaborik

Yeah, well, you know, Roberto was... he was the wall tonight. I don't know how many he stopped but it was, how you say, too many chances and he just, no. Every time they get a clean look he just closes the angle. And then we, we score when they don't expect it. That's the whole game.

Jan Kowalczyk

Your line, the Sundin line with Chistov. Chistov scores, Sundin gets the assist, you get none of it. How close was it? Did you feel like it was there, tonight, just the puck didn't go in?

Marian Gaborik

Yeah I feel it. I feel it, you know? Mats, he sees things before they happen, and I, I think I get to the net maybe one time, two times where it's, you know, almost. But almost is zero on the paper. And Chistov, he makes it, he scores, so I'm happy for him. That's the, the line is good. The line is finding it, just not for me tonight.

Jan Kowalczyk

Last January I asked you here, same building, after a loss where you scored your thirty-seventh. You told me nobody in the room cares about your goal total after a loss. Now the reverse happens. Zero for you, two points on the board. Does it feel different from the other side?

Marian Gaborik

Yeah. It does. Last year I, I score, I think okay maybe I can feel something, but we lose and it doesn't matter. Tonight I score nothing, I think okay what am I doing out there, and then the, the win. So yeah, it's better, I won't lie. You know? You want the win first. The goal can come tomorrow.

Jan Kowalczyk

What does the next stretch ask? You're sixteen and three on the road, the best road team in the league. Everybody's watching for the night you can't go on the road and win.

Marian Gaborik

Well we know, we know everybody's waiting for that night. So every game is, is that game now. We don't, you know, we don't look at the road record and say oh we are good on the road. We get on the bus, we play. The schedule is, is coming fast, that's what I can tell you. Everybody wants to beat Toronto now and that's fine. You know, we play good game, we go home.

Jan Kowalczyk

One more. You said the line is finding it. If the puck drops for you on the next one, what does it look like? Is it Chistov crossing, is it Mats feeding the slot, what does that goal look like in your head?

Marian Gaborik

In my head it always looks good, you know? But honestly, I think it's Mats, he carries it in, Chistov goes behind the D, and the puck, it comes back to me at the top of the circle or, or I go to the net and I just, you know, I put it in. It looks like that. Every night I think it looks like that.

Jan Kowalczyk

Marian Gaborik, thank you. Back to you.
