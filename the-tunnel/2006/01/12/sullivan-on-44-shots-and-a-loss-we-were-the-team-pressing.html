Sullivan on 44 shots and a loss: 'We were the team pressing'

The Bruins outshot Toronto 44 to 28 and lost 4-2, and Sullivan says the split he diagnosed last January has a new name: comfort.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelThursday, 12 January 2006

3:20 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Boston Bruins lost 2-4 at home to Toronto Maple Leafs on 2006-01-11 despite firing 44 shots on goal against 28 for the visitors, the largest shot gap in a Boston loss this season. Head coach Mike Sullivan sat down at the boards afterward to talk about what that gap means when the scoreboard goes the other way, and about a home record that now reads 8-13.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Mike Bell here with Mike Sullivan at the boards in Boston. Coach, 44 shots against 28, and you're down 2-4 in the building. Help me understand that game.

Mike Sullivan

Well, you know, the scoreboard's the scoreboard. Forty-four shots sounds good on paper, but I watched the tape in my head on the bench and, uh, a lot of those were from the outside, shots through bodies, shots that weren't really grade-A chances. They blocked the lane. They kept us to the perimeter and they made us earn it and we didn't earn it tonight.

Marcus Bell

You outshot them by sixteen and you gave up four goals. That's a lot of ice in front of Raycroft when you're getting that much traffic the other way.

Mike Sullivan

Andy played fine. He made the save at two-nil that kept us in it, the one off the post that came back to him. What I'd say about it is, and I've said this before, when you don't convert at the other end, eventually the weight of the night shifts. You get trapped in your own end for two minutes, three minutes, you change, you come back, you're chasing. That's not a goaltending problem. That's a transition problem on our part.

Marcus Bell

You told Jan Kowalczyk a year ago that the home/road split came from pressing, trying too much in front of your own fans. You're 8-13 here now, 16-5 on the road. Same explanation?

Mike Sullivan

Look, I remember that answer, you know, and I don't take it back entirely. But it's a different thing this year. Last year we were a young team pressing because we wanted to prove something here. This year I think it's almost the opposite. We're comfortable. We get to the rink, it's familiar, the guys don't have that edge they find on the road. And on the road, somebody's screaming at you all night and you've got a point to make. Here we've got to manufacture that. That's my job.

Marcus Bell

You're on a two-game losing streak, and if you look at the last ten it's a perfect alternating pattern. Win, loss, win, loss, ten straight. Is that a coincidence?

Mike Sullivan

I'll tell you what it is. It means we can't string two together right now. That's the thing I keep coming back to. Forty-four shots tonight, we should win that game, and that's what I'm going to lose sleep over. Not the pattern, the games. Two losses in a row at home, that's the one that stings.

Marcus Bell

You've got five guys out. Samsonov, Laperriere, Zamuner, O'Donnell, Samuelsson. Samsonov's got 27 points in 22 games. How much of that production are you asking other guys to find?

Mike Sullivan

All of it. You know, you don't get to feel sorry. Sergei's 27 in 22, obviously that's a difference-maker. But, uh, Thor has been carrying the load, he's got 52 in 42, and the supporting guys, Knuble, Lapointe, Hilbert, they're finding ways. Andy's got 16 points. We can't sit around waiting for Sergei's ankle to heal. The next man has to be the man. That's not a platitude in this room, it's just survival.

Marcus Bell

What does the room feel like after a night like tonight, a night like that? Two straight.

Mike Sullivan

It's quiet. You know, it's not, it's not angry, it's not finger-pointing. It's just, it's the quiet of a group that knows it should have two points tonight. And I think that's actually a good sign. You get a team that walks off happy after 44 shots and a loss, that's worse. These guys know. They're not satisfied. I can work with unsatisfied.

Marcus Bell

Pittsburgh on Sunday. They've won four in a row and they're 16-12-6. What has to be different?

Mike Sullivan

We have to finish. That's it. We had 44 tonight and we put in two. We need to put in four, five. You can't win hockey games at this level with two goals unless you're perfect behind it, and we weren't perfect tonight. The details weren't there in the defensive zone, the first goal, the third goal, we get caught coming the wrong way. Pittsburgh's a good skating team, they'll punish that even more than Toronto did tonight.

Marcus Bell

Mike Sullivan at the boards in Boston. Thank you, coach.

Mike Sullivan

Thank you, Marcus.
