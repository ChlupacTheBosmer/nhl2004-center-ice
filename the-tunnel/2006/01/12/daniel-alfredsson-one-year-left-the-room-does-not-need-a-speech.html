Daniel Alfredsson, one year left: 'The room does not need a speech'

The Ottawa captain on the 1997 trade demand, the Book's view of his hands, and what he actually does when the lights are off.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelThursday, 12 January 2006

3:33 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Daniel Alfredsson [10] has worn the "C" for Ottawa Senators since at least the 2000-01 season. At 33, he carries 44 points in 42 games this season, the Book has him at 91 overall, and his contract has one year left at $11.40M. Marcus Bell sat with him in the studio after the Sweater profile "Daniel Alfredsson, at 33: what the Book says, what the room says."

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here with Daniel Alfredsson, captain of the Ottawa Senators, in the studio tonight. Daniel, The Sweater ran your profile this week, "what the Book says, what the room says." You are 33, you wear the C, and the average age of your locker room is 27.8. From where you sit, what does the captaincy look like on a normal day?

Daniel Alfredsson

Well, it looks like coming in early. I get here, I do my work, and the young guys come in and they see me doing my work. That is most of it, I think. Martin Havlat is 90 on the Book, he does not need me to say anything about his game. I just try to be there, for sure.

Marcus Bell

The profile spent real time on your per-attribute grades. The Book has DEKG at 98, PASS at 97, HERO at 95. It thinks your stickwork is the best thing in that locker room. Does that sound right to you?

Daniel Alfredsson

No, I do not think that is right. Martin has better hands than me, for sure. And Wade, Redden, he sees where the play is going two seconds before me. I just make the play that is there. The Book, it is very nice, of course. But I am not the best on that team.

Marcus Bell

You have worn the C since at least 2000-01. That is five seasons. What did the early years teach you that you did not know when you first put it on?

Daniel Alfredsson

I was not very good at it, I think. I was young and I wanted to say the right thing to everybody, and you cannot do that. Now I just play and if a young guy wants to talk he talks, and if he does not, that is also fine. Wade came to me after a bad series a few years ago and he asked me what he should change. I said, nothing. Play your game. That is the job. That is the whole job.

Marcus Bell

I want to ask about your scoring. You have 44 points in 42 games this season. Last year you had 108 in 81 games. Your minutes are the same, 21.5 a night. And your penalty minutes are 50 now, versus 21 last year, and the Book has your FIGH at 50. What is different about your game?

Daniel Alfredsson

Well, the points, I should have more goals. That is my problem, I think. I have 20 assists, the Index has me up two, and I feel good, I do. But the one-timer, the drive to the net, I need more of those. And the penalty minutes, yes. I do not fight. I am not going to fight anybody. But sometimes a young guy is out there and somebody comes after him and I am the captain, so I go there. That is not fighting, I think. That is being there.

Marcus Bell

Let me put the contract in front of you. One year left. $11.40M. How does that sit?

Daniel Alfredsson

I try not to look at it. My job is to play good and let my people and the club talk about the rest. I am here now, I am captain of Ottawa. The year, it does not change what I do tomorrow.

Marcus Bell

You were the 133rd pick in 1994, a fifth-round pick from Gothenburg. You were not traded here, you were not signed here, Ottawa took you and you have been here ever since. But in 1997, you demanded a trade. Alexei Yashin was making a million dollars more a year than you. Yashin is with New York now. You are still here, at 33, still wearing the C. How does that road sit with you?

Daniel Alfredsson

I was drafted here. I am not a man who came to Ottawa, I was here. That is a different thing, I think. In 1997, I was 24, and I thought it was not fair. Maybe it was fair, maybe not. I do not remember exactly now. But I stayed and the room grew around us. Now Alexei is in New York and I am here. I am glad. Of course I am glad. It was not always glad, but it is now.

Marcus Bell

Gothenburg. A kid drafted 133rd overall who just wanted to play. When you look at where the Book puts you now, 91 overall, HERO at 95, morale at 97, do you see him? That kid from Gothenburg?

Daniel Alfredsson

He just worked. Every day, he worked. He did not look at 133, he did not care about the number. He wanted the next shift, the next game. I think he would look at this and he would say, good, now work more. That is what he would say.

Marcus Bell

Last one. The title asks what the Book says and what the room says. The Book has you at 91 overall, DEKG at 98, HERO at 95, morale at 97. What does the room say about Daniel Alfredsson?

Daniel Alfredsson

The room says we have a game in two days. No, I will try. The Book looks at me. The room looks at us. The room says that when Radek Bonk takes a hit along the boards, four people are going over for him. The Book does not see that. The morale is 97, that is not because of me. That is because the guys like each other. I just open the door when they come in.

Marcus Bell

Daniel Alfredsson, thank you very much for your time tonight. Marcus Bell, back to you.

Daniel Alfredsson

Thank you. Of course.
