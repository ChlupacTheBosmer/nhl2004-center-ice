Adam Oates on days off, a gas station, and $4.52M more

The 43-year-old center says the money is not the thing he carries home, and takes us back to why.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelThursday, 12 January 2006

4:46 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Adam Oates77 sits in the chair opposite Jan Kowalczyk in a Colorado Avalanche jersey, the number 77 catching the studio light. Forty-three years old. Oates has 22 points in 42 games this season, down from 71 last year, and a contract with $4.52M left on it and 3 years still to run. His morale on the Bureau's Scale is 100, the highest number they print.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here, in the studio with Adam Oates of Colorado. Adam, thanks for sitting down. Let's start away from hockey. You're forty-three. What does a day off look like?

Adam Oates

Well, you know, I wake up and nobody's yelling at me to be anywhere, and that's the whole thing. I'll walk the dog, get some coffee, put on a lacrosse game if there's one on. I grew up with the box stuff, you know, the whole reason I'm standing here is I almost went the other way. Five seasons with Etobicoke, I won the scoring title. Bobby Allan award and everything.

Jan Kowalczyk

You left it in 1984 to focus on hockey.

Adam Oates

Yeah. I was nineteen, I'd dropped out of high school, I was pumping gas. I remember thinking, I'm going to be standing on a street corner in Weston for the rest of my life. So I went back to school, finished my diploma, RPI took a look at me. And I haven't stopped since, obviously.

Jan Kowalczyk

Do you still watch it? The box stuff?

Adam Oates

I watch it. I watch all of it. You know what, I'll put on a game and I'm sitting there calling out plays like I'm back in the crease with a stick in my hands. My wife tells me I'm yelling at the television. I don't even know what league it is, half the time. Doesn't matter. The hands know what the hands are doing, you know?

Jan Kowalczyk

Let me take you back a step, though. You went undrafted. Scouts looked at you at Markham and said you were too slow. Did that sit with you?

Adam Oates

For a while. You know, when you're nineteen and nobody wants you, you take it personal. You think they're looking at you and seeing something broken. But I went to RPI, I worked with a skating coach over the summer, I changed the way I moved. By the time Detroit signed me I wasn't the kid from the waxers anymore. So yeah it sat, but it sat in a good way. It gave me something to prove to myself.

Jan Kowalczyk

You got a four-year deal with Detroit. Highest-paid rookie in the league. What did that feel like at twenty-three?

Adam Oates

I thought I'd died and gone to heaven. I'd been pumping gas the year before. I remember standing in that office signing papers, thinking, this is the part where somebody wakes me up. And nobody did, so I just went with it. You know, people talk about the money now like I've had it forever. I remember what not having it felt like.

Jan Kowalczyk

Which brings me to your contract. You've got three years and $4.52M left. This isn't a contract year. You're not going anywhere.

Adam Oates

No, it's not. And look, I know what people are going to say. I've got 22 points, they look at the salary and they do the math. But here's the thing, I'm forty-three. I knew what this contract meant when I signed it. I knew I was buying myself a runway. I don't need to go out and put up 70 again to justify a cheque. I'm here to help Forsberg and Kariya and Hejduk win a game of hockey. That's the job.

Jan Kowalczyk

You had 71 points last year. You won the Masterton. That was a full year of being yourself. Now you've got 22 through 42 games. What's different?

Adam Oates

Legs. You know, I'm not going to make it complicated. I was twenty-three, twenty-four a year at nineteen minutes a night and I felt great. Now I wake up and I've got to think about whether my back's going to cooperate. I'm getting 19.9 minutes, same as last year basically, but the first five minutes cost me more than they used to. That's just arithmetic. I'm not complaining about it. I just want you to know I'm aware.

Jan Kowalczyk

The Bureau's Index has you two ticks below your base right now. Does that bother you?

Adam Oates

You know what, I've been two ticks below my base for... honestly, I don't know, three years maybe. My base is a number that means something to a guy in an office. My base is Peter Forsberg's face when I put it on his stick and he goes. That's my base. The Index can sit where it sits.

Jan Kowalczyk

You mentioned the Masterton. You won it last season. What did that trophy mean to a man who's been in this league as long as you have?

Adam Oates

It meant a lot. I'll tell you why. Because the Masterton is for the guys who shouldn't be here. The undrafted kid, the one who worked the gas station, the one scouts passed over at every level. And they handed it to me. So every time I look at that thing on the shelf I think, you were right to not listen to them. Every single one of them.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're in a room with a lot of talent. Forsberg at 95, Kariya at 89, Hejduk at 88. You're the oldest man in that room by a stretch. How do you fit?

Adam Oates

I win the draw, I get it to Peter. That's my job. I'm not going to take the puck from him and try to be something. You know what I mean? I've got ninety-five on my faceoff grade for a reason. I win the draw, I give him an inch of time, and then he takes over. And I skate. I'm not fast, I was never fast, but I know where to be and I get there before I'm supposed to. That's twenty years of just, showing up and thinking.

Jan Kowalczyk

You were the only man under twenty-three on that last Calder-winning team in Minnesota, but that's the history books. In this room, you're the oldest by about ten years from the next one. Do you mentor?

Adam Oates

I don't think of it like that. Nobody asks me for advice. I don't walk up to Hejduk and say, here's how you play this game. Milan Hejduk knows how to play this game. He's got 88. But you know what I do? I work hard every day. And they watch. And I think that's enough. You don't have to talk. You just have to not be the guy who's phoning it in on a Tuesday.

Jan Kowalczyk

Three more years on the deal. You'll be forty-six if you play it all out.

Adam Oates

Maybe. Maybe not. I know what I know, which is I feel good right now and I want to be out there when it matters. I don't have a number. I don't have a year. I'll know when it's over. I think the same way I knew when I walked out of that gas station, you know. You just, feel the door closing and you go through it.

Jan Kowalczyk

Adam Oates, Colorado Avalanche. Thank you for your time.

Adam Oates

Thanks, Jan. Appreciate it. Back to the lacrosse game.
