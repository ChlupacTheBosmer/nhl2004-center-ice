Olczyk after the 7-1: 31 shots, one goal, and no excuses

The Penguins outshot Toronto 31 to 20 and lost by six; Olczyk talked about the finish, the two defencemen still on the shelf, and a goaltender playing 95 percent of the season.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelTuesday, 10 January 2006

4:05 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Pittsburgh Penguins dropped their 4-game win streak 7-1 at home to Toronto Maple Leafs on Monday night, despite outshooting the league's best team 31-20. Head coach Eddie Olczyk sat with The Tunnel in the corridor after the game to sort out how that happens, who plays behind the injured, and why Johan Hedberg86 hasn't rested since November.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here in the corridor at Mellon with Eddie Olczyk. Eddie, thirty-one shots to twenty, your side had the puck more, you had the looks, you put one in. Talk me through how a game goes 7-1 with the shot clock the way it went.

Eddie Olczyk

You know, the shot differential tells you we generated. I'll give the guys that, they pushed the pace, they got to the net, there were five, six Grade-A looks in the first period alone that we didn't bury. And then you turn the other way and Toronto, they don't need thirty-one shots. They need eight and they get six. That's, that's what separates the first team in this league from where we are right now. They're clinical and we're, we're wasteful, and that's the honest read on the night.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're on a four-game win streak going into tonight and you look comfortable in the first five minutes. What changed?

Eddie Olczyk

The first one, the, the one they score, it goes top shelf and it's, it's a heck of a shot. And after that you can see the shoulders drop a little bit, you know? And I didn't like that. I didn't like that at all. You don't get to look down on the ice in this league, you play the next shift. So that's on me, that's, the standard has to be higher than one bad goal and you fold. We didn't fold, I want to be fair to the guys, they kept coming, but we weren't, we weren't the same team after that second one that they scored.

Jan Kowalczyk

You've got Ross Lupaschuk out until the 31st and Rico Fata should be back in a couple days. You've got two defencemen in that regular rotation who aren't Lupaschuk. How are you filling it?

Eddie Olczyk

Well, Brooks Orpik has played some, Josef Melichar, Shane Hnidy. I've got guys I trust to, to clear the crease, to not turn the puck over in their own end. What I don't have right now is the puck-mover. Lupaschuk, when he's in the lineup, he joins the rush, he carries it through, he's a, he's an extra man. Orpik doesn't do that the same way. So we simplify. You dump it, you forecheck, you win the battle, you score that way. You can't play through them with that group back there.

Jan Kowalczyk

And with Fata coming back, does that change your wings or are you just going to roll it in?

Eddie Olczyk

Rico's had five games this year, he's got one point, he's been out with that, that lower body thing. So I'm not going to put him on the first line on day one. He's going to skate, he's going to, he's going to earn his minutes back. Maybe the fourth line to start, maybe some PK. I'll look at him Tuesday or Wednesday and make a call.

Jan Kowalczyk

Johan Hedberg has been in 40 of your 42 games. That's ninety-five percent. Most clubs I talk to this year are splitting it more. Is that a choice you've made or is that a choice he's made?

Eddie Olczyk

That's the choice we've made together, you know? Johan, he told me in training camp, he said, Eddie, I want to play every game and I can. And he's proven it. The man's had maybe two nights where I thought, okay, is he gassed? And he wasn't. He's 86 overall in the, in the Book, that doesn't happen by accident at thirty-two years old. You've got to be in shape and you've got to want it. Now, if we get to March and he's looking, we'll talk. But right now, he's our guy, and that's not going to change because we gave up seven goals on twenty shots one night.

Jan Kowalczyk

I don't want to put that on him. Your room's got the second-highest average morale in the league at ninety-five point nine. You've got Mario Lemieux at fifty-seven points, Martin Straka at fifty-six. The talent's there. What do you need from the bottom six to get from where you are, third in the Atlantic, to actually winning this division?

Eddie Olczyk

The details, Jan. It's always the details. You look at our top end, Mario, Marty, Eric Meloche, they're carrying us. But third line, fourth line, I need twenty minutes a night of, of north-south hockey. I need guys who will, who will go to the dirty areas and not come back out empty-handed. Kris Beech, he's got the skill, we all know that. Can he do it for four shifts with the puck on his stick in the offensive zone and not force it? Can Brian Holzinger win the draw and then sustain? That's the difference between forty-six points and fifty-five. And I'll take fifty-five right now and feel a lot better about the standings.

Jan Kowalczyk

No moves up front, nothing since the summer. You're comfortable with what's here?

Eddie Olczyk

You know, people ask me that every year. Look, I've got thirty-three guys and two of them are in the IR and one of them comes back Thursday. I've got a top line that can hang with anybody in this league and a goaltender playing the best hockey of his career. You want me to blow that up for a, for a sixth defenceman? I'll take what I've got. I trust these guys more than I trust a name on a phone call.

Jan Kowalczyk

Three games before the next break, Boston on Thursday, Carolina Saturday, Tampa Tuesday. Do you change anything about the way you manage the group between now and then?

Eddie Olczyk

Not really. We get on the bus Thursday, we go to Boston, we play a good team that's got injuries of their own. I'm not going to, to change the structure because of one bad night. You change your structure after 82 games when it's the playoffs and you need to win a series. Right now you show up, you compete, you take the next one. That's what we'll do.

Jan Kowalczyk

Eddie Olczyk, Pittsburgh Penguins head coach. Thank you for your time.

Eddie Olczyk

Thank you, Jan. Back to you.
