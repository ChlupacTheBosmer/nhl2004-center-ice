Pihlman on the Ceiling They Drew for Him

MIN's 21-year-old Calder winner talks about the coach who moved him up the order, the Development Index that says he has nowhere to go, and what he wants the day the skates come off.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelTuesday, 10 January 2006

3:52 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Tuomo Pihlman78 won the Calder last spring with 34 points in 73 games at 12.7 minutes a night for Minnesota Wild. This season he has 44 points through 39 games, 23.4 minutes a night, and the Bureau's Book has him at 78 overall with a base of 76 and +4 on the Development Index. The Sweater ran a profile this week titled "Potential 57, Current 76," and Pihlman has not hidden from it.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here with Tuomo Pihlman of Minnesota Wild, who sits in the chair tonight as the most productive winger on his own club, 44 points through 39 games, 23.4 minutes a night. Tuomo, a Calder Trophy in your first year, this leap in year two. I want to start back there. Where did the change come from?

Tuomo Pihlman

Oh, I think, I think it came in September. The coach, he just, you know, he sat me down before the exhibition schedule and said you're going to live on that top line with Saku. And I'd spent the whole first year on, well, the fourth line. Twelve and a half minutes. So I was like, okay, that's a lot of trust for a kid who's 20.

Jan Kowalczyk

And he stuck with you. Twenty-three, twenty-four minutes most nights, power play time, the top matchup. That is not a small thing to hand a second-year man.

Tuomo Pihlman

No, it's not. And I think, you know, I think the thing he did that, that most matters was he stopped coddling me. The first year I was on that fourth line and I scored 23 goals, which I'm proud of, sure. But I was playing safe, I was playing not to lose it. Second exhibition game we're doing the small-area stuff, the 2-on-2 through the neutral zone, and he stopped the drill and he said Pihlman, make the play or leave the ice. And I've got a, I've got a memory for those things.

Jan Kowalczyk

So he made you uncomfortable and you played through it. Now, I want to put something in front of you. The Sweater ran a profile this week, 'Potential 57, Current 76.' The Bureau's Development Index puts your base at 76, your form on the Index has you at plus 4 today, so 78 overall. But the potential number, the ceiling they gave you, is 57. That is below where you already are.

Tuomo Pihlman

Yeah.

Jan Kowalczyk

Does that sit with you?

Tuomo Pihlman

For about two weeks it sat with me. I read it and I thought, that's low. Obviously it's low, they put it on a page, everybody reads it, you know. And then I thought about what goes into it. My SPBI is 52, my FIGH is 51. I know what they're looking at. They say, this guy doesn't have the legs to get there, he doesn't have the grit to get there, he's going to be a middle-six guy forever. And I get the logic.

Jan Kowalczyk

But you disagree with the conclusion.

Tuomo Pihlman

I think the box they drew is too small. Look, my SHPW is 87, my TOUG is 87, my balance is 86. Those are the things I've got, and those are the things that are going to get better if I'm in a spot where I have to use them every night. The 57, I think that's them saying 'this is where he stops.' And maybe it is. I'm not going to sit here and tell you I'm going to be a 90 player, that's not, that's not the conversation. But I think the number undersells where I am right now and where I'm going to land if the coach keeps doing what he's doing.

Jan Kowalczyk

So let me ask you something most 21-year-olds wouldn't get asked. That number, 57, it's not just an insult. It's a clock. If the Bureau thinks you've already peaked, that changes how you think about the back end of a career. When the playing stops, what do you see?

Tuomo Pihlman

You know, I think I've thought about that more than most guys my age, yeah. You read a number like that and it makes you, it makes you look at the calendar differently. I've got two years left on this contract, I'm 21, I've got the Calder, morale is where it is. I feel good. But I think... I want to stay around the game. That's the honest answer. Whether that's coaching, whether that's something front-office, I don't know. I don't know yet.

Jan Kowalczyk

You said the coach in camp. You mentioned Saku. Are there guys in that room you watch and think, that's what I want to be when I can't run the play anymore?

Tuomo Pihlman

Saku Koivu, every day. The way he handles a bad shift, he just, he goes to the bench, he sits down, he's right back. Doesn't spiral. And Wes Walz, the guy does the boring work, the faceoffs, the defensive zone coverage, and he does it like it matters more than the goal. I think, you know, I think those two are showing me that the game is bigger than your stat line. That's, that's the thing I've been trying to learn this year.

Jan Kowalczyk

Your morale is 92 on the Morale Scale. That's near the top of the league for guys your age. What keeps it there?

Tuomo Pihlman

I like the room, I like the 6 AM skate, I like the bus. You know, Manny Fernandez is in the net, so you've got a 92 in goal and you feel good about everything. That's not the whole answer, but it's part of it.

Jan Kowalczyk

Forty-four points through 39 games is a pace of 93 over 82. If you hold that into April, what does it mean for you?

Tuomo Pihlman

I haven't really thought about it that way, honestly. I want the next win. But if the pace holds, yeah, that's a nice number. That says you're not a fluke, you know. That says last year wasn't, wasn't the whole story. And that's, that's the one thing I'd like the 57 to mean. That the Bureau got it wrong.

Jan Kowalczyk

Tuomo Pihlman, thank you for the time. Back to you.
