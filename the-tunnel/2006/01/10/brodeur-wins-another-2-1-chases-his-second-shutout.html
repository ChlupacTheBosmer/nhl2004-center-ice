Brodeur Wins Another 2-1, Chases His Second Shutout

New Jersey's goaltender held Montreal to one goal on 22 shots and says the low-scoring wins are who this team is.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelTuesday, 10 January 2006

1:56 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Martin Brodeur95 picked up the 2-1 win for New Jersey Devils over Montreal Canadiens at home on Saturday, giving up one goal on 22 Montreal shots. The Devils sit at 23-9-3 and 57 points in the Atlantic, three behind New York Rangers. Brodeur has one shutout this season, the same total he finished with in 2004-05.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here with Martin Brodeur. Martin, two to one, you get the win over Montreal, but they put 22 shots on you and you had 18 coming the other way. Walk me through that third period.

Martin Brodeur

Yeah... they came, for sure. Twenty-two, some of those were real good looks. But the guys in front of me, they blocked. They put their bodies in there. When you're up one in the third, you look at the bench, everybody is standing. You look at the ice, everybody is in the lane. That's... that's the game I like.

Jan Kowalczyk

You didn't get the clean one tonight. One shutout this season, same total you finished with last year. Marcus asked you about the zero back in January of 2005. Does the number still nag?

Martin Brodeur

The zero in January, that was different. I felt that. I felt it every game, for sure. Now I got one. One is... it's better. But I don't chase it no more, if that's what you're asking. Two to one, three to two, we win them that way. That's who we are. Tonight they get one goal, we get two, I'll take that every night.

Jan Kowalczyk

Friesen had a goal and two points tonight. Gomez buried the other one. From your net, do you see what those two are doing?

Martin Brodeur

Jeff is a shooter, you know? He gets his half second and he goes. And Scott, he's different, he goes into the little spots, where the defenseman is standing, where I can't see him. On the first one, I had a... there was a screen. I lost him. So yeah, good. When I can't see, they can't see. That's what I want.

Jan Kowalczyk

Eighteen shots for your own side. Does it get lonely back there when your offense isn't pushing?

Martin Brodeur

It gets long. In the second, maybe two, three minutes, nothing on my side. You're just... standing there. And the glove, she's... she gets sleepy. Then boom. They're coming and you're cold. You got to keep your legs, everything going. Eighteen, it happens. You just got to be ready when they turn it up.

Jan Kowalczyk

Twenty-three and nine and three. Fifty-seven points. New York Rangers are three ahead, on six straight. You're right in the Atlantic fight. What does the rest of this stretch ask?

Martin Brodeur

It asks us to keep winning these. Two to one, one to nothing, I don't care, we find a way. The Rangers, six straight, that's not easy, good for them. But we can't look across the room at New York. We look at tomorrow. For sure. Tomorrow is the game.

Jan Kowalczyk

Thank you, Martin. Back to you.
