He'll take finally: David Legwand and the game he waited for

The 2nd-overall pick tells Jan Kowalczyk which record he actually keeps, and names the coach who changed him.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelWednesday, 15 December 2004

3:37 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

David Legwand89 has 28 goals in 30 games for Nashville Predators, 45 points in 24.4 minutes a night, and the Bureau's Book has him at 87, three up on the Index. In the studio with Jan Kowalczyk, he walked through the record he carries and the one he keeps, the coach who changed him, and the penalty shot everybody asks about.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here with David Legwand in the studio. Twenty-eight goals in thirty games, forty-five points. And you look like you're bored of hearing it.

David Legwand

No, I'm not bored. I'm just, you know, I don't look at it. You get one, you get another, it's the next shift. The ice time's been there from the start this year, so you just go. That's the whole thing, honestly.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're up at 24.4 minutes a night. That's over three more than last year. Somebody decided that, didn't they.

David Legwand

The coach. He doesn't say much about it, he just puts me out there. Last five, the kill, the big draws in our end. When a coach trusts you like that, you wanna prove him right every shift. That's a good feeling, I'm not gonna lie.

Jan Kowalczyk

I want to take you to a record. December 23rd, 2000, overtime against the Rangers. First player ever to score on a penalty shot in overtime.

David Legwand

You know that one. Yeah. They still bring it up. It was a move and a flick and it went, and suddenly my name was all over. It's a funny record to have, you know, like it's trivia. Tiny corner of the game.

Jan Kowalczyk

Is that the one you'd call the proudest?

David Legwand

No. It's cool, it's on a card, but it's one shot in overtime. The one I keep going back to is the playoffs. Last year, six games, four goals, seven points, and the building was, it was something. That's what I remember. That's the one you actually feel.

Jan Kowalczyk

Walk me back to that night in New York. What do you remember?

David Legwand

The crowd goes quiet, cause it's their building, and you're out there alone. I remember thinking, just shoot, don't overthink it. And it went in. You can't plan those, you just hope. December, I remember it was cold, I don't know why I remember that.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let's go back further. You're drafted second overall in '98. That's a headline a seventeen-year-old carries.

David Legwand

Yeah. You carry it, you don't have a choice. Poile gave up two picks to get me, and I read that when I was a kid in Plymouth, and I thought I gotta be good right now. And then camp, I got sick, mono, and I was back on the bus. Looking back, that was the best thing that happened to me.

Jan Kowalczyk

How did going back to junior sit with you?

David Legwand

It stung. You're the pick and you're supposed to be up, and instead you're on a bus. But the game comes when it comes. I couldn't rush it, so I went and I played. I learned to be a player, not a number on a draft card. That year saved me, honestly.

Jan Kowalczyk

Then the staff told you to put on weight.

David Legwand

Yeah, they sat me down. I'd had a growth spurt and I was all legs, too light for center in this league. A coach said, you're gonna get thrown around, you gotta hold your own. He was right. You don't thank 'em at the time, you thank 'em later. He's the one who changed me, you know.

Jan Kowalczyk

So when did you stop being the second-overall pick and start being the player?

David Legwand

Hmm. I think this year, honestly. Last year I played forty-one games, and even the ones I had, I kept waiting for somebody to say I wasn't enough. This year it stopped. Twenty-eight already, and I say it out loud and it's strange. But it's a place I waited a long time to get.

Jan Kowalczyk

Forty-one games. Half the season. Does that still bother you?

David Legwand

More than I said at the time. You watch and you can't do anything. But you can't get it back, so this year I made sure, you show up, you take the minutes when they give 'em to you. And I have. That's the whole answer.

Jan Kowalczyk

The playoffs. What made that stand, beyond the numbers?

David Legwand

The whole building's dialed in, everybody's on the same page, every shift counts. We went four and four and we were right there. I want more of that. More than anything, that's why you do it. That's what I mean when I say the game comes.

Jan Kowalczyk

You were that kid, 105 points in Plymouth, Red Tilson. Is he anything like the man sitting here?

David Legwand

He's the same, I think, underneath. He just didn't know how to wait. I know now. You keep your head down, you take the minutes, you take the big draws, and the game comes to you. I'm glad it wasn't early. I'm glad I waited.

Jan Kowalczyk

So what's the record you keep, when all this is said?

David Legwand

Being the guy. The coach puts me out at the end, I take the draws, I play twenty-four minutes, I'm the one they hand the puck to. I've been waiting for that since I was seventeen. That's the one I keep. The penalty shot's trivia. That one's real.

Jan Kowalczyk

One more. The headline on all of this is 'second overall, finally.' Fair?

David Legwand

Finally. Yeah, I'll take finally. It's better than the other versions of that sentence, you know.

Jan Kowalczyk

David Legwand, thank you. No one's bored of it, I promise. From the studio, back to you.
