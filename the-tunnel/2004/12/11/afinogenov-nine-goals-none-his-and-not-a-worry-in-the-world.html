Afinogenov: Nine goals, none his, and not a worry in the world

After Toronto's 9-2 demolition of Pittsburgh, Maxim Afinogenov talks about going without a point on a night everybody scored, and about where his game sits at 16.6 minutes a night.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelSaturday, 11 December 2004

2:04 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Maxim Afinogenov86 was the rare Toronto Maple Leafs forward to come off the ice without a point in Friday's 9-2 win over Pittsburgh Penguins, the club's 13th straight victory. The 25-year-old winger, a 131-point man last season, is at 17 points through 16 games this year, playing 16.6 minutes a night after skating 22.2 a season ago. Morale, the Bureau notes, sits at 92.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here in the tunnel with Maxim Afinogenov after a 9-2 win over Pittsburgh. Max, thirteen straight now. What is it like in there right now?

Maxim Afinogenov

It is good, you know. Good room, good feeling. Thirteen, yes, but we don't count. We play tonight, we win, tomorrow we work again. That is all it is.

Marcus Bell

Nine goals tonight and the scoresheet is full. Rick Nash gets three. You're one of the few guys who comes off without a point in a game like that. Does that sit anywhere with you?

Maxim Afinogenov

No. Points come, they go. Tonight puck goes to other guys, fine. I play, I skate, we win nine-two. If I score zero and we win, I take this every night. Every night.

Marcus Bell

You've had 7 goals and 10 assists through 16, but the minutes are different. Last year you were at 22.2 a night, this year 16.6. Six minutes less. What's changed in your game to live with that?

Maxim Afinogenov

Is different, yes. But this team, look, so many guys can play. Coach puts four lines, everybody goes. So my minutes, I must make them count. Be dangerous fast. You don't wait, you don't feel the game, you just go, first shift. Maybe this year I am more, uh, straight to the net, because there is no time to wait.

Marcus Bell

Who's out there with you most nights now, and how is that line finding each other?

Maxim Afinogenov

Alex, Mogilny, you know him. He sees ice like nobody. And young guys around, they fly. For me it is easy. I give the puck, I go to space, it comes back. When line广角 works like this, you don't think, you just play.

Marcus Bell

Your book grade from the Bureau: checking 50, toughness 50. Coaches always want that from a winger. Do you hear that, or is that just noise?

Maxim Afinogenov

I hear it, of course. My job, I know what it is. Score, make play, come back, don't lose the man. I am not fighter, everybody knows. But I work on it. Maybe I am better than number says, maybe not. We will see.

Marcus Bell

Last one. The streak, the room on a run like this, what's the one thing you guard against?

Maxim Afinogenov

Soft. You win nine-two, next night you think is easy. Is never easy. December, everybody is tired, and other team, they want to be the one who stops you. So we must play sixty minutes, every game. That is important, more than the streak.

Marcus Bell

Maxim, appreciate the time. Back to you.
