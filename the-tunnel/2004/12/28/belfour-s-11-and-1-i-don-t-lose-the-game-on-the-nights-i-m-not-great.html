Belfour's 11 and 1: 'I don't lose the game on the nights I'm not great'

The 39-year-old goaltender owns the Bureau's consistency grade and explains why 11 wins in 12 starts still says everything.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelTuesday, 28 December 2004

3:06 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Ed Belfour93 took his 11th win in 12 starts for Toronto Maple Leafs on Monday night, a 3-2 overtime road win at Calgary Flames that pushed Toronto to 27-6-0. The 39-year-old goaltender spoke to The Tunnel in the tunnel afterward about what a clean penalty kill buys a man his age, and about a consistency grade of 50 that sits next to the best start of his season.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Ed Belfour, appreciate you stopping. Two on 26 tonight, both in regulation, and your club still finds a way in overtime. Walk me through the two you gave up.

Ed Belfour

Yeah. First one's a man down, they get the setup, quick puck across, I'm... I'm a half beat late across. That's on me. The second one I'm less happy with, even strength, right off the draw. I've got to be better. Two on 26... it'll do. But I've got to be better on that second one.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're at 103 goals against as a club in 36 games. From where you're standing, what does that penalty kill look like night in and night out?

Ed Belfour

It looks like a bunch of guys who don't want to give me a bad night. That's the honest answer. When they kill it clean, I'm just... reading. Tracking. Maybe one save if they find a lane. And I'll take that. At 39, I'll take every clean kill they hand me. Sounds like I'm complaining. I'm not. I love this job. But the PK is my best friend right now.

Jan Kowalczyk

You said it yourself, you don't want to move. Does a two-minute kill with only one save in it actually buy you something physically?

Ed Belfour

Yeah, it does. You're talking about 120 seconds where I'm not throwing my body across the crease. And a game with three kills is six minutes I'm... not. And in December, six minutes of not throwing yourself around adds up. You feel it in the second period of the second night back. So when the guys clean it up, I feel it. You feel it in your hips.

Jan Kowalczyk

Eleven wins in 12 starts. Eleven and one, 39 years old, backstopped a Cup run last spring. What does that number mean to you?

Ed Belfour

It means the one-timer from Mats went in. It means the kid Chlup buried one in overtime. It means 15 guys blocked a puck in front of me. That's what 11 and 1 means. I'm not gonna stand here and tell you I'm some miracle at 39. I'm a goalie in good shape with a team in front of him. And I'm grateful enough to know the difference.

Jan Kowalczyk

The Bureau has your consistency at 50. And you're 11-1. You want to reconcile those two things?

Ed Belfour

You want me to talk about a number on a website? Yeah, fine. I've been inconsistent my whole career. Some nights I'm great, some nights I'm... I'm there. That's the truth of me. That'll never change. But I don't lose the game on the nights I'm not great. And that's the difference this year. The nights I'm average, we still find a way. So the 50's right. I'm inconsistent. And the 11-1's right. I just don't lose when I'm inconsistent.

Jan Kowalczyk

You mentioned the 15 guys in front of you. You've got Aebischer in the room, 83 on the Book. What's it like sharing a crease with him at this stage?

Ed Belfour

It's good. I mean it's good. Bill's earned every minute he's gotten. And look, I don't need to carry the whole load. I don't want to. If he's in there and we're sharp, I'm happy. The old Ed wouldn't have said that. The old Ed would've said 'give me the puck and get out of my way.' But the old Ed's got a hip that talks to him in February.

Jan Kowalczyk

Twenty-seven and six. Five straight. You're 60 points with a half-season left. What do you tell yourself at the rink at ten in the morning?

Ed Belfour

I tell myself I'm too old for this. No, honestly. I tell myself play the game tonight. That's all. You stop looking at the 27-6, you start losing it. I've been in the 27-6 room before and I've watched it come apart in February. So I show up, I work, I let the young guys be young. And I try not to throw my back out at practice.

Jan Kowalczyk

Ed Belfour in Calgary. Back to you.
