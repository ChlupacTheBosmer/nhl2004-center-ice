Sutter on the Fourth Overtime Loss: "The Details Weren't There"

After a 3-2 loss in overtime to the league's best team, Calgary's coach talks about close games, the goaltending, and why 11-20-4 isn't an effort story.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelTuesday, 28 December 2004

3:09 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Calgary Flames lost 3-2 in overtime to Toronto Maple Leafs on Monday night at the Saddledome, their fourth overtime loss this season. The Flames are 11-20-4, 28 points, dead last in the Northwest and the lowest-scoring club in the league at 113 goals for. Head coach Darryl Sutter stayed longer than most at the rinkside mic.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here in the corridor at the Saddledome with Flames head coach Darryl Sutter. Darryl, you just watched that one go to overtime and come out on the wrong side. What did you see out there?

Darryl Sutter

Yeah. I thought we competed. Obviously you want to win, but the compete level was there tonight. They scored, we scored, went to overtime, and they get the puck and we don't. That's, uh, that's hockey.

Jan Kowalczyk

That's four overtime losses now. You're 11-20-4 with four games that went to the extra frame and didn't come your way. Is that a structure problem or is that a results problem?

Darryl Sutter

Well, look, I don't think it's either one, honestly. I think you've got to close. We had a chance tonight in regulation, a couple of chances in the third, and we didn't put one in. When you get to overtime, it's a coin flip. But before it gets to overtime, you need to find a way to put the puck in the net. That's the difference between winning and losing.

Jan Kowalczyk

You gave up 28 shots and you gave them 26. Your goal differential is minus-30 on the season. Where is the structural breakdown happening?

Darryl Sutter

Obviously I'm not going to stand here and dissect it for you on a Monday night. But the short answer is: we let too many second and third chances go. We don't have the puck enough in the other end to keep it out of ours. And when you're 113 for, you're not outscoring people. You know? You need your goalie to win you a game, or you need your top guys to score, and tonight both of those things were right there. They just went the other way.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let's talk about the goaltending then. Roman's started 30 of your 36 games and he's 9 and 19. That's the workload of a number one and the record of a, well, nobody. What does that position look like going forward?

Darryl Sutter

I'm not getting into goalie decisions after a game.

Jan Kowalczyk

...

Darryl Sutter

You know what, fine. The numbers aren't there. I see them every morning, same as you do. Jamie's got 11 games, he's got 2 and 5, that's not great either. Roman's been a hell of a player for a long time and I respect that, but I don't care about the last ten years when the last thirty games look like they do. The job's the job. You've got to play well enough to play. That's all I'm going to say about it.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're in the building Thursday against Mighty Ducks® of Anaheim, then you've got three more at home before Saturday. That's four games in seven nights with the road behind you. Does that change anything?

Darryl Sutter

It changes that we need to get home. That's what I told the boys tonight. We've been on the road too much and it shows in the second and third periods, you can see the legs. Four at home, that's not easy, Anaheim's a tough one, Montreal, Tampa, Minnesota. But we'd rather play 'em here than on a bus.

Jan Kowalczyk

One more thing. The room, by the numbers, has the lowest morale average in the league. You've got a 34-year-old goalie with three years left on a four-point-four contract, a record that isn't going to get better on its own, and you're in December. What do you say to those guys in there?

Darryl Sutter

Well, what I say in the room stays in the room. What I'll tell you is: you don't get out of it by feeling sorry. You get out of it by competing harder than the other team for sixty minutes. That's the whole thing. I've seen it from the other side, I've coached on teams that didn't want to be there, and the one thing that matters is the compete level. And tonight, the compete level was there. That's the thing I can live with. The rest, the standings, the numbers, the contracts, I don't care about any of that until the puck drops.

Jan Kowalczyk

Darryl Sutter, head coach of the Calgary Flames. Thank you, Darryl.

Darryl Sutter

Thank you, Jan.
