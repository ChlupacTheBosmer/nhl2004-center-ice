Sundin and the Quiet Ones: 11 Straight and He Didn't Score

The Toronto captain went pointless for the first time in this stretch, credited the young guys in front of him, and told Marcus Bell the winning streak has nothing to do with him.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelTuesday, 7 December 2004

1:24 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Mats Sundin89 sat at the end of the stall in the visiting dressing room at the Islanders' rink, jersey still damp, 0 points on the sheet. His Toronto Maple Leafs had just won 4-0 for an 11th straight, 42 points on the season, 76 goals against in 26 games. He is 33. The average age in that room is 25.6.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here with Mats Sundin in the Toronto dressing room. Four-nothing on the road, 11 straight. How do you take that one, Mats?

Mats Sundin

I think we played good. Everybody was, you know, everybody was in it tonight. First period we were not, not great. We took a while to get the legs going. But after, after that we were fine. The team played good.

Marcus Bell

You yourself were held off the scoresheet tonight. No goals, no assists. Does that sit strange, when you're used to carrying the box score?

Mats Sundin

I don't, uh, I don't think of it like that. No. I had some looks. Maybe I could have, I could have put one in. But it doesn't matter. For sure it doesn't matter if I score or if Marian scores or if Richards scores. We got the two points. That is the thing.

Marcus Bell

You missed some time before this one, a little over a week. You look like a man who's been back for a month. How's the body today?

Mats Sundin

It's fine. I feel good. Of course, you want your legs to be fresh, but I feel good. No, it's fine. I'm happy to be out there, for sure.

Marcus Bell

One more question and then I'll let you go. Seventeen men on that Toronto roster are 24 or younger. You're 33, you're the oldest. What does a room like that look like from the captain's chair?

Mats Sundin

The captain's chair. I think, I think people make too much of that. They are good players. Gaborik, Richards, the young guys on the back end. They know what to do. Of course sometimes I say something to, you know, Spacek, or maybe Eminger, a little thing. But they don't need me. They are good. I am just, I am just trying to play my game and let them play theirs.

Marcus Bell

Mats, thank you for your time. Back to you.
