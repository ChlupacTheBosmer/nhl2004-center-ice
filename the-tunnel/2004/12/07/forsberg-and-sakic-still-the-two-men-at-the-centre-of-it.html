Forsberg and Sakic, still the two men at the centre of it

One leads the league in scoring, the other is six weeks from his season and sitting in the studio talking about the first one, and neither of them sounds like he is running out of runway.

Jenna KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelTuesday, 7 December 2004

4:53 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Peter Forsberg89 has 55 points in 26 games for Colorado Avalanche this season, second in the league behind Bill Guerin87 and 3 behind his own pace from 2003-04 when he posted 101 points in 82. His partner at the centre of everything in Colorado, Joe Sakic84, has 19 points in 10 games and a neck that keeps him out until at least December 17. They sat down together at the studio desk for ten minutes.

Transcript

Jenna Kowalczyk

Peter, Joe, thank you both for sitting down. Jenna Kowalczyk at the desk. Peter, you're at 55 points in 26 games. You're three behind Bill Guerin in the Art Ross race. Does that number feel right to you, or does it feel like it's still early?

Peter Forsberg

It feels... I think it is good. Twenty-six games, yes, it is early but we are playing good hockey and I am getting the chances. For sure it is better than not getting them.

Joe Sakic

He's been doing that all fall. You know, the guy doesn't stop. I sit over here on the bench and watch him make those plays and I still... it hasn't gotten boring yet.

Jenna Kowalczyk

Joe, let me ask about the neck. When are we looking at you back in the lineup?

Joe Sakic

The fifteenth. That's the date they're giving me, December the fifteenth. So a couple more weeks, no rush. I'm doing the stuff on the side, the exercises, the light work, but you know, the neck is a funny one. You can't cheat it.

Peter Forsberg

I think he will be ready. He is always ready before... before we think he is.

Joe Sakic

Thanks, Pete. That's very reassuring.

Jenna Kowalczyk

You two have been at the centre of this franchise since Quebec. I look at the room and you're thirty-five and thirty-one. Is this still a young team's locker room or is it your room?

Joe Sakic

Well, obviously we're not the young guys anymore, you know? But look, we've got six kids in there twenty-four or under. Average age of the room is 28.1, that's dead league average. So it's not... it's not a retirement home up here.

Peter Forsberg

I agree with Joe. We have young players, yes, and they are... of course they are learning. But the room is not old. I think people look at Joe and me and they forget there are twenty-seven other men in there.

Jenna Kowalczyk

Peter, you played 82 games last season, 101 points, and you were on the ice for every one of those playoff runs to the final. This year you're at 20.0 minutes a night, 26 games in. Is the load different now?

Peter Forsberg

It is... it is similar. Maybe slightly less, yes. But I think that is the same as last year. 19.8, last year, almost the same. So no, I am not... I am not resting. That is not what is happening.

Joe Sakic

The Book's got him at 89 and his best is 97. People want to write that the wheels are coming off. They're not. The guy's passing me pucks every night like he's thirty-one years old.

Jenna Kowalczyk

You're thirty-five.

Joe Sakic

I know! That's the point! I'm thirty-five and I can't match him and I've had four years on him, you know. That's a little embarrassing.

Jenna Kowalczyk

Peter, you want to respond to that?

Peter Forsberg

No. Joe is being nice. I mean, Joe was here first. I came in on a trade, yes, and he was already the captain. I think I just... I was trying to not make him look bad. For the first two or three years.

Joe Sakic

Oh, is that what we're calling it?

Jenna Kowalczyk

The Lindros trade brought you to Quebec in '94, Peter. Do you remember walking into that room and seeing Joe for the first time as a teammate?

Peter Forsberg

I remember I was... nervous. A little. He was the captain, he was already a star, and I was... twenty years old, yes, still a kid really. But he was good to me from the first day. That is what I remember.

Joe Sakic

You wouldn't shut up. That's what I remember. You'd come to the bench and talk about everything. The ice, the stick, the food in the arena, the... I couldn't get rid of you.

Peter Forsberg

Yes, I was... I had many questions. I think that is what young players do.

Jenna Kowalczyk

Markus Näslund's in Vancouver now, 49 points in 26, same as Zigmund Palffy. You and Markus grew up ten days apart in the same town. Do you watch those numbers?

Peter Forsberg

I do, yes. I speak to him... sometimes. Not about hockey. But yes, I see his numbers. He is doing good. 49 points, that is... that is not bad for a man from Örnsköldsvik.

Joe Sakic

You two were gonna say 'a man from Örnsköldsvik' like that town produced forty of you, you know?

Peter Forsberg

It did produce one other, yes. For sure that is enough.

Jenna Kowalczyk

Joe, last season you put up 102 points in 64 games. You've got 19 in 10 this year, and you've been in the lineup for ten of the last week or so. When you get back, does the team look different?

Joe Sakic

It should. I think... you know, you watch from the side and the guys are finding ways, they're winning, but there's an element missing when I'm not out there. And I don't mean that as a compliment to myself. I mean, the structure isn't the same without a body down the middle who knows what Pete's gonna do before Pete does it. Obviously nobody knows what Pete's gonna do before Pete does it.

Peter Forsberg

Joe knows, though. Joe knows. He is sitting there and he... I can see him on the bench, sometimes, he moves his stick to where I am going to pass. He cannot see it, I think, but his body knows.

Jenna Kowalczyk

That's the nicest thing anyone's said about you this month, Joe.

Joe Sakic

I'm saving that quote. Framed it. Going over the mantle in Burnaby.

Jenna Kowalczyk

Peter, you've got two years left on the deal, $9.90. Joe, four years, $9.40. Are you two thinking about how this ends?

Peter Forsberg

No. I am thinking about tonight. And tomorrow. That is... that is how I have always done it. Two years is not short, but I do not look at it.

Joe Sakic

Four more years. I'm gonna be thirty-nine. I'll be the oldest thing on the ice and some kid's gonna cross-check me and I'll just... that's fine. That's the job.

Peter Forsberg

You will not be the oldest. They will find someone older. They always do.

Joe Sakic

See, that's what I'm afraid of.

Jenna Kowalczyk

Peter Forsberg, Joe Sakic. Thank you both. Back to you.
