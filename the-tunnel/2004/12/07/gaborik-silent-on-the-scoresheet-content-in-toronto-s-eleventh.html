Gaborik silent on the scoresheet, content in Toronto's eleventh

The new right winger posted zero points in a 4-0 road win and says the young room is the reason Toronto is winning, not the reason they will stall.

Jenna KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelTuesday, 7 December 2004

2:24 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Marian Gaborik95 had no points in Toronto Maple Leafs's 4-0 win at New York Islanders on Sunday, his first quiet night since joining the club on 2004-12-01. Toronto is 19-5-0 and riding an 11-game winning streak with 42 points and a league-best 76 goals allowed. Gaborik is the third-youngest man on a 32-player roster that averages 25.6 years old.

Jenna Kowalczyk

Jenna Kowalczyk here in the tunnel at Nassau Coliseum. Marian Gaborik, forty zero, eleventh straight, the whole building just filed out. You don't have a point in that one, how does that feel?

Marian Gaborik

Feels good, you know? We win, that is the thing. I look at scoresheet, zero zero, okay, I say fine. The team, the team win and that is what matter today.

Jenna Kowalczyk

You've got twenty goals in twenty-six games this year, so people notice the quiet nights. Is there pressure to fill the column every night at a club like this?

Marian Gaborik

No, no. Here is... how you say... the room, the room is different. Everybody here can score, you know? I look, Mats, Alex, Gary, all of them can put it in. So I don't think, okay I must do it every night. I think, who is open tonight, who is the man.

Jenna Kowalczyk

You came in December first, so about a week in this dressing room. What was the first thing you noticed?

Marian Gaborik

Young. Everybody say, Toronto, Toronto, big market, veterans, but you look at the room, we are young. Seventeen of us twenty-four or under. I'm twenty-two. Spezza is, what, twenty-one? So many young guys. I think that is why we win eleven straight, because everybody is fast, everybody hungry, you know. We not sit on our, how you say, our resume.

Jenna Kowalczyk

You lead the team in goals and points this season with thirty-four. That's a lot of weight on a twenty-two-year-old in his first week in a new city.

Marian Gaborik

I don't think of it like that. I think of the game, that's all. I get the puck, I go. My speed is my game, everybody know. The Development Index, plus six, okay, fine, but I don't read that stuff. I play hockey. In Trencin we play hockey, here we play hockey. I just... I run and I shoot and the guys make me better, how you say, around me.

Jenna Kowalczyk

Eleven straight. You've won four shutouts in that stretch, thirty-six goals scored against nine allowed. Do you feel it in there, that this thing is special?

Marian Gaborik

I feel the games. I feel every shift, you know? I don't... I think, we play good, we win, tomorrow we practice, we play next. Eleven is big, yes. But I play twenty-six games and twenty is wins already, so I think, okay, now we must do it in April. That is when it count. You know what I mean?

Jenna Kowalczyk

I do. One more, and then you get on that bus. You got a year left on that contract, $1.1 million, and you're twenty-two with thirty-four points in twenty-six games. Do you think about the future here, or is the present enough?

Marian Gaborik

You want me talk contract in the tunnel? I think about next game. That is all I think about. $1.1 million, okay, I sign it, it's done. I play. I go. My future is Tuesday when we practice. That is my future.

Jenna Kowalczyk

Marian Gaborik, thank you. Enjoy the ride up the parkway. Back to you.
