Sundin sits quiet, the line still scores

Back in the lineup after a short absence, the captain had no points in a 7-1 win and said the game slowed down once he let Gaborik and Richards work.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelMonday, 20 December 2004

3:17 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Mats Sundin88 was 0-for-1 on the scoresheet in Toronto Maple Leafs's 7-1 road win at Tampa Bay Lightning on Sunday, his first game back after missing time with a lower-body issue that kept him out since the week before. TOR is 25-6-0, 54 points, and Sundin carries 44 points in 33 games at 17.9 minutes a night.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here in the tunnel at St. Pete. Mats, welcome back. First game in, what, six or seven days? How did it feel out there tonight?

Mats Sundin

Thank you, Jan. Yeah, it was, it was nice to be out there. The first shift, you know, you do not know how the legs are, so you go easy, you find your spacing. By the second period I think I was okay. The legs were there.

Jan Kowalczyk

You did not put a point up. Does that sit strange, when the line scores around you and you are in the middle of it?

Mats Sundin

Well, I watched tonight. The goals, they came, and I was happy for the guys. Marian, Rick, Brad, they all scored. That is the job. I do not need to touch the puck for it to be a good night. For sure it is better when I contribute, but... no. The team won seven-one on the road. That is what matters.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let me ask about the line then. You have been with those guys a while, but you have not been out there with them the last week. How do you find each other again? Is it automatic after one shift or does it take a period?

Mats Sundin

It takes, I think, two periods. You know, in the first period I am still thinking too much. Where do I go, where does Marian want the puck, does Brad want it high or deep. By the second period, you stop thinking. You just read. Marian goes, so you follow, or you go the other way. Brad moves the puck quick, so you find the open ice. It comes back fast when the players are good, you know. The good players, they find you.

Jan Kowalczyk

Gaborik told Marcus tonight that the coach is keeping his shifts short. Nineteen minutes, come off, breathe, go again. Does that change what you do up the middle when he is that fresh every time he comes over the board?

Mats Sundin

It changes it, yes. Because if Marian is fresh, he is going all the time. He does not wait for you. So I must be... I must already be there when he arrives. If I am slow to the dot, if I am one beat late, he is already gone. So I play a little more ahead of the play tonight. I think. The coach wants us quick, and that is fine. I like it.

Jan Kowalczyk

TOR is 10-3 on the road right now, you are 25-6 overall, and December does not slow down. What does this stretch ask of a room like this, where half the men are still learning how to play on a bus in late December?

Mats Sundin

I think it asks for the same thing every night. You do not get tired in December. You get tired when you think about how many games are left. You just play the one. Tonight we played well, the structure was there, the first period was good. That is all you can do. The young guys, they are fine. They listen, they work. They do not ask me how many games are left. They ask if we practise tomorrow. That is good.

Jan Kowalczyk

Last year you were at 116, 23 minutes a night. This year 44 in 33, about 18 minutes. The coach is using four lines, spreading it. Does that feel different from your side of the board, less time, more rest, more urgency per shift?

Mats Sundin

It is different, yes. 17 or 18 minutes, every shift counts more. You do not get the long shift where you feel the game, you know, you get five or six, maybe ten seconds in and you must be there already. It suits me. I am 33, my legs are good but they are not what they were at 25. Shorter shifts, more speed, that is... that is honest. I am fine with it.

Jan Kowalczyk

One more. You came back tonight, quiet night, the team still won by six. When you go to the video room tomorrow, what are you looking for in your own game?

Mats Sundin

I am looking for the first period. I think I was a little back on my heels. Not bad, not a mistake, just... not sharp enough in the first five minutes. After that I was okay. The legs needed the warm-up, maybe. The ice here is a little different. So I look at that. The rest, I watch what the other lines do and I try to help them tomorrow in practice.

Jan Kowalczyk

Mats Sundin, back after the injury, no points, seven-one win. Thank you. Back to you.
