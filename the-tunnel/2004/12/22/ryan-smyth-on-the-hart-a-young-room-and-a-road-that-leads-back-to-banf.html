Ryan Smyth on the Hart, a Young Room, and a Road That Leads Back to Banff

The Hart Trophy winner talks about leading without a letter, about Mike Comrie, and about what his mother says when he calls home from a hotel in Columbus.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelWednesday, 22 December 2004

7:04 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Ryan Smyth77 won the Hart Trophy last season after a 163-point year that nobody saw coming from a man the league had already written down. Now he sits in a room at Edmonton Oilers where 12 of 34 players are 24 or younger, with one year left on his contract and a Development Index that has him one tick below his base. He sat down with The Tunnel's Marcus Bell in the studio.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here with Ryan Smyth, and Ryan, thank you for sitting down with me. I want to start with the obvious, but the profile ran yesterday and I think the title tells the story. A Trophy on the Shelf. Where is the Hart right now, actually?

Ryan Smyth

Yeah, thanks Marcus. Uh, it's in the basement. My mother's got it in a closet in Banff, actually. She, uh, she doesn't want the boys at school seeing it and saying, oh, well, that's what Ryan does. She says it belongs to everybody who drove me to the rink at six in the morning, and I can't argue with that. So it lives in a closet in a house I grew up in. That's probably, that's probably the right place for it.

Marcus Bell

That's a good story. But it changed something, didn't it? You won the Hart, you scored 163 points, the league called you the best player. Does the room look at you different?

Ryan Smyth

No. I mean, not really, no. The room's got 34 guys in it and most of them, uh, they don't care about your trophy case. They care about whether you're going to the net on the power play, whether you're back-checking, whether you're the guy who blocks a shot at 18 minutes when your legs are gone. And that, that's what I've done my whole career, you know. The Hart's nice, obviously it's nice, but Comrie's still the one who steals my coffee and Yorky still doesn't listen to a word I say in the room. So nothing's different.

Marcus Bell

You just mentioned Mike Comrie. He's 24, he's got 58 points this year, he's running your line. What's the relationship between the Hart Trophy winner and the guy who might take the trophy himself in three years?

Ryan Smyth

Well, I hope he does. I really do. Because, you know, Mike, he's, he's a better player than I was at 24. He's got the, the hands, he sees the ice, and he, he works. The thing is, I don't, I don't stand over him. He's 24, he's a man, he knows his game. I just, I tell him, hey, here's what I do on the half-wall, here's where I want the puck, and we sort it out on the ice. That's, that's the whole thing. I'm not his coach. I'm his left winger.

Marcus Bell

You've been in Edmonton since you were 19. You were drafted sixth overall in 1994 by this club, you made your debut in January of 1995 against LA. Ten years in the same building. What does that do to a man when he walks into a room where 12 of the 34 are 24 or younger?

Ryan Smyth

You know, I think about that sometimes, actually. I walked in here and I was a kid, and the guys on that team, they, they didn't care who I was either. I was just the young guy who had to earn it. And now I'm the guy who's been here the longest, more or less, and the young guys, they look at me and they see, uh, they see ten years of, of staying. That's it. That's the whole thing. You stay. You come in, you work, you stay. And eventually you're the guy they ask, uh, Ryan, what do I do on the second shift in the third period? And I tell them what I know. That's the job.

Marcus Bell

You don't wear the C. You're clearly the leader of that room. Does that bother you at all?

Ryan Smyth

No. It doesn't. It never has. I've played with guys who wore the C and it didn't, it didn't make them leaders. And I've played with guys who never wore anything and they were the, the loudest voice in the room. The letter's a letter. You can wear a C and say nothing worth hearing. I just, I play hard, I talk when it matters, and I, I don't, I don't try to be somebody I'm not. That's, that's not the kind of guy who leads, you know? The guys follow the play, not the patch.

Marcus Bell

You mentioned your mother keeping the trophy in Banff. Your family, your parents, they drove you to the rink at six in the morning. Do they come to the games?

Ryan Smyth

Not enough. They, they drive down from Banff when they can. My dad, he's not, he's not a big arena guy, you know, he'd rather be on a mountain or a river. But my mom, she comes when she can, and she sits there with, uh, a program and a thermos and she watches the whole game like it's a play. She doesn't miss a shift. And she, she'll call me after, whether we win or lose, and she says, 'Ry, you looked tired tonight, did you eat?' That's, that's the Hart Trophy mother. She doesn't say a word about the goals. She says, did you eat, and, and did you sleep.

Marcus Bell

You're 28, you've been on the road since you were 19. December's the grind of the year, you're 33 games in. What's the thing that gets old after ten years of this?

Ryan Smyth

The hotels. You know, people think it's the hockey, but it's the hotels. You walk into the same room in Columbus and Pittsburgh and St. Louis, same carpet, same ice machine down the hall that doesn't work right, same, same little Bible in the drawer. You've seen it ten, eleven times. And, and then you call home and your mother's asking about dinner and you're standing in a room that looks exactly like the room you stood in three weeks ago in Nashville. And you think, I'd give a lot to be in Banff right now. Not the mountains, just, just the house. The kitchen. The dog. That's, that's the thing that gets old. Not the hockey. Never the hockey.

Marcus Bell

Let me ask you about the numbers, because they've been part of the conversation all season. You put up 163 last year. This year you're at 54 through 33 games, which projects to 134. The Development Index has you one tick below your base. Do you see that drop the same way?

Ryan Smyth

I see it, yeah. I know the numbers. I know where I am. But, you know, last year was, was a, a special year. Everything went in. The puck found me, the power play was clicking, I had a, a run where the game just, just opened up. And this year, it's, it's not that it's bad, 134 over 82 would've made me happy for a long time, you know? It's just that, that last year was the, was the exception. I know that. I've been here ten years, I've had the highs and the lows, and I know that 163 is, is not who I am every single year. I'm still scoring, I'm still, I'm still getting there. The game's a little tighter, teams pay more attention now, and that's, that's fine. I'd rather have 134 and win than 163 and miss the dance.

Marcus Bell

One year left on your contract. $2.50 million. You were an arbitration case before you signed it, back in August of 2003. What goes through a man's head when he's in the last year and the room is young and the team's trying to figure itself out?

Ryan Smyth

You know, I try not to think about it. I really do. I've been in Edmonton my whole career, I don't want to leave, I don't, I don't think about other jerseys. But, you know, it's, it's in the back of your mind. You're 28, you're, you're in your prime, and you want to be somewhere for a long time. And the, the kids in that room, I love them, but, but they're not going to be the same team in four years. Some of them are going to be gone. And you think about, do I want to be here when they figure it out, or do I want to be here when it's already figured out? I don't have an answer for that yet. Ask me in April.

Marcus Bell

I'll do that. One more thing. You broke Wayne Gretzky's team record for the fastest hat trick. Two minutes and one second against San Jose. In Edmonton, how do you live with the ghosts of that building?

Ryan Smyth

You don't. You just, you stop counting banners. I mean, it's, it's real. You walk into that building and you see the numbers up there and you think, those guys, they did something, they did something that hasn't been done since. And I've been here ten years and we haven't, haven't been back. So, you carry it, I guess. You carry it and you try to, to pull the jersey over it. And, and maybe that hat trick, maybe that was the one night where, where I wasn't thinking about Gretzky, I was just thinking about the puck. And that's, that's the best night I've had in that building. And it still, it still bugs me that we lost.

Marcus Bell

Ryan Smyth, thank you very much for sitting down with us.

Ryan Smyth

Thank you, Marcus. Tell the folks to be good to each other.

Marcus Bell

We will. Back to you.
