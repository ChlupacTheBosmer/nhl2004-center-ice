Hasek's geometry: the goalie who tells Lidstrom when to breathe

After the fifth straight, Dominik Hasek on the read that takes him to the faceoff circle, the kill he says belongs to the defensemen, and what he sees from the only seat in the building where you watch both power plays at once.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelWednesday, 22 December 2004

3:34 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Dominik Hasek92 won his 16th of 27 starts, stopping 21 of 24 shots in Detroit Red Wings's 5-3 win over Dallas Stars on 2004-12-19. The Red Wings are 15-10-4 on the season, riding a five-game win streak, 13-5 at home. Hasek, 39, sat down in the corridor with Marcus Bell.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Dominik, Marcus Bell here, post-game. Fifth straight, three on twenty-four, a clean win. You had to be sharp in there tonight.

Dominik Hasek

Yeah. Well. Three is three, you know. But, uh, the boys score five, so, it is, how you say, the game is open. I see more ice. That is the thing people don't get. When we score, the other team, they open up, and I see the play before it is there. The first one tonight, it is from the point, quick release, I see it, I make it. The second, there is a body in front. Three goals, twenty-four shots. It is not, it is not a night I go home and think about.

Marcus Bell

There was a sequence in the second, rush up the middle, and you were out at the edge of the circle. That's a long way from your crease. Was that a read you'd made on video?

Dominik Hasek

It is not instinct. People say, oh, Hasek, he go crazy out there. No, no. I see the D, he is on his back, he cannot get across. So the man, he think the net is big. I come. The net is not big. It is geometry. You know? The angle is smaller. He must go around and around is, is slower. Nicklas, he know. I go, he cover the slot. We do it together. Many, many practice. It is not, how you say, a stunt.

Marcus Bell

You see more than anyone in that building, the penalty kill. You see the open man, the gap, the cross-seam. Does the D hear you back there?

Dominik Hasek

No. No, they don't hear. The crowd, they are loud, and I am yelling, 'the man, the man,' and nobody... That is the thing about the kill. Everybody say the goalie is the hero. No. The D, they make the kill. Body between, stick on the puck, clear the zone. If I make the save and then the point, he still has the room for the next one, that is not my save. That is the save I have to make again. So you see, it is five guys in front. They shoot the puck. I just, how you say, clean the mess.

Marcus Bell

You said the game opens up when they score. Nicklas Lidstrom got an assist tonight. Does he join the rush on a night like this more than, say, on a one-nil night?

Dominik Hasek

Big, yes. You know, when it is zero-zero, Nicklas, he stay home. He is careful. That is his game, you know, he is always right. But when we are two up, three up, he carry. He join the rush and he is... he is fast. For a big man, he is fast. And I see the whole ice because I know he is there. When he join the play and the D get caught, I see the two-on-one before the center even think about it. So I can, how you say, come early. Be where the puck is going. When Nicklas is at home, I worry. When Nicklas is up there, I play.

Marcus Bell

They are nineteen and eleven, Dallas. Good team. Did they throw something at you tonight that another club doesn't?

Dominik Hasek

They shoot from the outside. That is their thing, you know. They carry in, they set the man, quick shot from the point. And from here, it is not bad. I see it, I read it. I like it, actually. The one I don't like is the close one. The man right here, with the stick and the body and the screen on top, that shot, you cannot see it. From the point, I know where it go. The point is open, yes, but I read the shoulder. I like the point. I am happy with the point.

Marcus Bell

One last one. You watch your own power play from behind the net too. Does seeing it from back there give you something on the next shift when you're on the other side of it?

Dominik Hasek

You know what, yes. A little. I see how they set up. The one-time, the cross-seam, the one where the man fake and the puck go the other way. And then on the kill, I see the same thing. The same play. So maybe, you know, maybe the next time I know a little more. It is the best seat in the building. The only seat where you watch both power plays and you get paid for it.

Marcus Bell

Dominik Hasek, thank you. Marcus Bell, back to you.
