Komisarek: "I'm not the guy I was at Michigan anymore, and that's the problem"

The 23-year-old defenseman who put up 82 points on a 21-win Montreal Canadiens® club talks about the jump, the contract year ahead, and what waits after the last shift.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelSunday, 29 May 2005

5:04 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Mike Komisarek87 finished the 2004-05 season with 82 points in 82 games at 30.4 minutes a night for Montreal Canadiens, a club that won 21 games. The Bureau's Development Index has him up 6, his Book grade at 82, his potential at 92. He sits down in the studio with Jan Kowalczyk to talk about the difference between five years ago and tonight.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here with Mike Komisarek. Mike, first time on this desk, so let me start simple. Eighty-two points, eighty-two games, thirty minutes a night, twenty-three years old. On a team that won 21 games. What is your role now, and what was it five years ago?

Mike Komisarek

Five years ago I was, uh, finishing my freshman year at Michigan and trying not to get chewed out by my coach for losing a puck along the boards. You know, I was 18. Now I'm, yeah. Thirty minutes a night on a team that needs you to touch the puck on every line because nobody else is doing it. That's the difference. The role got bigger because, well, the room around it got smaller.

Jan Kowalczyk

You mentioned Michigan. You were at Michigan two seasons, put up 46 points in 80 games, won a CCHA title. Did you look like this player then, even a little?

Mike Komisarek

Not even a little. At Michigan I was a, I was a hitter who could move a little. Forty-six points in two years, that's not a point man. I came in here and for a while I was still that. Twenty-four games last year, three points, eleven minutes a night. I was the extra guy, you know? The call-up who sits third pairing and gets happy with a block.

Jan Kowalczyk

And then this season you go from 11.8 minutes to 30.4. That's a full shift more every period, roughly. Where did the legs come from?

Mike Komisarek

The legs came from, yeah, the legs came from summer work. Look, I'm not going to stand here and tell you I figured something out. I went from eleven to thirty and the first month I was gassed. I could feel my legs at the end of the third and I was like, okay, you've got to be better. I just, you know, I put in the work in August and September. The minutes were there because the club needed them and I just, I had to make them work.

Jan Kowalczyk

Thirty minutes a night at twenty-three. Do you feel like the old guys, like the guys who've done this for ten years? Because you're on your third season and you're already the guy they lean on.

Mike Komisarek

No. No, definitely not. The old guys, they've got a rhythm, you know? They know when to back off. I don't back off because I'm scared if I back off somebody scores on my shift. Andrei, Markov, he's right there next to me and he makes it look easy, the twenty-five, twenty-six minutes he eats. I'm watching him all season trying to figure out how he does it without dying. I think the answer is he just, he just knows more. He reads it faster.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let me ask you something a little different. You're twenty-three, morale at 94 on the Bureau's scale, and you just played on a team that won 21 games. How does that sit?

Mike Komisarek

It's, you know, you don't choose that. You wake up in October and you're playing a game and the building's half-full and your team's down three and you just, you play the next shift. You can't think about the standings every night or you'll, you'll lose it. Personally I'm happy with my season. I don't want to be one of those guys who puts up a number on a bad team and, and pretends that's winning. It isn't winning. But I can, I can be honest that I grew as a player.

Jan Kowalczyk

And your role specifically. The coach, what does he draw up for you now versus what you were doing two seasons ago?

Mike Komisarek

Two seasons ago I was, I was the big defenseman. Go down in the corner, separate the guy from the puck, get the puck to someone who can do something with it. Now, now I've got to be the first pass, the second pass, sometimes the first shot. I'm shooting 87 on the power play, I'm, you know, the coach wants me walking the blue line and finding the seam. It's a different job. I spent a whole year being a, a wrecking ball and now he's asking me to be a quarterback.

Jan Kowalczyk

Does that worry you? The Development Index has you up six, your Book grade is 82 with a base of 77, potential at 92. Everyone in this league looks at that and says he's got a long way to go. Do you look at it the same way?

Mike Komisarek

I know where I am. I'm 82 today. That's where I sit. I know I've got, I've got things to fix, obviously, and the room knows I've got things to fix. But I think the thing that gets missed is, you know, I went from a 77 to an 82 in one season. The, the jump from the 77 to wherever the 92 is, that's not something I can just, I can't promise that. Nobody can. I just play and hope the numbers follow.

Jan Kowalczyk

One year left on your contract. $1.10 million. You know what you're worth now, do you?

Mike Komisarek

You're not gonna get me to say a number, Jan. I'll tell you this. I'm happy right now. I'm twenty-three, I'm playing thirty minutes a night, the room knows me, I'm not looking past October. Whatever happens with the contract, that's a conversation for a different room.

Jan Kowalczyk

So let's go forward in a different direction. When the playing stops, when you're done with this, what do you see? You're from West Islip, you grew up on Long Island, you went to Michigan, you went to the NHL draft. Is there a road home?

Mike Komisarek

There's always a road home. I played Suffolk PAL when I was a kid, you know, before St. Anthony's, before any of this. I think, I think if I'm honest, the part of this I like most is the room. The bus, the rink, the guys. I think I'd want to stay in it. Whether that's coaching, whether that's development, whether that's, I don't know, scouting. I'd go back to the Junior Bureau. But I don't know the shape of it yet.

Jan Kowalczyk

You coached nobody, did you? At Michigan you were the player.

Mike Komisarek

No, I was the guy getting coached. I was, I was one of the loud ones actually, I was, I'd help the younger guys in the weight room and stuff. But that's not, that's not coaching. I don't know if I'd be good at it. I think the, the patience part, the part where you explain the same thing for the, the fortieth time, that might be where I fall short.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're the loudest guy in the room though, right? On the bench, you're the one making noise.

Mike Komisarek

On the bench, yeah. Yeah, I'm, I'm loud. But there's a difference between yelling on the bench and teaching a nineteen-year-old why his first step is wrong. I think I'd be the yelling one, not the patient one. You know what I mean?

Jan Kowalczyk

One last thing. The potential grade says 92. The base is 77. You're at 82 today. Is there a version of this that, that doesn't plateau? That keeps going?

Mike Komisarek

I hope so. Honestly, I hope so. Because I don't feel like I've peaked. I feel like I just, you know, I just got to thirty minutes and I haven't even figured out how to be good at thirty minutes yet. Let alone whatever comes after. So yeah. I'm not, I'm not scared of the ceiling. I just want to see what's above the next step.

Jan Kowalczyk

Mike Komisarek, thank you very much for your time. Appreciate it.

Mike Komisarek

Thank you, Jan.
