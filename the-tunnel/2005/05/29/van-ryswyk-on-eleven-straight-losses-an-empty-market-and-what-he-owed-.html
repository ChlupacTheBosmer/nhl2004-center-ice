Van Ryswyk on eleven straight losses, an empty market, and what he owed the coach

The Florida GM sits down ten days before the draft and explains why his club let a deadline pass, why 16 overtime losses are the number that keeps him up, and why the $13.64M profit line does not comfort him.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelSunday, 29 May 2005

3:26 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Florida Panthers finished the 2004-05 season 25-36-16 with 76 points, dead last in the Southeast, and closed with 11 straight losses. General manager Boris Van Ryswyk sat down with Marcus Bell in the studio to talk about a deadline that came and went, a coach who never had a roster that could close a game, and a draft ten days away.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here with Boris Van Ryswyk in the studio. Boris, eleven straight losses to end a season. What did that look like from the front office? You were watching that unravel.

Boris Van Ryswyk

It looked like every other game we'd been in all year, which is the problem. We were right there. We were right there eleven times in a row and we couldn't put the thing away. You watch the last ten minutes and it's the same thing over and over, we can't close. That's not a system issue, that's not a coaching issue. That's the players on the ice not having that last bite.

Marcus Bell

Sixteen overtime losses. That's the most in the league by a wide margin. You were the closest team in hockey that couldn't win.

Boris Van Ryswyk

Sixteen. Yeah. And look, people want to frame that as, oh, they were competitive, they were building something. No. Losing in overtime means you got to the promised land and you couldn't cash the check. Every single one of those games was winnable. Sixteen games that should have been sixteen points, or thirty-two points. That's the difference between 76 and 92, 94. That's a playoff team's number. We were a playoff team that couldn't finish.

Marcus Bell

The deadline came and went, seventy-one days ago now. The record shows nothing from Florida. No incoming, no outgoing. Was that a decision to stand pat, or was it a case of not finding what you needed?

Boris Van Ryswyk

Both. Here's the thing. When the deadline hits, I've got a choice. I go for a rental, I give up a pick, a young body, and I get somebody for twenty games who may or may not make a difference. With the group I had, I didn't think one piece gets us into that playoff spot. We were already out. And I've got $51.07M on the books. If I start mortgaging futures for a rental at 76 points, what am I really doing? I'm not fixing anything. I'm just spending more.

Marcus Bell

But you were making $13.64M. The gate is there, 14,971 a night. You had the room to add, and the free-agent pool was empty. So the only way to get a player was to take one off another club's list.

Boris Van Ryswyk

And I know that. I know the pool is empty. Everybody knows the pool is empty. It's not a secret. But here's what the profit line tells me. It tells me I'm spending right, relatively. $51.07M for 76 points is, you look at it, that's not efficient. I know that. $0.67M per point. I'd like that to be $0.40. But I can't just throw money at a roster that doesn't have the closing piece. Where does the extra go? A winger who's good but not good enough? That doesn't fix sixteen overtime losses.

Marcus Bell

Talk to me about the coach. He was behind that bench for eleven straight. Did he have what he needed, or is the roster the excuse?

Boris Van Ryswyk

The roster is not the excuse, it's the reason. I'm not going to put that on him. I'm not. The system was fine. The structure was fine. We had enough structure to get to the third period of every game, and we didn't have enough structure to close them. That's a talent problem. You give a coach a team that can't kill a five-on-five late, you're asking him to coach something that isn't there. That's my job, not his.

Marcus Bell

The draft is ten days out. What are you looking for? You've got to add somebody who can close.

Boris Van Ryswyk

I'm looking for the same thing every team at the bottom of the standings is looking for. Somebody who makes the other team's life miserable in the last ten minutes. I don't know if that's in the draft. Honestly, I don't know. Maybe it's two or three drafts away. That's the honest answer and I think the fans deserve that. I can't sit here and tell you the sixth pick fixes eleven straight losses. It probably doesn't. But it starts. You build from where you are.

Boris Van Ryswyk

Thank you, Marcus.
