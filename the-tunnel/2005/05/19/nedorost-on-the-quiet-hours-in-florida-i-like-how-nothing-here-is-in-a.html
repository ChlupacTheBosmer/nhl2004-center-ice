Nedorost on the quiet hours in Florida: 'I like how nothing here is in a hurry'

The 23-year-old center, up six on the Bureau's Development Index and playing 13.4 minutes a night for the first time, talks about the city, the leg that cost him six weeks, and what a day off looks like when you are far from Ceske Budejovice.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelThursday, 19 May 2005

4:22 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Vaclav Nedorost84 came off the bench for 7 games last season at 7.1 minutes a night. This year he played 69, at 13.4, and the Bureau's Development Index has him up six from a base of 75. At 23, the man who was the 14th pick of the 2000 draft says his road through Colorado and Hershey was not wasted time. He sat down with Jan Kowalczyk of The Tunnel to talk about where he lives and what he does when the hockey stops.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here with Vaclav Nedorost of the Florida Panthers®. Vaclav, thank you for sitting down. Let's start with the obvious. Seven games last year, 69 this year. 7.1 minutes to 13.4. How did that jump feel from inside the lineup?

Vaclav Nedorost

Yeah. Well, the first year was, you know, not easy. You play one game, then maybe you are in the stands for ten, then you play again. You are, how you say, in and out. This year is different. The coach, he puts me out there and I stay. I don't think about when am I going back on the bench. I just play my shift, I come back, I wait, I go again. That is, that is what I needed.

Jan Kowalczyk

You missed six weeks there in March though. The leg came back April 24. What was it like watching from outside when you finally had that spot locked down?

Vaclav Nedorost

Terrible. I was sitting at home and the guys are winning, losing, I don't know, I just watch the TV. You want to be out there, especially when you play only 13 minutes a night, you think, every game I miss I lose one chance, one period to show the coach, you know? And I was fine, the leg, it was... it had to heal. So I just waited. But I hated it.

Jan Kowalczyk

You've been here since the summer of 2003 now. Do you feel like you've had time to get to know the place? What does a day off look like for you here?

Vaclav Nedorost

Oh, yeah, I feel settled here. I've been in this city, almost two years now. On a day off I try, how you say, I try to not do nothing. I sleep late, I maybe drive somewhere, eat somewhere. There are Czech restaurants, not many, but one or two I know. I go there, I talk in my language a little. It's good, you know. And sometimes I just sit by the water and I do nothing. Nothing. Just sit. In Ceske Budejovice when I was young, I was always... always something. Hockey, school, friends, the rink. Here I learn to just sit.

Jan Kowalczyk

That's an interesting thing. You came here at 19 from the Czech Extraliga, went to Hershey, then Colorado, then here. Did it ever feel like the long way around?

Vaclav Nedorost

Yeah. Yeah it does, sometimes. When I'm at Hershey, playing in the AHL, and I see the guys who played 82 games the year before, I think, where am I. And then Colorado, 25 games, and I think, okay now I'm here, and then the next year, 42 games, and then I'm traded. And then seven games the first year here. You start to think, maybe I'm not good enough, maybe I go back to Europe. But my family, they say, Vaclav, you are young, you have time. And I listen. And here it worked out.

Jan Kowalczyk

And the city itself. South Florida is not Ceske Budejovice. What do you miss about home?

Vaclav Nedorost

My mother's cooking. That's the big one. And winter. You know here there's no winter, the snow, it never comes. I like... you know in Budweiser, in December, you can go skating outside if you want. The whole thing, the cold, it's... how you say, it's more real. Here you walk outside in March, you are sweating. My friends from back home, they call me and I say, it is 30 degrees today, and they don't believe me.

Jan Kowalczyk

What about playing here? The fans, the building. What is it like to take that first shift in front of this crowd?

Vaclav Nedorost

The building is good. It's loud when we play good, you know? And last year we made the playoffs, the first time for many of these guys, and the building, it was... yes. The fans, they were happy, they didn't expect maybe. I remember a game in February, maybe third period, Olli scored, and the noise, you couldn't hear the bench. That is what I remember. Not so many, but when they get going, they are good.

Jan Kowalczyk

The Bureau's Book has you at 93 for speed, 91 for passing, 90 for puck control. You've got the hands and the feet. When you watch what you still need to add to play more minutes and in tougher situations, what do you see?

Vaclav Nedorost

I need to get stronger. That's the one. You play in the Czech league, you can use your... the stick, your body, maybe okay. Here, the defensemen, they are big, they are strong, they just, they run you into the boards and you stop. You think, okay, I stop. And you cannot stop. So I go in the gym, I work on this. I watch Joe . He's, what, 39, and he still takes hits, still gives them. I want that. Not the same, but, you know, I want to be harder to play against.

Jan Kowalczyk

You've got a year left on your contract. $1.00M. You're 23. Do you think about what comes next, the next contract, the next jump, or do you try to stay out of that?

Vaclav Nedorost

I think about it, a little. Everyone does. But you know, if I play good, if I play more minutes, the contract is, it comes. I just play. That is what my agent says too. He says, Vaclav, don't worry, just play. So I try to listen to him. And to my mother.

Jan Kowalczyk

One last thing. You look back at the kid in Ceske Budejovice who got drafted 14th overall. Would he recognize what you've got now? 13.4 minutes, a full schedule, 69 games?

Vaclav Nedorost

I think, yeah, he would be happy. He would not be surprised, because he always believed he would play here. But he would be happy it worked. He would say, good, you made it. And then he would ask, can you play more? That is the question, always more. 13 is good but I want 17, 18. That's... that's the plan.

Jan Kowalczyk

Vaclav Nedorost, thank you for your time. Good luck this summer.
