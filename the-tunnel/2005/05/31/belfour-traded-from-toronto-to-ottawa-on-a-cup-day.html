Belfour traded from Toronto to Ottawa on a Cup day

The 40-year-old goaltender, who split the season between Toronto's crease and the bench during their Cup run, was dealt to a 77-point Ottawa room and says the talent in it made the move simple.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelTuesday, 31 May 2005

2:58 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Ed Belfour arrives in Ottawa after Toronto Maple Leafs finished 59-13-5 and won the Stanley Cup. He was traded to Ottawa Senators today, a club that managed 77 points and an L1 streak. He carries a $6.50M contract with 1 year left and 6 shutouts to a 2-5 record from his season in Toronto.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here with Ed Belfour, who just put on an Ottawa jersey for the first time in the dressing room down the hall. Ed, you came out of a season with Toronto that finished 59 wins and 133 points and a Cup, and you land here where the record book says 77 points. How's that sit?

Ed Belfour

You know, it sits fine. I mean, obviously, a guy likes to be somewhere that's winning, but look at this room. Look at who's in it. Alfie's still here, Havlat's still here, Hossa is there, Redden behind them. You don't go 28 wins with those guys in your lineup and not know that something's off, right. So you come in, you try to give 'em something in goal, and see what happens. That's the job.

Marcus Bell

You allowed 70 goals in 58 games and put up 6 shutouts but only got the decision twice. That's the odd number of the night for me. How do you carry six clean games and a two-and-five mark?

Ed Belfour

Yeah, I've been a part of that before. You know, when you're on a team that's, uh, not exactly hanging around your blue line at the other end, you can be perfect and still not get the win. The puck doesn't go in the other net, the clock runs out, it's an overtime or a shootout, and there's the two. Six clean games though. I'll take that. I'll take that at forty.

Marcus Bell

You told me in January, after that Calgary game where you gave up one on twenty-one, that third-period work is what builds habits for April. You've got a whole different April now. What do you see from this back end that Toronto's back end gave you, or didn't?

Ed Belfour

Well. Toronto's back end... I mean Redden's here too, right. So I've got a piece of that. What I see here is, you know, the guys can move. Havlat, Hossa, they can make the first pass out of trouble. What they need is somebody to, uh, clean it up the last ten minutes, the way we did in Toronto at the end of the year. That's not a coach's problem. That's a body language thing when you're down one with four to go. And I think that's fixable.

Marcus Bell

You're the oldest man in the room by a wide stretch. Average age here is 27.8, you've got six guys twenty-four and under. Are you the one who's got to set the clock, so to speak?

Ed Belfour

Well, yeah, I'm forty. I'm the oldest guy here by, uh, quite a bit, and look, I'll be in the room, I'll talk, you know, I won't shut up. That's what I do. But I don't walk around telling these guys how to live. Alfie's been here longer than me. Hossa has won more than I have in the playoffs. You don't lecture guys like that. You just show up, you do your thing, and the young ones, they watch, and they either like what they see or they don't.

Marcus Bell

Your contract's got one year on it, $6.50M. Do you see yourself in this crease come October, or is this a, uh, a bridge?

Ed Belfour

You know, I've had that question a hundred times the last five years. Every year it's one more year, right. And here we are. I've got one year on the deal, I've got, what, a couple weeks before summer break, and I'll be ready in September. You want to know more than that, go talk to the GM. I'll play. I'll try to win some games for these guys. That's all I control.

Marcus Bell

Last one. Your morale on the Bureau's scale is 99. Is that the Cup talking, or are you genuinely excited to walk into a room that went twenty-eight wins?

Ed Belfour

Both, man. Both. The Cup's great, I won it, I'm happy, I'll watch the tape for the next twenty years. But I've been on teams that had guys like Alfie and I knew we could turn the corner. That's the thing that gets you up in September. Not the parade. The parade's over, you know? It's Monday and you've got to go to the rink and there's still a hole in your net and you've got to fill it. So yeah. Ninety-nine. I like it here.

Marcus Bell

Ed Belfour, welcome to Ottawa. Thanks for your time, back to you.
