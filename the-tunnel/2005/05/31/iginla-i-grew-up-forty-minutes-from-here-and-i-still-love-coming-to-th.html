Iginla: "I grew up forty minutes from here, and I still love coming to the rink"

The Flames' right wing talks about playing in his home province through another missed playoff run, and what 76 goals in 76 games taught him about the team around him.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelTuesday, 31 May 2005

5:15 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Jarome Iginla92 sat down with Jan Kowalczyk after the Calgary Flames season ended without a playoff berth, the second straight year the Flames missed the post-season. Iginla finished with 128 points in 76 games and his second consecutive Maurice Richard Trophy, but Calgary Flames never got above the middle of the standings. At 27, four years and $11.09M still on his deal, he talked about the building, the room, and what he carried out of another long season.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here, sitting down with Jarome Iginla. Jarome, good to see you again. We spoke back in December, after one of those ones against Toronto that got away in overtime. You looked like a man who wanted to be anywhere else that night.

Jarome Iginla

Yeah, I remember. I think you asked me about the two points and I gave you the, what's the thing, the stock answer. 'The puck found Buzek.' Like that was supposed to explain something.

Jan Kowalczyk

I'll take the stock answer if the man gives it honestly. You've been here a long time now. You were traded here in '95, you were eighteen years old. What's it like playing ninety miles from where you grew up?

Jarome Iginla

St. Albert's actually closer than ninety, you know, it's more like, I grew up right next to Edmonton, and this is, what's it, a three-hour drive south. But the feeling of it, the province, the time zone, the cold in January, it's, it's home. I don't have to explain it to people. I grew up watching the Oilers on TV and Grant Fuhr was my guy. And now I'm the one on the other channel and people call their buddies in St. Albert and say 'I saw you Saturday.' It's nice. It's different from a kid from, I don't know, New York, having to figure out a whole new life in a city he doesn't know.

Jan Kowalczyk

You've got four years left on the deal, so you're not going anywhere in a hurry either way. But the building, the fans, does it feel like enough of a hockey town for a guy who grew up in one?

Jarome Iginla

Yeah. Yeah it does. You know what I mean, it's not, it's not Edmonton where the Oilers are the whole city's identity. Calgary, there's the football team, there's the stampede, there's a lot going on. But when we've got somebody like Conroy, like Craig's been here a while, when he's in the middle of a scramble in front of the net, you can hear the whole building stand up. So yeah, the building's got it. It's a good building to play in. You know what I mean, the sight lines, the ice's always good. I don't have any complaints.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let me talk about this season then. 76 goals, 52 assists, 128 points. Second straight Richard Trophy. You scored a goal a game. And the team didn't make it.

Jarome Iginla

Yeah.

Jan Kowalczyk

That's all you've got for that?

Jarome Iginla

Well, no, I, I mean last year I said it's about the team, and that's, you know, that's the cliché, and everybody in this league has said it a hundred times on this couch, but the cliché's there because it's the truth. I can't, I can't go out and score 92 goals and 140 points and then turn around and say 'I should have done more' because, what, I should have scored 92 a game? You know what I mean. I did my job. The question is whether we've got the guys around me to, to make the other thing happen. And I think, I think we've got pieces. I think Gelinas is better than people give him credit for, I think Conroy should be getting more respect. But at some point you need, you need more than, than, you know, the third line scoring forty, forty-something goals and, and, and the goaltending being, I mean Turek's been good for us. Jamie's been good behind him. But, we didn't have enough depth. You need four lines to go four rounds. We had maybe two and a half.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're twenty-seven. You'll be thirty-one when that deal's up. Four years to win. You're not a young kid anymore in the sense that, I mean, four years is the window.

Jarome Iginla

You're twenty-seven, yeah. I mean, I'm not twenty-two, that's, that's true. I feel good, I feel strong. My game's not, it's not built on just speed anymore, obviously, you know, I've got to, I've got to be smarter. But, four years is a long time. We've got guys coming. There's some young blood in the room. And, I don't know, I think if we can, if we can get one or two pieces in, one or two real pieces, this room knows what it can do. I've seen it in practice. I've seen what we can look like on a Tuesday when everything clicks. We just can't, we can't string it together for eighty-two nights.

Jan Kowalczyk

Is that the one thing you take from this year, then? You scored 76 goals, that's a goal a game, and the season still went nowhere. You learned that the numbers don't, don't carry you?

Jarome Iginla

You know, when I was younger, and I'm talking, I don't know, twenty, twenty-one, twenty-two, I wanted to score fifty goals. I wanted to be, you know, I wanted to make the All-Star team, and I thought if I did those things it meant I'd, I'd made it. And, yeah, now, I want to, I want to be in April. I want to, I want to, what's the expression, I want to, I want to be playing when the, when the weather gets warm. That's what I want. And I'd trade the Richard, I mean, it's nice to hold it, it's, I'm not knocking it. I'm not. But I'd trade the Richard for a, for a second round home-ice advantage. I really would. So, yeah. That's what this year taught me. You can score a hundred goals and it don't, it don't matter if you're watching from the couch.

Jan Kowalczyk

Last time we talked you were up in the lead in scoring and you said the looks kept coming, the chances kept coming, just the puck wasn't going in. This year they went in. 76 times. Did you change anything in your summer between those two seasons?

Jarome Iginla

I worked on my first step. You know, I'd been, I'd been, what's the thing, coasting, I was, my game was, 'I'll get the puck on my backhand and spin and shoot.' And I thought, alright, I'm twenty-six, twenty-seven, I can't rely on that forever. So I worked on my acceleration, on the, on that first few strides. And I think, I think you see it. I think I get to spots earlier. You know, the goals came from that, from arriving early, from being where I shouldn't be, where nobody expects me to be yet. So yeah. It was one of the better summers I've had.

Jan Kowalczyk

And what about this summer? What does a guy who's just played seventy-six games, who's just missed the playoffs for the second year in a row, what does he do between now and September?

Jarome Iginla

Go home. I mean, go to St. Albert, see my mom, see my, my grandpa. I've been, I've been away for, what, six, seven months? It's, you know, it's the time to, to be normal. I'll hit the rink in July, maybe earlier, depending on how I feel. But right now, right now I'm going to go sit in somebody's backyard in St. Albert, and I'm not going to think about hockey for a while. I'm going to think about baseball, honestly. I used to catch, you know, before hockey took over. I played catcher for the national team. That's, that's a thing people forget. I used to want to be a two-sport guy. Bo Jackson. You remember Bo Jackson, right? He played football and baseball and everybody said he was crazy, said he was going to blow himself up. And he did, he did tear his hip. But for a while there, that was the dream. And I was, I was that kid. St. Albert, little kid, catching, throwing, thinking maybe hockey's just something I do on the side. That didn't quite work out.

Jan Kowalczyk

Jarome Iginla, two-time Maurice Richard champion, three-time All-Star, and a baseball catcher who decided to come to work in the wrong sport. Thanks for sitting down with us.

Jarome Iginla

Appreciate it, Jan. Thanks.
