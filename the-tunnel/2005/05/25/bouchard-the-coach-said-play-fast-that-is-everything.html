Bouchard: "The coach said, play fast, that is everything"

The 21-year-old center talks about the voice that turned his season, leaving Sherbrooke at thirteen, and why he won't try to hit people.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelWednesday, 25 May 2005

4:32 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Pierre-Marc Bouchard79 went from 36 games and 10 points with Minnesota Wild in 2003-04 to 82 games and 29 points in 2004-05. The Bureau's Book has him at 77 overall, speed 90, checking 52, up six on the Development Index. He sat down in the studio after the Wild's season closed.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here with Pierre-Marc Bouchard. Pierre-Marc, thanks for coming in. You just played 82 games. Last year you played 36. What happened between those two numbers?

Pierre-Marc Bouchard

Thank you, Jan. Yeah. So, the coach, he told me something at the beginning. He said, you play fast, you don't try to be someone else, you use your speed, you use your hands, that's your game. And he believed it, you know? He put me out there and he said, go. Don't think about the checking, don't think about the boards. So I stopped thinking. That's what changed.

Jan Kowalczyk

Was it a conversation? Like he pulled you aside?

Pierre-Marc Bouchard

Yeah, at the camp. First day almost. He watched me at the small-area game and he comes over, and he says, he says, why are you trying to hit people? You are the fastest guy here. You just... go around them. And that was it. That was the whole conversation.

Jan Kowalczyk

And your minutes only went from 11.0 to 11.6. That's not a big jump. But you went from part-time to every night. Is that how you felt it?

Pierre-Marc Bouchard

For sure, that's what it is. The minutes, they are not... I mean, I know I'm not a 20-minute guy yet. I know where I sit. But playing every night, that's the thing. You get the rhythm, you know? 36 games you're, how do you say, on and off. You're looking for it. 82 games, you have it. Your hands are there. Your legs are there. The guys see you every day, they trust you with the puck.

Jan Kowalczyk

You mentioned the guys. Who was the one who made that difference for you? On the ice.

Pierre-Marc Bouchard

Saku. Saku Koivu. He's a guy, he doesn't look for you, he just knows where you are. You're coming, the puck is there, you don't even have to call for it. And he's patient with me, you know? I make a bad play, he looks at me, he just says, next one. Very calm. That's nice when you're young, you need that. A veteran like that, he doesn't yell, he just... he just waits for you to be better.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let me take you back a little. Sherbrooke, born there, 1984. But your Pee-Wee team was College-Français Rive-Sud, from the South Shore of Montreal. So you were already leaving home pretty young.

Pierre-Marc Bouchard

Yeah. I was, I think, 13 or 14 when I went to the South Shore. That's... that's the first move. Before Minnesota, it was the first one. And my mother, she did not want to let me go, for sure. But she knew, for the hockey, you have to go. And then the Pee-Wee tournament in 1998, that was the first time the big scouts, they were there, you see? So from Sherbrooke to the South Shore to... this. It's a long road.

Jan Kowalczyk

And then Minnesota. You're 18, drafted 8th overall in 2002, and basically you're in the pros right away. 50 games that first year. What did your family say when they realized it was Minnesota?

Pierre-Marc Bouchard

Minnesota. They said, where is that? No, but seriously, my father he knew the Wild, he follows hockey, but Minnesota... it's not close to Sherbrooke. That's the thing. My mother, she cried when I left, the first time, you know, for the camp. And then she gets on the phone every week, every week, same thing: are you eating well? Are you cold? I said, mama, I am in Minnesota, yes I am cold.

Jan Kowalczyk

Do they come up? To the games?

Pierre-Marc Bouchard

For sure. When they can. The drive is not easy, you know, but they come. My father, he drives. He doesn't like to fly, he says the plane is too small, he can't stretch his legs. So he drives, and he parks the car, and he's in the building. He doesn't say much. He just nods. After the game he calls, he says, good game. That's it. Good game. And that means everything, for him. Because he never says good game.

Jan Kowalczyk

You nod at that. Good game from your father is the trophy.

Pierre-Marc Bouchard

That's right. That's the trophy, yes.

Jan Kowalczyk

Now, the Book has you at speed 90, deking 88, passing 88, puck control 88. And it has you at checking 52, toughness 50. That gap. People see that gap. You see that gap?

Pierre-Marc Bouchard

I see it. I know what they see. But the coach, he told me already, first day: you don't try to be a hitter. You will not win, you are 165 pounds, you try to hit a guy, you end up on the ice. So I don't. I use my hands, I use my speed, I go the other way. That's my game. I will not pretend to be someone else.

Jan Kowalczyk

But you're 21. If you look ahead, the guys who play long careers, they get stronger. Do you think about that?

Pierre-Marc Bouchard

Yeah, I think about it. I work in the summer, I work on the legs, on the core. I will get stronger, for sure. I'm not the same guy at 21 I was at 18, you know, 165 pounds, I was, how do you say, a kid. But I won't try to check like a big man. That's not who I am. And the coach doesn't ask me that. He asks me to play fast. So I play fast.

Jan Kowalczyk

You've got one year left on your deal. Does that weigh on you? Twenty-nine points and a new contract at the end of next year?

Pierre-Marc Bouchard

One year. For sure I know. But right now the season is done, my legs are done, I want to go home to Sherbrooke, see my mother, eat her cooking. I'll think about next year in the summer. I know what I want to do. I want to play here. The guys are good, Saku is there, the coach believes in me. So... for sure, I want to be here. And then we see.

Jan Kowalczyk

Pierre-Marc Bouchard, thank you for your time. Enjoy the summer. Go eat your mother's cooking.

Pierre-Marc Bouchard

Thank you, Jan. For sure. Good game.
