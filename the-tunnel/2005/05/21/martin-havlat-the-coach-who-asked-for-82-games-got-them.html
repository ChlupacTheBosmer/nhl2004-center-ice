Martin Havlat: the coach who asked for 82 games got them

The Ottawa winger, 103 points in 82 games this season and past his Bureau ceiling at 24, talks about his road from Mlada Boleslav, the coach's ultimatum after his suspensions, and the one year left on his contract.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelSaturday, 21 May 2005

4:41 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Martin Havlat89 scored 40 goals and 103 points for Ottawa Senators this season, more than doubling last year's pace while cutting his penalty minutes to 15 in 82 games after two suspensions totalling four games in 2003-04. The 24-year-old left winger sits one point per game above his pace last year and three above his base grade on the Bureau's Development Index, with one year left on his $0.90M contract. Marcus Bell sat down with him in the studio.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here, thanks for taking the time, Martin. First time on this desk, so welcome. Let me start with the simple one. 40 goals, 103 points, 82 games. How does the year sit with you now that it's over?

Martin Havlat

Thank you. Yeah, it sits good. You know, I don't think about it so much yet. The season ends, you go home, you sleep maybe two days and then you think, okay, what do I do in the summer. But 82 games, yes. That part I am proud of.

Marcus Bell

Let me go back further than this season. You were the 26th pick in 1999, drafted out of the Czech league. You went home and played another year before you came over. A lot of kids that age, they'd take the shot right away.

Martin Havlat

I was 18. You know, I play in Mlada Boleslav, and I think... no. Not yet. The men there, they are men. And here, the games are... 82. Every two days. I think I need, I need one more year to be ready. And I think it was the right call, you know. 19, I come and I am maybe a little more ready than I would be at 18.

Marcus Bell

What was the thing that hit you when you first put on the jersey here? The speed? The size?

Martin Havlat

The travel, actually. I don't know, everyone say the speed, and yes, the speed is... here it's different. But the travel is what I didn't know. You play, you fly, you play, you fly. In Czech Republic, I go home every night. Here you sleep in the hotel, you eat, you go to the rink. That is, you know, the first month I think, where is home?

Marcus Bell

You've been here now since that first season, 2000-01. Five years. What about this room now, how does it feel different from that first year?

Martin Havlat

First year I don't talk to nobody. I sit, I put my skates on, I leave. Now I talk. Daniel, he... he is the same. Always the same. But now I can talk to him and he talk back, you know? It is different when you are not afraid.

Marcus Bell

Daniel Alfredsson. He's been your linemate. Talk about what that line gives you.

Martin Havlat

He open the ice. That is what he do. He stand there, he look, and the defenseman, he respect him. So I have... I have a little room. And Marian, on the other side, he see the play before it happen. I just go. I go and he find me. That is, you know, that is the easy part for me.

Marcus Bell

Let me ask about last year. Two suspensions, four games missed. And this year, 15 minutes in the box in 82 games. What changed?

Martin Havlat

The coach. He, uh... he talk to me after the second suspension. He say, I need you 82 games. I can not play without you, you are not good enough... no, that is not what he say. He say, the team can not win without you. And I think, he is right. I miss four games, and we lose in four in the playoffs. Four games I don't help.

Marcus Bell

Did that land right away, or did you need a few weeks?

Martin Havlat

The first two weeks I am... how you say, I don't like it. I think, okay, I don't hit, I don't shoot the guy in the face, I just play my game. And the coach say, yes, play your game. And I play my game, and it is enough. I was maybe... making it more than it needed to be. The kicking thing, the high-sticking, I don't... it doesn't matter now. I play different.

Marcus Bell

The Development Index has you up three from your base. Your potential in the Book was 86 when you came in, and you're at 88 now. You're past the ceiling they drew for you at 18.

Martin Havlat

Yes, I see. The people tell me this. But you know, I don't read the... the website, you know? My agent, he send me something, I look. But I don't think about it. I think about August. What I need in August. If I work and I am better in September, then I am better. That is all I think about.

Marcus Bell

You've got one year left on your contract, $0.90M. At 24, scoring 103 points. That's not going to be your number next year.

Martin Havlat

Yeah. I think about it, how can I not? But I play. My agent, he do the talking. I don't... I am not good at the talking. I play, and maybe I play good, and then they say okay. That is... I hope so.

Marcus Bell

Does the contract year change how you play? Do you play different knowing it's your showcase?

Martin Havlat

No. I play the same. But, you know... maybe I want to show them. That is natural. Everybody want to play good when the money is there. I am not a saint. But the game, the game I play the same way.

Marcus Bell

You were swept in four games by Philadelphia. Two goals, two assists in four. What did that feel like?

Martin Havlat

Bad. It feel bad because I know we can play. We play... not good, the four games. Not our game. And to go out like that, in four, it is... I want to be back. I want the next one where we play four that we win.

Marcus Bell

Zdeno Chara is your teammate and your countryman. What is the Czech thing in that room?

Martin Havlat

He is big. And quiet. Very quiet. But when he say something, the whole room, you know, they stop and they listen. And for me, when I come here first, he is here already. He show me, like, the, how you say, the way of the room. Where to sit, how to... nothing big. But he is there.

Marcus Bell

Last one. You go home to Mlada Boleslav this summer. What does Martin Havlat do when he's not playing hockey?

Martin Havlat

I sit. I do nothing. My mother, she make the food, I eat. I walk. I see my friends. And then... August I go back to the gym. That is the plan. But first, the nothing.

Marcus Bell

Martin Havlat, Ottawa's number 9. Thanks very much, Martin. Back to you.
