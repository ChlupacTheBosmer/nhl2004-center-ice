Boris Van Ryswyk turns $13.64M and leaves three rooms at zero

The Florida GM says the profit is real, the rooms are not, and he wants to fix that before he fixes the record.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelWednesday, 4 May 2005

3:50 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Boris Van Ryswyk runs hockey operations for Florida Panthers, where a seat costs $79 and 14,971 people a night bought it last season. The club turned $13.64M in profit on $72.88M of revenue and $51.07M in payroll, and finished 25-36-16 for 76 points. The equipment room, the workout room and the practice rink are all at level 0, the floor of the scale. Travel sits at 3.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here in the studio with Boris Van Ryswyk, who runs the hockey side in Florida. Boris, $79 a seat, 14,971 a night, $13.64M at the end of the year. What does a fan in that building get for his money?

Boris Van Ryswyk

He gets a hockey game. Eighty-two of them, give or take. And I know what you're after, Jan. You're going to ask me about 76 points. About 25 wins. I'll take it. But let me just put the gate out there first. People showed up. The building wasn't empty. That's... that's a start.

Jan Kowalczyk

A start. You lost 11 straight to close it out. 25 wins at $79 a seat, that's a tough sell.

Boris Van Ryswyk

It is. It really is. And I'm not going to stand here and tell you I sleep great about losing 11 in a row. I don't. But I'm not the coach. I don't dress a line for Tuesday. What I dress is the roster and... the rooms. And that's where we are tonight.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let's get into the rooms then. Equipment, level 0. Workout, level 0. Practice, level 0. You and Boston are the only two clubs in this league with all three at the floor. Travel is the one you've built, at 3.

Boris Van Ryswyk

Travel was easy. You play in this division, you're on planes. You land at one, two in the morning, and if the travel budget doesn't reach, the next night you're skating in mud. We fixed that. That one was obvious.

Jan Kowalczyk

And the other three?

Boris Van Ryswyk

Yeah. The other three. Equipment, workout, practice. I know where they sit. And I'm not going to pretend I haven't looked at them, Jan, because I have. Every edition, I look. We made $13.64M and some of that is supposed to go into the building, into those rooms. I won't argue with you on that.

Jan Kowalczyk

So what's the priority? You're $51.07M in payroll. Every dollar you're not spending on players could go into one of those rooms.

Boris Van Ryswyk

That's the balance, right. And here's how I see it. The roster keeps the lights on tonight. You go cheaper on players, you lose the gate. The rooms... the rooms help you in three years, four years. So if I have to sequence it, I've been putting the player money first. You get wins, you get the $79 tickets sold, you get 14,971 a night. That feeds the profit. The profit feeds the rooms. That's the order I've been working in.

Jan Kowalczyk

And when does that order flip? You've got David Aebischer at $0.50M, 86 overall, one year left. That's a cheap contract. You could redirect that money.

Boris Van Ryswyk

Aeb. Yeah, he's... $0.50M, 86 overall, that's the kind of thing you grab and you hold. What he does in July, I can't tell you today. But that's the point, isn't it? If I hold those deals together, if I keep the payroll lean, there's money after the season. There's money before the draft, before July. And that money... yeah. I'd rather put it where it lasts. A rental helps you six weeks. A workout room helps you every day.

Jan Kowalczyk

So the draft in 35 days, then free agency. After that, you'd move on the rooms?

Boris Van Ryswyk

I'd move. I will tell you that. I won't give you a date. I've watched too many guys in this league promise a facility and then miss it by a year. But I'll say this. I'm not going into the next season looking at a workout room at zero. That... that's a direction. That's where I want to be.

Jan Kowalczyk

One last thing. 14,971 is middle of the pack. You're not selling out. Does that make the rooms harder to justify to whoever sits above you?

Boris Van Ryswyk

You want to know what 14,971 looks like with 40 wins? I do. I'd love to know. But you can't build the crowd first. You build the team, you build the rooms that make the players better, and the crowd... the crowd finds you. I have to believe that. Because the other option is just... just selling tickets to 25 wins forever. And that's not a business.

Jan Kowalczyk

Boris Van Ryswyk, general manager of Florida. Thank you for sitting with us. We'll have you back after the draft and see if those rooms moved.

Boris Van Ryswyk

Anytime, Jan. I'll bring numbers next time. Actual numbers.
