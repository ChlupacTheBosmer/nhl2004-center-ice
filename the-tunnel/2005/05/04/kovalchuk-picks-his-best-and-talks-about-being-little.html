Kovalchuk picks his best and talks about being little

The 22-year-old names Dany Heatley the best player he's shared a line with, then walks back through Tver, Spartak Moscow, and why he wears Kharlamov's number.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelWednesday, 4 May 2005

2:41 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Ilya Kovalchuk91 is 22 and in his fourth season at Atlanta Thrashers. He finished this year with 63 goals, 75 assists, 138 points, the most he has scored at any point in his career. He sits with Marcus Bell and talks about the best player beside him and the smallest kid on the ice before him.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here in the studio with Ilya Kovalchuk. Ilya, 138 points is a career high, your assists went from 62 to 75, but you missed three games in March with the shoulder. Let's start there, the shoulder, and then where those extra assists came from.

Ilya Kovalchuk

The shoulder, yes. It is not bad. Three games, I come back. The first year I miss 17 games. So, we go forward. The assists, of course I see more guys now. Before I just shoot. Always shoot. Now, Dany is there, I pass. I learn to look up.

Marcus Bell

That name takes us to Dany Heatley, 65 goals this year. Is he the best player you've played with?

Ilya Kovalchuk

Yes. Of course. Dany, he sees the ice like me. I go, I know where he is. I don't look. I know. It is fast with him. Very fast.

Marcus Bell

What does it look like when it clicks?

Ilya Kovalchuk

When it clicks, people in the stands stop talking. We go, I shoot or he shoots, nobody decides before. The puck just goes and somebody puts it in. And when it is not working, we are two guys trying to carry, everybody carries, nobody passes. That is bad. Worse than bad.

Marcus Bell

Let me take you back further. You're from Tver. What's the hockey there?

Ilya Kovalchuk

Tver is small. The rink is there. I go back when I can. I was little, very little, and I play with boys bigger. They don't pass to me because I am small. So I take the puck, I go, I shoot. My shot, it comes from being little in Tver.

Marcus Bell

Then Spartak Moscow, two seasons in the Vysshaya Liga before the NHL. You go from Tver to Spartak, what changes?

Ilya Kovalchuk

Spartak, that is where I learn. In Tver I play because I love it. Spartak I play because I must. The men there, they are hard. You do stupid thing, they look at you. No second mistake. I make many, many mistakes in the first year.

Marcus Bell

You wear 17. That's Valeri Kharlamov's number, from the Soviet teams in the seventies.

Ilya Kovalchuk

Yes. Kharlamov. Everyone in Russia knows him. He was small, like me, but he scored like nobody. I see old tapes, I read about him. I say, I take this. It is heavy. But I like the heavy.

Marcus Bell

People back home talk about the Russian game, the system, the small ice, preparing you for this. Does it?

Ilya Kovalchuk

People want me to say yes. Russia makes you smart, you see the ice different. But here the ice is big. The guys are fast. The space, it is gone before you find it. In Spartak I have one second. Here I have... I have space, but I have to find it so fast. I learn two years. Maybe three. I don't know.

Marcus Bell

The Bureau's Development Index has you up five this season. Your base is 89, you're at 90 overall today. Do you feel that rise?

Ilya Kovalchuk

My agent tells me. I look at goals. I look at losses. The grade, yes, it is nice. But I want to play in June. Not March, not April. June. That is the thing.

Marcus Bell

One last thing. There's a kid at that rink in Tver, maybe the same size you were, watching you on the television. What do you say to him?

Ilya Kovalchuk

I say go. You go, you try. The road is long, it is... I don't know the word. But you go. I was little in that rink. Now I am not so little.

Marcus Bell

Ilya Kovalchuk, thank you very much. Back to you.
