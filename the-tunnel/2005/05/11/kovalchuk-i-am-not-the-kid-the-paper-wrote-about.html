Kovalchuk: 'I am not the kid the paper wrote about'

The 22-year-old first overall pick, now six points past his projected ceiling, says the number he'd rather see is a win.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelWednesday, 11 May 2005

3:38 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Ilya Kovalchuk90 sat down with Marcus Bell to talk about the road from Tver, the number 17, and the [[Bureau's Book]] that put his potential at 84 while he is playing at 90. The 22-year-old Atlanta Thrashers winger had 138 points and 63 goals this season, but he kept coming back to the one number that wasn't his own.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here, from the studio. Ilya Kovalchuk, thanks for the time. Let's start where the profile starts. Tver. How old were you when you left?

Ilya Kovalchuk

I think seventeen. Maybe eighteen. Time is... when you are young, time is strange. In Tver, my father take me to rink. Then I go to Spartak Moscow. Vysshaya Liga. Two years there. Then the draft. Then... Atlanta Thrashers®.

Marcus Bell

Draft night. 2001. First Russian ever picked first overall. Were you watching?

Ilya Kovalchuk

Yes. I am with my family. They say my name and I think... I think it is a joke. First Russian, first pick. They keep saying first. And then my father, he is crying. In Russia, a man... a man don't cry. He is crying. And then I know. This is not a joke. This is big.

Marcus Bell

You wear 17. Kharlamov's number. Was that a decision you made?

Ilya Kovalchuk

Not me. My father. He say: take 17. Kharlamov. You know Kharlamov? Soviet hockey, 1970s. For us, in Russia, he is... Gretzky. No. More than Gretzky. He is the game. So I wear 17 for Kharlamov. For my father. Not for me.

Marcus Bell

Five years ago you were in the Vysshaya Liga. Nobody's household name. Now you're 90 overall, the best player in that room. What's different?

Ilya Kovalchuk

Five years ago I don't know what line change is. I don't know what forecheck is. I know how to skate, I know how to shoot. That is nothing here. Now I know: position. When to stop, when to go. Sixty minutes is important. When I come here, everybody say that. Sixty minutes, Ilya. I don't understand. Now I understand. You play sixty or you don't play.

Marcus Bell

Here's something the profile digs into. The Book has your potential at 84. Your overall today is 90. You're six points above what the scouts wrote down for you. That's a ceiling you broke at 22.

Ilya Kovalchuk

Potential. I don't know about that. I know I play better than last year. Maybe the men with papers, they write wrong number. Or maybe they write right number for the kid I was, and I am not that kid now. That is... that is good.

Marcus Bell

Your shot grades are 99 power, 99 speed. But your assists went from 62 to 75 this year. You're passing more.

Ilya Kovalchuk

Because of Dany. Dany Heatley. He is here now, I have more room. Before, I shoot always. Everybody say shoot, Ilya, shoot. So I shoot. Now I see him open, I pass. 75 assists. I don't care about the assist. But Dany score because of the pass. That is good enough for me.

Marcus Bell

What's the day to day actually like, him and you? People talk about you two like a package deal.

Ilya Kovalchuk

Dany is fast. He has good hands. I pass, he is there. I go to net, he pass back. We play, it is easy. And he is 24, he listen. Sami Kapanen, he listen too. I am 22, I listen. In this room, you don't listen, you don't play. So we listen.

Marcus Bell

You missed a stretch in March. Shoulder. Back inside days.

Ilya Kovalchuk

Shoulder. Yes. I come back, I play. It is small. Can we talk about hockey?

Marcus Bell

Alright. 138 points, 63 goals. And Atlanta Thrashers® is at the bottom of the table. How does that sit?

Ilya Kovalchuk

This is the thing. I want to tell you about this. 138 points, okay. And I look at the standings and we are... What is it? It does not matter what I do if we don't win. I score, we lose 4-3. That is nothing. Maybe I am greedy. I want goals and wins, both. But I get only goals. That is... that is not enough.

Marcus Bell

You've got $1.10M on the deal with one year left. You're a 90 overall, you're the best player on that roster. Does that bother you?

Ilya Kovalchuk

I am 22. I don't think about money. When I am 25, 26, maybe I think. Maybe I say, okay, this is what I am worth. Maybe I say nothing. I don't know yet. Today I play hockey. That is enough.

Marcus Bell

Five years from now. You'll be 27. What do you want to be?

Ilya Kovalchuk

I want to win. That is all. I want this team in playoffs, real playoffs. With Dany, with Sami, with Patrik Stefan. We need more players. Good players. And then maybe we are not last. I want to be player who wins. The goals, that is fine. But winning. That is the thing.

Marcus Bell

Ilya Kovalchuk, thank you for the time. Back to you.
