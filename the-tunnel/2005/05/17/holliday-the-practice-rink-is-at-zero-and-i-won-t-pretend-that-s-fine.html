Holliday: 'The practice rink is at zero and I won't pretend that's fine'

Washington's general manager sits down with The Tunnel to talk about the resources he's given the coaching staff, the scouting room he just moved up, and what a $27.21M profit buys before June 8.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelTuesday, 17 May 2005

4:55 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Shawn Holliday runs the Washington Capitals from the front office, where the payroll of $55.92M returned 104 points and a second-round exit. The club turned $27.21M of profit on $93.29M in revenue, and the practice room still sits at the bottom of the file. Jan Kowalczyk sat across from him in the studio to ask what that means for the man behind the bench and for the roster.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here in the studio with Shawn Holliday. Shawn, 39 wins, 104 points, a second-round exit, and now what, two losses to close out your spring. How does this one sit with you?

Shawn Holliday

It sits the way any first round or second round exit sits. You're disappointed. You wanted more. The 104 points, I think that tells you the group competed. We weren't good enough in the round after that, and that's on the roster I built. I own that part.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let me start where the coach angle lives. Your practice room sits at level zero. That's the floor on the sheet. Your workout room is at one. Does the man behind the bench have what he needs to get the most out of 287 goals for and 250 against?

Shawn Holliday

Look, I'll be straight with you. The practice rink at zero is... it's not where I want it. I won't stand here and tell you that's fine, it isn't. But the coach is not asking me for practice rink, he's asking me for players. He wants bodies who can do what he draws up. The rooms matter, I know they do, but they're a longer thing. You fix a practice room and it helps in three years, four years.

Jan Kowalczyk

And the workout room at one? Strength, checking, endurance, that's what the tuning file says that track moves. You're at one. The league par is five.

Shawn Holliday

I know where we sit. I do. Equipment moved up in January, scouting moved up a few weeks ago. The record will show those. The workout room and the practice room, they're on the list. I can't tell you when, and I can't tell you how much they cost, that's not how this works with me. But they're on the list. The coach, he gets what I give him, and what I give him is a roster. He's good at what he does with the roster.

Jan Kowalczyk

The scouting room moved up between the ninth and the eleventh of May. That's recent. You don't move that unless you think you're getting better information out of it.

Shawn Holliday

You're reading into it more than the record lets me say. The room moved up. I'm happy it moved up. That's what I'll tell you. The draft is 22 days away. Whatever I got from the scouting room between the eleventh and the eighth of June, you'll see it in who's wearing a Washington jersey in October, if I draft well. And that's a big if, Jan.

Jan Kowalczyk

Fair enough. Let's move to the money, because this is the part that makes people stop. You made $27.21M in profit. Your payroll is $55.92M. You're not spending it all, and you're not the richest club in this league. What would you change about this roster if the money were simply there, no ceiling, no limit?

Shawn Holliday

Okay. So. If the money were there. You know what I look at first? I look at the back end. 250 against is respectable. It's not good enough to win four rounds. And I look at what's available in the pool right now, and the free-agent pool is... empty. There's nobody unsigned. So if I want a difference-maker, I'm buying him off another club's roster. That costs more than the salary. You know that.

Jan Kowalczyk

And the gate tells you something, doesn't it? 16203 a night, tickets at $78. The building is paying for itself. You made $93.29M on the season.

Shawn Holliday

Yeah. And that's what I'd tell you. The gate is there. The people show up. So when I look at the payroll, $55.92M, and I look at the profit line, I know I've got room. I know the building can carry more. The question is what do I spend it on. A winger who scores 40? A defenseman who eats 26 minutes? Those cost different things. A winger costs money and picks. A defenseman costs picks you don't want to move in May because the draft is in 22 days and your scouting room is telling you things you want to use.

Jan Kowalczyk

You've got Bondra out with a knee. Return date is the 29th of May, which is after the draft. Does that change how you think about the front end heading into the summer?

Shawn Holliday

Peter's coming back. The return is the 29th. He'll be here. But I don't build a roster around what one man's knee does in May. I build it for October. And October is 139 days away. I've got a draft to prepare for, I've got a summer to work, and I've got a payroll I want to spend smarter than I spent it last year. That's where my head is.

Jan Kowalczyk

One last one. You're in your office, the season's done, you've had a week to breathe. You look at the file. Practice at zero, workout at one. What do you tell yourself about what you owe that coach?

Shawn Holliday

I tell myself I owe him better rooms. I owe him a practice rink that isn't at the floor. And I owe him... look, I owe him the chance to work with the people he's got and not have me cutting the travel budget in February because I decided to move a scouting room in May. The rooms are the thing that takes the longest. I'm going to get them. It's going to take time. And I'm not going to promise you a date. I'll just tell you they're the thing I think about most.

Jan Kowalczyk

Shawn Holliday, thank you for your time. Enjoy the summer, you've earned it. Back to you.
