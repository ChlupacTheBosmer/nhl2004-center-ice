Mike Cammalleri: 'He said, prove it. And that was the whole conversation'

The 22-year-old right wing credits one office visit after his elbow for turning 20 points into 60, says staying at Michigan made him ready for the room, and wants to be trusted in June.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelTuesday, 17 May 2005

6:06 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Mike Cammalleri87 finished the 2004-05 season with 60 points in 61 games for Los Angeles Kings, up from 20 points in 69 games a year ago. The 22-year-old right wing, drafted 49th overall in 2001, wore an assistant captain's letter and carried 20.8 minutes a night after an elbow kept him out until December 14th. He sat down with Jan Kowalczyk to talk about the coach who changed his role, the decision to stay at the University of Michigan, and what a FIGH grade of 50 feels like on a Tuesday night in February.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here in the studio with Mike Cammalleri from Los Angeles Kings®. Mike, you finished with 60 points, 23 goals, 37 assists, 20.8 minutes a night. Last year 20 points, 11.8 minutes. How do you even process that?

Mike Cammalleri

You know, you don't process it during the year. You're just trying to keep up. But yeah, looking back, 11 minutes to 20, 20 points to 60... it's a different job. That's the best way I can put it. Last year I had a job. This year I had, I don't know, a real one.

Jan Kowalczyk

And you wore an A. You're 22.

Mike Cammalleri

Yeah. And I'll be honest, when they told me at camp I laughed. I literally laughed. I said, 'You got the wrong guy.' And they're like, no, you're it. So I carried it around for about a week before it felt real.

Jan Kowalczyk

Did it change how the veterans in that room treated you? Adam Deadmarsh, Zigmund Palffy, the guys who've been around a while?

Mike Cammalleri

A little. I think they started talking to me more. Before, I was just the young guy, the energy guy. After the letter, they'd come up in the room and ask me what I was seeing on the ice. And I don't mean to sound important. I'm not. But the door opened, you know?

Jan Kowalczyk

So what changed? Last year to this year. Was it the coach? Was it you? Was it the elbow, the December return? I want the story.

Mike Cammalleri

It was the coach. I'll say that up front. Last year I was, you know, floating. Fourth line, third line, wherever. Not bad, but not me. And when I came back from the elbow, coach pulled me into his office. And he said, 'I'm not going to ease you back in. I need you on the top power play and I need you with the second line.' And I said, 'I'm ready.' And he looked at me and said, 'Prove it.' And that was it. That was the whole conversation.

Jan Kowalczyk

He handed you a role and said go prove it. You'd just come off an elbow. Did that not rattle you?

Mike Cammalleri

It rattled me. I was thinking, 'I hope I can still skate.' But here's the thing. I missed the start of the season, came back December 14th, and in that time I sat and I watched. I saw what the coach wanted and I didn't have to be in it yet. So when I got out there I wasn't guessing. And the coach, he wasn't going to hand me anything. I had to earn every shift back. It was nerve-wracking but it was clean.

Jan Kowalczyk

Five years ago you're 17. You walk into Michigan, freshman year, 13 goals in 39 games. What is that kid thinking?

Mike Cammalleri

He's thinking he's way out of his league, you know? I was a year ahead of most of those guys. I'd just graduated at 17 and I'm on a college campus and I'm tiny. And the guys on that team, some of them were 22, 23. So my whole game was, don't get hurt, don't look stupid, get the puck out. That was it. That was the whole game plan.

Jan Kowalczyk

You got drafted 49th overall by LA in 2001. You said no. You went back to Michigan. The Sweater's asking: did that cost you two years or did it give you everything?

Mike Cammalleri

It gave me everything. And I don't say that because it worked out. I'd say that if I was sitting in the AHL right now. Because I got to be a guy. At Michigan I was the top guy, I was in a Frozen Four, I played that outdoor game in front of 74,554 people. You don't get that in the AHL at 19. And more than that, I grew up. I think that's the thing people miss. A 19-year-old kid in a locker room with guys who are 28, 30, 35... I wasn't ready for that conversation. Michigan made me ready. I could look Adam Deadmarsh in the eye and say something and mean it. At 19? No way.

Jan Kowalczyk

A 22-year-old with an A and 60 points telling me he wasn't ready at 19.

Mike Cammalleri

Hey, I'm telling you what I felt. You're a kid. You don't even know what the room is. You don't know who's going to say what at 7 in the morning when nobody wants to be there. Michigan taught me how to be in a room full of adults, you know? And this room is easier because most of those guys want to help you.

Jan Kowalczyk

You played a lot of that year with Zigmund Palffy. He finished with 104 points.

Mike Cammalleri

104. Yeah. Ziggy made me look good. He's got hands that, I can't even, he'll pull it back on a defenceman and I'm like, 'How did he do that?' And he's 33, he's been in this league forever, and he still plays like he's proving something. So I just try to be there, make myself open, and he'll find me. That was about the best job I had all year.

Jan Kowalczyk

And Jason Allison at centre.

Mike Cammalleri

Alzo's a different kind of hockey player. He's not a burner, but he sees the play two seconds before I do. I used to want to make the play too fast. This year I waited half a beat more, and the lane opened. That's Alzo. I just watched him all year and tried to steal it.

Jan Kowalczyk

37 assists. Last year 9. What are you seeing?

Mike Cammalleri

Time. That's all it is. When you're in for 11 minutes you're on a short shift, you want to make one play, get off the ice. You're not looking for the second option. At 20, 21 minutes you're in the game. You've got 40 seconds with the puck and you actually look up, you actually see the guy in the slot, and you make the pass. The 37 came because I had time to make them.

Jan Kowalczyk

The Bureau has you at 83 overall, base 79, up six on the Development Index. Potential at 98. What do you do with a 98?

Mike Cammalleri

I don't, I genuinely don't know what to do with a 98. 83 is what I feel, because I'm not even a first-liner, I'm second. And I know what FIGH 50 means because I get pushed off the puck every night by a defenceman who doesn't care about my feelings. The 98 is nice, it's flattering, but it's not a Tuesday night in February, you know?

Jan Kowalczyk

Let's stay there. FIGH 50, SPBI 55, PCBI 60. What are you doing about it?

Mike Cammalleri

Getting hit. On purpose. It's not the highlight reel, you know? I go to the corners, behind the net, and I just, I stay there longer this year than last year. I think I've gotten better. I don't think I've fixed it. I need another year, maybe two. Reps. That's all it is.

Jan Kowalczyk

Your team won 25 games. 25 and 39. You had 60 points on a team that lost 39. How do you stay hungry when the record looks like that?

Mike Cammalleri

You don't look at it. I'm serious. If I look at 25 wins I can't play the next night the same way. So I look at my shift. Did I make the right read? Did I backcheck? Did I win my battle? That's the whole thing. If I'm getting better every week I'm not going to dwell on the record. We'll fix it. They've got guys coming.

Jan Kowalczyk

One year left on the deal. $1.10M. Does that go through your head?

Mike Cammalleri

What goes through my head is I better not get hurt before it's up. No, seriously, it's a business. I don't make contracts, I play. If I play the way I did this year, or better, the money takes care of itself. Numbers talk. That's all I can do.

Jan Kowalczyk

Five years ago you were 17, walking onto a college campus. Where do you want to be in five?

Mike Cammalleri

I want to be the guy they trust in June. That's it. I don't care about the A anymore, I don't care about 60 points. I want a team that's good enough to be playing when it's hot outside and I want to be a piece of it that they lean on. If the points come with it, sure. But I want to be the guy in the playoff run. That's the whole goal.

Jan Kowalczyk

Mike Cammalleri from Los Angeles Kings®. Thank you for sitting down, Mike.

Mike Cammalleri

Thanks, Jan. Appreciate it.
