Brodeur Holds The Ice, And The Devils Roll To Three Straight

The goaltender says the third period is where the shutouts were dying and that he stopped thinking about the score; the Devils are on a three-game win streak with the lowest goals against in the Atlantic.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelTuesday, 20 December 2005

2:01 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Martin Brodeur93 stopped all 17 shots as New Jersey Devils beat Ottawa 3-0 on Sunday night. The Devils sit at 43 points in 29 games, two back of the Rangers, on a three-game win streak with 65 goals against, lowest in the Atlantic division. In January Brodeur told The Tunnel's Marcus Bell the shutouts weren't coming because of a late third-period goal; tonight he held the ice clean for sixty.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here in the corridor with Martin Brodeur after a 3-0. You told Marcus in January the shutouts were dying on one late third-period goal and you couldn't make the save that stopped it. Tonight, sixty, nothing gets through.

Martin Brodeur

Yeah, for sure. The guys, they stayed on their assignments in that last period. We had the lead and usually I'm expecting, you know, one loose puck, one screen, somebody gets a free look from the side. Tonight nobody got that look. That's the defense doing the work.

Jan Kowalczyk

Seventeen shots all night from Ottawa. Were you tested at all, or was this mostly about staying ready for the one that might sneak through?

Martin Brodeur

No, no, I had work to do. There was a sequence maybe eight minutes in the third, Langenbrunner blocked one, then the second one came out and the puck, she's right there in the crease and I'm fighting for it. So it's not, you know, it's not a 42-save night, but you can't relax. That's the thing. In a two-nothing game it's harder to stay in than a four-four game. For sure.

Jan Kowalczyk

Three straight wins, 43 points in 29 games, 65 goals against which is the lowest in your division by a wide margin. What does that room feel like right now?

Martin Brodeur

I think we're tighter than we were in November. The structure, the guys are, they know where they're supposed to be without looking at the bench. And you can see it. Sixty-five in twenty-nine, that's not luck. Rafalski scored two tonight from the blue line. Two goals by a defenseman, in a 3-0 game. That's, that's when you know everyone's playing the right way.

Jan Kowalczyk

So the approach in that third period, has it changed since January? You said the problem then was thinking about the shutout, getting tight in the last ten minutes.

Martin Brodeur

I don't think it's changed. I think I stopped listening to it. If I'm in the third and I'm thinking, oh, don't let one in tonight, I'm already not ready. So the approach is every shot, first to last, same thing. I don't protect anything. I play the game. And if the zero stays, the zero stays. That's it.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're up one on the Bureau's Development Index right now, the first time he's moved on you all season. Does that track with how you feel?

Martin Brodeur

I don't look at it. I know people do, that's fine. For me the work is the same every day. The Index, the grades, that's outside. What's inside is the next save.

Jan Kowalczyk

Martin Brodeur, three in a row and a shutout. Thank you very much. Good night.

Martin Brodeur

Merci, Jan. Good night.
