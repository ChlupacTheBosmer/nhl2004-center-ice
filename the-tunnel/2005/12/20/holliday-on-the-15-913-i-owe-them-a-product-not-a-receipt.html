Holliday on the 15,913: "I owe them a product, not a receipt"

Washington's general manager sits with The Tunnel to talk about $80 seats, the league's worst per-point figure, and what he sees when he looks at Tampa Bay.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelTuesday, 20 December 2005

4:01 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Shawn Holliday runs Washington Capitals with a payroll of $57.05M that has returned 23 points through 30 games, the worst cost-per-point in the league at $2.48M. The building still holds 15913 on a season average at $80 a seat, and the club books a $21.57M profit regardless. Marcus Bell sits with him in the studio.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here, studio sit-down. Shawn Holliday, general manager of Washington. You're 7 and 18 and 5, last in the Southeast, on a seven-game streak I'd rather not read to you. But 15,913 people are still buying $80 tickets. What do you make of that?

Shawn Holliday

I make of it that they're better people than the product on the ice right now. You know, that's not a line I'd run in a press release. But you sit in that box and you watch 15,913 of them stand up after a period where we're outshot 14 to 6, and they're still clapping, and you think, they didn't have to. $80 is not nothing. They put it on a kid's birthday, they put it on a date, they drive from Baltimore on a Tuesday. And we give them what we gave them last week. I don't sleep great about it.

Marcus Bell

Let me press on the business side, though, because the numbers are unusual. You're taking in $51.43M, you're turning a $21.57M profit, and you've got the worst per-point figure in the league. $2.48M per point. Does the business working make it easier to sleep, or harder?

Shawn Holliday

Harder. I mean, you'd think easier. The owner's not calling me at midnight asking where the money went. But here's the thing. If we were losing money too, at least the pain was shared. The whole building would feel it. Instead I've got a healthy P and L sheet and 23 points on the board, and that means the money isn't the problem. The problem is what I did with it. And that's on me. Nobody else's chair.

Marcus Bell

You mentioned it's not the money. But Tampa Bay is in your division. They've got $59.79M committed, they're 14 and 10 and 5, they're paying $1.46M per point. You're at $57.05M. What do you see when you look at what they've built?

Shawn Holliday

I see a club that got its first piece right and then built around it. Vincent Lecavalier, 25 years old, $2.60M on the contract, 39 points in 33 games. You know what that is? That is a man on the same deal as a guy who's giving you a fraction of that, and the contract was done two years ago when nobody else wanted him on those numbers. That's not a payroll story. That's an evaluation story. We've got pieces, I'll give you that, but I haven't got a center like that and we both know it.

Marcus Bell

So if you look at the deadline, 89 days out on the calendar, what are you looking for? Not a name, but the shape of the thing.

Shawn Holliday

You're asking me to tell you what I want to buy on television, Marcus. I'm not going to do that to my own room. But I'll tell you the shape. I need top-six help up the middle. I've got bodies, I've got two guys making over $4.00M who could play a third line on most nights, and that's not a complaint, those men work their tails off. But I'm not finding a first-center floor on the roster and you can't coach your way to one. The scouting room has been feeding me evaluations every week since October. I'm patient but I'm not patient forever.

Marcus Bell

You've got the best travel budget in the league, level 5. The players fly well, the rest is good. Does that buy you anything you don't see on the scoresheet?

Shawn Holliday

It buys you a Thursday in Vancouver where a man can sleep in a real bed and not a coach bus through the Okanagan. That's real. That's worth something. Does it win me a game? No. I've been 5 and 15 on the road this year and the plane was comfortable every single time. You've got to build the rest of the house when the man gets off it, and I haven't done that yet. The practice rink is where the rest of the house gets built, and I'll be the first to tell you mine is behind where I want it.

Marcus Bell

Last one. You got the 15,913. What do you say to them?

Shawn Holliday

I say I see you. I see you buying the ticket, I see you standing up after we've just scored nothing for a long stretch, and I see you coming back the next game. That's not a transaction, $80 and a hot dog. That's a person choosing this team on a Tuesday night in December when the standings say don't. And I'm going to fix this. I can't promise you a Cup by May. I can promise you I'm not going to stop trying to get it right. Shawn Holliday, thanks Marcus.
