Martin Havlat: 'That year teach me I can't play if I want to give it back every time'

The Martin Havlat sits at 50 points in 48 games after a season that nearly ended his own, and his $0.90M contract expires in June.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelTuesday, 20 December 2005

2:52 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Martin Havlat90 posted 103 points as a 22-year-old, then gave away almost the whole of 2004-05: 30 games, 29 points, 35 penalty minutes. At 24, Havlat sits at 50 points in 48 games for the Ottawa Senators Senators, six points above his base on the Bureau's Development Index and past the 86 ceiling they once projected, with a contract expiring after this season.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here from the studio with Martin Havlat of the Ottawa Senators. Thanks for sitting down with us, Martin. You've been good this year.

Martin Havlat

Yeah, thank you. I think it's going okay.

Marcus Bell

We ran a profile on you this week. The Bureau had you at a ceiling of 86. You're sitting at an 88 overall now, six points above your base on the Development Index. You're past the number they gave you. Does that land at all?

Martin Havlat

I mean, I don't think about this number every day, you know? I just try to play my game. If they say 86, 88, that's okay. I want the team to win. That matter more than the number for me.

Marcus Bell

50 points in 48 games. That's the highest point total in the league right now. You're playing alongside Daniel Alfredsson, Marian Hossa. What's making this click?

Martin Havlat

Yeah, Alfredsson is, how you say, the best guy I play with. He make me better. He see the ice different, you know? And the team, we all help each other. This year we just, we play the good game together. That's the difference.

Marcus Bell

Let's go to that last year. 30 games, 29 points, 35 penalty minutes. You came off 82 games and 103 points. What happened?

Martin Havlat

That year was difficult. I don't play the good way, I think. And the 35 minutes, that's not me. I know that. I was, how you say... I was angry on the ice. Every time someone hit me, I want to give it back. And you can't play if you want to give it back every shift.

Marcus Bell

You got suspended twice in 2003-04. Four games between them, a kick and a high-stick. Hitchcock said somebody was going to make you eat your lunch. That stuff was following you.

Martin Havlat

No, I don't think it follow me. I make it myself. I think that's, that's important. Nobody make me do the high-stick, nobody make me kick. It's me. And then the 35 last year, same thing. I make it. It's not somebody following me. I follow my own bad idea.

Marcus Bell

This year: 4 penalty minutes in 48 games. That's the answer?

Martin Havlat

Yeah. I control more. If someone hit me, okay, I get up, I go next shift. That's it. I learn this from that year. That year teach me: speed is not everything. You need to be patient. I have the speed, okay, but last year I think I can do everything by myself, and it doesn't work.

Marcus Bell

You're 24. You just lost a year you don't get back. Does that change how you think about what comes after the playing? Even at 24?

Martin Havlat

Yeah, it does. Before that year I think, I'm young, I have time, I don't think about this. After the year I only play 30 games, I think: you don't know when it stop. Maybe I go back to Czech. Mlada Boleslav is home, you know? Small city but it's home. Maybe I do something with hockey, coaching maybe. I don't know. But not yet. I don't want to think too much.

Marcus Bell

You're in the last year of your contract. $0.90 million. Does the Ottawa part of your future sit in your mind?

Martin Havlat

I like it here. Alfredsson is here, Hossa, the group, it's good. I think if I play good, the team will want me here. The money, I don't worry about this. I worry about the game.

Marcus Bell

Speed 99 in the Book. The top attribute you've got. How does it feel out there when you get open?

Martin Havlat

You know, I don't think about it when I'm skating. I just go. But sometimes I have the puck and the defenseman is turning and I think, he's not getting back. And I use it. I don't show it for the fans. I use it.

Marcus Bell

Martin Havlat, thanks very much for the time. Back to you.

Martin Havlat

Thank you, Marcus. Thank you.
