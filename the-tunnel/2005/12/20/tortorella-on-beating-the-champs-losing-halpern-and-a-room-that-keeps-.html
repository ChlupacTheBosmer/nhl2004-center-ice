Tortorella on beating the champs, losing Halpern, and a room that keeps finding a way

After a 4-3 overtime win over the top club in the league, the Lightning coach talked about his fourth-line center's absence, a goalie carrying 30 of 33 starts, and why he isn't asking for anything from upstairs.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelTuesday, 20 December 2005

4:26 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

John Tortorella's Tampa Bay Lightning beat Toronto Maple Leafs 4-3 in overtime on December 19, the Lightning's second straight win and their sixth in their last eight. Tortorella has played Nikolai Khabibulin92 in 30 of 33 games this season, and with Jeff Halpern77 out until at least January 13 with an abdomen injury, the bottom-6 center spot has been passed around. The Lightning sit first in the Southeast at 41 points and play four times in the next eight days.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here in the corridor at St. Petersburg. Coach, you just beat the best team in the league, the Cup champions, 4-3 in overtime. What did you see from that group tonight that you haven't seen some nights?

John Tortorella

Well, first thing, give those guys credit. Thirty-three games in and they're fifty-one points deep, they're not there by accident. But I thought our compete level tonight, obviously, was high. We managed the puck better in the second and third than we did in the first. We gave up some rushes we can't give up against a team like that, and we got away with it. We didn't get away with it the other night against them but tonight we did. So you take it.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're on a two-game win streak now, six in your last eight. But the season hasn't been a straight line. How do you read this group's ceiling when you look at it right now?

John Tortorella

I'm not going to sit here and talk about a ceiling in December. You're asking me what's the best version of this team and I'll tell you I don't know yet. What I do know is the character is there. Forty-one points, that's not where we wanted to be, but the dressing room's not in anybody's face, nobody's pointing. That tells me more than a win streak, you know. That tells me when we get healthy, when Gaborik's not there but when Halpern's back, when we've got a full deck, this thing could be dangerous.

Jan Kowalczyk

Halpern. How is he? You said January 13 on the injury report, but what have you been told about the timeline and what does it do to your bottom six in the meantime?

John Tortorella

Abdomen, that's the designation. I've spoken to the medical staff, they say thirteen. I'm not going to second-guess them, that's not my job. What it does is... you lose a guy who kills penalties, you lose a faceoff guy, you lose a body in the corners. Svitov's been in there, Sutherby's been in there, Robitaille's carried a load. They're not Halpern offensively but I didn't expect that. I need those guys to win their battles and manage the puck in their own end, and mostly they've done that.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're using Khabibulin in thirty of your thirty-three games. That's ninety-one percent of the starts. He's thirty-two years old. Are you watching that number and thinking about what happens in April?

John Tortorella

Yeah, I'm watching it. What do you want me to do? Sit him and put Grahame in who hasn't played in a month and a half? Look, Khabi's our guy, he's been our guy since the Cup run, he's got the most starts in the league and he's winning them. I trust him. I'm not going to, you know, protect him from himself at the expense of tonight. We're in a race, every point matters in this division. If I start having conversations about March in December, I've already lost the room.

Jan Kowalczyk

The room's mood. You've got the highest average in the league according to the Bureau's Morale Scale, ninety-eight point two. Is that the wins, or is something else going on in there?

John Tortorella

You're asking me about a number somebody published. I don't look at it, I don't need a number to tell me what's going on in that room. I've been here since 2001, I know what those guys look like on a Tuesday morning when nobody's watching. They're together. I think the character we had in 2004, that thing that made the Cup, I think it's still in the building. They don't quit on each other, they don't quit on the system, and that goes a long way when you don't have everybody.

Jan Kowalczyk

Coach, you've got four games in eight days before Christmas. Nashville Tuesday, Washington Thursday, Boston on the twenty-seventh. Is that a stretch you've circled on the calendar as a real test?

John Tortorella

Every game in this league's a test, you know? That's the cliché and I hate it, but it's true. Nashville's forty-five points, they're right there with us, that's a four-point game basically. Washington's struggling but they'll play you, they always play you. And Boston's on a three-game win streak, they're forty-two points, they're not going to roll over. So yeah, it matters. It matters a lot. We're one point back of New York in the division race and we can't afford to give any of those away.

Jan Kowalczyk

One more question before I let you go. You've got a gap at fourth-line center, you've got Khabi playing a ton, and you're in a tight race. Does the front office hear from you about what you need before the trade deadline?

John Tortorella

The front office hears from me every day. That's the question every coach gets in December and I've never given one that's interesting, so I'll give you the same one. We've got a good group, I like our people, I like where we are. We'll see what's available. I'm not going to go on television and tell a roomful of guys I need somebody else. That's not how this works. But yeah, obviously, if there's a guy who can help us in the bottom six, kill penalties, win a draw, who's available, then we're going to look at it. That's not a secret.

Jan Kowalczyk

Coach, thank you for your time. Enjoy the win tonight. Jan Kowalczyk, St. Petersburg, back to you.
