Luongo lost one puck in overtime, and it cost two points

The Vezina-winning netminder faced 36 shots and felt every one of the four that went in, starting with the one in overtime.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelTuesday, 20 December 2005

2:03 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Roberto Luongo95 faced 36 shots, gave up four, and watched Tampa Bay Lightning score in overtime for a 4-3 win at home. His Toronto Maple Leafs lose their first since a stretch that had them 21-7-1, top of the league, 3 points clear of Dallas Stars. Luongo, the reigning Conn Smythe winner, said the overtime goal was on him.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here with Roberto Luongo, who just sat in that net for sixty-plus minutes and watched Tampa Bay win it in overtime. Thirty-six shots, four that went in. Did you lose track of the traffic at any point?

Roberto Luongo

Yeah, 36, that's, that's a lot of bodies. In the third, I was losing pucks from, I think, the left side of the hash, two, three times, and the screen was, for sure, it was set up good. But you know, I had to find the puck better. That's... that's on me.

Marcus Bell

Talk me through overtime. One chance, one goal. Was there a save in there you think you get back?

Roberto Luongo

Yeah, well, the one, the one that went in. I was... I lost sight of it for, I think, a half a second. And in overtime, four-on-four, everything's, everything's wide open. You can't, you can't afford that half a second. No, I don't think it was a great angle. I could have, I could have been... better.

Marcus Bell

The defensive pairings looked different in the third. You could see the shift on the bench. Did you feel that from behind the net?

Roberto Luongo

Yeah, for sure. I could feel it. More... more engaged in the corners, less giving ground, you know? The guys in front of me, they, they came out different. And Klesla, he was, the pairing, he was holding the line, keeping me cleaner in the slot. And I felt that. I definitely felt that. But overtime, four-on-four, it's a different game. All that structure, it, it opens up and you're just... you're just reading the puck.

Marcus Bell

I spoke to you around this time last December, when you were still in Florida. You were 8-11 and you told me wins meant more to you than shutouts. You're on the best team in the league now, 51 points. Does that answer still hold?

Roberto Luongo

Yeah, I remember, I remember that one, I was so... you know, when you're 8-11 every goal is, is the end of the world. Every single one. Here, when Nashy scores, or when the guys play like they're capable of, like, Gaborik tonight, Mogilny... it's, it's a different feeling. You trust the guys more. And yeah, the win is everything. 51 points, who cares about a shutout in a 4-3? Nobody. I don't.

Marcus Bell

Last one. Your streak's over, one game back from... well, you're still 3 points ahead of Dallas. Do you let this one sit or do you put it down tonight?

Roberto Luongo

I put it down. You have to, for sure. Because tomorrow, or the next game, you're back in there and you can't carry this. But tonight? Tonight I'm thinking about that half second. And I'm going to think about it on the bus and I'm going to think about it when I get home. And then I'm done. The guys don't, the guys don't need me thinking about it the next night. That's... that's it.

Marcus Bell

Roberto Luongo, thank you. Back to you.
