Rick DiPietro Wants the Boring Save

The Islanders' goaltender holds a 99 pass grade and a 50 consistency grade, and with zero years left on his contract, he thinks he knows which one the summer will decide on.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelThursday, 8 December 2005

9:44 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Rick DiPietro86 went from 3 wins in 20 games as a nineteen-year-old to 63 games and 32 wins in 2003-04, the most he has played. His pass grade of 99 is his best mark and his consistency is 50. With zero years left on his $1.10M contract, the New York Islanders goaltender sat down with Jan Kowalczyk.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here with Rick DiPietro, the goaltender for the New York Islanders. Rick, you're averaging 58.8 minutes a game this season, basically every night. You were drafted first overall in 2000 and you made your debut against Buffalo on January 27, 2001. Five years later, is there any resemblance between the kid who walked in then and the man sitting here now?

Rick DiPietro

Barely. Nineteen years old, first game, and I didn't know where to stand. I mean, I knew the textbook stuff, but the game itself was going a hundred miles an hour and I was just chasing pucks all night. The kid who walked in there doesn't know that putting it on Roman Hamrlik's tape at the blue line is worth more than a punt to center ice. He doesn't know what a breakout read is. The guy here now, I think he's got that. Everything else is what it is.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let's start with the passing. The Bureau has your pass grade at 99. That's the top mark on your book. What does a 99 mean to a goaltender?

Rick DiPietro

It means the coaches think I put the puck where it's supposed to go, you know. Nobody works on passing. There's no passing circuit in a goalie's practice. You're supposed to freeze it or clear it out, that's the whole job. I grew up watching goalies who could throw it a hundred feet up the ice and I just liked doing it. So the 99 is flattering. I'll take it. I don't know what else to do with it.

Jan Kowalczyk

Does it change how you work with your defensemen specifically?

Rick DiPietro

Roman and Janne Niinimaa, they know I can find them through the neutral zone. A lot of nights you're dumping and chasing and that's fine, sometimes that's just the game. But when we've got a little ice and Roman's walking the blue line, I can put it on his stick from the crease and he's got numbers on the other side. Alexei is in the slot before the defense turns around. That's a goal. I've seen it enough times now where I can feel it before the pass, you know. That's the 99. It's not a trick. It's just, do they trust you with it.

Jan Kowalczyk

You went from 3 wins in 20 games in your first season to 63 games and 32 wins in 2003-04. That's a whole different career. What happened in between?

Rick DiPietro

Ten games. That's the answer. Ten games my third year, a playoff game, and that was it. I sat. I watched. And Garth Snow was in the other net, so I got a front-row seat to how a guy prepares every single morning. Not just the saves, the morning of. The bus time, the walk-through, the way he handles the first ten minutes of practice. And then the fourth year the coach says you're it. Sixty-three games. And volume changes everything. You see a two-on-one twenty times a season and your feet go on their own by the fiftieth time. Twenty games, you never see it enough.

Jan Kowalczyk

Garth Snow's still there. Eighty overall. What does having that caliber of backup do to you?

Rick DiPietro

It means I can't coast. Garth will grab you after practice, it's a Tuesday in January, you want to go home, and he starts talking about your rebound angle like you're in a film session. And he's right. He's got no patience for a guy who sleepwalks through warm-ups. A guy with his resume could start almost anywhere and he just takes the role. That's harder to watch than you'd think, and it keeps me honest.

Jan Kowalczyk

Your consistency grade from the Bureau is 50. You're 7 and 12 in 21 games this year. I think we both know those numbers line up.

Rick DiPietro

Yeah. They do. I can see it on tape. There's a game where I'm across the crease, I catch a shot with my glove, and it looks, I don't know, it looks like something from a video game. And the next night, a defenseman gets screened behind the net and I'm a half-step slow and the puck's in. The 50 is me saying I haven't figured out how to be good for sixty minutes straight. I can be great for twenty. I can be poor for ten. The sixty is where I've got to live, and I'm not there yet.

Jan Kowalczyk

The Bureau's Development Index has you nine spots above your base grade of 79. You're showing 86 overall right now. That's a big move. Does that change how you feel in the crease?

Rick DiPietro

I'm not checking my number every morning. But you feel it. The reads come a half-second faster this year. A winger crosses the blue line and I'm already on the post before the shot comes. At nineteen that didn't happen. At nineteen I was reacting after the puck moved. This year I'm reacting before it moves. And I think that's just, you've seen it enough times now. Sixty-three the year before, twenty-two last year, twenty-one so far this year. You see a play for the hundredth time, your body just knows.

Jan Kowalczyk

The record you're proudest of. Sixty-three games in 2003-04. Why that one?

Rick DiPietro

Because I started it as a question mark. Three and fifteen your first year. Ten games your third year. And then you've got sixty-three games in front of you and you think, can my body do this? Can my head do this? Every other day for four months? And I did it. Thirty-two wins, one shutout. And the shutout, I remember the last five minutes. You're trying not to look at the clock, your defenseman is in front of you blocking shots, and you just hold it together with both hands. That's the year I keep. Not the shutout specifically. The sixty-three. That's proof I can do the job every day.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're 24 now. Your body at 19 versus 24, what's different?

Rick DiPietro

Twenty-four, I make forty saves and I'm on the bike the next morning fine. Nineteen, I was sore for two days. The legs are better, obviously. But the mind, I think the mind is more tired now. At nineteen you don't know what a bad night costs you. You just skate out and hope. At twenty-four I know exactly what a soft goal at 7-12 of the second period does to that locker room on the bus. I've seen it. I've been on the bus. That's heavier than the sore legs.

Jan Kowalczyk

Your contract has zero years left on it.

Rick DiPietro

Zero.

Jan Kowalczyk

How much does that sit with you day to day?

Rick DiPietro

At night. I think it sits with me more at night than in the room. In the room I'm on the next read, the next save, the next game. At night I'll lie there and think, I'm twenty-four, I've spent four years figuring this out, and the team gets to decide in the summer if four years is enough. You don't get to decide that. They do. And I think about that more than I'd like to admit on television.

Jan Kowalczyk

You grew up in Lewiston, Maine, moved to Winthrop at ten. Do you want to stay?

Rick DiPietro

Jan, I've been an Islander since they drafted me. My family drives down to games. Where else would I go? I want to play here. But I know how this works. They're allowed to look. They look at every position, every summer, every contract. I understand that part completely. I just hope they look at what I can give them and think it's enough.

Jan Kowalczyk

Alexei Yashin is at 34 points. Mark Parrish is at 31. What does this team's offense look like from your crease?

Rick DiPietro

It's a different kind of game. Alexei's got 34 points and I don't think he's even at his best. Mark is the guy who won't tell you he's having a good night, but he is. And when they're moving the puck in the offensive zone, you get the rush the other way and you can see the three-on-two forming, and you've got to be sharp. You're not making the same dump-and-chase save forty times. You're making real saves. And that's what I want. I'd rather make ten hard saves than forty routine ones. Sounds backwards, I know.

Jan Kowalczyk

One last one. If you could trade either the 99 pass or the 50 consistency, and make the other one the same, which goes?

Rick DiPietro

The 99. You know, I'd trade the pass grade away in a heartbeat if it meant I was a 99 every single night. The pass is, it's nice. People notice it. But a 99 consistency, that's the guy the coach puts in Game 7 and forgets about. You stop thinking about whether he's going to be good tonight. That's what I'm chasing. The 50 is what's keeping me up, not the 99.

Jan Kowalczyk

Seven and twelve in twenty-one. What does the rest of the season need to look like?

Rick DiPietro

Boring. I want boring. I don't need the impossible glove save every night. I need to not give up the soft one at 7-something in the second period. If I can just make the routine save for sixty minutes, we're a better team and the record fixes itself. I just haven't done it enough yet. That's the whole thing. Not enough yet.

Jan Kowalczyk

Rick DiPietro, thank you very much for your time.

Rick DiPietro

Thank you, Jan. Appreciate it.
