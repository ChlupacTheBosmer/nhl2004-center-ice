Luongo: 'We were half a beat slow, and that's the whole game'

The Toronto goalie on a 28-of-31 night, the bench going quiet, and why he thinks the fix is shorter than anyone wants to admit.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelThursday, 8 December 2005

2:28 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Roberto Luongo [593] and Toronto Maple Leafs sat first in the Northeast with 40 points in 27 games before Tuesday night. He stopped 28 of 31 in a 1-3 home loss to New Jersey Devils, who pushed their own win streak to 2. Kowalczyk caught him in the corridor after the handshake.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Roberto, Jan Kowalczyk, The Tunnel. I said back in January, when you were still a Panther, let's talk again when the record is longer than four. You're at 16 and 7 now, first in the Northeast, 40 points, and tonight you gave up 3 on 31 at home. How does this one land?

Roberto Luongo

Yeah. January. You remember that. It lands bad, for sure. 28 of 31 at home, that's not... when we win 4-1 the night before and you come back here and give up 3, you know, you're not happy. That's the honest answer.

Jan Kowalczyk

Is there a moment where you felt the night was going? You only give up a shade over 2 a game, 59 in 27, and tonight it's 3.

Roberto Luongo

Yeah, it's, it's the second one for me. You're in a 1-1 game, you're fine, you're tracking, and then you get the second and suddenly I'm chasing all night. 31 pucks, every time I made a save the next shift we're on our heels. I lost him once in the second, I think, behind a screen. I don't see the puck and then it's 2-1 and that's the one that, you know, that's the one that tells you tonight is not your night.

Jan Kowalczyk

You've been winning all season, 8 and 1 on the road even. From inside, what happens in a room that's been winning for two months when it hits a wall like this?

Roberto Luongo

You get quiet. That's what it is. Usually when we go down a goal, there's guys on the bench, there's shouting, everybody's saying something. Tonight there was maybe 2, 3 shifts where nobody said a word. And for this group, that's... you know, that's not normal. The guys are quiet and I'm quiet. That's when I knew.

Jan Kowalczyk

Special teams took it from you tonight?

Roberto Luongo

No, I don't think it's, I don't think it's just special teams. We weren't killing it well, that's true, not enough pressure on the puck, the man in front is getting free and nobody's moving him. But even 5-on-5 I felt like we were half a beat slow. They shoot, they just shoot, they get to the point and they put it on net, and we were standing. That's not special teams, that's us not being, I don't know, tight enough. We have to be better, that's it.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're 8 and 1 on the road, which is the best mark in the division. Tonight's at home. What's the difference in your net?

Roberto Luongo

I don't know. I don't know if there is one, honestly. On the road everything is simple, you get on the bus, you get to the rink, you play. Here the guys want to give something to the people, and I think sometimes that makes you, you know, force it a little. Try too hard. And when you're trying too hard you're thinking, and when you're thinking you're a half beat slow. And then I'm a half beat slow too, because they're in front of me.

Jan Kowalczyk

40 points, first in the Northeast, 16 and 7. Is tonight a blip or is it a warning?

Roberto Luongo

It's one game. I'll tell you that. But nobody in this room wants it to be two. One night, you sleep on it, you fix it tomorrow. Two nights in a row and the standings start looking different. So we fix it. It's a short thing, the fix. It's not, it's not complicated. We just weren't there tonight and tomorrow we will be.

Jan Kowalczyk

Roberto Luongo, thank you. Back to you.
