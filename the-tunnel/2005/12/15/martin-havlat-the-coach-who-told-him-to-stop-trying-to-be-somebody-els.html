Martin Havlat: the coach who told him to stop trying to be somebody else

The Ottawa winger, whose Development Index sits at +6 this season, describes the coaching instruction that dropped his penalties from 18 to 4, and what Daniel Alfredsson teaches without words.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelThursday, 15 December 2005

6:48 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Martin Havlat88 is in his sixth NHL season with Ottawa Senators and sits at 88 in the Bureau's Book, up six on the Development Index, past a ceiling grade of 86 set when he was a prospect. The Sweater profile that opened this sit-down called his speed 99 and his ceiling 86; he broke through it before his twenty-fifth birthday. He spoke with Jan Kowalczyk in the studio about the coaching change that followed his two suspensions in 2003-04, about Daniel Alfredsson89, and about being the guy the young ones watch.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here. This is the studio sit-down, no pads, no boards, just the two chairs. Martin Havlat of Ottawa. Thanks for coming in.

Martin Havlat

Thank you. It's good to be here, you know. I like the chair better than the boards.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let's start where the Sweater started. The profile last month: Speed 99, Ceiling 86. The Bureau's Book has you at 88 today. Up six on the Development Index. You were drafted twenty-sixth in 1999 and the people who put those numbers together said, this is the top. You've already walked through the door they built for you. Does that land anywhere?

Martin Havlat

You know, I see the number, of course. Everyone shows you the number. But when I'm on the ice I'm not thinking, oh, I'm an eighty-eight tonight. No. I play my game. The puck goes in, it doesn't. The Development Index, the plus six, that is good, I feel good. My legs, they feel good. That is what it means to me. The page, how you say, the page is for you guys.

Jan Kowalczyk

But let me push on this. Your Speed is 99. You are the fastest man in this league. Your Fight grade is 50, your Faceoff is 51, your Shooting Percentage is 54. That is the widest gap I think I have ever seen in the Book between what you can do with your feet and what the Book says you can do in a corner. The ceiling of 86 was a projection built around that gap. A man with a 50 Fight does not become a 95. Do you buy that?

Martin Havlat

Yeah. I get it. You look at my Fight at 50 and you think, okay, this is not, this is not a guy who will, how you say, a guy who will push people. But I think, I think they were wrong, you know? Not about the Fight, the Fight is the Fight. But about what that means. You don't have to be a nine in Fight to be a ninety-five overall. You just have to be the fastest and make the other guy stupid. And that is what I do.

Jan Kowalczyk

Now, 2003-04 is the year I want to get to. You score 40 goals, 63 assists, 103 points. Best year you have ever had. And then Ken Hitchcock starts making noise about you after a high-sticking incident, says somebody is going to make you eat your lunch. You get suspended twice that season. Kicking, high-sticking, four games total. Were you getting under people because you were scoring, or because you were trying to be somebody you were not?

Martin Havlat

You know, maybe both. That year I scored a lot and maybe, maybe the goal was enough to get under them. But the other thing, I was trying. I was trying to, how you say, I was trying to show I was not, not a soft guy. Everybody knew I was fast. Everybody knew I could skate. And maybe I thought, no, I need to, I need to give it back. I need to make them scared of me. And the kicking, the high-sticking, that was, that was stupid. I was 22 and I was angry and I thought that is what a real player does.

Jan Kowalczyk

Was it the coach who told you it wasn't?

Martin Havlat

Yeah. He said, he said, Martin, you are trying to be somebody you are not. You are the fastest guy in this league. Make them chase you. Do not chase them. Do not try to finish a check when your whole game is avoiding them. And I remember I was sitting there and I thought, you know, he is right. I am 190 pounds and I am trying to finish a check on guys who are built for it and I am the one who gets hurt. Or suspended. And the penalties went down after that. I saw it in the numbers.

Jan Kowalczyk

Eighteen penalty minutes last season. Fifteen the year before that. Four this year. That is a different player.

Martin Havlat

Yeah. But you know, it is not, it is not the coach fixed it. The coach opened the door, you know? He said the words but I had to listen. And I had to stop trying to prove something I did not need to prove. I do not need to be tough. I need to be fast. They see that now. Maybe the Fight grade will always be 50 and that is fine. I don't want to be a 50 in Fight. But I don't need to be 80 either. I need to be 50 and, and be 99 somewhere else. You know?

Jan Kowalczyk

You are in the room with Daniel Alfredsson. You joined Ottawa in 2000-01 and he has been the man here longer than you have been in this league. He is 89 in the Book, your best teammate. From the left wing, what does his leadership look like?

Martin Havlat

He is quiet. That is the first thing. He is not, he is not the guy yelling in the room, you know? But practice. Every day, he is on the ice first, he leaves last. Same drills. Every day. And you watch that. You are 19 years old and you watch Daniel Alfredsson do the same, the same boring, how you say, the same boring drill for the tenth time and he does it like it is the first. And you think, okay. That is the job. Not the speech. The drill.

Jan Kowalczyk

Do you go to him for anything? A read, a read on the power play?

Martin Havlat

Sometimes. I go to him, I say, Danny, what do you see here? On the power play, on a breakout, something. And he gives you, he gives you three words. Maybe two. Not more. And it is the right two words. He never, he never over-tells you. He just, he gives you the piece and you go. That is the thing I learned most from him. Less is more.

Jan Kowalczyk

You are 24. The room has five guys 24 or under. Are you one of the ones they look at?

Martin Havlat

I don't know. I don't, I don't think of myself like that. But, you know, the other day Prommersberger comes in, the youngest, and he looks around the room and he sees me and I see him see me and I think, okay, I am the, I am the guy in that chair now. Not the rookie in the corner. And that is, that is a feeling you don't plan. It just happens. And I try, I try to be the guy who if they ask, I answer. I don't go around and tell people what to do. That is Danny's room. But if a young guy wants to know something, I tell him. Be yourself. Do not try to be Danny. Do not try to be me. Just, just work.

Jan Kowalczyk

You and Marian Hossa. Czech and Slovak, not exactly neighbors but not far. Does that change anything in the room?

Martin Havlat

We are neighbors. You know how it is. It is nice, you know? You are in the locker room, 30 guys, and you talk and nobody really gets, gets the way you grew up, the, how you say, the food at home, the, the way your mother talks. And then there is Marian and you say one word and he gets it. And you don't have to explain. That is, that is worth something. Not about hockey. About, about the little things.

Jan Kowalczyk

50 points in 48 games this year. Almost a point a game. Last season was 28 in 27. The year before was 103. You are not in the scoring race with Naslund at 46 in 29. Where does this go?

Martin Havlat

You know, I don't look at Naslund. I look at my game. The points are, are fine. 50 in 48, I am happy. But you know what I think about? The four games. Four games in the playoffs last year and out. That is what I think about. Not my point total. I can score in December and I am nothing in April if I am not there in the third period. So, you know, the next step is not more points. It is, it is the same speed, the same legs, the same game when the building is shaking and everybody is, is trying to stop you. And still you make them wrong.

Jan Kowalczyk

That is your HERO grade. 92. The Bureau says you get bigger when it matters.

Martin Havlat

I don't know what the Bureau says. I know what I feel. And when the game is big, I want the puck more. I don't go away. I think, I think that is the one they got right. The HERO 92. Yeah, that one I believe.

Jan Kowalczyk

Martin Havlat of Ottawa. Thanks for the time.

Martin Havlat

Thank you, Jan. It was good, you know. Better than the boards.
