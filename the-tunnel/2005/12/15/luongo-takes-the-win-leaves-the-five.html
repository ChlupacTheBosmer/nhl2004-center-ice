Luongo takes the win, leaves the five

The Maple Leafs goalie on giving up five goals in a 7-5 win, on what Mats Sundin does from behind the net, and on the difference between a number you like and a number that sits easy.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelThursday, 15 December 2005

2:08 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Roberto Luongo Roberto Luongo95 earned his second win in as many nights as Toronto Maple Leafs beat New York Rangers 7-5 at home on 2005-12-14, extending the Maple Leafs' winning streak to three and their record to 19-7-0, 46 points, alone at the top of the Northeast. Mats Sundin92 had 3 goals and 4 points in the last 2 games and lit it up again tonight. After the game, Luongo told Marcus Bell in the tunnel what five goals against costs a man even in a win.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here, in the tunnel at the Air Canada Centre, with Roberto Luongo after a 7-5 win over New York tonight. Roberto, Mats Sundin's got 3 goals and 4 points in the last two, he's everywhere tonight. What's it like watching that from the other end?

Roberto Luongo

Yeah. Mats, he... he's the captain, for sure. He knows when. Three in two games, that's Mats. And you know, when the guys are putting them in like that, it, it lifts a little weight off your shoulders. You breathe easier. You can play your game, not just hang on.

Marcus Bell

You saw 33 shots tonight. You gave up 5. You've got 2 wins in 2 this week. Does the 5 bother you, or are you just happy to have the two?

Roberto Luongo

Yeah. Yeah, it does, for sure. I don't... look, the guys put up seven, so I can't, I don't have the right to... I can't complain. But five goals, no. I don't want that. I have to be better, that's it. Five is five.

Marcus Bell

You're 19 and 7 now, 46 points, 8 ahead of second place in the division. Three straight wins. What does that feel like inside the room when you're in it?

Roberto Luongo

It's good, for sure. The guys are, they're having fun out there. We trust each other right now, I think that's the difference. Nineteen and seven, yeah, those are numbers you like. But we're not... we don't, we don't sit around looking at the standings. The game, she doesn't care about the standings. You play the next one, you play your game, c'est ça.

Marcus Bell

You've got a young D corps in front of you. Is there one guy you look at when you need to steady yourself, when it gets messy?

Roberto Luongo

Klesla. Rostislav, he's... when I need a body between me and the net, he's there. Every time. I look up and he's in the right spot and it's like, okay, we're fine on this one. He's not the loudest, but he's there, for sure.

Marcus Bell

The way this team is built, the way the offense comes at you in waves, does it change how you prepare? Or you just do your thing and trust the 33 shots won't all come at once?

Roberto Luongo

Thirty-three tonight, yeah, she all came at once. No, look, when you've got Mats, you've got Brad, you've got Mogilny putting up what they put up, you know the game opens up. Both ends. They score five, the other team comes at you harder because they have to. You prepare for that. You can't just, you can't get comfortable because they put seven in.

Marcus Bell

When I caught you in Florida, first night with the Panthers, you told me a win meant more to you than a shutout. After giving up five tonight, does that still hold?

Roberto Luongo

Yeah. It holds. A win is a win, for sure. I'll take two and five over one and zero. I'll take it. But but I want both, you know? I want the two and I want the zero. I don't, I can't, I can't pick. I want both. That's the job, hein.

Marcus Bell

Roberto, thank you for your time. Good night. Back to you.
