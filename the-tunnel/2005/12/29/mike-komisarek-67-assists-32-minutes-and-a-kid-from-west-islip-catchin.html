Mike Komisarek: 67 Assists, 32 Minutes, and a Kid From West Islip Catching Up to Himself

The Canadiens' 23-year-old defenseman sits down with Jan Kowalczyk to talk about the assists he's proud of, the 32 minutes a night he's eating this season, and being a high school kid five years ago.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelThursday, 29 December 2005

7:24 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

Mike Komisarek81 had 82 points in 82 games last season, 67 of them assists, in his first year as a full-time NHL defenseman at age 22. He's 23 now, playing 32.0 minutes a night for Montreal Canadiens, and his contract has 0 years left. He sat down in the studio with Jan Kowalczyk to talk about what changed and what he's proudest of.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here, sitting down with Mike Komisarek from the Montreal Canadiens®. Mike, thanks for taking the afternoon. You're 23, you're playing 32.0 minutes a night this year, you had 82 points last season. Most people your age don't know what they want for breakfast yet. How do you sit with all that?

Mike Komisarek

Yeah, I mean, you don't really think about it as a whole. I just, you know, I go to the rink and I play. The 32 minutes, I guess, I'd think it's a lot if I stopped to, like, think about it. But the shifts go by and the next one comes and before you know it you're done. That's, uh, that's the answer I think.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let me go to the record you put up last season. 15 goals, 67 assists, 82 games, 30.4 minutes a night. That's a Norris conversation, even though the Norris went to Bryan McCabe. 67 assists from the blue line, Mike. What stands out to you in that?

Mike Komisarek

Yeah. The 67. Obviously, you know, the goals are nice, 15 is not nothing for a defenseman. But the assists, that's, uh, that's what I wanted to be. When I was coming up, and I was 18 and on Long Island and watching tape, the guys I liked weren't the goal-scorers. They were the guys who could see the ice and make the first pass. So, yeah. The 67 is, I'm proud of that. That's the one that means something to me.

Jan Kowalczyk

You've got a 93 for speed on the Book, at 6 foot 5, 225. That doesn't really go together, does it? I mean, people see the size and then the first shift they watch, they're surprised.

Mike Komisarek

I get it. Everyone gets it. People in West Islip, they watched me at St. Anthony's, and I was all arms and legs, just running around trying not to fall. And then I got longer and faster at the same time. Michigan helped, obviously, Nikiforov's system. He's Lithuanian, you know, he's got this whole thing about edges, about how you get from a to b without, like, loading up. If you're 225 and you can actually open up your stride, people on the other side, they can't, they can't catch you and they can't pin you into the boards. So yeah. The speed is, that's the thing that makes the rest of it work.

Jan Kowalczyk

And with that speed, with a 91 shot speed grade, do you find that defenders step up to block the shot and that opens the passing lane for the 67? Is that the mechanism, the reason for the assists?

Mike Komisarek

Well, it's, it's both. You know, if you can walk in from the point and they know you're going to rip one, the first guy, he steps up, and now his man is open. And the second guy is thinking about stepping up too, so there's this, this moment. And if you've got the feet to get to the half-wall and turn and pick the man on the far side, you know, that's where the assists come from. It's not like I'm out there looking to dangle. I just want to make the read before they close the gap. And a lot of it is the guys in front. Zednik, Michael, those guys find soft spots. You just got to get them the puck.

Jan Kowalczyk

Right, Richard Zednik's got 48 points this year and Michael Nylander's in the mix too. You're playing behind them with Jose Theodore, who's at 87 on the Book. Does it make your job easier, having a guy like that back there?

Mike Komisarek

Yeah, it does. Obviously. If Theo gives up a soft one on a third-period rush, it changes how you play the next shift. You tighten up, you get conservative. But he's, you know, he's been so good this year, 87, that's up there with anyone in the league. And he's just, he calls for the puck, he talks to you, you know? It's not like he's hiding back there. So you relax a little, and when you relax, you make the smarter play instead of the safe play.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let me jump back five years. In 2000, 2001, you're 18. You're at St. Anthony's on Long Island, you're about to go to the NTDP, and then 2001 draft, seventh overall to Montreal. Five years ago you were in high school. What's the difference between that kid and the guy sitting here, besides the obvious?

Mike Komisarek

The obvious is... a lot, yeah. I mean, the kid was, he was scared. I don't, I think people forget that. You get drafted seventh and everyone goes, wow, and you go home to West Islip and your neighbors, they say, well you got it made, and you go to practice the next week and you're the same scrawny kid. Michigan was where I actually, I think I got real. You play college hockey with guys who've been in the program a year longer than you and they don't care that you were drafted. You're just a freshman. So that humbled me. And then the NHL, the first year was 24 games and I played 11 minutes a night and I scored 3 points and I thought, this is where I belong? And then the next year, 82, and I just kept going.

Jan Kowalczyk

11.8 minutes to 32.0. That's, I mean, that's the whole range of what a coach can give you. You've gone from a kid they throw in there for one shift on a power play to a guy who plays against the other team's top line and then jumps on the power play. What's that transition feel like from the inside?

Mike Komisarek

You don't feel it as a jump. That's the thing. It's, you know, you play 14, you play 18, you play 22, and then one night you look at the screen after the game and it says 28 and you go, huh, when did that happen? And now it's 32. And you're tired, obviously, 32 is a long night. You're not, you know, you're not fresh in the third period the way you were when you were playing 15. But you're also, I think, sharper, because you're in every situation. You're on the ice with their best players and you learn their tendencies. You learn who goes short side, who goes far side, who's going to try and get behind you. You learn that stuff by playing, not by sitting.

Jan Kowalczyk

You've got 62 penalty minutes this year versus 40 last year. That's a 55 percent jump. Are you playing a different physical game now?

Mike Komisarek

I think so. Yeah. You know, the first year, I think I was, I was trying not to get penalized, trying to stay out of trouble, keep my shifts clean. And this year I'm in more battles, obviously, because of the minutes, and I think I'm more willing to, like, finish a check instead of turning away from it. 62 is, it's a lot. I don't love it. But, you know, I'm 225 pounds. If I can put a guy into the boards and get him off the puck, that's, that's part of the game. You don't want to be the guy who's big and soft. That's, that's, that's not a line I'm going to use on television, but you know what I mean.

Jan Kowalczyk

I'm going to use it. Mike, the Book has you at 51 for fighting. You're 6 foot 5, 225, you've got an 82 for aggression. People watch you play and they expect you to drop the gloves. Is that a conversation you've had with yourself?

Mike Komisarek

Yeah, everyone wants the big guy to fight. It's, look, I've never been a fighter. I don't want to be one. You know what I mean? I can hit people, I can win battles along the wall, I can be hard to play against without taking my gloves off. And honestly, at 51, if I get in a fight, the other guy, he's, he's not scared, right? I don't have that, that mean look. I'm just, I'm trying to play defense and not go to the box. So, no, that's not a conversation I've had with myself. My coach hasn't asked me either. So, you know, maybe I'm fine with the 51.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let's talk about this year's production. 9 goals, 21 assists, 30 points in 36 games. That's a 68-point pace over 82. Last year was 82. The Development Index has you at plus 10 from your base, overall 81 on the Book. Do you feel like you're playing better or worse than last year?

Mike Komisarek

I think I'm playing better. Honestly. The points are down, but, you know, the role is heavier. Last year, 30.4 minutes, this year 32. I'm in more tough situations, more short-handed, more against top lines. And I think the 9 goals, I'm getting there more, I'm not, I'm not as shy on the rush. So the, the game's different. I don't feel like I'm playing less well. I feel like I'm just being asked to do more of everything and it's, you know, the points spread out a little because of it. But plus 10 on the Index, yeah, that feels right. I feel like I'm, I'm still getting better.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're on $1.10 million with 0 years left. I'll just put it out there. The Sweater ran a profile about what Montreal has to figure out by July. What's the conversation like inside your own head when you think about that?

Mike Komisarek

You know, I try not to think about it during the season. I really do. I've got people for that, I've got my agent. And I love it here. I've been here since I was, what, 19, 20? I came up through this organization, this is my team. I don't know, $1.10 million, I grew up watching hockey in West Islip and a kid in West Islip, he'd take $1.10 million and not ask questions. But, you know, I'm 23 and I had 82 points last year. I think, I think I've earned the right to let my people have the conversation and not think about it on a Tuesday night in December when I've got 32 minutes to play.

Jan Kowalczyk

One last one, Mike. You came to Michigan for two years, 46 points in 80 games. You came to Montreal, first year, 3 points in 24 games. And now you're here at 23, 32 minutes a night. If you could send a message back to that kid at St. Anthony's, the one who was just running around trying not to fall, what do you tell him?

Mike Komisarek

I'd tell him he's going to be fine. He's worried about everything. He's worried about his skating, he's worried about his size, he's worried about whether anyone's going to notice him. And I'd say, yeah, you're going to get a little bigger. You're going to get a little faster. And the game's going to slow down more than you think. Not right away. Not when you're 18. But by 23, maybe 22, you'll wake up and you won't be scared anymore. And that's, that's what I'd tell him. You don't have to be scared. You just have to keep showing up.

Jan Kowalczyk

Mike Komisarek from the Montreal Canadiens®, thank you for the afternoon.
