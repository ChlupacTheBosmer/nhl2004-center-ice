Turco watches from the bench and calls the room the best he's been in

Dallas leads the Pacific at 21-9-5 and Marty Turco is the backup, and he says the road is finally where he said it would be back in January.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelThursday, 29 December 2005

2:34 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Marty Turco95 sat the net for 21 minutes at Nashville Predators on Saturday and lost it 4-1, then watched Jason Bacashihua89 take the next one 4-2 at Florida Panthers on Sunday. Dallas is 21-9-5, first in the Pacific, and Turco knows where he sits in it.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here in the corridor at Sunrise. Marty, Jason Bacashihua stops 20 of 22 tonight and gets the win, and you're the one standing here talking to me. Does that mean something about how the room works?

Marty Turco

Yeah, well, I think it means the media room is short-handed. Look, the guy was sharp. Twenty-two shots, gives up two, they were ugly goals but they were ugly goals against anybody. He made the one I wanted to make. That's all a guy can ask.

Jan Kowalczyk

You were in it Saturday at Nashville and that one got away from you, four goals on 21 shots. How do you process that when the next day your backup wins and you're back to normal?

Marty Turco

You don't process it. I mean, I watched tape this morning. First goal, I'm over-committing to the post and the guy buries the five-hole. I've made that save a hundred times, I didn't make it Saturday. It's in, it's out. I don't sit here and pick at it. You get one in the morning, you watch it once, you move on. What else are you going to do?

Jan Kowalczyk

We talked back in January about your road record and you said the whole thing was legs and sleep, nothing mystical. You're 13 and 10 on the road now. Did the legs come around?

Marty Turco

You remember that. Yeah, and I stand by it. You know what changed? The bus. Coach got us on a schedule that doesn't have us rolling into a building at two in the afternoon after a midnight ride through Manitoba. Legs are legs. You put 'em on the ice tired, they're tired. It's not, I don't know, some voodoo thing about road hockey. We're just less tired now than we were and that shows up in the first ten minutes.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're sitting 21 and 9 and 5, first in the Pacific. What do you see from the bench that the guys on the ice might not see?

Marty Turco

The depth. I watch Arnott from the side, he had two goals the last two games. You look up and Morrow is grinding a shift that nobody's going to notice, nobody's going to put in a highlight reel, and then Modano finds him in the slot and it's a goal. You know what I mean? The guys who aren't scoring are still making the thing work. That's what a team looks like when it's good. From the bench you see all that stuff the cameras miss.

Jan Kowalczyk

And you're watching, not playing, and you're the guy who was the number one here for three years. Is that still a hard thing to do?

Marty Turco

Every day a little less hard. I'm 30. I've done the All-Star Game, I've done the games I wanted to do. What I want now is to be ready when the number comes and I'm not, I'm not sitting here feeling sorry for myself. If the kid plays 80 games and we make a run, I'm the guy in the corner warming up when the guy needs a rest and that's a job. And honestly, watching the way this room works right now? I'd rather be in it as a backup than someplace else as a starter.

Jan Kowalczyk

Last one. You get the number next week, what does the week before look like so you're not coming off ice like Saturday?

Marty Turco

Lots of pucks. I'll tell you that. I'm working every day anyway, so it's not like I'm coming in cold. The thing about Saturday was I hadn't played in a while and the first period I was a half step behind on two shots that weren't real dangerous. You just need the reps, that's it. I'll get 'em.

Jan Kowalczyk

Appreciate the time, Marty. Jan Kowalczyk, corridor at Sunrise. Back to you.
