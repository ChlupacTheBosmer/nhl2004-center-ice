Quenneville after another one at home: 'We were outworked in stretches'

The Avs are 7 and 12 at their own building, and their coach says the shots on goal tell him more than the standings do.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelThursday, 29 December 2005

4:02 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Colorado Avalanche lost 3-2 to Philadelphia in overtime on 2005-12-27, their third home loss in four nights and the 30th time an opponent has outshot them at home this season. Colorado sits 14 and 12 and 7, second in the Northwest, 13 points behind New Jersey Devils. Joel Quenneville sat with The Tunnel after the game and talked about the gap between how his team plays on the road and what happens when they lace up in front of their own building.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here in the tunnel at the Coliseum with Joel Quenneville. Joel, two goals, one point, and you're back in the dressing room knowing you gave up 30 shots in your own building. What'd you see?

Joel Quenneville

We saw, uh, a team that got after us in the first period. Twenty shots, maybe more, and we were, I mean, we were flat-footed on our coverage. The second and third period were better. We tightened up, we managed the puck a little more, we didn't turn it over in the neutral zone. But you give up 30 at home and it's going to be a long night.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're 7 and 12 at home. That's the worst mark in the Northwest. You're 10 and 7 on the road. What's the difference?

Joel Quenneville

I don't know, Jan. I really don't. Obviously we'd like to have the same energy, the same compete level, every night. We haven't. And at home, you can look around the room and, you know, the guys can feed off the crowd when it's going. But when it's not, when we're down a goal in our building, there's this, uh, there's this quiet that settles in. We're not helping each other through it the way we do on the road.

Jan Kowalczyk

Philadelphia came in here having just lost 4 to 1 in Ottawa, and they outshot you 30 to 21. Is that an effort thing, or is that structure?

Joel Quenneville

Both. In the first period it's structure, you know, we're not getting pucks deep, we're turning it over on our own blue line, and they're just cycling it and wearing us down. By the third we're matching them, we're getting more time. But 9 goals in 4 games, Jan, that's the number. We allow too many chances and we don't protect the middle of the ice the way we should. That's not an effort problem, that's, that's a detail problem.

Jan Kowalczyk

Jamie Storr has started 25 and he's 11 and 14. Trevor Kidd has 10 starts and is 6 and 4. Are you thinking about that rotation?

Joel Quenneville

I've started with Jamie because he's earned it in camp, in preseason, and he's given us a chance every night. Eleven and 14 doesn't tell you the whole picture. You look at the quality of chances against at home, 30, 32 shots a night, the guy's standing on his head half the time and we still put him in a hole. I'm not going to put that on a goaltender. He's fine. He'll get the next one.

Jan Kowalczyk

Martin Skoula's out through January 8th with a back. That leaves your blue line thin. Duvie Westcott played 10 games and then didn't come back. What's happening up there?

Joel Quenneville

Duvie's, he's, he's working, he's in the lineup when we need him. We've gone with the seven we've got and, you know, Adam Foote is carrying minutes he didn't plan on carrying at this stage of his career. Skrastins is eating a lot of ice. We've just got to make it work. We don't have a lot of bodies back there and we're asking the guys who are here to give us more.

Jan Kowalczyk

Your front office hasn't made a move this winter. The room, and the Bureau's Morale Scale puts this group at 98.1, the highest in the league. Is that a sign they're fine without depth help, or are they just happy guys watching the standings slip?

Joel Quenneville

They're happy. That's real. These guys, you look at Foote, you look at Oates, they've been around, they know the game's not over in December. But I'll tell you this: nobody in that room is content. You lose at home in overtime after being outshot, you don't feel great about yourself no matter what the Bureau prints. The morale's high because the men like each other. That's not the same as being satisfied.

Jan Kowalczyk

Peter Forsberg and Paul Kariya both have 41 points. Hejduk has 37. Teemu's got 32. Your top four carry the scoring. And yet you allow 93 goals. What does the coach say about that?

Joel Quenneville

I say we've got to score five to win a hockey game right now. And that's not sustainable. I know that. But the back end, that's the one I lose sleep on. Our top guys are doing their jobs. The structure has to come from every line, every defensive pairing, and right now we're leaking goals in bunches. That's on me to fix.

Jan Kowalczyk

You play San Jose tomorrow night, Dallas on Saturday, then Van on Sunday. Four games in six nights. Does the load change how you use the top guys?

Joel Quenneville

It changes everything. You manage shifts, you shorten the bench if you have to. At 33 or 35, these guys need the rest more than they used to. We'll watch the minutes closely. If Peter's over 22, I'll pull him whether he wants to go back or not. You can't win a road trip with tired legs and we've got three in a row away from here.

Jan Kowalczyk

Last one. Your home crowd is watching 7 and 12. What do you say to them?

Joel Quenneville

They deserve better. I get it. They buy the ticket, they come in, they expect us to compete for sixty minutes in front of them. We haven't done that consistently. I can't make excuses for it. I just ask them to keep coming, because we're going to be a better home team. I believe that.

Jan Kowalczyk

Joel Quenneville, Colorado, back to you.
