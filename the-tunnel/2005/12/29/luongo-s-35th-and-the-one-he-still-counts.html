Luongo's 35th, and the one he still counts

Toronto's goaltender picks up win 35 on the season, fourth straight, and says the goal Edmonton scored lives with him on the bus

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelThursday, 29 December 2005

2:28 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Roberto Luongo95 faced 18 shots and let in one as Toronto Maple Leafs won 6-1 on the road at Edmonton Oilers on 2005-12-28, his 35th win of the season and Toronto Maple Leafs's 25th of the year, extending the streak to four.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here, rinkside in Edmonton. Roberto, six-one, fourth straight, and that's number thirty-five for you on the year. Calgary last night, Edmonton tonight, back to back. How does thirty-five land?

Roberto Luongo

Thirty-five, yeah, it's a number. But I look up, we're twenty-five wins. That's what I see. Two cities, Alberta, the bus is short, Calgary to Edmonton is, uh, is nothing. The guys in front of me, they skate tonight. That's it.

Marcus Bell

Eighteen shots you face. Eighteen. And one gets through. In a 6-1 game, does a goalie count the one?

Roberto Luongo

Yeah. Yeah, I count it. People think, 6-1, the night is done, the night's over. No. The one, I watch it in the room, on the replay, I watch it four times. He gets the puck at the circle, I'm late to my left, he makes the right read. Good hockey. I have to take it. But don't, don't think the night was easy. Edmonton, they come at us in the second, we're 2-1 and they get three shots in a row, clean looks. It was a game tonight. A real game.

Marcus Bell

Mats Sundin, five points in two games. The captain, picking up the pace. What does it look like from your crease?

Roberto Luongo

It looks like I'm standing there and the puck is going the wrong way. No, no. Mats, he sees the ice, he sees the ice different than us. He gets in the slot and the play slows down for him, you know? He finds the guy, he makes the read, the puck, she... it goes in. I'm happy to watch from here. He's the captain. He does that.

Marcus Bell

Stanislav Chistov, two points in two games. Tips pucks in front of you. Do you see him?

Roberto Luongo

You don't want to see him. You want to see the puck. Chistov, he's big, he's right in front of me and he blocks my... and I can't see the, the release. The puck comes off his stick and it's in before I move. It's the kind of thing you want to kill. He does it on the other side too. I just want to see the puck first, that's all. That's it.

Marcus Bell

Roberto, you told Jan Kowalczyk in January, the team hadn't changed, the pucks were going in now, the young guys played the same way three weeks ago. That's almost a year. Does that still hold?

Roberto Luongo

I said that? Yeah, I think I said it right. The young guys, they play the same way. Same moves. They just breathe different now. When you're winning, the game gets slower for you. When you're losing, everything is too fast, you can't, you can't find the puck on your stick. That's the only difference. Same kids, different breathing. And when you trust it, you don't think. You just play.

Marcus Bell

We talked back in Florida, December of 2004. Your first game with the Panthers. You told me wins matter more than shutouts. Thirty-five and three now. Still the same?

Roberto Luongo

You remember that. Yeah. Same answer, for sure. I sleep good at thirty-five wins. Three shutouts, four, five, it looks good on the card, it does. But I play for the two. I play for the two points. That's, uh, that's the job. The zero, it comes when the guys back there give it to me and I'm happy when it does. I don't go to bed thinking about the zero. I don't.

Marcus Bell

Roberto, thanks for your time. Enjoy the flight. Marcus Bell rinkside in Edmonton, back to you.
