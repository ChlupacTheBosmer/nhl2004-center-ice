Tortorella on the road, the middle, and the 23 in net

The Lightning coach calls their 4-10 away record a compete problem and will not slow down Nikolai Khabibulin.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelSunday, 4 December 2005

5:29 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Tampa Bay Lightning lost 4-3 at Toronto Maple Leafs on December 3, dropping to 10-8-5 and 29 points while sitting 8-3 at home and 4-10 on the road. Head coach John Tortorella spoke to The Tunnel after the game about the road, the center spot without Alexander Svitov77, and the workload on Nikolai Khabibulin93.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here in the corridor with John Tortorella. Coach, 4-3 in Toronto, one of the best rooms in the league to play in. What did you see tonight?

John Tortorella

Yeah. Well, they're good, you know. They're 15 and 6, they've got the top record in the East for a reason. But I thought our first period we were, I mean, we were flat. We didn't have the puck enough. We just gave them too many zones and then you're chasing the game against that kind of hockey club and it gets tough. The second period and third were better. Obviously not enough.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're 8 and 3 at home, 4 and 10 on the road. That's the biggest home-away split in the league right now. What's the difference?

John Tortorella

I don't think it's one thing. I think when you're on the road you need to, you need to play a tighter game, you need to be more honest with the puck and less, less, you know, less free-wheeling. And we haven't done that consistently enough. I think some of the guys think they can go on the road and play the same game they play at home, where the building's full and pushing you. You don't get that push when you're in someone else's building. You've got to manufacture it yourself. And that's a compete-level thing. I don't think we've manufactured it enough.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let me ask about the middle. Alexander Svitov's out, elbow, back December 7. Who fills that hole for you?

John Tortorella

Well, we've got Jeff Halpern and Brian Sutherby. Both of them can play that spot. Halpy gives us faceoffs, he's not going to score you 20 but he wins draws and he's reliable in his own end. Sutherby's younger, he's got a little more wheels but he's still learning the, the, you know, the position at this level. So we're managing it. We've dropped Vincent out of the third-line center slot, put him up where he belongs, and we're just going to have to grind through until Svitov's back. That's the reality.

Jan Kowalczyk

Khabibulin's started 23 of your 25 games. That's 92 percent. Are you riding him?

John Tortorella

No. No, we're not riding him. He's our hockey player. He's our starter. John Grahame has played four games and he's fine, he's ready, but you don't pull a guy who's 11 and 12 with two shutouts. I mean, you'd be nuts. The kid's playing well enough to deserve the ice. I'm not going to sit here and protect a starting goalie from his own success, you know? He's 32, he's been in the league, he knows how to take care of himself. We'll manage it, obviously, but I'm not making a change because someone on the outside says it's too many games.

Jan Kowalczyk

Patrik Svedberg, 21 years old, only four games this year. Is he in your plans for this road trip?

John Tortorella

He's a young kid, he's got tools, but you know what, he's got to earn it. Four games is four games. I'm not going to throw him in just because we've got a tough road schedule. If someone gets hurt, yeah, maybe. But I've got guys ahead of him who are playing. You don't get minutes by being 21. You get them by being better than the guy who's 31 sitting the next shift over.

Jan Kowalczyk

What about the Lecavalier move? You dropped him from the 32C slot this week. That's a change.

John Tortorella

Yeah. He was playing too many positions for me. Third line center, fourth line center, power play, PK, faceoffs. That's a lot. I think we were asking him to do too much in the defensive zone and it wasn't helping the hockey club because he's our, he's one of our top two players offensively. So now he's 41C, he's on the power play, he's on the PK, he's taking draws, but he's not starting on the third line. That's a simplification. That's me trying to get him more of his game instead of all of his games.

Jan Kowalczyk

Four games on the road in the next eight days. Edmonton, Boston, Chicago, Minnesota. Does that road record weigh on you going in?

John Tortorella

What road record? You mean 4 and 10? Yeah, it weighs on me. I'd like to be 10 and 4. But you don't look at the road record, you look at the next game. We play Edmonton on the 5th and they're a team that's been playing better hockey lately. If we go there and play flat again, we'll lose. That's not complicated. I don't need to look at the standings to tell me we need to be better on the road.

Jan Kowalczyk

Bates Battaglia moved from the fourth line to the third line right wing this week. Dan Boyle dropped a right-side slot. Small shuffles, but it's something. What's the reasoning?

John Tortorella

Batts can play right wing, he's not a natural left winger and we've had him out of position for half the year. I think he's more effective on the right side, more effective with his shot, more effective in the corners on that side. And Boyle, you know, we just simplified his game. He's a left-handed shot, he doesn't need to take that right-side draw or whatever it was, whatever we were asking. It's all just, you know, putting guys where they're more comfortable so they play faster. That's the whole coaching job sometimes.

Jan Kowalczyk

Last one. Your room, by the numbers, is the second-highest morale club in the league. 98.6 average, COL is only ahead of you at 98.6. You're at 98.0. You've got a 4 and 10 road record and 29 points. Does that bother you that the room seems loose when the wins aren't there?

John Tortorella

I don't know about those numbers, I don't look at them. You know what I look at? I look at practice. I look at, are they competing? Are they engaged? And yeah, they are. The morale thing, that tells me they like each other, they like being in the room. That's not a bad thing. I'd rather have guys who like each other and aren't winning than guys who don't like each other and aren't winning. At least I've got something to work with. So no, it doesn't bother me. The wins bother me. Not the room.

Jan Kowalczyk

Coach, thank you for your time. Back to you.
