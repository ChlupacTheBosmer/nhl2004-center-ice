Luongo, three in the net and a win in hand

The Maple Leafs' goaltender faced 23 shots and let 3 pass in a 4-3 win over Tampa Bay, and says the goals are his to answer for even when the team gets the two points.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelSunday, 4 December 2005

2:49 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Roberto Luongo95 made 20 saves on 23 shots as Toronto Maple Leafs beat Tampa Bay Lightning 4-3 at home on Saturday, holding the lead the Maple Leafs built through three periods while giving up three goals in front of 25 shots on the other end. TOR sits alone at the top of the Northeast at 38 points through 25 games, and Luongo's line since November 30 reads one win, zero shutouts.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here in the tunnel with Roberto Luongo. Forty-three wins, Roberto... sorry, fifteen wins, thirty-eight points, first place in the Northeast, and you let three past you on twenty-three shots. How do you feel tonight?

Roberto Luongo

The two points are the two points, for sure. I'm not going to say I don't care because that's the win. But two of those three, they're mine. I know what I can do and I didn't do it on two of them tonight. So I'm not... I'm happy for the guys, the two points are big against this team. But the rest I gotta watch tonight.

Jan Kowalczyk

Did any one of the three sit with you on the bench, the one you kept turning over?

Roberto Luongo

The third one. It came off the stick kind of quick, the screen was there, but I should... I should've set up sooner. That's the one. The second one I'd probably take again, maybe it goes in anyway. But the third, I was late. I was a half-beat late and against a team like Tampa you don't get away with that, that's it.

Jan Kowalczyk

Three different defensemen got on the board tonight. Barret Jackman scored, Ron Hainsey and Rostislav Klesla had the helpers. When your blue line is that involved on the other end, does that change what you see in front of you?

Roberto Luongo

Yeah. Yeah it does. When Klesla or Jackman, they're in the play at the other end, they come back a little flat, you know? They're not... they're not as set when they're getting back. But the guys, they've been doing it all year and the forwards are doing it too. Chlup, the goal he scored tonight, he came back after that and was right in the slot. So it's a balance. You're asking for it, the D to be involved, and then you gotta trust that her... him, sorry, the D are gonna get back and be there. And they are. Most nights they are.

Jan Kowalczyk

You've been here since late January now. You've got Klesla in front of you, Chistov on the wing, and you've seen this group grow. How is it finding its shape?

Roberto Luongo

The guys, they're fast. When you've got a guy like Klesla who can carry the puck out, and Chistov, he's everywhere, he's coming back to help the D, he's getting to the point... you know how much easier that makes it for me? I don't have to play the whole game alone in front. The structure is there. I remember, you know, in January I said the young guys play the same way they did three weeks ago, and the pucks just weren't going in. Now they're going in. Forty-three... eighty-two goals for. That's the same group. That's the same thing I said.

Jan Kowalczyk

That was when you were still in Florida, actually. But the sentiment holds. Eighty-two goals in twenty-five games, you're averaging about three and a third a night for and two and a fifth against.

Roberto Luongo

No, wait, you're right, I was in Florida. You know what, I'm not gonna argue the memory. The point is the same. The point is these guys play the same way and the pucks go in now. Two and a fifth against is okay but I know it could be two flat. It could be better. I could be better. So that's... yeah. That's the work.

Jan Kowalczyk

One more. The Book has you at ninety-five overall, the top goaltender in the league, and your Development Index is up five off your base. But consistency is your lowest mark, fifty. Is that gap what the three goals feel like?

Roberto Luongo

I know my game. Everybody knows it. I can make the save nobody else makes, I can make it ten times in a row, and then I get caught. I get lazy on a read. And the fifty, it's... yeah. It's fair. That's what it is. I'm not mad at it, I just want to change it. And the only way I change it is more of these nights where I don't let three in, where I let one in. So tonight's the reminder. Not the bad kind. The good kind. You win and you still have something to work on. That's it.

Jan Kowalczyk

Roberto Luongo, Maple Leafs goaltender, thank you. Back to you.
