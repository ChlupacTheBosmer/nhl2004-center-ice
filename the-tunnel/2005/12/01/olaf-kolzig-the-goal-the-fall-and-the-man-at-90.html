Olaf Kolzig: The Goal, The Fall, and The Man at 90

The Capitals' goaltender on the one number he keeps in his head, what the Bureau's projection means to a 35-year-old, and playing behind Jaromir Jagr.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelThursday, 1 December 2005

4:16 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

Olaf Kolzig89 sits down after a 70-game season that put a Vezina Trophy on the mantel and the League Scouting Bureau's Development Index at minus 2. His overall grade sits at 89 against a base of 92; his potential reads 72. The Sweater's profile calls the gap the 20-grade fall. He is 35, from Johannesburg, and he is settled.

I make of it, it's what they see. I can't argue with the Bureau, you know. They watch the tape, they grade it. But I'm here. I'm playing 70 games. I'm winning. The Vezina is in my house. So they see the fall coming and maybe they're right. But today I'm still here and I'm still doing the job.

Two years left on the deal. The Bureau sees the fall. You're 35. Are you thinking about what the next two years look like?

Olaf Kolzig

I think about tomorrow. That's all. I think about tomorrow's practice and tomorrow's game. If I can do that, the two years take care of themselves. If I can't, then, then the two years end early. But I'm not thinking about it tonight.

Olaf Kolzig, thank you very much. Back to you.
