Nedorost: 25 in 33, one year left, and the phone has not rung yet

The Panthers' 23-year-old center says his jump to 80 on the Book has nothing to do with July and everything to do with playing 22 minutes a night instead of 13.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelSaturday, 24 December 2005

5:32 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Vaclav Nedorost84 turns 23 in March. He scored 25 points in 33 games last season after managing the same total in 69 the year before, his ice time doubled from 13.4 minutes to 22.3, and the Bureau's Development Index has him twelve points above his base grade. He is on $1.00M with no years left on the deal, and Florida Panthers finished 25 and 36 last season.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here in the studio with Vaclav Nedorost of Florida Panthers®. Vaclav, thanks for sitting down. I'll start with the thing everyone at home is thinking: you're on $1.00M, zero years left, the Bureau has you at 80, up twelve on the Development Index. You're a free agent in July. Do you sleep at night?

Vaclav Nedorost

Yeah. I sleep good. My mother says I sleep like the dog at home. But, you know, the number I know. I'm not stupid. I just... I play the game, I go home, I eat, I sleep. July is far away right now.

Marcus Bell

Fair enough. But look at the jump. 25 points in 69 games your first year here, same total in only 33 games last year. Your minutes went from 13.4 a night to 22.3. That's not a small thing.

Vaclav Nedorost

No, it is not small. In Colorado I am... how you say... fourth center, fifth center, maybe I play, maybe I don't. 25 games one year, 42 the next. Here I play with Olli Jokinen and Viktor Kozlov most nights. I play the power play, I play the PK. More ice, you play more hockey. You get better when you play every night, you know?

Marcus Bell

So did Colorado see that, or did they look at a 21-year-old in a bottom-six role and decide he wasn't part of the plan? You and Eric Messier for Peter Worrell and a second-rounder. That's not a blockbuster.

Vaclav Nedorost

I think they have the good centers, yes? Peter Forsberg is there. They have the good team. I understand the trade. I am young, they want the pick. I go to Florida and I get the minutes. That is the trade for me. I don't think about the second round. I think about tonight's game.

Marcus Bell

The Sweater ran a piece on you called 'The Contract Year: Nedorost plays for July at the league's worst address.' Twenty-five and thirty-six last season, seventy-six points. Does that sting?

Vaclav Nedorost

Montreal has 66 points. We have 76. We are not the worst. But, yeah, it is not the best either. Maybe I am the... what is it... the wrong man for the title. I don't care about the worst address. I care that I play 22 minutes and my linemates are good and the coach trusts me. The standings, maybe next year better. But I have 97 morale, Marcus. On a 25-win team. You know why? Because I play. That is why.

Marcus Bell

Let me take you back. The 2000 draft, 14th overall, Colorado takes you. You're 18, you're from České Budějovice. Where were you when your name went up?

Vaclav Nedorost

At home. In Budweis, we call it. My family, we have the TV on and the whole street almost, you know? My mother she cry, big cry. My father he... he don't cry. But he has the glass of beer in his hand and he is shaking it a little, you know? My brothers they run around the living room like, like the children. That night, that is the best night. Before all of this, before the hockey gets hard. The best night in our house.

Marcus Bell

Budweis. How far is that from here?

Vaclav Nedorost

It is... many hours. You fly to Prague, then you drive, then you... Florida is the end of the world, how you say. When I go to Hershey, that was already far. Here I look at the map and I think, my mother is on the other side of the ocean. That is a long way to watch your boy play hockey.

Marcus Bell

Does she come? Does the family get here?

Vaclav Nedorost

My mother, yeah, she come last year. One time. My father he stay, he work in Budweis, he can't take the summer off. My brother, he is... 26 now, maybe he come. But the road is so long, you know? And in the summer I need the rest. The season is 82 games and the travel and the... it takes everything. So maybe the next summer. Maybe.

Marcus Bell

You've been here since the summer of 2003. Almost two and a half years. Is Florida home yet?

Vaclav Nedorost

I have my apartment. I know the roads. Olli and I, we go for the dinner some nights, we talk the Czech... I mean, he doesn't speak Czech. He is Finnish. But we talk the hockey, you know? And Viktor he is Czech too so we eat together some time. Florida is... the work. The good work. Home is Budweis. Home is my mother's kitchen. That will be home always.

Marcus Bell

The leg. Sometime around March 9th, you went down, and you didn't come back until April 24th. You missed the last six weeks of the best season of your career, in a year where you're trying to prove you're worth more than a million dollars. How do you process that?

Vaclav Nedorost

You don't. You just... you sit and you wait and the doctors tell you the date. March... I think 9th, maybe 10th. I try to play one game, it is bad, the leg is not there. So I stop. April 24th I'm back. And in that time I think: the season is going, the points are going, and the July is coming, and I am on the couch watching. It is the worst. You know? The worst part is not the pain. The worst part is you are useful on the ice and you are not there.

Marcus Bell

Does that make you play different now? Careful, maybe, with the leg?

Vaclav Nedorost

I try not. If I play the careful, the leg is fine but I play like the leg is broken still. That is the fear. So I play, I go full speed. My speed is 99, they say. So I use it, I don't hide it.

Marcus Bell

99 speed, 92 passing, 91 agility, 91 puckhandling. Those are the top of the league numbers for a 23-year-old. But your fight grade is 50, your heroics 56. You know those numbers too?

Vaclav Nedorost

Yeah. I am not the... how you say... the mean guy. In Czech we have the saying, you don't need to be the fist if you are the feet. But here, the league, you need a little bit mean. I know. I work. Maybe this year I am a little bit meaner. I don't drop the gloves, I don't need to. But the body check, the little push along the boards, I learn. You have to learn or they eat you.

Marcus Bell

The Bureau says your potential is 94. You're at 80. What does 94 look like to you? Whose game is that?

Vaclav Nedorost

Nobody's. It is nobody's game. It is my game when I am 26, 27, when I score 40, 50 points, when I play the All-Star. That is the dream. But you don't go to the building thinking about 94. You go thinking about tonight's opponent. You think about Forsberg coming through your neutral zone, what do I do? That is the thinking. Not 94.

Marcus Bell

One last thing. You said earlier that July is far. But you know it isn't. Six months. Do you play for yourself this year, or do you play for the contract?

Vaclav Nedorost

I play for the team. That is what the coach says and that is what I believe. But... the contract is there. Everyone knows the contract. If I play good, the phone rings. If I play bad, I go back to Budweis and I play the Czech league and that is fine too, my mother is there. But I don't want to go back. I want to stay here and play the 22 minutes and score the 40 points. So I play the game. The game tells the truth.

Marcus Bell

Vaclav Nedorost, thank you very much. Back to you.
