Luongo says the coach is sending his defensemen further, and he wants it that way

The Toronto goaltender says the new aggressive pairing style opens angles he needs, and that he refuses to count the games left in December.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelSaturday, 24 December 2005

1:55 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Roberto Luongo95 stopped 33 of 35 shots in Toronto Maple Leafs's 4-2 win over Atlanta Thrashers on Thursday, extending the defending Cup champions to a two-game win streak and 55 points. The Toronto dressing room has a new look behind the lead, with four defencemen recording assists in a single night, and Luongo after the game said the change felt like breathing room.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk down the tunnel here with Roberto Luongo. Forty-two at home over Atlanta, second in a row, fifty-five points on the board. Thirty-five shots came at you tonight. How was that one from the crease?

Roberto Luongo

It was busy, for sure. Thirty-five, yeah. The second period they were, I mean the first was, was under control, but the second there was two or three where I just, I dropped the pads and hoped. That's, that's not a way to, to play the whole night, you know.

Jan Kowalczyk

You had two days off before this one. December is a grind. What does the schedule ahead ask of you?

Roberto Luongo

I don't count, you know. When I start counting games I get tired before the puck drops. For me it's the next save, the next game. The guys take their rest when they can, I take mine when I can. That's it.

Jan Kowalczyk

I noticed four defencemen with an assist tonight. McCabe, Fischer, Klesla, Kaberle. Is that a change? Did the coach alter something in how the pairings are used?

Roberto Luongo

Yeah, for sure, yeah. He's been, he's been pushing them up. Before they were holding, you know, watching, flat. Now McCabe, Klesla, they're coming. And when they're coming the other team backs up, and I get more time to track. Less scrambling. I need that.

Jan Kowalczyk

Does that mean more traffic in front of you, though? You're giving up the blue line.

Roberto Luongo

A little. A little. But, you know, when the defence is, is flat and they're just watching the play go by, that's worse. That's a lot worse. I'd take the traffic, for sure.

Jan Kowalczyk

When we spoke in January you told me the team hadn't changed, the pucks were just going in now. I asked you then to revisit when the record was longer than four games. You're at thirty-five. Are you saying the same thing?

Roberto Luongo

Yeah, for sure, for sure. The guys play the same. The same way they did in January. The difference now is we're winning more than, than we maybe should. And that's, that's a good place to be in December.

Jan Kowalczyk

Atlanta put thirty-five shots on you tonight. They've got twenty-seven points. Does that get talked about in the room?

Roberto Luongo

Yeah. Yeah, we talk about it. When a team's down there and they're shooting like that it means we're not, we're not closing the first shift, the second. You're up two-nil and you think, okay, easy, and then they come. We have to be better, that's it.

Jan Kowalczyk

Roberto Luongo. Thirty-three saves, the win, fifty-five points in second place in the Northeast. Thanks for your time, Roberto.

Roberto Luongo

Thank you, Jan. Thank you.
