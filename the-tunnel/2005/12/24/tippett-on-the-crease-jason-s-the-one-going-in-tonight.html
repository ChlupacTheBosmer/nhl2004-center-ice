Tippett on the crease: 'Jason's the one going in tonight'

After a 4-3 overtime win in Carolina, the Stars coach confirmed the switch to Jason Bacashihua without dressing Marty Turco and talked through what comes next on a three-in-four road trip.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelSaturday, 24 December 2005

4:05 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Dallas Stars beat Carolina Hurricanes 4-3 in overtime on 2005-12-22, moving to 20-8-5 and into first place in the Pacific. The result was not the story that night. Jason Bacashihua89, 23, started the game and the dressing-room slot that followed; Marty Turco95, 28 starts, 19-9, overall 97, did not dress.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here in the corridor at Raleigh with Dave Tippett. Coach, you win it 4-3 on the road in overtime. That's not a bad way to spend a Friday.

Dave Tippett

No, it's not. And look, we didn't play a great game. Carolina came out hard, their first two periods were, I think they outshot us pretty good in the second, and we were, you know, we were lucky to be in it going into the third. But the boys found a way. That's what this group does. They don't panic.

Jan Kowalczyk

You put Jason Bacashihua in net tonight and you sat Marty Turco out. That's a move people are going to talk about for a while. Walk me through that decision.

Dave Tippett

Well, obviously Marty's our guy. He's been our guy all year, nineteen wins, he's won every close one for us. But Jason's been waiting his turn and, you know, you've got to give a young man an opportunity when the moment calls for it. We played Tuesday night, we play again Saturday, Saturday night we play Sunday. The schedule, it makes some of these calls for you. I don't sit here and think of it as, uh, I don't think of it as Marty losing his spot. I think of it as managing a three-in-four stretch with a group of guys who are going to need everyone.

Jan Kowalczyk

But he's not dressing. You brought Ron Tugnutt in as the backup. That's a message, isn't it?

Dave Tippett

It's a management decision. Look, I'll be honest with you, Jan. When you put a man in the healthy scratch situation, there's always a message, whether you intend one or not. And yeah, that's something I weigh. But Jason's earned it. Four and three in seven games, he's been sharp every time we've thrown him in. I'm not going to let him sit a second game on the bench when he's the one who's been giving us the edge in practice and in warmups. You know, I don't care if you're nineteen or thirty-nine, if you're the best guy that week, you play.

Jan Kowalczyk

So Saturday at Nashville, is it Jason again?

Dave Tippett

We'll see. I'll give you what I know right now, which is Jason's the one going in tonight, he's got the first one in this stretch, and I'll assess how the legs are after tomorrow. I've got three games in four nights and, you know, I'll worry about the details when I'm at the rink Saturday morning. I won't stand in a hallway and promise you a starting lineup for a game that's forty-eight hours out.

Jan Kowalczyk

I looked at your lines tonight and they're the same four forward lines you've carried most of December. No one's up, no one's down. Is that deliberate?

Dave Tippett

Yeah. Obviously. You know, people always want you to shuffle the deck, especially after a loss. We play Tuesday, we lose 3-1 in New Jersey, and everyone's saying, well, who are you going to move up? Well, nobody. I like what the lines are giving me. Mike's still producing, thirty-five points in thirty games at his age, that's the kind of consistency that lets you leave things alone. Jason Arnott's in every game, thirty-six of thirty-six. Brenden's got twenty-two assists, he's moving the puck. Why would I change it? You change it because something's broken, and nothing's broken here.

Jan Kowalczyk

What does that defensive structure look like, because you've given up 19 goals in your last 10, and you're outscoring people 28-19? That's a different kind of team from what people expected from Dallas.

Dave Tippett

That's what we're built to do. You know, there's a perception, and it's a fair one, that this club has skill and it should score four, five a night. And we can. We've got the guys. But the way we win games is we get a structure that's hard to play against, and we get a guy in net who makes the one or two saves that keep you in it. And when you've got a thirty-three-goal margin over ten games, you're doing something right in the defensive zone. The details are where it lives. Backchecks, gap control, not getting caught with your defensemen out of position. It's not glamorous, but it's what wins in this league.

Jan Kowalczyk

You've got Nashville, then Florida, both on the road, and then back home for two against Detroit and Edmonton. Three in four starts Saturday. Does that affect how you play those first two, the structure, the minutes on your older guys?

Dave Tippett

It affects everything. I mean, obviously. Mess is going to get fewer minutes, we're going to lean more on the younger legs, Kristian Timander's going to see some power-play time maybe, Steve Ott might get a look. And defensively you tighten things up a bit. You don't take as many chances when you're on the second night of a back-to-back in Florida, you know, the rink feels smaller, the puck feels heavier. We've got to be smart with our bodies. Twelve and nine on the road says we handle it OK, but you don't survive a stretch like this with the same eleven or twelve guys you used in December's first half. You need everybody.

Jan Kowalczyk

Coach, thank you. Enjoy the flight home.

Dave Tippett

Thanks, Jan. Back at you.
