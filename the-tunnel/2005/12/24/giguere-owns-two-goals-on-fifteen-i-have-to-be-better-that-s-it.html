Giguere owns two goals on fifteen: 'I have to be better, that's it'

The Ducks' goaltender took the second goal personally in a 2 to 1 home loss to Phoenix and said what it costs to give the building a night like this.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelSaturday, 24 December 2005

2:54 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Jean-Sebastien Giguere97 gave up 2 goals on 15 shots as Mighty Ducks of Anaheim dropped a 2 to 1 home game to Phoenix Coyotes on 2005-12-22, a contest Anaheim led 19 to 15 in shots. The loss left the Ducks 11-6 at home this season. Jan Kowalczyk caught him in the corridor.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here with Jean-Sebastien Giguere in the corridor after a 2 to 1 home loss to Phoenix. You outshot them 19 to 15, you scored one, they scored two. What does that look like from the crease?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

You know, it's hard. It's hard because, uh, the guys, the guys, they create, they shoot the puck, they generate. And me, I'm looking at two goals on fifteen. That's, uh... that's not where I have to be. I'll take that. I'll take that right now, that's on me.

Jan Kowalczyk

Is the second goal the one that's still in your head, or is it something earlier?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

The second one. Yeah. I think I'm late on the angle. And it's, the puck is coming low, I should see it, I should, I should have it. You know that feeling when you know before the puck crosses, you know that it's going in? That's what it was. I'm going to watch it tonight. I'm going to understand why.

Jan Kowalczyk

Havelid put in your one goal. One goal in a hockey game, what does it look like from where you're standing when that's the whole evening?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

You know what it is? The one goal, okay, you think, I keep us in this, I keep us in this. And then, the second one goes in and now it's you against them in the last period, and they're playing to hold, they're not, they're not taking chances, they're just... clearing the puck, sitting on it. And you need that second goal from the guys and it's not coming. And you can't call a timeout on my end, you know?

Jan Kowalczyk

We talked about this fourteen days ago. I was in this room asking about the shake-up, Cole at center, the back end changes. You told me it gave the group speed. Is that speed turning into points?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

It gives us speed, I'll say that. The transitions are, they're different, they're better than what we had. I see it every night. But speed without... speed without the second goal, you know? I can stop pucks, I can keep us in a game, but I need that one more tally. And I can't get it for them. From the crease I can't do that.

Jan Kowalczyk

Phoenix is ten wins and nineteen losses. You drop two points at home, eleven and six in this building, to a team with ten wins. Does that sit right?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

I don't look at their record, I'm not going to lie to you. I look at my own net, I look at what I gave up, and I gave up too much on fifteen shots. But no, it doesn't sit. You come here, we're eleven and six, the guys should know what to do in this building, and we didn't do it. They deserved it more tonight. That's the hard part.

Jan Kowalczyk

Talk to me about this crowd. They were on their feet late, I could hear them from the press box. You play eleven home games here before the break. What do they mean on a night like tonight?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

They mean everything. For sure. Because you know, a season, it's, uh, it's up, it's down, and you look up after a period, you're down, and they're still standing, and they're yelling, and they want you to come back. And you give them a night like tonight and you, you feel bad. The loss, the loss is part of hockey, everyone knows that. But giving them a Thursday night in December, a night like that... that gets to me. I want to give them the two points. That's all.

Jan Kowalczyk

So what happens tonight when you get home?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

I watch the second goal. I watch it ten times if I have to. And then tomorrow I come back and I try again. We have to be better, that's it. I have to be better. That's the job.

Jan Kowalczyk

Thank you, Jean-Sebastien. Jan Kowalczyk, back to you.
