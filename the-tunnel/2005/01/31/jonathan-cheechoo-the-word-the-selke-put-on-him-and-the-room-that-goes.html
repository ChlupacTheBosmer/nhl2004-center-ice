Jonathan Cheechoo: The Word the Selke Put on Him, and the Room That Goes First

Cheechoo says the trophy named him a checker before anyone met him, calls Ryan Smyth the best player he has played with, and says Edmonton's room listens to the men who go first, not the loudest.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelMonday, 31 January 2005

3:23 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Jonathan Cheechoo70 won the Frank J. Selke Trophy last season and has spent this one in the checking minutes: 25 points in 51 games at 19.4 a night for Edmonton Oilers. He sat down in the studio with Marcus Bell to talk about the trophy that named him, the best player he has played with, and who the room listens to.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Jonathan, Marcus Bell, we're in the studio, thanks for sitting down. Take me back to Belleville. What do you remember from the Bulls?

Jonathan Cheechoo

Yeah, Belleville. I got there at, uh, sixteen, seventeen years old. You know what I remember? The bus. Long rides, cold rinks, games in small towns. That's junior hockey, and it was good for me.

Marcus Bell

Nineteen ninety-nine, the OHL final against London. Game seven, five goals from you, 9-2, Belleville's first championship.

Jonathan Cheechoo

Five, yeah. I don't remember all five, you know. I remember one, maybe the last one. But winning it, that stays. First one Belleville ever had. You don't forget the room after a night like that.

Marcus Bell

Then the 1998 draft, 29th overall, and a lot of talk about that pick.

Jonathan Cheechoo

There was talk, yeah. I heard it. Slower forwards than most guys skate backwards. Something like that. I was 18. You hear it, and there isn't much you can say back. You go play.

Marcus Bell

Kentucky, Cleveland, then the call in 2002-03. What was that day like?

Jonathan Cheechoo

Cleveland, and then the phone. You pack, you fly, whatever they tell you. I don't remember the ride. I remember the room, first day, trying not to be in the way. That's all it is at the start.

Marcus Bell

Last season you won the Selke. Where is it right now?

Jonathan Cheechoo

On a shelf. Really, that's where it is. At home. I walk past it.

Marcus Bell

What did it do to you?

Jonathan Cheechoo

That's the question, right. I think it put a word on me. Two-way. Selke. Before that I was a guy fighting to stay in the lineup. After, I'm a checking forward, and a new guy knows that before he knows my name. It's a strange way to meet somebody.

Marcus Bell

This season, 3 goals, 22 assists, 25 points in 51 games, 19.4 minutes a night. Last year, 21 points in 47 games. Did the Selke take your goals?

Jonathan Cheechoo

You mean did the trophy take them. No. That's the job. You play 19 minutes, you're out there against the other team's top line, and some nights the puck goes in and some nights it doesn't. I'm not going to sit here and tell you 3 goals is where I want to be. It isn't.

Marcus Bell

The Bureau's Book has you 74 overall. The Development Index has you level this year, no movement.

Jonathan Cheechoo

Level. That's fair, and that's on me. There's a difference between playing 19 minutes and getting better in them. I know that. I'm not going to argue with the Bureau.

Marcus Bell

Best player you have played with. Give me the why, not the name.

Jonathan Cheechoo

Smitty. Ryan Smyth. That one's easy. Hart last year, 74 goals, 163 points, and he comes in every day like he's at tryouts. You watch that, you can't be the guy who takes a day off.

Marcus Bell

The Book has your goalie, Tommy Salo, at 86, the highest grade among your teammates.

Jonathan Cheechoo

Tommy, yeah. 68 games last year, 37 wins. He's seen everything. When you're 24 and you look down that room and there's Tommy, that helps you. It's a long season.

Marcus Bell

Who is on the other side of those 19 minutes?

Jonathan Cheechoo

Whoever's going, you know. You get Naslund, 53 goals. You get Jagr, 45. That's, uh, that's the challenge. That's why you play.

Marcus Bell

Leadership in the room. From your seat, who talks?

Jonathan Cheechoo

It's not one guy standing on a chair. We've got five guys 33 and over. They don't give speeches. They go first. In the drills, in the room, first on the ice. That's how it works here.

Marcus Bell

This is one of the younger rooms in the league, 26.7 on average against 28.3. Does that change it?

Jonathan Cheechoo

Younger, yeah. So the older guys carry more of the day, you know. Not the hockey. The rest of it. Making sure the young guys are ready, eating right, at the rink on time. Small stuff nobody sees.

Marcus Bell

Where do you sit in all of that?

Jonathan Cheechoo

Me? I'm 24. Not the old guy yet. But I've been around a while now. When a young guy comes in and he's lost, I know that feeling. You help with the small stuff. Where to be, what time.

Marcus Bell

Does the Selke ever feel like a shelf you cannot get off?

Jonathan Cheechoo

Some days, yeah. But it doesn't play for you. It's a trophy. You still lace up. Some shift the puck's on your stick and nobody's thinking about a trophy. That's the honest part.

Marcus Bell

What did the Selke give you that you keep?

Jonathan Cheechoo

Proof. Not for you. For me. That I can be in this league. For a guy who went 29th and everybody said he skated backwards. That's the part I keep.

Marcus Bell

Jonathan Cheechoo, thanks for sitting down. Back to you.
