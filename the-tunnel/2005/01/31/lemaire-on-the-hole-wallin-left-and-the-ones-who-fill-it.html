Lemaire on the hole Wallin left, and the ones who fill it

After another overtime loss, the Wild coach talks about who steps into a lineup that keeps losing men to the injured list.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelMonday, 31 January 2005

2:40 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Minnesota Wild lost 3-2 in overtime at Edmonton Oilers on 2005-01-29, their third straight defeat. Coach Jacques Lemaire spoke to Marcus Bell at the bench about a forward group thinned by two recent injuries and a front office that has made no moves this week.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here with Jacques Lemaire on the Minnesota bench after a 3-2 overtime loss in Edmonton. Coach, you had 20 shots to their 23. Two goals on the road against the league's second-best offence in points. What's the verdict on tonight?

Jacques Lemaire

The guys competed, that's the first thing. You come to Edmonton, you play your game close like that, and it goes to overtime, and they have the extra man on the ice in the third period to win it, well, that is hockey. We managed the puck better in the second and third than we did in the first. That is the one thing I'm satisfied with.

Marcus Bell

You're on a three-game losing streak now, and you've lost four of your last five going by the sequence of results. Is there a pattern to these close losses?

Jacques Lemaire

The pattern is we play them close and we can't close them. That's the pattern. You look at the shots, tonight we're 20 to 23, that is competitive. But the last ten games we're giving up 25 a night and scoring 27. That means we're winning the race and still losing the game. Something is wrong in the last ten, last five minutes.

Marcus Bell

Rickard Wallin is out with a head injury until February 11. Mikko Eloranta's elbow keeps him out until the 27th. Who steps into that fourth-line role right now?

Jacques Lemaire

For Rickard, we go with the guys we have. Darby Hendrickson can center, he's played there. Stephane Veilleux is healthy, he'll dress. And maybe we give the young one more minutes, Pihlman. He's played 43 games already this year, he's not a baby anymore. He's 20 and he's been in the fire all season.

Marcus Bell

Is losing Wallin, even for a couple weeks, enough to change how you line things up? He's got 5 points in 32 games but he's a structure guy.

Jacques Lemaire

That's the problem exactly. You lose a guy who doesn't give you offense but he gives you his shift, every shift, clean. That is worth more than the 5 points tells you. So the line has to find a new rhythm without him, and that takes, what, four, five games? We don't have four, five games to find it. That's the frustration.

Marcus Bell

Manny Fernandez has played 45 of your 52 games. Is there any thought to giving Dwayne Roloson more work down the stretch?

Jacques Lemaire

Roloson is ready, for sure. When the schedule gets tight we'll use him, we have to. Manny's a workhorse, everybody knows that. But you don't want a goalie who's dead in March, you know? The workload is something I manage, yes. Not tonight. Tonight I need him. I'll give you Roloson on Thursday against Chicago, maybe. We'll see.

Marcus Bell

Coach, the front office has been quiet. No trades, no call-ups that we've seen. Is the room talking about it?

Jacques Lemaire

The room talks about what's on the ice. I'll say this to you. We have a young group. Some of these guys are getting their first real minutes in this league and the trade deadline, the rumors, that's outside work. My job is to make sure it stays outside. Bouchard doesn't need to know about trade rumors. He needs to know his backcheck.

Marcus Bell

You've got Chicago on February 5 and St. Louis on the 7th. Two games against the two worst teams in the Central.

Jacques Lemaire

That is what everybody says, yes. Chicago has 42 points, St. Louis has 44. On paper, yes. On the ice, Chicago has won one of their last two, St. Louis just won. There is no soft game in this league anymore, not at this time of year. You play for pride and for draft position and for ice, and those guys will be just as hard to beat as Edmonton was tonight. Maybe harder.

Marcus Bell

Coach, thank you for your time.

Jacques Lemaire

Thank you, Marcus.
