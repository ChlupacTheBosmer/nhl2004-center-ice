Turco: Nineteen and Five Here Means Something, and Tonight We Wasted It

The Stars goaltender owns the loss to Calgary and calls the home ice an edge his club keeps choosing to squander.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelMonday, 31 January 2005

2:26 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Marty Turco92 watched Calgary Flames score twice at the American Airlines Center on Saturday, and the Dallas Stars goaltender was done being polite about it. The loss dropped Dallas to back-to-back defeats for the first time in a while, and their 19-5 home mark suddenly looked like wasted real estate. Turco had 26 wins on the season before the puck dropped.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here with Marty Turco at the boards. Two and one, Calgary, at home. You've dropped two straight, which you haven't done in some time. What happened in that building tonight?

Marty Turco

Yeah, look, uh, we didn't play enough sixty. Calgary came in here with forty-two points and they played tight, they stacked the middle, and we didn't solve it. I gave up two and that's... yeah, that's what happens when you let them sit on their hands like that.

Jan Kowalczyk

The first one, was that a shot you get back nine times out of ten?

Marty Turco

Yeah. I mean, I think so. I was, you know, I think I was a half-inch late on the glove side and he buried it. Those are the ones that haunt you on the drive home. I've got 124 against this year and the one that gets me is always the soft one, you know, not the one where a guy rips it top shelf and he deserves it.

Jan Kowalczyk

I want to take you back to the first of January. You told me the home-ice edge here was physical. Legs and sleep. You're nineteen and five at home, fourteen and fifteen on the road. Tonight's a home game. Where's the edge tonight?

Marty Turco

It's still there. It's still physical, legs and sleep, that's, that's real. But the edge doesn't matter if you come out flat. And we came out flat tonight. Calgary made us earn every inch and we didn't earn it in the first period, and by the time we got our legs going it was a one-goal game against a team that was gonna park the bus for sixty minutes.

Jan Kowalczyk

Twenty-eight and eighteen and two, sixty-eight points, first place in the Pacific. But back-to-back losses to the worst team in the Northwest, and a team that's nine points behind you. Does it bother you that the record doesn't feel as safe as it looks?

Marty Turco

Well, we've got a cushion, sure. But you're right, when you lose to a forty-two-point team at home, you look in the mirror and you say, okay, what are we actually doing here? You can't coast. I've been around long enough, I was undrafted, I played Jr. B in Cambridge before Michigan, I know what it looks like when a team starts believing its own press. We're not there yet. But tonight was a warning.

Jan Kowalczyk

You've got 26 wins in 43 games, one shutout. What does the rest of this season ask of you, personally?

Marty Turco

You know, I don't look at 26 and think about a number. I look at tonight and think about the glove-side one I should've swallowed. The wins are the byproduct, you know. If I'm sharp, if I'm back on the puck, if my rebound control is where it needs to be, the wins come. I'm not going to chase a number. I'm going to chase getting pucks out of the crease clean.

Jan Kowalczyk

And the schedule from here, whatever comes next, what do you need to see from this room?

Marty Turco

We need to be who we are at home. Nineteen and five, that record is good, and we just made it... well, it's still nineteen and five but we made it feel cheap tonight. We need to make this building a place nobody wants to come to. We've got the players. Modano, Arnott, Bill, Morrow, those guys can score from anywhere on the ice. We just have to get them the puck and get out of the way, you know.

Jan Kowalczyk

Marty Turco, thank you. Back to you in the studio.
