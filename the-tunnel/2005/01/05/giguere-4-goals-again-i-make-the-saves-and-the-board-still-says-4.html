Giguere, 4 goals again: 'I make the saves and the board still says 4'

The goaltender, up 3 on the Bureau's Index with an overall of 97, pointed at his own side's 18 shots as the one variable he cannot control from the crease.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelWednesday, 5 January 2005

2:14 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Jean-Sebastien Giguere93 and Mighty Ducks of Anaheim dropped a 4-1 decision to Philadelphia Flyers on January 3 at Anaheim Pond, surrendering four goals on 26 shots while his own side managed 18. It is Mighty Ducks of Anaheim's 2nd loss in a row and they remain without a shutout in 34 games this season. His Index sits up 3 on his base grade and the Bureau's Book carries him at 97 overall.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here in the tunnel with Jean-Sebastien Giguere. Jean-Sebastien, 4-1, Philadelphia outshot you 26 to 18 on your own ice. What did they do tonight that made the difference?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

Well, for sure, they shoot. That's the simple answer, they put 26 pucks on goal and we can't. You're always defending, always moving side to side, and the legs get heavy, you know? And the body just... it takes 4 of 26 and that's the game.

Marcus Bell

Eighteen shots on your side. And here's what gets me: Philadelphia is a 36-point team, they've allowed 165 goals this season, and they held you to one. How does that happen?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

That's the frustration, that's the whole thing, for sure. The guys are trying. Sasha gets 1, Erik gets 1. They're making plays, they're getting into position. But we're not, we're not finishing and we're not creating second chances. 18 shots, you know, you can't win a hockey game. You just can't. That's it, that's the problem.

Marcus Bell

Last time I spoke to you, we were discussing four on 20 and the back end pushing in and what that costs you out here. Tonight it's four on 26. You're up 3 on the Index, the Bureau's got you at 97. How do you hold those two things at once?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

I know how I feel. I feel good tonight, for sure. I'm square, I'm tracking, I'm making the saves I should be making. And then there's traffic and there's a guy on the far side who doesn't get back quick enough and it goes in, and the scoreboard says 4 and I can't... I can't put the puck in the other net. You know? That's not my job. I make the stops, sometimes I can't, but 18 is what's killing us.

Marcus Bell

Is there one of those four that's going to stay with you?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

The one in the third. I'm set, I'm square, I can see the puck right there and I just, I can't hold it. Those are the ones, yeah. You know which ones. The ones where the body's right and the hands just don't close.

Marcus Bell

2 in a row, at home. What's the room like right now, walking off?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

Quiet. Everyone knows. Nobody needs a speech in there tonight. Everyone saw the 18. You don't have to say anything when everyone saw the same game.

Marcus Bell

You told me last month that Fedorov's 3 points was your cushion on a rough night. Tonight he gets 1. Is there a thing where you need Sasha to carry it every single night?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

No, no. No, that's not, that's not fair. Sasha's done it all year, everyone knows that. But you can't ask him every night, that's not how this works. Erik's got to step, the whole group steps. We can't lean on one man, that's just not, it's not the answer. That's not fair to him.

Marcus Bell

Jean-Sebastien Giguere, thank you for your time. Back to you.
