Geoff Sanderson has been everywhere, so why is he scoring like a man with something to prove?

The Columbus captain on the 2,000-mile road back to the Blue Jackets, the system that unlocked his game, and why 38 goals in 38 games at 32 feels less like a fluke than a homecoming.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelWednesday, 5 January 2005

5:30 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Geoff Sanderson79 sits second in the league in goals this season, 38 in 38 games for Columbus Blue Jackets, on a 166-point pace that would stand above anything he did before the summer of 2004. The Blue Jackets drafted him, the Blue Jackets lost him, and the Blue Jackets got him back. He has been the captain here since before the record opened, and he tells Marcus Bell why this feels like the right year for a career season.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here, studio, with Geoff Sanderson of the Columbus Blue Jackets. Geoff, the Sweater's piece is titled 'The Long Way Round.' That fits, doesn't it. Hartford, Carolina, Vancouver, Buffalo, Columbus, back to Vancouver, back to Columbus. How many miles is that?

Geoff Sanderson

You know, I've never added it up, Marcus. And I'm not about to start now, because it's depressing. No, it's, it's part of it. You get moved enough times and you stop being a guy who belongs somewhere. You're just a guy with a suitcase. But I'll tell you, coming back here, to Columbus, that was the first time I picked a place. Not the other way around.

Marcus Bell

Take me to that first time. The expansion draft, summer of 2000. Columbus picks you, and what's your reaction? You've been around since Hartford. You're a guy who survived a relocation, two trades in one season. What do you think when Columbus takes your name?

Geoff Sanderson

Well, I don't know Columbus. That's the honest answer. I knew it was a cold building, I knew it was a new team, and I knew the, uh, the whole thing was, you know, they're starting from scratch. I wasn't going there to win the Cup tomorrow. I went there to play, you know? I'd been bounced so many times that a new locker room was, was familiar. At least it wasn't a surprise.

Marcus Bell

And the numbers followed. You scored 30-plus in your first year, 30-plus again in 2002-03. Then the trade deadline in 2003-04 and you're back in a Vancouver sweater. What went wrong?

Geoff Sanderson

Nothing went wrong. That's the thing people get wrong about that. I wasn't unhappy. The team needed a different piece, I wasn't it, and the deal made sense for everybody. I scored a goal in Calgary in the playoffs that year, we lost the series, and I thought, okay, that's the summer. I'll figure out what's next.

Marcus Bell

And what's next was, a call from Columbus?

Geoff Sanderson

A lot of phone calls actually. But yeah, Columbus was the first one that felt right. You know why? Because they asked me to be a captain. Before I'd even signed, before the ink. Somebody said, we want you here to hold this together. I've never had that. Thirty years old, twelve seasons in, and nobody had ever stood in front of me and said, you're the one. So yeah, I picked Columbus.

Marcus Bell

That changes the profile of the signing. This wasn't a depth deal. This was a piece of the room they wanted.

Geoff Sanderson

And I told them, look, I'm not a coach on skates, I'm not going to lecture the young guys. I'll just play and if they want to watch, they can watch. But it means something. When you're twenty-three, twenty-four, and the captain is in front of the net late in the third, and he's getting hit, and he's not going down? That's a conversation without words.

Marcus Bell

Let's talk about this year, because 38 goals in 38 games is, I think, the highest goal total in the league right now. Are you surprised? At 32, with 14 seasons behind you?

Geoff Sanderson

Surprised is too strong. I'm, I'm pleased. I'll take that word. Look, I had a good year last season, 115 points in 64, that was a good year. But I didn't feel like I was in the right system. Not that I was bad. I was, you know, a piece of the puzzle. Not the center of it.

Marcus Bell

So what's different in the system here?

Geoff Sanderson

They play to my speed. That's the whole thing. I'm, I'm not the fastest guy in the league at 32 anymore, I don't kid myself. But this setup, they get me open in space, they give me one beat, and I can still separate. With Todd on my wing, he wins the puck on the wall and he's not trying to be the hero, he just slides it across and I have a three-on-two before the defense sets. David , he reads that same play without me looking. So it's not, it's not me suddenly being a different player. It's the space I'm playing in.

Marcus Bell

You're on the Development Index up one. That's not a lot, but at 32 with a base of 87, that tells you he's moving up.

Geoff Sanderson

I know what those numbers are, Marcus. You don't have to sell me. I've read 'em. But I'll tell you what, the Index can say whatever it wants. What I feel is that my legs are there in the third period. I haven't had a game this year where I thought, I can't get back for the next shift. That's different from 2002-03, I won't lie to you. And if that's an Index number, fine. I'll take the Index number if the legs feel like that.

Marcus Bell

You missed a game in that stretch between Christmas and New Year's. A knock. You're back now, how is it?

Geoff Sanderson

It's done. I'm fine. The doctors cleared me and I'm on the ice. I don't like sitting, you know? I sat one game and I was pacing in the room, so I'm back. It was nothing serious, the staff made sure of that.

Marcus Bell

This room has three guys 24 or younger, and Pascal Leclaire in goal, and Marc Denis behind him. What does that room feel like from the inside when you're the captain?

Geoff Sanderson

It feels hungry. That's what it is. You know, in Vancouver, or in Buffalo, you had guys who'd been there ten years and the hunger was, uh, settled. It was there but it was comfortable. Here, Leclaire is twenty-four and he's got 84 in the Book and he plays like he's got something to prove to you personally. Not you, I'm just saying. To anybody. So that's good for me. I don't want a room full of old guys patting each other on the back in March.

Marcus Bell

What's the target for you personally? You're on 166 pace. Do you look at that?

Geoff Sanderson

I don't, not really. I know it's there. You can't be in this league for twelve years and not know the math. But I think about, okay, when did we get hot? When did the goals start coming easier? I think it was the third week of November. That's when things clicked. And I think if we stay on that, I'll be at 60 goals. Maybe 55. The pace, the pace is what it is. It doesn't matter if I get 60 and we don't make the playoffs.

Marcus Bell

And if you do make them? You've been in one Final. Buffalo, 1999. Dallas, the triple-overtime goal from Brett Hull.

Geoff Sanderson

You know, that one, that one I don't think about much. You remember it, obviously. But it was a long time ago. What I remember more is, I was on a team that got close. And I've been on teams that were a thousand miles from close. So this one, this room, we're not there yet. But the feeling is different than it was in Vancouver at the deadline, I'll tell you that. The feeling is, we're building something and I'm part of it. That's more than I had the last time around.

Marcus Bell

Two years left on the contract. $6.34 million. Does that matter, what you want to leave as your legacy here in Columbus?

Geoff Sanderson

I don't think about legacy. I've never thought about legacy. I think about Thursday, and then I think about Saturday. But what I'll say is this, I want these young guys to remember that somebody came here who could have stayed wherever and chose this. And then showed up every day. That's, that's what I want. Not a banner. Not a number on the wall. Just, the guys remember.

Marcus Bell

Geoff Sanderson, captain, Columbus Blue Jackets. Geoff, thanks for your time.

Geoff Sanderson

Thanks, Marcus. Appreciate it.
