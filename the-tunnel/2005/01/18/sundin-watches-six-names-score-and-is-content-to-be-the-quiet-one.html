Sundin watches six names score and is content to be the quiet one

The captain took one point in a 6 and 1, says the room smells where the ice opens when a team presses, and circles back to the first period he has been fixing since December.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelTuesday, 18 January 2005

2:21 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Mats Sundin88 had one assist in Toronto Maple Leafs's 6 and 1 win over Washington Capitals on 2005-01-17, a night the Capitals sent 35 shots toward the Toronto net and the Leafs buried six of their 19. Toronto sits at 34 and 8 and 1, 75 points, the best record in the league. Sundin carries 28 goals and 55 points through 46 games at 18.1 minutes a night.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here with Mats Sundin, straight off the ice. Thirty-five shots against, nineteen for your side, and six on the scoresheet. How does a game like that happen?

Mats Sundin

Well, they press a lot. When you score on them early, the two, the three, they have to open up, and then their guys are coming but they are leaving the back door. For sure, thirty-five shots, most of those are from the outside, from the point, nothing clean. And when we get the puck out, we are four, five guys going, and the ice is there. So you score on the counter and they keep coming and you score again. It gets like that.

Jan Kowalczyk

Six different names scored tonight. Mogilny, Roberts, Chlup, Richards, Svehla, Afinogenov. And you had the one assist. Is there a feeling in that room when everybody finds the scoresheet?

Mats Sundin

Yeah, that is the best way to win, of course. When it is one guy, the other team, they key on him. When it is six, you do not know who to watch. I had the one point, that is fine. The points, you know, they come and go. But the win, that is the thing.

Jan Kowalczyk

You had that one assist on one of the early ones, before it opened up?

Mats Sundin

I think so. I think Mogilny had the one, I was coming through the middle, he put it in the slot, I pushed it across. Something like that. I do not remember exactly. There are so many of these nights.

Jan Kowalczyk

You and Alexander, you have been together a while. In the old days you played together in Toronto. Does it get easier every year?

Mats Sundin

Well, we know each other. I know where he is going before he goes, and he knows where I am. That helps. But also, I am 33 now, so I am not coming through the middle the same way. I play more ahead of the play, I am there first, the puck comes to me. That is how I still get these. You have to change, or you fall behind.

Jan Kowalczyk

We spoke in December and I asked you about the first period, and then we got on to something else. Has that changed? The start of games?

Mats Sundin

Yeah, that one. I think it is better. The coach, he watches the video, he shows us the first five minutes, and we just, we do not chase the puck anymore. We wait. In December we were, I think, too eager, too much energy in the wrong place. Now the first period is like the second. The same speed. It took some time but I think it is there.

Jan Kowalczyk

Thirty-four and eight, 75 points through 46. Everyone around the league is talking about you. Does any of that get into the room?

Mats Sundin

No. Well, you see the standings, of course, everybody does. But in the room, it is the next shift, the next game. The coach gives me 18 minutes now instead of 23, and I am fresh, and the young guys, Gaborik, Richards, they go hard in short shifts, and everyone is fresh. That is how it works. We are not thinking about 75.

Jan Kowalczyk

Mats, thank you. Enjoy the rest of the evening. Back to you.
