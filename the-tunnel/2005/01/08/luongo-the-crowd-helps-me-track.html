Luongo: the crowd helps me track

Florida's goaltender on winning in a building on edge, and what three different goal scorers do to the man behind them.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelSaturday, 8 January 2005

2:16 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Roberto Luongo97 stopped 19 of 21 shots as Florida Panthers won 3-2 at Vancouver Canucks on Tuesday night, their fourth straight win. The Panthers are now 10-11 on the road this season, and Luongo's Development Index sits at +2 with a 94 overall in the Bureau's Book.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here, Roberto. 3-2 in their building, fourth straight. Todd Bertuzzi said out there that you came in and hit them, set the tone early. Did that change things for you back here?

Roberto Luongo

Yeah, for sure. When the building gets... you know, when they're angry and the guys come out of the zone fast, everybody's trying to make the play. The first shift they want to put one in and the crowd is right there, and everyone's pushing, and the puck is moving quick. So for me, it's... I like it. When they're trying, the puck is easy to track. The one-nil comes fast and then, you know, the energy goes and the guys get comfortable. I just have to be there.

Jan Kowalczyk

You face 21 tonight. That's about the fewest any goaltender has faced this week. Three different guys on the scoresheet, Nieuwendyk, Jokinen, Kozlov. Does that feel different in front of you?

Roberto Luongo

Oh yeah. Different game. When the three of them are going, the play, it stays in their end. So I'm watching the game, I'm moving side to side, but nobody's on me. Nobody in my paint. And the defense, they can breathe, they get the puck out clean, they change. It's the easy night for me, you know? I don't want to take anything away from it. They scored, I made the saves they give me.

Jan Kowalczyk

And when the goals come that way, from three different lines, does that shift how the guys in front of you play? Do they trust you more, or do you trust them more?

Roberto Luongo

I think it's both. Yeah. When the puck is in the other zone, the D, they step up. They're not scared to pinch because they know the forwards are coming back. So I'm not, you know, I'm not watching two-on-one after two-on-one. It's clean. And when it's clean, I can read the play early. I can see the pass before he makes it. That's where I'm good. That's the whole thing.

Jan Kowalczyk

Fourth straight win, you're 14 and 15 on the season. Marcus asked you back in December about the young core and when the record turns. It's turned. Four in a row.

Roberto Luongo

Yeah, Marcus, I remember. He said, come talk to me when it's going good, you know? I'm not gonna... I don't have a speech about the young guys tonight. I just... the guys are playing the same way they were three weeks ago. The pucks go in now. That's the difference. Three weeks ago the puck hits the post, tonight it goes in. I don't think the young guys changed. I think the game changed around them.

Jan Kowalczyk

Felix Potvin behind you tonight, 87 in the Book. Does having him in that locker room affect how you prepare for a road game?

Roberto Luongo

Yeah, for sure. He's been everywhere, you know? Every building in this league, he knows the... the angles of the glass, the way the light hits at seven o'clock versus nine. So I ask him. On the road, before the game, I say 'Felix, where's the sun tonight, where's the bad corner.' He tells me. That's... yeah, that's part of it. You don't waste that.

Jan Kowalczyk

Roberto, thanks for stopping. Enjoy the win.
