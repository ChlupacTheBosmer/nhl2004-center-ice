Richards names the invisible job, finally

Toronto's 24-year-old centre scored the fifth goal in a 5-0 win over Boston, talked through the goalie switch and the line changes, and gave Jan Kowalczyk the answer he owed him since December.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelSaturday, 8 January 2005

2:58 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Brad Richards85 had 1 goal and 1 point in Toronto Maple Leafs's 5-0 win over Boston Bruins on Friday night, one of five different Toronto scorers. He is at 46 points in 41 games this season, up from 28 in 51 last year, and the Bureau's Development Index has him 4 above his base grade. Toronto sits atop the league at 31-6-1, 69 points, on a three-game win streak.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here in the tunnel. Toronto five, Boston zero, 14 shots on the other side, three straight, 69 points. You were one of five different scorers tonight. How did that one go in?

Brad Richards

Yeah, it, you know, I came in the slot and the D, they were, they were pinched, both of them, and there was just a window. I put it top shelf. One of five, you look at the scoresheet and that's, that's a team that clicked tonight. Five guys, different ways, and the 14 shots tells you the rest of the story.

Jan Kowalczyk

And Belfour's on the bench. That's the Jennings winner out there watching a shutout from the side. Walk me through that.

Brad Richards

You know, Ed's Ed. Nobody in that room thinks any less of Ed. But the young guy's earned it. Practice every day, sharp, square, and tonight he's tested maybe eight, nine times and he makes every one. You can't, you can't pull a goalie who's giving you a game like that. Coach made the call, it was the right one.

Jan Kowalczyk

The lines looked shuffled. Did you feel a difference out there, or is it just a blowout and the ice opens up?

Brad Richards

It's not a blowout in the first period, Jan. It's two-zero and it's tight and we're still earning it. But yeah, I had different guys on my wings, and, you know, you get a different rhythm. Gary was pushing their D into their own corner every shift, and Mats was starting plays from behind their blue line, so my job was just, just to get there first and not overthink it. When a guy like Gaborik is on the far wing, you don't think. You put it where he's going and the rest is his.

Jan Kowalczyk

I asked you in early December to name the one job in this room that doesn't show up on the sheet. You never gave me an answer. You owe me one.

Brad Richards

Yeah. Yeah, I know. I've been chewing on it, actually, I have. It's the first read in the neutral zone. Before the puck touches your stick, you've got to know where four other guys are going. Not the pass, not the carry, the look before either one. And if you get it right, the play works, the next man's open, and nobody writes anything down. And if you get it wrong, the whole thing folds and nobody writes anything down either. That's the invisible one. You make the read or you don't, and it doesn't count on a stat line.

Jan Kowalczyk

46 in 41. Last year you had 28 in 51. That's a big jump. Is it the system, the guys around you, or just a 24-year-old who got stronger?

Brad Richards

Probably all three. The system gives me space I didn't have before, which helps, obviously. I've got a guy like Gaborik on the wing and Mats drawing a matchup every night, so I'm not, I'm not the man they double all evening. And yeah, I'm 24, I've got more legs than I did at 22. But I'll tell you what, there are shifts I played this year where nobody got a point and I walked to the bench thinking, that was the best shift I've taken all night. The number's not the whole thing.

Jan Kowalczyk

Last one. You're 21 and 3 here, 13 and 4 on the road, and January is coming in heavy. What does the next stretch ask you?

Brad Richards

It asks you to not coast. That's the honest one. You win five, six straight, 69 points, you're the best record in the league, and every room you walk into, the other guy's game plan is you. The legs get heavy on the road, the bounces go the wrong way, and you start trying to make the play that doesn't need making. The schedule doesn't get easier. You've just got to be the team that doesn't get soft.

Jan Kowalczyk

Brad Richards, three straight, five different scorers, and you finally gave me an answer. Thank you. Back to you.
