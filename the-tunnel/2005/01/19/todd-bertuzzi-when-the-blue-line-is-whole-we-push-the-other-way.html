Todd Bertuzzi: When the Blue Line Is Whole, We Push the Other Way

The Canucks' right wing credits Marc Crawford's tighter structure and the return of Mattias Ohlund for the feel of a 3-2 road win that puts Vancouver on a two-game streak.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelWednesday, 19 January 2005

2:33 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Todd Bertuzzi89 collected 2 points in Vancouver Canucks's 3-2 road win over Chicago Blackhawks on Monday, bringing him to 83 points in 46 games this season. The Canucks sit at 25-13-5 and are on a two-game win streak. Jan Kowalczyk spoke with him in the corridor after the game.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here with Todd Bertuzzi of Vancouver Canucks®, 2 points tonight in a 3-2 win over Chicago Blackhawks®. Two weeks ago I was in your room after Montreal and you told me four defensemen were gassed by the third, the team was chasing. Tonight you looked like a different group. 21 shots to 19, nobody flinched in a tough building. What's changed?

Todd Bertuzzi

Yeah, well. Marc's tightened it up, you know? He got everybody on the same page and the lines are clicking. When you've got Markus Naslund driving the net and Brendan Morrison making the first play, you just get to the slot and be ready for the second chance. We're, you know, we're not chasing pucks anymore. We're making the first pass and getting there.

Jan Kowalczyk

The simplified approach, you mentioned it two weeks ago too. So what's shifted since then? Is it something specific Crawford changed?

Todd Bertuzzi

I think it's the gap. The first defenseman's gap. He's been on us about it every single day in practice. Get your gap, make the first read, don't gamble. And it sounds stupidly simple but when you're tired in the third period, you lose it. He keeps hammering it. That's just how he is.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let me go back two weeks. Ed Jovanovski and Mattias Ohlund both out, down to four D. You told me everyone was cooked by the third. Who carried the load during that stretch?

Todd Bertuzzi

Carried the load. The guys back there were fine, Jan. They just had more ice than they're used to. And Trevor Linden was out there blocking things he shouldn't have to block at this point in his career. Nobody was bad. You just lose two of your top four and everybody eats a little extra. That's the whole thing.

Jan Kowalczyk

And now Mattias Ohlund is back. 2 points tonight. Trevor Linden a point too. You can feel the difference back there?

Todd Bertuzzi

Yeah, obviously. You want the obvious answer, here it is. Mattias Ohlund moves the puck, he jumps into plays, he clears the zone clean. You're not scrambling. And when you're not scrambling up ice, you push it the other way. You get your guys back, you're a better team. That's not a mystery.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're sitting at 83 points in 46 games, 21.3 minutes a night. How are the legs?

Todd Bertuzzi

They're good. They feel good, I won't lie. But I'm not making those plays by myself. Brendan Morrison puts it on my tape every night, Markus Naslund is dragging a defenseman with him every shift so I've got half a step. And half a step at this level, that's everything. It's not me. It's the guys around me making me look good.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're 13 and 9 on the road and tonight fits that. Hostile building, tight one, you don't blink. Easier away from home?

Todd Bertuzzi

It's the same game. I promise you, same game every night. Maybe you carry less on the road, I don't know. You know the crowd's against you so you don't try to give them a show. You go out, you work, you take the two. And at home you press, you try to do too much, and the mistakes come. That's how it feels to me anyway.

Jan Kowalczyk

Todd Bertuzzi, thank you as always. Jan Kowalczyk, back to you.
