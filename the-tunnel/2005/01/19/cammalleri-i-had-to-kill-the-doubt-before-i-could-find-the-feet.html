Cammalleri: 'I had to kill the doubt before I could find the feet'

The 22-year-old alternate captain tells Marcus Bell what 20 points in 69 games taught him, why the West Regional MVP is the record he keeps, and what changed in a summer at the rink alone.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelWednesday, 19 January 2005

5:18 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Mike Cammalleri87 sat down with Marcus Bell in the studio on the night Center Ice published '20 Points, Then 92,' the profile on his jump from 20 points in 69 games last season to 27 in 24 for Los Angeles Kings this year, a 92-point pace at 20.8 minutes a night. He's 22, the alternate captain, and up six on the Bureau's Development Index with a morale of 98. He came out of Michigan a two-time All-American and the MVP of a Frozen Four run, and managed 11.8 minutes a shift in his first NHL season.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here with Mike Cammalleri in the studio. Mike, the profile went up tonight on Center Ice. '20 Points, Then 92.' You saw it?

Mike Cammalleri

Yeah, obviously. Twenty last year, twenty-seven in twenty-four this year, that's the 92. You know, it's a nice number to have, but I mean, the team's the thing. I just want to score and help the boys. The number's fine. It's fine.

Marcus Bell

But you don't go from 11.8 minutes a night to 20.8 without something changing. Last year you're in and out, 20 points in 69 games. This year you're the A and you're every night. What changed?

Mike Cammalleri

I don't know that it's one thing. I mean, yeah, last year was... it was rough. I came out of Michigan and I thought I knew what was coming. I didn't. The speed, every shift you get and you've got to prove yourself all over again. I was getting 11, 12 minutes and I'm, I'm not showing anyone what I can do in that. And by the end of the year I was going through the motions a little.

Marcus Bell

And the summer?

Mike Cammalleri

The summer I went back to the basics. I was at Michigan four years and I thought I had the hands, the deking, I thought I had all that. And then you get here and you realize the hands are the first thing that slows you down if your feet aren't there. So I spent the whole summer on just being light on my feet, the edges. Not trying to be stronger. You're not gonna be stronger than these guys. You just gotta be quicker with it.

Marcus Bell

You were drafted 49th in 2001 and you stayed at Michigan. Three more years. Led them to two Frozen Fours. MVP of the West Regional. You ever think the kid who came in last year, the 2003-04 you, was playing a college game in an NHL building?

Mike Cammalleri

You know, I've thought about that. A lot. Michigan, we had the puck, we moved it, and I was the guy making the play. Every play. And then you get here and you're playing with guys who are as good as you are at every position. Zigmund Palffy's on the other wing and he's been doing this, what, ten years? And he's better than me, obviously. That's fine. But the first year I was trying to play the Michigan game in here. And it wasn't working. I had to learn to be the second option. And that's humbling, for sure.

Marcus Bell

The elbow to start this year. You come back on December 14th. And then it's like a switch flips. 24 games since you came back, 27 points. Did sitting out help?

Mike Cammalleri

The elbow? I mean, obviously I didn't want to be hurt. But I sat there for a couple weeks and I watched the guys from the room. And I could see where the space was. Where Zigmund gets the puck and Jason Allison's at the half-wall and I'm the third man, the one making the read. And when I came back I think I was seeing it differently. Not as the guy who has to make it all happen. Just be there, make the play.

Marcus Bell

You've got the A. That's on the sheet. What does that mean to you at this point in your road?

Mike Cammalleri

It means I've got a vote in the room and I'm trying to be honest with it. I'm not gonna stand up and give speeches. I talk to the guys. I remember being in it last year, thinking I'm not good enough to be here. And I'm not saying that to put myself down. I'm just saying I know what that feels like and I try to make sure nobody else sits in it for as long as I did.

Marcus Bell

Let's go back. 20 points, 69 games, last year. You said it was rough. What's the thing from that year you take and put in a box?

Mike Cammalleri

The doubt. That's the thing. I had four years where I was All-American twice, I was MVP of a tournament, I led Michigan in scoring. And then the first year here you're sitting in the room after a game and you got one shot on net and you're thinking, okay, maybe they were wrong. Maybe I don't belong. And that... I had to kill that. That's the summer work. It wasn't just the feet. It was believing I had a shot here. That's the box. That goes in the box.

Marcus Bell

So the question for the sweater. What's the record on the sheet you're proudest of?

Mike Cammalleri

The West Regional. Leading Michigan back to the Frozen Four in '02. That was, I was 19 and we were a young team. Nobody picked us. And I got the MVP and we were going to the national tournament again. That was... that was the answer. I'd said no to the OHL at 15 and everyone thought I was losing my mind. And that was me proving it worked. Not to me. To everybody else. The college path, the whole thing. It worked out.

Marcus Bell

You said no to the OHL. St. Michael's Majors, the Priority Draft. You turned it down at 15 to keep your college eligibility.

Mike Cammalleri

I was. And my parents thought I was insane. My mom, you know, she's like, you're playing Junior A in Bramalea, you're not in an OHL arena. And I'm like, well, I'm scoring 103 points in 41 games in the OPJHL. But yeah. You're 15 and you're supposed to know what you want to do for the rest of your life. And I just... I wanted to go to school. I wanted Michigan. And I was going. That was the plan. I don't think I ever second-guessed it, even the bad year here.

Marcus Bell

And the Cold War. Michigan State, outdoors, 74,554 people. You nearly didn't play because of the hip flexor. You figured in all three Michigan goals in a 3-3 draw.

Mike Cammalleri

Yeah. I was in the trainer's room trying to stretch, trying to get it loose enough to skate. And my roommate's like, you're not gonna play. And I'm like, yeah I am. And I go out there and I've got 74,554 people and the ice is terrible, obviously, it's outside, and I just... I just played. Best game I ever played. Maybe. Before this year.

Marcus Bell

The room you're in now. Zigmund with 79 points, Jason Allison sitting 81 on the Book, Roman with 85. Where's this team's ceiling?

Mike Cammalleri

I think it's high. You know, Zigmund... he's the one I watch every night. He's putting up goals and he's still doing it. He's the reason I get the looks I get, because nobody can just leave him on the wing. Jason makes everything go. And Roman... you get a goalie like that and you're not losing games you should win. I want to be in April. That's the thing. We weren't close last year and I want that this year.

Marcus Bell

Last one. The profile says 'one-summer education.' What was the one thing that summer taught you?

Mike Cammalleri

If I had to say one thing... that I was allowed to be the player I am. I didn't have to be bigger. I didn't have to be the guy in front of the net. I'm 180 pounds, I'm 6'9" but I'm not... I'm not going to be that. I'm the guy making the play from the half-wall. And once I accepted that, once I stopped trying to be something else, the game opened up. And the minutes came. And the points came with it.

Marcus Bell

Mike Cammalleri, thank you for the time.

Mike Cammalleri

Thank you, Marcus. Appreciate it.

Marcus Bell

Back to you.
