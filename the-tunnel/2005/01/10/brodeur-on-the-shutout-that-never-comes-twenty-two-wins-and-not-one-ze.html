Brodeur on the shutout that never comes: "Twenty-two wins and not one zero"

The Devils' goaltender, 22 wins in 33 games and still no shutout, talks about the protection in front of him and the one save he keeps missing.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelMonday, 10 January 2005

2:51 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Martin Brodeur92 picked up his 22nd win of the season, a 3-2 decision over Montreal Canadiens on Friday night at the Meadowlands. He faced 19 shots and stopped 17. In 33 games this year, the shutout column reads zero.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here with Martin Brodeur. Martin, 3-2, a win, you're back on the win column after a couple days off. How did that one feel from your side? 19 shots, it looked like it got quiet at times.

Martin Brodeur

Yeah, for sure. It's... you know, you sit there and you watch them forecheck, and the guys in front of me, they're clearing the middle, so I'm just... tracking pucks from the point, making the easy ones. But then they get one on a screen and the second one is a rebound and you're back in it. It's not a quiet game for me. It's a game where you have to stay there for sixty minutes even if they only send you twenty pucks.

Marcus Bell

The defense had 20 shots on the other side tonight. Marshall had two, Rafalski one. That's a lot of ice time in their zone. Does it feel different, the protection, when your guys are camping in the other end versus when it's a back-and-forth?

Martin Brodeur

Oh, totally. When Grant is driving the net and the D are, you know, they're breathing, they're not scrambling... the puck comes to me clean, I see it early, I'm set. That's when I'm good. When we're, uh, when we're turning it over in our own end and their guys are coming back the other way? That's a different game. That's where I'm not as... that's where the inconsistency, you know? That's where it gets ugly.

Marcus Bell

You just said inconsistency. I want to put that on the board. 33 games, 22 wins, and zero shutouts. Zero. You're the Vezina winner from last year. What's happening with that last save, the one that gets you the zero?

Martin Brodeur

Yeah. I know. I see it too, every morning. Twenty-two and not one zero. The guys, they make it hard for me! No, no... I'm joking. But look, the game is tight and we're up one, two and the other team is throwing everything, they're pulling the goalie, they're crashing the net, and I'm making the first save, the second save, and the third one... the third one I'm late. I'm a half-inch late on the glove. And that's it. That's the difference between a win and a zero.

Marcus Bell

You mentioned Ari Ahonen. He's behind you, 86 overall in the Book. Does that change anything in your room, having a guy that good ready to go?

Martin Brodeur

Yeah, it does. It's... for sure, it's good for me. I don't have to... you know, the old days I was playing sixty-four games, every night, and you're... the legs, they go. This year, Vesa gets some starts, and I'm fresh, and Ari pushes in practice. So when I'm out there, my legs are there. That's why my game looks, you know, it looks like last year's game. The movement's there. I just need to close the door on the last one.

Marcus Bell

You were out a few days over the New Year. Came back tonight and played sixty. Did you feel any rust?

Martin Brodeur

No, no. I was, uh, I was skating every day. It's a little thing, the doctors said a few days and it's done, so I'm, I'm fine. The first period I'm, I'm finding my feet, yeah, but by the second I'm back. That's the job. You don't get a week off. You don't get a week off in this league, for sure.

Marcus Bell

One more. You're at the top of the standings in the Atlantic, but Toronto is 71 points, you're at 51. They're scoring 239, you're at 141. Do you look at that gap?

Martin Brodeur

Every morning I look, Marcus. I'm not going to lie to you. They've got... what, five guys that can score on any night. We've got Patrik, we've got the guys, and we win 3-2, 2-1. And that's... that's how I know them. That's my game. I win them tight. The problem is I don't win them clean. And in April, the clean ones, they matter. They matter a lot. So I want... I want those zeros. Twenty-two and one zero would be a different interview tonight.

Marcus Bell

Martin Brodeur, thank you. Back to you.
