Ken Hitchcock, after Toronto: the road win, the two men out, and the net

The Flyers took the best record in the league to overtime and won 2-1 on 32 shots, and Hitchcock said the good mood lasts about as long as the flight home.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelSaturday, 22 January 2005

2:54 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Philadelphia Flyers won 2-1 in overtime at Toronto Maple Leafs on 2005-01-21, outshooting the club with the best record in the league 32-21, and moved to 47 points with a 7-17 road mark. Ken Hitchcock, the Flyers' coach since 2002, took questions in the tunnel on the road record, the two forwards out of his lineup, and a goaltending workload he manages one morning at a time.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk, The Tunnel, with Flyers head coach Ken Hitchcock, after the 2-1 overtime win here in Toronto. Coach, 32 shots on the road against the best team in the league. What did you see?

Ken Hitchcock

Well, you know, the first ten minutes I didn't see much I liked. They had us. We were, we were backing in and the puck was in our end the whole time. Then we got our feet, we started skating, and the last half of that game, that's the part I want. Thirty-two shots in that building against that team. That's a good night. We'll take it.

Jan Kowalczyk

It's the road that's been the puzzle. 7-17 away from home, 14-9 in your own building. What's different out there?

Ken Hitchcock

If I had one answer, Jan, I'd have fixed it by November. I don't. What I know is, on the road you get one matchup you don't like and you spend the night chasing it. Tonight we didn't chase. We got above the puck, we managed it, and we won a tight game. That's the recipe. We just don't follow it every night.

Jan Kowalczyk

Tampa at home, the overtime in New York, tonight. What's the mood in the room after a week like that?

Ken Hitchcock

Better than it was Wednesday night, I'll say that. You lose in overtime in New York and you fly home and you don't sleep. Tonight's a good plane. But I've been around too long to think one night changes a room. We're 16-21-5. Tomorrow we watch the video and I'm yelling about the first period again.

Jan Kowalczyk

You haven't moved a line all week.

Ken Hitchcock

No, and I'm not going to. You shuffle four lines, you buy yourself a month of guys looking over their shoulder, wondering if the next shift is their last one. We've got the pairs we like and the men know where they stand. That's worth more to me than a spark for one night.

Jan Kowalczyk

Two forwards out. One back on the 28th, the other not until late February. Who fills the hole?

Ken Hitchcock

That's the part that doesn't show up anywhere. You lose two bodies and it isn't the goals you miss, it's the minutes. Somebody has to eat them. We've got the next man up, he's been in the room, he knows the job. Some nights you can see him learning it on our ice. I'd rather have the men back. I'm not going to use it, though. Nobody in that room is going to hear me use it.

Jan Kowalczyk

The net. Jeff Hackett has played 39 of your 47 games. Can you keep asking that of him?

Ken Hitchcock

Jeff Hackett is a pro and he wants it, and 39 games tells you what we think of him. But that's a lot of hockey for anybody, and we've got Robert Esche at 13 games and he needs to play, for himself and for us. I don't make that call Monday for Friday. I make it the morning of. Both of them are fine with that. They have to be.

Jan Kowalczyk

Robert Esche is 2-7 in those 13. Does a record like that change how you use him?

Ken Hitchcock

No. And I'll tell you why. Two and seven, that's a number that sits next to his name in a newspaper. It isn't the whole player. He's given us good minutes. He's a goaltender learning a hard position behind a team that, some nights, hangs him out. You want to write the 2 and the 7, that's your job. He's our guy and he's going to play.

Jan Kowalczyk

The Islanders Monday, Boston Tuesday, Tampa back home Friday.

Ken Hitchcock

Every one of those is a four-point night for a team like us. New York Islanders Monday, then Boston, then Tampa Friday, and I don't look past Monday. We're 47 points. There's ground to make up and we've given ourselves a hole to climb out of.

Jan Kowalczyk

Coach, thank you for your time. Jan Kowalczyk, in Toronto.
