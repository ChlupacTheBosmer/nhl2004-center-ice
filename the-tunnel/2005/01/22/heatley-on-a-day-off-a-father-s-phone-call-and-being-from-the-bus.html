Heatley on a day off, a father's phone call, and being from the bus

The Thrashers' right wing, 24 yesterday, talks about cooking fish in Atlanta, calling his mother in Calgary, and the city he was born in but has not seen since he was small.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelSaturday, 22 January 2005

4:04 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Dany Heatley90 turned 24 on Monday night, one night before sitting down with Marcus Bell. In his fourth season, he is at 39 goals and 75 points in 43 games, carrying Atlanta Thrashers through a 14-win stretch, on a contract with 1 year left. The profile ran when he was still 23. He was born in Freiburg, Germany, where his father played professional hockey, raised in Calgary, and schooled in Madison.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here from Center Ice, and in the chair across from me is Dany Heatley. Dany, happy belated birthday, you turned 24 yesterday. Did you do anything or was it just another day in a schedule that does not care about birthdays?

Dany Heatley

Thank you, Marcus. It was a day off, which is fortunate. I went to the building early for treatment, and then in the evening I went out to a restaurant downtown with one of the guys, the one who lives with me. I had not been there before. And I called my mother in the evening. That is the birthday. A meal and a phone call.

Marcus Bell

Your mother is in Calgary?

Dany Heatley

My mother Karin is in Calgary, yes. My father is not, I think he moved again. When you grow up the way I did, your father playing professional hockey, you get used to the idea that the family is in different places and the phone is the schedule. That is how it works. You do not always know where someone is on a given Tuesday.

Marcus Bell

Your father Murray was playing in Freiburg when you were born there in 1981. Do you remember that, or is it just a story your mother tells you?

Dany Heatley

I arrived in the world in the middle of a contract, that is what my mother tells me. She says it is the only explanation for why I ended up in this sport. I do not remember Freiburg at all. I was there two years, perhaps less. My earliest memory is Calgary. So Freiburg is a fact, it is not a feeling.

Marcus Bell

Does your father still watch from wherever he is? Call the next morning, the way your family does?

Dany Heatley

Yes. He retired, he is not playing anymore. But he watches every game on television, I am sure of that. And the next morning the phone rings and he has seen the goal. It is a short call. He does not need to say very much. He says one thing about the shift, and then he is done. I think that is his ritual. I respect it.

Marcus Bell

You played minor hockey in Calgary for the Buffaloes, then Junior A with the Canucks. The plan was always college in the United States?

Dany Heatley

Yes. The plan was the NCAA. I wanted to go to school in the States. So I went to the Junior A league in Calgary to keep my eligibility, to stay amateur. That was the structure, you understand. I was 16, 17, and the plan was: go to a university, play four years, then see. The university took two. Then Atlanta took me second overall. So the plan was, what, half a plan.

Marcus Bell

Madison, Wisconsin. How old were you when you got there?

Dany Heatley

Seventeen. And I think that is the most important thing that happened to me besides hockey. I had to cook. I had to study and get myself to the rink and make the schedule work. Before that, everything was arranged by my mother or by the team. In Madison I had two years where I was, I would say, alone in a practical sense. And that prepared me for this. For living in a hotel in Columbus on a Tuesday.

Marcus Bell

So on a day off now, in Atlanta, what does that practical twenty-four year old do?

Dany Heatley

I go to the store. I get what I need and I make something. Pasta, or fish. Nothing complicated. And then I walk. I have been finding restaurants downtown, some that I like. The city is not, I would say, a hockey city, and that is good for me. I can go and eat and nobody asks about the score. The rest of the day I read, I do nothing. It is important that I have a day where I am not, you know, the player. I am just the guy making pasta in his apartment.

Marcus Bell

Does the road feel different now that you're into your fourth season? When you were twenty and first coming to Atlanta, was it the same routine?

Dany Heatley

When you are twenty it is exciting. Every building is new. You are looking at the ice, the crowd, the city. You are, I do not know, hungry in a different way. Now I know which hotel in St. Louis has a quiet lobby. I know which restaurant has fish that is fresh. I know what time the bus arrives. It is not romantic. But it is efficient. And I would not trade it.

Marcus Bell

When you are on a three-game trip, is there something from Calgary that you miss specifically? Or is the feeling more general?

Dany Heatley

The silence. Calgary is a quiet city. Not silent. But quiet in the sense that nobody at the coffee shop knows your name. Nobody expects anything from you. And on the road you are always expected. You are always in the hotel, always the visitor. The city is somebody else's city and you are there for one thing only. So I call my mother. It helps. I do not need a long conversation. I just need to hear that the snow is falling in Calgary and the world is in its order.

Marcus Bell

You've never been back to Freiburg as a professional. Would you go, if you could get a weekend there?

Dany Heatley

I would go. I would walk to the stadium where my father played, I would see it. But I do not think it would be home. I am not from Freiburg. I am from Calgary, I went to school in Wisconsin, I live in Atlanta. I think when you are in this life, you are from the schedule. You are from the bus. You can go to Freiburg for a weekend and the people there would be kind, but you would not belong to it anymore. You belong to the next building.

Marcus Bell

Dany Heatley, thank you very much. Enjoy the fish.
