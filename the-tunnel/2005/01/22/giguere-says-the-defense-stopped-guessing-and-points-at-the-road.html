Giguere says the defense stopped guessing, and points at the road

The Ducks' goaltender says the back end has found its reads since December, but 9 wins in 25 road games is the number that will decide whether this group has anywhere to go.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelSaturday, 22 January 2005

2:31 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Jean-Sebastien Giguere97 stopped 16 of 18 as Mighty Ducks of Anaheim beat Minnesota Wild 3-2 in overtime on 2005-01-20, splitting a two-game set with the Wild. He carries a 21-20 record in 42 games with zero shutouts this season, and his club sits 14-8 at home against 9-16 on the road.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here with Jean-Sebastien Giguere, winner tonight, 3-2 in overtime against Minnesota. Jean-Sebastien, second game in three days, the second one that went the extra frame. How do you feel?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

Well, relieved. You know, we go there and we lose one we should not lose, and tonight we take the two. It's not pretty. It's never pretty against them. They, they just sit so low, they wait for you to make the mistake. But the guys held. We held. That's the two points.

Jan Kowalczyk

When I talked to you in early December you told me the coach's changes were giving this group speed. Six weeks later, the defense looked more settled around you tonight than it did then. What's the difference?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

The communication. That's the thing. In December the D, they're still guessing. You know, Niclas Havelid steps up and he's looking over his shoulder, he doesn't know if the forward is coming back. Now he knows. So the defense, she's not scrambling anymore. The gap between them and the forwards is clean and I'm not seeing those odd-man rushes that I saw, I don't know, four, five weeks ago. That's the difference.

Jan Kowalczyk

You told me in December that the zero next to the shutout column is something you own. Forty-two games, still no zero. After a two-goal game tonight, are the guys closer to getting you that one?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

They're closer. I'll tell you that much. The structure is tighter, they're getting me the saves clean, I'm making them. But the last one, the one that breaks the zero, it's always something small. A bad line change, a puck off a skate, a screen from the point that, that I didn't see. Always something small. I own it. I told you that and I'll tell you again, I own it. But I want it. You know, you don't get it by wanting it.

Jan Kowalczyk

From the crease, what do you see from Sergei Fedorov right now? Three points in two games, same as Erik Cole. Does their game look different to you than it did six weeks ago?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

From here? I see the other team turn their head. That's, that's what I see. They look for Sergei and the second they do, their blue line goes thin and Erik Cole gets the space. You know, when the D on the other side is watching Fedorov instead of watching their own gap, our guys are getting the first pass clean. And when they get the first pass clean, our defense is not stuck in our end. That's what I see from the crease. They're pulling a man out of the structure and the structure falls apart a little.

Jan Kowalczyk

Nine wins in twenty-five games on the road. Fourteen and eight here. What does the next stretch ask of this group?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

It asks us to win somewhere else. That's, that's it. You cannot lean on this building and the noise and everything, you can't lean on it every night and get to the place we want. On the road it's, it's flat. You're alone. And we've been alone too often this year. The guys know it. I know it. We've got to find it somewhere else.

Jan Kowalczyk

Jean-Sebastien Giguere, winner tonight. Thank you, enjoy the night.

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

Merci, Jan. Merci.
