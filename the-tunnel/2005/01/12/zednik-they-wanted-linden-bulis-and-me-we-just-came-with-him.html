Zednik: 'They wanted Linden. Bulis and me, we just came with him'

The 249th pick in 1994 has 72 points in 42 games on a contract that expires in June, and he tells Marcus Bell he still plays like the kid nobody drafted until round nine.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelWednesday, 12 January 2005

4:54 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Richard Zednik79 sits in the studio chair with 39 goals and 33 assists in 42 games for Montreal Canadiens, a pace that would carry him to 141 points if the season ran the distance. His contract has 0 years left. He came to Montreal from Washington on March 13, 2001, and he has not left since.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here, sitting down with Richard Zednik of the . Richard, good afternoon. Thirty-nine goals through forty-two games. You're sitting seventeenth in the league in scoring right now. Where does that live in your mind, the standings, the numbers, all that noise?

Richard Zednik

Well, you know, I try not to look at it too much. The guys, Saku, everybody, we just... we play the next game. You know? The numbers are nice, sure. But I don't think about seventeenth place. I think about tomorrow's practice.

Marcus Bell

Fair enough. But let me go back a ways. Nineteen ninety-four, ninth round, 249th overall. The Capitals take you out of Portland. That number, 249, does it mean anything to you still?

Richard Zednik

You know, I don't think about the number. But I think about... when you're twenty-three, twenty-four, in Portland, and the call doesn't come... you think maybe nobody will call. Ninth round. You hear your name and you think, okay. One door is open. That's enough.

Marcus Bell

And you made the roster. Your first full season, 1997-98, sixty-five games, seventeen goals. You were in Washington for six seasons. There's a story from there, the blonde hair. DC101 had a promotion, free tickets if fans dyed their hair blonde like yours. What do you remember about that?

Richard Zednik

Oh, I remember! I walk into the building, and there is, like, forty guys in the stands with blonde hair. Forty! And they're screaming my name. And I'm thinking, you know, this is stupid, but it's great. The first time people want a piece of you. It's funny, because I look terrible with the hair. But the fans, they don't care. They just want to be part of it.

Marcus Bell

March thirteenth, 2001. The Capitals send you and Jan Bulis and a first-round pick to Montreal for Trevor Linden, Dainius Zubrus and a second-rounder. The first-round pick became Alexander Perezhogin. Walk me through that day from where you were sitting.

Richard Zednik

You know, I don't think about it like a trade where they send me. I think about... they get Linden. Trevor Linden. A captain. A big man. That's the trade. They wanted Linden. And Bulis and me, we just... we go with him. You know? Like, we're not the package. Linden is the package. We are what you get when you open the box.

Marcus Bell

You and Bulis, though. You came together, and he's still in that room with you. Four years later. What does that mean, to have that thread back to where you started here?

Richard Zednik

Jan is... you know, he's like my brother in this building. We go together, the plane, the bus, we sit together on the road. He speaks Slovak now, a little. A very bad Slovak. But we try. You know, when you're new somewhere, and there's one other guy who came from the same place, the same day, same box... that helps. That really helps.

Marcus Bell

April 2002. Playoffs, Boston. Kyle McLaren catches you high. It's a dark night in that building. Did that change something about how you carried yourself, after?

Richard Zednik

I don't want to talk about that too much. You know? It's... bad night. The boys, they come to the hospital, Jose, Saku, everybody. And then you come back. And you play. That is the whole thing. You come back and you play.

Marcus Bell

Understood. Let me come forward to the ice. You scored 59 last season, 114 points. This year you're 39 and 33 in 42, different shape. The goals are the same pace, but your assists are down a touch. What's different about how they use you?

Richard Zednik

You know, in Washington, I play... I play like I have to prove something. Every shift, I try to do everything. Carry the puck, go to the net, fight, everything. Here, the bench says, okay, go here, be here, and you go, and you make the play, and you go. It's simpler. Saku, he takes the faceoff, he gets the puck, and I just... I go where I'm supposed to go. And the puck comes. And I put it in. That's the whole thing.

Marcus Bell

So the change was not one coach, not one system, but... the simplicity of it? Being told your spot and trusting it?

Richard Zednik

Yeah. And the room. You know, when you're in Washington, young, you try to be the guy every night. Here, Jose is in the net, Saku is the captain, and I'm not the man they look at. And that's... that's good. I don't need to be the man. I just need to be where the puck is going. And here, they tell me where it's going.

Marcus Bell

Your contract has zero years left. The Sweater ran a profile this week that called it your last winter in Montreal. Is that how it feels?

Richard Zednik

You know, I read that. I'm not stupid, Marcus. I read it. But when I'm on the ice, I don't think about June. I think about the next shift, next game, the next line change. But... but at night, in the apartment, quiet, sometimes you think. This is my building now. Four years. My kids, they know this room. They know where Jan sits. They know where Saku tapes his stick. And you think... is this where we stay? I don't know. I don't know yet.

Marcus Bell

You're twenty-nine. Your base grade is 87. You're on a one-year deal worth $1.80M. You know the arithmetic better than I do.

Richard Zednik

You say the numbers like that, Marcus, like it's a math problem. I know the numbers. Everybody knows the numbers. But what can I do with them? I can score goals. I can go to the net. I can help Jose. That's what I can do. The agent, he calls. I say, let's talk in May. Not now. Now I just want to play good hockey.

Marcus Bell

Last one. You went from a 249th-round kid nobody wanted to a guy sitting on a 141-point pace with the Canadiens. What would you say to the kid who gets picked late now, ninth round, maybe later?

Richard Zednik

I would say... you don't have to be the first guy. You don't have to be the best one in the room. You just have to not quit. Ninth round. You're the last one they call and everybody says you won't make it. But you, you just... you keep going. You keep going and you keep going, and one day you're here, sitting in the chair, talking to Marcus Bell. And that's... that's good enough. That's good enough.

Marcus Bell

Richard Zednik, thank you very much. Bring Jan Bulis next time, I'd like to hear what his Slovak sounds like.

Richard Zednik

You don't want that, Marcus. But I ask him. Thanks.
