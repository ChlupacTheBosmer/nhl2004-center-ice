Bullard: 40 points at $1.33M a night, and a travel budget that has cost 20 losses

Montreal's general manager sits down to account for the worst record in the Northeast, the money that bought it, and what he will sell by March.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelTuesday, 25 January 2005

4:23 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Stefaan Bullard runs Montreal Canadiens, a club 13-27-4 with 40 points and a $53.20M payroll that has returned $1.33M per point, the second-worst value in the league. His away record is 6-20, his travel budget is at zero, and his best player is 28-year-old goalie Jose Theodore [989] in the last year of a $5.50M deal. Marcus Bell sat across from him for five minutes.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Stefaan Bullard, general manager of Montreal, joins me in the studio. Stefaan, thirteen and twenty-seven and four. Forty points through forty-nine games. You're a long way from the fourth spot. How do you characterize this season to yourself right now?

Stefaan Bullard

It's a season where the results don't match the money, and I think that's the only honest way to put it. We've spent $53.20M. We have forty points. That's $1.33M a night for a standing, and I don't need you to tell me how that reads in the papers. I read them too. But I'll say this. The record is the record. I'm not going to make excuses for it. We're not playing well enough and the wins aren't there.

Marcus Bell

You mentioned the money. Your profit is $16.89M, your gate is $56.20M. Fans are coming. Attendance is 15,588. But they're watching 13 wins in 49. Is there a disconnect you feel between what the building supports and what the team delivers?

Stefaan Bullard

I feel it every time I look at the gate sheet. People pay $77 a seat and they get what they get. I don't blame them. I blame myself. The ticket price is what it is, the money comes in, and I've got to put a product on the ice that justifies it. Right now we don't. I own that. But I'll tell you something else. We were never going to compete with the top payroll clubs in this division. That's not an excuse, that's arithmetic. I've got to find a different way to win.

Marcus Bell

And that different way, for me, comes back to development. You've got young guys coming. Who is the player you're building this around? Who's the man in five years?

Stefaan Bullard

That's a fair question and I wish I had a cleaner answer than I'm going to give you. We moved Zednik for Linden, right? We went one direction. We wanted a captain, a body, a guy who changes how a room feels. That was the trade. But building around a veteran captain is one thing, building around a twenty-one-year-old center who's going to score eighty is another thing, and I don't have that kid yet. I've got pieces. I've got guys trying. The draft is in 134 days and maybe the answer is in there. Maybe I find him at the deadline. But today? I won't stand here and point at a number six forward and call him a franchise center.

Marcus Bell

You upgraded the workout room in December. Between the seventh and the eighth. That tells me you're trying to develop people in that building. How much does the rest of the situation cost you on that front?

Stefaan Bullard

We got the workout room up. That was one step, and I'll take it. Strength, endurance, those tracks, it's real. But I'll be honest with you about something nobody asks about. Our travel budget is zero. Not low. Zero. The worst in the league. And the modifier on that is minus six on every attribute when we're on the road. Six. I'll show you my road record and you'll tell me that's a coincidence. Six and twenty. You put a young player on a bus for twelve hours to a rink in Calgary and he's flat before the warmup. He's not learning how to win on the road because he can't. I want that fixed. It's a line item I'm fighting for right now.

Marcus Bell

That's a figure I can put in my notes then. Travel budget at zero, minus six on the road, and your record reflects it.

Stefaan Bullard

Write what you want. The record is six and twenty on the road. I'm telling you the budget is the reason. Maybe it's not the only reason. But it's a big one. And a young player in that situation doesn't develop. He survives. There's a difference.

Marcus Bell

Let's talk March. The deadline is 53 days away. You've got Jose Theodore in a contract year, $5.50M, 89 overall, 41 games in. Does he wear the sweater in February? Does he wear it in March?

Stefaan Bullard

Jose is our goalie today. He's 28, he's under contract through the end of the season. I'm not going to trade my number one goalie in January. What happens after... that's a summer conversation. He's in a contract year, sure, and we'll have that conversation. But right now he's in net and we're playing hockey. You ask me in March, I'll give you a different answer maybe.

Marcus Bell

What do you need to acquire at the deadline? If money isn't the lever, what's the currency?

Stefaan Bullard

Draft picks. That's what I've got to sell to make the thing work. I can't outspend anybody. I've got a payroll that's already where it needs to be for a team that's supposed to win more than thirteen games. So what I do by March is I look at who on my roster is worth more to somebody else than to me, and I convert that into a second round or a third round or a prospect who's going to be twenty years old in two years and might be the guy I couldn't name for you earlier. That's the currency. Not money.

Marcus Bell

Stefaan Bullard, general manager of Montreal. Thank you for your time.

Stefaan Bullard

Anytime, Marcus.
