Forsberg: 'The 2 they scored, that is the part that stays'

The Avalanche winger talks about filling the hole left by Kariya, Storr's first start in weeks, and why 25 shots still leaves a bad taste.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelTuesday, 25 January 2005

2:42 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Peter Forsberg95 had a goal and an assist for the Colorado Avalanche in a 3-2 win over Columbus Blue Jackets on Sunday night, his 89th point in 47 games. The Avalanche outshot Columbus 25 to 14 but let 2 of those 14 through, and with Paul Kariya90 out of the lineup, head coach Tony Granato sent Jordan Krestanovich onto the wing.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here in the corridor with Peter Forsberg. Three and two over Columbus, the second straight, and the Avalanche had 25 shots to their 14. Not exactly what I would call tight.

Peter Forsberg

Well. We are happy for the 2 points, for sure. But, no. It was tight. They had 14 and 2 went in. That is not good. You cannot, when you have 25 yourself, you cannot give them 2 on 14 and think, okay, that is fine. It is not fine.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let me ask about the lineup tonight. Paul Kariya was not in it. You had Jordan Krestanovich out there on the left side. How did that change the shape of your lines?

Peter Forsberg

Uh. It changes it, of course. Paul, he is a different player, he opens ice with his speed. Kresta, he is more, he is more, you know, he goes and he gets the puck deep and he makes them pay on the forecheck. So the rhythm is not the same. But he played good tonight. For a guy who does not get so many minutes, he was in the right spots. I think that is a big thing for him.

Jan Kowalczyk

Did you notice the difference in how the defense reacted? CBJ maybe keyed on the speed you had before and Kresta brings a different look.

Peter Forsberg

I think so. Yeah. They did not expect him on the wall in the second period, and he won a couple of battles there that gave us a lot of ice. That is what he can do. He is not going to beat you with speed, for sure, but he wins the puck and then Joe can do what Joe does. And Joe, of course, Joe had 3 points tonight. He carried us some of the time.

Jan Kowalczyk

Jamie Storr in net tonight. Last time we talked you were frustrated about what was happening in front of the crease, the screens, the traffic. 14 shots, 2 go in. Did it look different from out there?

Peter Forsberg

It looked better, for sure. I think the first period we were, we were not clearing the middle enough and that is on us, the forwards and the defense. But the second and third, we got the bodies out of front and Jamie could see. The 2 they scored, some of that is still, you know, the puck moves fast up close. But the 14 is not Jamie's doing, that is the guys in front. And they did good tonight.

Jan Kowalczyk

You scored the second one, I think. That is your 39th of the year, 89 points in 47 games. You and Joe and Milan, all three of you on the scoresheet. Does that first line still feel like the engine?

Peter Forsberg

I do not know about engine. I scored 1 and Joe had 3 and Milan had 2. That is 6 points on the line, sure. But the thing I see is, we get 25 shots and 3 go in. We should get more. The passing is there, Joe sees everything, Milan is open all night, and still we only get 3. So, of course it is nice that all three of us scored. But 3 on 25 is not, it is not enough for where we want to be.

Jan Kowalczyk

You are 26 and 15 and 3 now, on a 2-game run. You were talking about the slide last time we stood here. Does it feel like the turn?

Peter Forsberg

I think every game is a new game. We lost some tight ones, we won some tight ones, and now we are here. 26 and 15, that is not, it is good. It is not what it was in December, that is for sure. But you cannot look back and say, oh, we were 23 and 9, that was better. No. Tonight is what we have. 2 points. We go home.

Jan Kowalczyk

Peter Forsberg, Avalanche, in the corridor after a 3 and 2 win. Thank you, Peter. Back to you.
