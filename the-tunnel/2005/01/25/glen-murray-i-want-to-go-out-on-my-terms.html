Glen Murray: 'I want to go out on my terms'

The 168-point captain came back from an arm injury to find his contract expiring, a rival he admires in Jaromir Jagr, and a season to reclaim.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelTuesday, 25 January 2005

3:46 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Glen Murray83 won the Art Ross with 168 points last season, then played just 14 games this year after an arm injury kept him out until January 15. The Boston Bruins captain has 20 points and is playing on a contract that expires this summer.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here in the studio with Glen Murray, the Boston Bruins® captain. Glen, you're back. Ten days out there now, 20 points in 14 games. How does it feel?

Glen Murray

Good. That's the short answer. You sit in the room, you watch games you can't play in, and you think, just give me one shift. One. And then you're out there and the noise and the ice, everything is where it's supposed to be. You know, I didn't know I needed that as much as I did.

Marcus Bell

Let me take you back to the arm. When did you know something was wrong?

Glen Murray

Yeah, I knew. You play your whole career, you get sore, you get banged up, the regular stuff, you play through it. And this wasn't that. First time I took a hit along the boards and felt it, I said, okay, that's different. And the next day I tried to shoot and the snap wasn't there. And you know, for a guy like me, the snap is the job. When it isn't there, you know right away.

Marcus Bell

You stayed in the building the whole time. Day in, day out, in the room but not on the ice. What was that?

Glen Murray

You feel like you belong there whether you're wearing skates or not. But you sit in there and you smell the wintergreen and the boys are buzzing and you hear the music and you can't go. And you start thinking, I'm taking up a spot. I'm here and I'm not helping. And that's the hard part. It's not the arm. The arm, you heal. It's sitting in the room.

Marcus Bell

Glen, I'm going to ask the one everybody wants. Last season: 168 points, 22 in the playoffs, Art Ross, Lady Byng. And now 20 in 14. The word they keep writing is cliff. Does that land?

Glen Murray

Yeah, I see the headlines. 168 then 20, looks like a cliff. But it's not 20 in 82. It's 20 in 14. That's a different thing. And I know what I can do, I did it last year, 68 goals and 100 assists. I know what my game looks like. Does it bother me? No. Not being out there bothered me. The 20 is fine. The 20 will keep going.

Marcus Bell

You're 32. You missed most of the season. I'll put it to you straight. Does the body talk to you now in a way it didn't when you were 24?

Glen Murray

Yeah, it talks. I listen. I always listened, but you know, when you're young you don't hear all of it. You're 32, you hear every little thing. Every twinge, every tight spot. And I know my body isn't what it was. You take the hits I've taken, you play as long as I have, it stacks up. But the arm is healed. And the rest is good enough to play. I don't need it to be perfect. I just need it to get me through a game.

Marcus Bell

You've been watching a lot of hockey from the bench this year. Who do you watch on the other side? Who gets your attention?

Glen Murray

Jaromir. I watch him. That's who I watch. The guy's not even trying. That's the thing. You watch him, he's just skating and somehow the puck is on his stick and somehow there's a teammate open and somehow it's top shelf. He's not working. I'm grinding in the corners and this guy is out there painting. And he's 32, same as me, and he's leading the league. So I'm impressed. Not jealous. Impressed.

Marcus Bell

Both of you, early nineties, first round. Jaromir Jagr went fifth in 1990, you went 18th in 1991. Still the first guy on your team. Is there a thing there? A nod?

Glen Murray

There's a nod. That's what it is. You shake hands after and there's a look that says, hey, still here. Everybody else from our years, they're gone. They're coaching or they're in the booth or they're doing something else. And we're still out there. First line. That means something. I'd be lying if I said it didn't.

Marcus Bell

You've got zero years left on your contract. You're 32. You just came off a major injury. How do you think about the summer?

Glen Murray

Yeah, I think about it. I'd be stupid not to. But here's what I know. I want to play. I want to go out on my terms. If somebody asks, can you still be that guy, the one from last year, I'd say yes. I can. The arm's done, the shot is still there. So I want another run. I don't know what that looks like yet, the money, the deal, any of it. I don't know that. But I know I can play. And I don't want somebody else deciding that for me.

Marcus Bell

Last one. You've got Joe Thornton at 25 with a 91, Sergei Samsonov at 87. What are you to those guys? The teacher? The captain? The guy who had 168?

Glen Murray

I'm a player. I want to be clear about that. I'm not here to pass a torch. Joe's the future, obviously. The kid's 25, he's got a 91, he's going to be here ten years. Good. But when I put the skates on, I'm not thinking about who comes after me. I'm thinking, can I get one open tonight? Can I make a play on the power play? The letter's nice. I like the letter. But I'm a player first.

Marcus Bell

Glen Murray, thank you very much. Appreciate you sitting down. Back to you.
