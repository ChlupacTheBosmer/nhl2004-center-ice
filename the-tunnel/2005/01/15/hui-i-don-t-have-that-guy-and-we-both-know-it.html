Hui: I don't have that guy, and we both know it

The Ottawa general manager on a seven-game skid, a $26.27M profit he'd trade in a heartbeat, a practice rink below par, and the top-end young talent his club does not have.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelSaturday, 15 January 2005

4:02 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Michael Hui runs Ottawa Senators, a club with 40 points through 43 games, a season average of 15091 at the gate, and a per-game profit of $26.27M. The business works. The ice product is the worst in the Northeast division, and Hui knows the gap between those two sentences is what every Ottawa fan feels on a $80 seat. Marcus Bell sat down with him this morning.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here in the studio with Michael Hui, the general manager of Ottawa Senators®. Michael, seven in a row. Fifteen wins, twenty-one losses, forty points. Walk me through the last two weeks.

Michael Hui

It's been heavy. You look at the standings every morning, same thing, another loss, and you think, all right, here we go again. It doesn't hit different on the seventh night than the first. It just gets heavier. You're in meetings every day, you're on the phone, and nothing moves. That's the part that wears on you.

Marcus Bell

And yet. $80 a seat. Fifteen thousand people on average. They keep coming back even when you lose seven straight. How does that work?

Michael Hui

You want me to explain the fans to you? They've been here a long time. They know what they're watching and they come anyway. The revenue's there because they believe the next season is different. I hope they're right. I don't have a great answer for why a room full of people keep buying in when the product hasn't been good enough. They just do.

Marcus Bell

Your revenue is $56.27M a game. Your profit is $26.27M. You make more money in a year than most clubs that are winning games. Does that sit right?

Michael Hui

It sits like a rock, if I'm honest. I'm not proud of it the way you think I'd be proud. I look at our payroll, $42.80M, and I see a club spending that money and getting $1.07M per point. I'd take a worse profit number and 55 points any day of the week. The profit number doesn't help me at four in the morning when I'm trying to figure out why we can't win a hockey game.

Marcus Bell

So let's talk about what that payroll is buying. I look at the building and the practice surface is below where it needs to be, the equipment room is the same story. What are you giving your young guys in here?

Michael Hui

Not enough. I'll give you that flat out. Our practice rink is not where it should be. Our equipment room, same thing. You put a young player out there for a first full stretch and he's not getting the same tools a kid gets in a building that's spent the money on those rooms. You feel that in the second year, maybe the first. It's not the whole problem. But it's part of it, and I can't pretend it isn't.

Marcus Bell

Which brings me to this. Who's the young guy? Who do you look at in your room and say, this one's the future? The one you build around?

Michael Hui

I don't have him. And that's the honest answer. Everybody wants me to point at a kid and say, there's our man, that's the core. We've got young guys, they're in the room, they're working. But I don't have a player I can look at and say, there's the top-end talent we build the next five years around. That's why we're fifteen and twenty-one. It's not the system, it's not the coach, it's the pieces. We don't have them yet.

Marcus Bell

Patrick Lalime is in his last year. Thirty years old, $1.90M. If he walks, what happens to the present and the future at the same time?

Michael Hui

That's the problem with where we are. Patrick's done a good job, he's been our guy, and if we're not in a position to re-sign him at a number that makes sense, then I'd rather trade him in March and get something back for the pipeline than lose him for nothing in July. I've had that conversation with myself. I haven't had it with him yet. But yeah, he's 30 and he's on an expiring deal and we're not close to him in the standings. I've got to think about what helps two years from now.

Marcus Bell

Sixty-three days to what the calendar has as the trade deadline. What does that conversation look like for you?

Michael Hui

I'm looking. Every phone call is a phone call. But here's what nobody wants to hear from a GM sitting at 40 points: I don't think a trade at the deadline fixes this. You can go get a rental, a guy for a stretch run you're not going to make. Or you can go get a draft pick, a prospect, something that helps three years out. I'm in the second conversation. I'd rather lose the next two months and build something than go all-in on a team that's not close. That's not fun to hear. I know.

Marcus Bell

One last question. You said the fans believe something's coming. Do you believe something's coming?

Michael Hui

I believe we've got work to do. I believe the practice rink needs to get better and the equipment room needs to get better. I believe those things cost time and they cost a process I can't shortcut. I believe the young guys in our room deserve better tools than what we're giving them today. That's what I believe. A Cup? No. That's not what I believe. Not yet. I believe we can be competitive in three, four years if I do my job right. That's the honest answer.

Marcus Bell

Michael Hui, general manager of Ottawa Senators®. Michael, thank you for your time. Back to you.
