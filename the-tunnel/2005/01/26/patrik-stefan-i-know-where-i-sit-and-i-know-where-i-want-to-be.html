Patrik Stefan: I know where I sit, and I know where I want to be

The 1999 first overall pick talks contract year, his role behind Kovalchuk and Heatley, and what a first overall pick owes a team that is still rebuilding at 24.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelWednesday, 26 January 2005

4:10 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Patrik Stefan81 sat down with Marcus Bell in the studio after the game, one season into the last year of his $1.10M contract with Atlanta Thrashers. He was the first overall pick of 1999. At 24, he is the third center on a club built around Ilya Kovalchuk92 and Dany Heatley90. His grade sits at 75, six below his base of 81.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here with Patrik Stefan, thanks for coming in, Patrik. Let me start where everybody's starting. You've got zero years left on that contract. $1.10M for one more season. Do you play different?

Patrik Stefan

No, no. I play the same game. You know, everybody ask me this, like it's, how you say, the pressure is new. It is not new. I was first pick, everybody was watching from the first day. So a contract year, it is, it is just the same thing, you know? I play my game.

Marcus Bell

But here's what's different from 1999. Back then you were the center. First overall, the franchise man. Now you're the third name on the scoresheet, behind Ilya and Dany. How do you reconcile that?

Patrik Stefan

I don't reconcile. I... I play center. I play my minutes. Ilya is 21 and he has, you know, he has something I don't have. Dany same. They are, how you say, they are more the... the scorer. I am the guy who gets the puck in, who makes the second play. I don't need to be the name on top. I know where I am in the room.

Marcus Bell

You say you know where you sit. Do you think the Bureau knows where you sit? They have you at 75. Your base grade is 81. That's six points below where they think you should be.

Patrik Stefan

Yeah. I see it. I see the number. But the Index is plus two right now. I am going the right direction. I am not... I am not going down. I just need more time to come back to where I should be.

Marcus Bell

You've got 37 points in 48 games. Last year 47 in 68. Your pace is actually up. So what's wrong with the season?

Patrik Stefan

The wrong thing is... the team is not where it should be. Last year we were last. This year, Ilya has 87 points, Dany has 80, and we are still... we are still not winning enough games. That is the wrong thing. My points are fine. The team is not.

Marcus Bell

Let me ask about the room. Ilya is 21. Dany is 24. You're 24 but you've been here longer than both of them. Do you talk to them? Do you lead?

Patrik Stefan

I try, you know? I try. But, um... they don't need me to tell them how to play. Ilya, he just, he knows. You show him once, he does it three times better. Dany same. So my job is not to talk. My job is to be in the room, to be there when the young guys come in, to show them how I practice. How I eat. How I prepare. That is how you lead. Not with speeches.

Marcus Bell

But here's the thing that bugs people about you, Patrik. Your fighting, your toughness, your checking — the Bureau grades those at 50, 50, 51. You're a center. You're supposed to be the guy who sets the tone physically.

Patrik Stefan

Yeah, yeah. I know. They look at me, first overall, center, and they think, he should be, how you say, a monster. He should hit everybody. But I am not that player. I was never that player. My speed is 89. My shot is 85. That is me. I will not be a fighter. I will be fast and I will be smart and I will make the play. That is what I can give.

Marcus Bell

Do you think that's why you're the third option? Because you won't be that guy?

Patrik Stefan

No. I think I am the third option because Ilya is 21 and he scores 87 points and Dany is 24 and he scores 80. That is why. Not because of checking. You know? If I hit twenty guys a game and score 20 points, I am still third. So what does it matter?

Marcus Bell

Fair enough. What about the captaincy? You're not the captain. You're not an assistant, are you?

Patrik Stefan

I am not. And I don't, I don't want it right now. You know why? Because I need to fix my own game first. I need to get back to 81, to 84, to where I should be. I can't be the voice in the room when I am not the best version of myself. Maybe in two years. Not now.

Marcus Bell

So the summer conversation. You've got zero years left. You're a restricted free agent, the team has the rights. What do you want?

Patrik Stefan

I want to play here. I like Atlanta. The building, the city, it is good for me. Ilya, Dany, we are good friends, we play good together. I want to stay. But, you know, I also want a team that will win. If I stay and we are last again... then what is the point? I need to know, in July, that this team is going to be better. That is what I need.

Marcus Bell

What does better look like to you? What would you say to the front office?

Patrik Stefan

I would say, get us a defenseman. Get us a body on the back end. We are fast up front, you know? Ilya, Dany, me, Kapa. We can score. But when the other team comes, we are... we are soft back there. I don't care about my number. I care about the wins. Give me a team that wins and I will score 30 points and I will be happy. I will take it.

Marcus Bell

One last one. The Development Index has you plus two. Morale at 92. You seem fine. Are you?

Patrik Stefan

I am fine. I am 24, you know? I have, how you say, I have the time. My legs are good. My speed is there. I believe I am not done. First overall, I hear it every day. First overall, first overall, first overall. And yeah, I was. But I am more than the number where they picked me. I am still here. That is something.

Marcus Bell

Patrik Stefan, thank you very much. Back to you.
