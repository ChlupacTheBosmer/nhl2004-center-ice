Bowness: 'We weren't lost. We just couldn't score.'

The Coyotes coach defended his line combinations, called the penalty kill fine and the power play not, and shut down a question about Daymond Langkow after a 2-0 blank at Edmonton.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelWednesday, 26 January 2005

4:11 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Rick Bowness' Phoenix Coyotes lost 2-0 on the road at Edmonton Oilers on 2005-01-24, ending a five-game winning streak. His men put 18 shots on net to Edmonton's 17 and scored none, dropping to 20-24-4, 46 points. Bowness met Marcus Bell in the tunnel afterward.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here in the tunnel in Edmonton. Rick Bowness, 18 shots on net tonight, more than the home club, and you leave with zero. What did you get from your group?

Rick Bowness

You know, the work was there. I thought we were connected through the neutral zone, we were getting pucks deep, we were getting bodies near the crease. The structure held for about fifty-eight minutes and then, you know, you pull the goalie and it's, it's an ugly thing on the scoresheet. But the whole night, I don't think we were out of structure. We just didn't put one in the net.

Marcus Bell

You'd won five straight. How does a 2-0 road loss land after winning five?

Rick Bowness

Bad. Obviously it lands bad. You build something over a week and then the next night you're staring at a zero. I've been in this long enough to tell you the difference though. I can take the one-nil, two-nil loss where your guys are in position and the other team makes a play. I can't take the night where you look lost, where the gaps are a mile wide and everyone's playing their own game. Tonight we weren't lost. We just couldn't finish.

Marcus Bell

Eighteen shots. Was it the first one at the net or the second one? What's missing there?

Rick Bowness

The second one, I think. You get a puck on net, they block it, it comes back to the point and now you need a second body in the slot, a third body at the side of the net. And we had, I don't know, one or two times tonight where we had a man open at the side and the puck didn't find him. That's, you know, that's practice. That's repetition. And I use the word 'detail' and everybody's always using that word, but in this case, we are not getting there on the second shot. And we're not getting there on the third.

Marcus Bell

Can I ask about your lines? Ladislav Nagy sits at 86 in the Book and he's centering a third line. Mike Sillinger sits at 88 and he's playing right wing on the third line. You must know what people are saying.

Rick Bowness

I'm aware of a lot of things people say. Listen. The Book is the Book. It's one tool. I dress four lines that do four different jobs and when I put Lada on the third line, he's giving me transition, he's giving me hands in the neutral zone, he's winning pucks clean and making the first pass so we can get out of our own end without a scramble. And Sills on the wing, I get a faceoff capability on that side and I can flip the wings mid-game without calling a timeout. That's not a demotion. That's a role. I need different things on different lines.

Marcus Bell

But Nikolai Antropov, rated 81, he's first line and he's on the first power play unit. The two guys rated higher than him aren't. How do you make that call?

Rick Bowness

Because the Ants gives me something Lada doesn't on that line. He's going to battle in the corner, he's going to win the puck back, he's going to get us a change when we need one. And when we get a fresh zone start and the matchup opens up, Lada can go to work. It's not a, it's not a ranking exercise. I'm not seating guys by a number. I'm seating them by what the shift requires.

Marcus Bell

The special teams. What did you get from them tonight?

Rick Bowness

The kill was fine. I thought we did what we talked about. Savage in there, Reichel in there, they were in position, they blocked what they needed to block, they got us out of a couple of tight ones clean. The power play, that's where I want more. We set up, they collapsed the middle and we just shot around them. We didn't make the extra pass. We didn't get the puck to the seam. That's the one I'm not happy about.

Marcus Bell

Drake Berehowsky on your first penalty kill unit. He's rated 78 and Cale Hulse is sitting at 72. Is that a matchup thing?

Rick Bowness

Well, you look at the number and sure, I get it. But killing isn't hands. It's being willing to stand there when a 200-pound forward is bearing down and eat a puck off the shin and get back on the ice. Drake has been our guy on the penalty kill since I got here and he's been our guy because he does that. Cale's a good player. I need him fresh when he goes in. That's not going to change.

Marcus Bell

Two guys you don't have right now. David Tanabe with a concussion, back February 12th. Daymond Langkow, a neck, back February 24th. How much does that thin it out?

Rick Bowness

It's not great. Obviously it's not great. Tanabe was logging third-pair minutes and some second, and when you lose a guy like that, the bottom of the depth chart starts to wobble. Langkow was the fourth center and with the schedule we've got, VAN in two days and then CGY at home, we've got a little breathing room. So it's, it's manageable. Barely. But manageable.

Marcus Bell

Langkow has eight points in 39 games. When he walks back in, is that a problem?

Rick Bowness

Daymond is a hockey player who's got an injury and he's in a medical room trying to get his neck right. And I'm not going to stand in a hallway and dissect his point total while he's doing that. We'll see him in a uniform and then we'll have a conversation. That's the end of it.

Marcus Bell

Twenty and twenty-four and four, 46 points, fourth in the Pacific. What does this group look like to you right now?

Rick Bowness

A team in a fight. That's what I see. You're in the middle of the standings and every night is a battle for two points and nobody's giving you anything for free. You saw that tonight. 18 shots, 0 goals. That's a fight. We're not out of it and we're not in it either, and that's the hardest place to be because you can't see the end of the tunnel yet. You just keep going. Back to you.
