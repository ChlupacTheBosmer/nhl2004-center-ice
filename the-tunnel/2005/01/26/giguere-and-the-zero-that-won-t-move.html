Giguere and the zero that won't move

Anaheim's goaltender has played 44 games without a shutout and says the number is not about him.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelWednesday, 26 January 2005

2:55 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Jean-Sebastien Giguere Jean-Sebastien Giguere93 came off a 2-1 overtime loss to New Jersey Devils with 18 shots against, his Mighty Ducks of Anaheim losing streak at one, and a shutout total that reads zero through 44 games. The 27-year-old has been here before on this desk about that number. Tonight he was less patient with it.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here in the corridor at Anaheim, with Jean-Sebastien Giguere. Two-one in overtime to New Jersey, eighteen shots from your side, twenty against. How do you feel about that one?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

We had it, you know? One-one, we're in it, and then the second one and it's over. I made the save on the first one and the second one, the screen, I don't see it. But we win that game in regulation and it's two points. It's the eighteen that's the problem.

Marcus Bell

You named eighteen in Philadelphia last month as the same problem. Eighteen shots generated, you lose. Tonight, eighteen again. Is that a system issue or is it just, the guys aren't getting there?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

I don't know. It's not, I can't answer that. I'm in the crease, I see the shots, I see the saves I make and the ones I don't. The eighteen is, you know, Sergei gets the puck and there's nowhere to go with it. The guys, the first three, four forwards, they're back checking and they're not coming out. That's what I see. That's not, I'm not blaming, but that's what the eighteen means.

Marcus Bell

Let me ask about the number. Forty-four games this season, zero shutouts. Last year you had two in sixty-six. In '02-'03 you had eight. Where does that sit with you tonight?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

Yeah. I know where it sits. It sits in the papers, right? It's a nice number for the writers. But you know what, the shutout is the last guy gets credit for, I don't, nobody wants it more than me, trust me. But it's five guys in front of me plus me. It's not, you can't put that on one man. When we're killing five for two periods, when the D is blocking and the corners are clean, then maybe. Tonight we're not that team.

Marcus Bell

You've owned that number before on this desk. You said in December it was a consistency issue you own. Has the feeling changed now that it's forty-four?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

It's the same feeling, it's just, you know, when you say it the first time it's honest, when you say it the fourth time it sounds like an excuse. I own it. I've always owned it. But I'm also telling you, the shutout is a team thing. Look at New Jersey tonight. Twenty shots, they take two. Their D is in the shooting lanes all night. That's a shutout team in front of their goalie. We're not that yet.

Marcus Bell

Havelid, Salei, Ward, Berg. What are you seeing from that group? Are they finding each other?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

Nic is good, yeah. Nic is, he's steady, he's square, he's not the guy who makes the mistake. Rus is physical, he wins the battles. The problem is, we don't have the same four every night. The combinations change. And when the combinations change, the communication takes a shift or two, and on the road that's a goal. Tonight at home, one shift where we're not talking, and it's two-one. That's the game right there.

Marcus Bell

Fedorov had a point tonight. One goal, one assist. You've talked about him carrying it before.

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

He had the point, yeah, and that's good. But one point on eighteen shots, that means the rest of us, the other eleven guys, they're not, they're not finishing the plays. And I'm not going to sit here and say Sergei is the answer every night. He's thirty, he's playing twenty-four, twenty-five minutes. He's great. But the eighteen, the eighteen is all of us.

Marcus Bell

You're 22 and 21 with zero shutouts. The team's one game back of Edmonton in the Northwest. What's the path back for you?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

We play better hockey. We generate, I don't know, twenty-five, twenty-eight a night instead of eighteen, and the wins come and the shutouts come. It's that simple. I'm not worried about the zero. I'm worried about the eighteen.

Marcus Bell

Jean-Sebastien Giguere, thank you as always. Back to you.
