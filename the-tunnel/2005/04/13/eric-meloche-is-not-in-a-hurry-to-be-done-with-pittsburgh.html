Eric Meloche is not in a hurry to be done with Pittsburgh

The Calder winner from last season has one year left at $0.40M and 3 goals in 2 playoff games, and he says the money bothers him less than the 186th pick still does.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelWednesday, 13 April 2005

3:48 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Eric Meloche75 scored 88 points last season for Pittsburgh Penguins and won the Calder Trophy, the cap on a road that began with the 186th pick in 1996. He has 63 points in 64 games this year, one year left on a $0.40M deal, and 3 goals in 2 games this postseason. Jan Kowalczyk92 sat down with him in the studio.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here with Eric Meloche of the Pittsburgh Penguins. Eric, thanks for sitting down. The Sweater ran a profile this week that opened with the 186th pick. Do you still hear that? People still count it?

Eric Meloche

For sure, for sure. Every time I sign something, somebody at the table, they go, 'the 186th, right?' And I go, 'yeah.' You get used to it. But you don't forget where you started, no.

Jan Kowalczyk

Seventh round, and it takes until last season before the Calder is sitting on your mantle. Was there a stretch where you thought this was just the job, nothing more?

Eric Meloche

Yeah. Maybe a while back, I don't know. You're in the minors, you're, I mean, the game is fast up here and when you're on a one-way and you're not playing, you start to ask yourself, 'am I this? Is this really me?' And then you get a call up and you play some games and you think, 'well maybe.' And then you come back down. So for sure, there were stretches where you think, 'the 186th is the 186th.' That's it.

Jan Kowalczyk

Last year: 82 games, 23 goals, 65 assists, 88 points, 4 penalty minutes. This year: 64 games, 63 points, 22 penalty minutes. You're on 22.0 minutes now, up from 21.3. Same role?

Eric Meloche

It's different, yeah. Last year the game came to me, the room was, I had the room and the points came. This year the foot, I miss the start of December, and then the lines are different. And the PIM, 22, yeah. That's not, the 4 last year was me. This year I'm just in the way more. I'm trying to use my body because the game is not, not as smooth. So I'm hitting, I'm trying to make my mark a different way.

Jan Kowalczyk

The Bureau's Book has you at 78 overall, base 79, the Development Index has you up one. The Morale Scale has you at 99. How do you read a 99 when the points aren't what they were?

Eric Meloche

Yeah, 99, I saw that. You know what, the room is good. Mario is still here, Martin is still here, the guys are fighting. And I've got 3 goals in 2 games in the playoffs. So, the morale is fair. Yeah.

Jan Kowalczyk

Three goals in two playoff games. That's your most goals in any postseason run. Does that surprise you?

Eric Meloche

Yeah, it surprises me. The first one, the puck just came off my stick and I go, that's in. And the second game, two more. You take it. You don't ask questions.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let's talk about the contract. One year left at $0.40M. You scored 88 points last season and won the Calder. Does that number bother you?

Eric Meloche

It bothers me. Yeah, a little. Because I worked a long time for that 88 and the Calder and the, you know, the recognition. But you go home, you look at the family, you're fine. And the next day you come here and you think, 'maybe I should make more.' And then you get on the ice and you forget. I let my agent talk to them. That's his job. I play.

Jan Kowalczyk

Five years ago you were fighting for a roster spot, maybe still in the minors. What does this job feel like compared to what you thought it would be?

Eric Meloche

Five years ago I was just surviving, you know? Trying not to get sent down. And now I'm a guy they want on the ice, I get the power play, I get the tough shifts sometimes. And I want to be honest, five years ago I didn't think I'd play 82 games and have 88 points. I didn't. So the role is bigger. And that's what I want, the responsibility. It feels right.

Jan Kowalczyk

You play alongside Mario Lemieux at 39, Martin Straka, Aleksey Morozov. What do you take from those guys?

Eric Meloche

Mario, the first time I got called up I was scared to pass him the puck. You don't feel that anymore, but at 39 he still finds things I don't see. And Martin, the way he protects the puck on the wall, I watch him every day. He plays like he's got something to prove every single shift. And Aleksey, the soft hands, you know, you give him the puck in tight and he makes something. That's what you want next to you.

Jan Kowalczyk

The foot injury. You were back December 14th. First real injury. Did it change how you play?

Eric Meloche

It made me think about my body. Before, I just skated and I didn't think. The speed was the thing, the speed. And now I'm careful, maybe too careful some days. And then I forget, I get the puck and I go, and I remember after the shift. It's healed. But you miss a couple games, the rhythm is gone, and it takes a while to find it again. That's part of the 64.

Jan Kowalczyk

Last one. One year left. Is this the year where you make them call you and the number changes?

Eric Meloche

Yeah. For sure. I want to prove it every year, I don't need the contract to do that. But if I can get deep in the playoffs and score goals and make it so the conversation is not about the 186th anymore, that's the goal. And if Pittsburgh wants me back, I'm here. I like it here. Mario is here, the guys are good. That's enough.

Jan Kowalczyk

Eric Meloche, thank you for sitting down. Enjoy the rest of this run.
