Martin Havlat: 103 points and the year he stopped trying to be the tough guy

The 23-year-old on a $0.90M deal explains why 103 points came the season after two suspensions taught him to skate instead of fight.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelTuesday, 12 April 2005

3:41 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Martin Havlat92 put up 103 points in 82 games for Ottawa Senators at $0.90M, then watched the first round end in four games with no wins. The Tunnel sat him in the studio to talk about days off, the coach who told him to use his feet, and what that April left behind.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here, studio desk. With me is Martin Havlat of Ottawa Senators®. Eighty-two games, forty goals, 103 points, and a four-game sweep that ended it all in the first round. Martin, thanks for sitting down.

Martin Havlat

Thank you. Good to be here, you know.

Marcus Bell

Let's start with the number everyone's talking about. 103 points on a $0.90M contract. The Sweater's practically wrote the headline for you. Does that feel like a season you're proud of?

Martin Havlat

Proud, yes. Forty goals is good. But sixty-three assists, that is the other guys giving me the puck. Alfredsson, Hossa, they make me look better than I am. You know? It is not one man does all that.

Marcus Bell

Step away from the scoresheet. You've been in Ottawa since 2001, four full seasons. Walk me through a day off.

Martin Havlat

Day off. So, I sleep. Very late, maybe until eleven. Then I get up, I make some food. Something Czech, if I can find it. Maybe the TV, Czech channels, some football. And I drive. Just, you know, drive around Ottawa. It is quiet here when you are not at the rink. I like the quiet.

Marcus Bell

You've got Hossa and Chara here with you. A lot of Czech and Slovak in one city. Do the three of you end up together on the off days?

Martin Havlat

Sometimes, yes. Chara cooks. Very good food, and he is tall, so the kitchen, he fits everything. So we go to his place, and I wash the dishes. That is my job. I am the dishwasher. And Hossa comes too, and we talk in Czech, and nobody else understands, so we joke about the other guys. You know.

Marcus Bell

Your third year, 2002-03. Twenty-four goals, 59 points, and Ottawa Senators® goes to the Conference Finals. That was a jump. What changed in that room?

Martin Havlat

I was still young. First year, second year, I just ran. I ran and I shot, but I didn't think about it. And the coach, he said, use your feet. You are not the biggest, you are not the strongest, but you can go places they cannot go. And, you know, that is when I understood. I was trying to be somebody else, and he said, be yourself. Use the speed. And the game opened up.

Marcus Bell

Last season though. Two suspensions, four games. One for kicking, one for high-sticking. And Ken Hitchcock made that comment about somebody making you eat your lunch. You come back this year, 15 PIM in 82 games, 103 points. What happened between those two seasons?

Martin Havlat

The suspensions. Yes. I will say, the kicking was stupid. That was not me. The high-sticking, maybe it was a little high, but the comment from the other bench, that made me angry. But the coach, the next day, he told me, you want to fight everybody, you sit. You play your game or you don't play. And I have feet, yes? I can go around. I don't need to push. So I stopped trying to prove I was tough. And everything got slower. The ice got wider. You know?

Marcus Bell

The spring. Four games, no wins. Two goals, two assists for you, 24.3 minutes a night. What does that feel like coming off the ice after game four?

Martin Havlat

You score, you have the assist, and it doesn't matter. The series is gone. It is April and the others are still playing and you go home. Something in the chest. You feel the room, everybody is quiet, nobody talks on the bus. And you know you can play. But four games, no wins, that is what is on the board.

Marcus Bell

You're 23 years old. The Bureau's Morale Scale has you at 92. One year left on $0.90M. You just put up 103 points. I'd be thinking about that number every single day. Do you?

Martin Havlat

Everyone asks about the contract. I play hockey. Tomorrow, practice, I try to score, I try to help Alfredsson and the boys. That is my job. The contract, my agent, he thinks about that. I sleep very good, you know. Very good.

Marcus Bell

One more on the day off. You grew up in Mlada Boleslav. Four years here now. What's the one thing about Ottawa you don't find back home?

Martin Havlat

The quiet. You know, where I grew up, everybody knows everybody. Here, I can go, buy milk, nobody looks at me. Just take your milk, pay, go. And the lake. In summer, the lake here, it is very good. I miss the Czech food, maybe, but the lake, no. I keep the lake.

Marcus Bell

Martin Havlat of Ottawa Senators®, back in a few weeks with a whole summer ahead of him. Thank you, Martin. Back to you.
