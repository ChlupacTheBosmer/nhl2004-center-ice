Steve McCarthy: The Coach Who Told Him to Stop Chasing the Game

McCarthy played 82 games and logged 29.9 minutes a night for Chicago this season; he talked with Jan Kowalczyk about how he actually rests, and about the man who taught him to be where the puck finds him.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelSaturday, 23 April 2005

3:11 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Steve McCarthy77 played 82 games for Chicago Blackhawks this season, 7 goals and 37 assists, and logged 29.9 minutes a night doing it. A year ago the number was 15.2, over 51 games. The Bureau's Book has him at 77 overall, four up on his Development Index, and he came into the studio to talk about a day off and the coach who changed him.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk, in the studio with Steve McCarthy of Chicago Blackhawks®. Steve, thanks for sitting down. The year's over. How's the body?

Steve McCarthy

Uh, tired. No, it's, you know, it's a strange week. 82 games, you're going every night, and then it just, it stops. You wake up and there's no skate, no meeting. So I'm still kind of waiting for the alarm, honestly.

Jan Kowalczyk

That's where I wanted to start. What does a real day off look like for you? Not the one on the schedule. The one where nobody wants anything.

Steve McCarthy

Honestly? I sleep. That's it. I sleep till noon and I'm not going to apologize for it. Then I eat, and then, uh, I don't know, I put the phone somewhere and I don't look at it. The guys think that's funny, but, you know, your head needs the day too. Not just the legs.

Jan Kowalczyk

You averaged 29.9 minutes a night this season. Can the legs actually come back from that in a single day?

Steve McCarthy

I mean, no, not really. The legs come back. It's everything else. You get a day and you're, you know, you're not sore by the end of it, but you're not fresh either. People say rest and it sounds simple. It's not simple. It's a routine. You do the same things every day so that the day off works.

Jan Kowalczyk

A year ago it was 15.2 minutes, 51 games, 14 points. This year, 82 games and 44 points. The Bureau has you at 77 overall, four up on the Index. Did you feel that as it happened?

Steve McCarthy

No. You never feel it in the moment, you know? Somebody tells you the number after. I just knew I was playing more. 15.2 to 29.9, that's a different job. You'd be done by December.

Jan Kowalczyk

So what changed in there? Was there a coach in it somewhere?

Steve McCarthy

Yeah. There was. There's a guy, he had me when I was a kid, and he, uh. He used to tell me I was chasing the game. That's the phrase, chasing it. I'd see the puck and I'd go, and next thing I'm three strides out of position and I think I'm helping. He'd stop practice and make me stand there and watch where I was supposed to be. I hated it. It's the best thing anybody ever did for me.

Jan Kowalczyk

What did he say that stuck? Not the drill. The words.

Steve McCarthy

He said the puck's going to come to you if you're where you're supposed to be. That's it. That's the whole thing. I still, you know, I still hear it. Every shift. It sounds like nothing when I say it out loud. It isn't nothing.

Jan Kowalczyk

You grew up in Trail, British Columbia. Does a day off look different when you're home?

Steve McCarthy

Yeah, a little. When I'm out there, in the summer, I'm outside, you know? That's the BC in me. Here you get a day and it's, uh, you're in a hotel or you're in your place and it's the same four walls. Home, it's, I don't know. It's quieter. You can breathe out.

Jan Kowalczyk

Who do you lean on in that room? Jocelyn Thibault's been around forever. Eric Daze, Theoren Fleury.

Steve McCarthy

All of them, honestly. Theo, you watch how hard that guy works and you, you can't take a day. Daze, he's quiet, but he's the one who'll tell you the truth. And Thibault back there, he sees everything. He'll yell at you before the coach does. That's a good thing. Most of the time.

Jan Kowalczyk

The Bureau's Morale Scale has you at 96. That's about as high as it goes. Does that match how the year felt?

Steve McCarthy

I didn't know that. That's, uh. Yeah, I'd say it's close. I like coming to the rink. That's, you know, that's not small. Some years you don't, and everybody can tell. This year I like the room.

Jan Kowalczyk

Two years left at $0.90M. For a guy playing 29.9 minutes, that's a bargain. Does that enter your head at all?

Steve McCarthy

Not while I'm playing. It's, you know, it's not my job to count it. My agent does that. I just want to play where I'm playing. That's all I think about.

Jan Kowalczyk

So what's the summer? What do you do with a day off that lasts four months?

Steve McCarthy

Go home. Sleep a lot. Skate, but not for anybody. Just me and the ice and no clock. And then, you know, August comes and you start wanting the games again. Every year. You forget how much you missed it.

Jan Kowalczyk

Before I let you go. Anything about this year you'd want said that I haven't asked?

Steve McCarthy

Uh. Not really. Just, you know, I'm 24. I've got a lot of hockey left and I know that now. So that's, that's the whole thing. I'm going to keep playing.

Jan Kowalczyk

Steve, thanks for your time. Jan Kowalczyk, in the studio.
