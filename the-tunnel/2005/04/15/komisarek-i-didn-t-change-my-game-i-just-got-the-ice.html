Komisarek: 'I Didn't Change My Game, I Just Got the Ice'

The 23-year-old defenseman went from 24 games and 11.8 minutes a night to third among defensemen with 82 points, and he is done being called a shutdown guy.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelFriday, 15 April 2005

3:49 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Mike Komisarek82 played 24 games for Montreal Canadiens last season, averaging 11.8 minutes a night with 3 points. This season he played all 82, logged 30.4 minutes a night, and finished third among defensemen with 82 points. The Bureau's Book grades him 82, a base of 77 plus 6 on the Development Index.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here in the studio with Mike Komisarek, the Montreal Canadiens® defenseman. Mike, 24 games last year, 11.8 minutes a night, 3 points. This year: 82 games, 30.4 minutes, 82 points, third among defensemen in the league. What happened?

Mike Komisarek

Yeah, I mean, honestly, the first thing is I just got healthy. I think the whole thing started there. And the coaching staff, they just trusted me to be out there more. And when you get 30.4 minutes, you know, you're actually in the game. You're not coming on for a shift and sitting two periods. You're building a feel. You're reading things, you know, you're not just hoping for a bounce.

Jan Kowalczyk

Five years ago you were 18. You'd come through the NTDP and you'd just gotten to Michigan. Where was your head?

Mike Komisarek

You know, at that point I was just trying to make the team. I came from Long Island, through Suffolk PAL and St. Anthony's, and you don't sit around imagining yourself as, you know, a guy putting up 80 points. You imagine playing for a living somewhere. And you get to the NTDP and for the first time you're around guys who are, like, really talented. And you go to Michigan and you just, you're trying to earn a spot. 46 points in 80 games, that's what I put up there, and I think people back then, they were happy with that. Nobody was talking about what I'd do here.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let me throw a number at you. The Bureau's Book has you at 92 in checking, 89 in speed, 88 in aggression. That reads like a shutdown defenseman. 67 assists is not what I expect from that.

Mike Komisarek

Yeah, everybody wants to stick me in the, you know, the big hit, no points box. And I get it. I like to hit. I like to be physical. But I've always wanted to move the puck. At Michigan we had a system where the defensemen were supposed to carry it and create, and I think that just, that just carried over. The Book's got me at 88 in puck handling, 85 in passing. Those aren't shutdown numbers. I didn't change my game at all this year. I just got the ice to show it.

Jan Kowalczyk

You went from 11.8 minutes to 30.4. You're 75 inches, 225 pounds. How do you physically handle that?

Mike Komisarek

It's different. When you're at 11.8 you're fresh every shift, you just go out and, you know, empty the tank and sit. At 30.4 you've got to manage yourself. You've got to be smart about when you push and when you hold. And off the ice it's, you know, it's the boring stuff. Eating, sleeping. The 225 helps a little, I won't lie. You've got some weight to lean on in the corners. But the 30.4, that, that changes how your legs feel in the third period. You notice it.

Jan Kowalczyk

Andrei Markov is your partner. The Book has him at 81.

Mike Komisarek

Yeah, and he makes it easy, you know. He reads the zone, he gets the puck out, he doesn't panic. And that lets me jump into the rush because I know, I know he's covered behind me. And you play with a guy like that, you just, you relax. You know what I mean? You don't have to worry about the back end. He, he handles it.

Jan Kowalczyk

The club won 21 games. Does 82 points feel hollow when the wins aren't there?

Mike Komisarek

Yeah. I mean, I'm happy with the year, obviously. But we're not where we want to be and, and 21 wins is not good enough. As a defenseman, I think you're supposed to be the guy who makes the whole thing better. And 21, that says maybe I didn't do that. Maybe I need to do more. But it's not just one guy, you know. It's the whole thing.

Jan Kowalczyk

Who out there makes you work the hardest? Who do you respect?

Mike Komisarek

I think, for me, it's Robert Svehla in Toronto Maple Leafs®.

Jan Kowalczyk

The Norris winner.

Mike Komisarek

Yeah, obviously. And I don't think people talk about him enough. You know, Toronto, they've got a lot of guys you can point at, and that's fair. But what he does, he does it every single night. He's not trying to be the flashiest guy on the ice. He's just always in the right place, and when you're matched up against him you're in for a long night. He makes you play his game. I watch tape, you know, and I just, I pay attention to where he stands, when he steps up. It's, it's like chess, kind of.

Jan Kowalczyk

You've got a year left on your deal. $1.10M. What do you say to the fans who want you in that sweater for the next five years?

Mike Komisarek

That's above my pay grade, obviously. I play the game. My agent handles that. I love it here. I've been here a while and I want to keep playing here. But the business part, that's for other guys to worry about. You know? You just, you just go play hockey and let it happen.

Jan Kowalczyk

Last one. After a game, what do you look at? Not goals, not assists. What's the thing that tells you you had a good night?

Mike Komisarek

Shifts. I want to know I went out there every time they asked me and I did the job. And I don't care if it's a blocked shot or a hit in the corner or carrying it through the zone. I just, I want to be the guy who's out there 30 minutes and still gets that next one done. That's, that's what I look at.

Jan Kowalczyk

Mike Komisarek, thank you for your time tonight. Back to you.
