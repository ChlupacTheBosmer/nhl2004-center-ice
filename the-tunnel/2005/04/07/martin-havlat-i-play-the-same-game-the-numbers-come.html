Martin Havlat: "I play the same game, the numbers come"

The Ottawa winger talks about skating before he learned to stickhandle in Mlada Boleslav, why his 103-point year isn't a breakout, and trying a move on Jaromir Jagr and finding him already there.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelThursday, 7 April 2005

6:06 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Martin Havlat88 finished the regular season with 40 goals and 63 assists in 82 games for Ottawa Senators, his first 100-point year. The Senators missed the playoffs. The 23-year-old left winger from Mlada Boleslav sits at 88 on the Bureau's Book, his base of 87 plus 3 on the Development Index, with 1 year left on a $0.90M contract. Marcus Bell sat down with him in the studio after the season.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here, studio desk. Martin Havlat, Ottawa Senators, joins us. Martin, 40 goals, 63 assists, 103 points. That's your best season in the league by more than 50 points over last year. What was different?

Martin Havlat

Yeah, well... I play the same game, you know. Maybe the puck, it find me more this year. The linemates, Danny, Marian, they, how you say, they make me better. And I play 82 games. Last year is 48. You feel different when you play 82 games, you know? The body is there. The head is there. That is the difference.

Marcus Bell

I want to take you back before all that. Mlada Boleslav. What did the game look like to you when you were 10 years old?

Martin Havlat

Small town, you know. Small rink. The ice, how you say, the ice is not so good. In the winter we play outside, the frozen, the lake, the neighborhood. We play all day. And the older boys, they are bigger. Always bigger than me. They take the puck, they hit you maybe. So you learn to keep it close. I think that is why I do what I do. I keep the puck close because if I don't, they take it.

Marcus Bell

Is there a coach, a person who put the stick in your hands and said, you've got something?

Martin Havlat

The coaches, yes. In the Czech, the coaches, they are old school. They say, 'skate, skate, skate.' I hear this word before I play one game. But the boys teach you more. You play with the boys, 15, 16, 17 years old, and you are 12, 13, and they give you no time. You learn what is the game, not from the coach. From the boys. The boys, they are the real teacher.

Marcus Bell

You're drafted 26th overall in '99. You stay back in the Czech Republic one more season, then walk into Ottawa's dressing room at 19. What was that first night like?

Martin Havlat

The room is big. I am looking around and everybody, they are men. I am... how you say... I am a boy. And the game here, the ice is small, the puck come fast. In the Czech, we have more time, you know. Here, you have maybe one second. Maybe less. I think the first year, I just, I try not to be a fool.

Marcus Bell

You were in that room for a year and you figured out that one second. How much of your game now is built on that first season?

Martin Havlat

All of it, you know. Before, in the Czech, I think, 'I have time, I will do the move.' Here, no time. You get the puck, you know in one second what you do. Pass, shoot, keep. That is the game. I learn that in the first year when the puck is hot and I don't know what to do.

Marcus Bell

The Bureau has you at 99 in agility, 99 in speed, 98 in acceleration. That's as high as those grades go. Where does the skating come from?

Martin Havlat

I don't know what to tell you. I skated. You know, in the Czech, we learn the skate before the stick. You cannot play if you cannot skate. That is the first thing the coach say, that is the first thing the boys say, that is the first thing, how you say, the town say. The stick, the stick is what you do with the skate. I skated since I can stand, you know. Since maybe before I can talk.

Marcus Bell

Some people call this a breakout season. You had 50 points last year, 59 the year before. I don't think you agree with that.

Martin Havlat

No, I play the same game. Every year, same game. The number, it go up, it go down, maybe the shots go in or they go outside. Last year, 48 games, and you come back in the next season and you are not the same player, you know? Your body, it is not the same. This year the body is there, I play all 82, the number is big. But I am the same. You know, if I think I am a different player, I think I am... I think wrong.

Marcus Bell

A lot of people watch your game and see a pure skill player. Never in the corners, never in the dirty areas, just skating circles around people. Is that fair?

Martin Havlat

No. I go to the net. I am not... how you say, I fight nobody, I do not hit into the wall. But the net, I go there. I am there for the rebound, for the second chance. The corners, maybe I don't love the corners. But I play the game. The game is not only the corners, you know? The game is also the open ice.

Marcus Bell

Daniel Alfredsson, 108 points this year. He's right there on your wing. What is it like playing next to him night in, night out?

Martin Havlat

Danny, he is simple. You look up, he is there. Half a second, he is there. He do nothing fancy. And that is the best thing, you know? The fancy is nice on television but Danny show you: simple is better. Simple is the best hockey. I watch him every shift and I think, 'okay, be there, be simple.' He make everybody around him better, I think.

Marcus Bell

You and Marian Hossa are both from the region. Is there a Czech-Slovak thing going on in that room? Do you understand each other differently than you understand, say, Todd White?

Martin Havlat

We understand, yes. Czech, Slovak, is close, you know? We talk, sometimes. But I think the biggest thing is we understand the hockey. The way we grew up, the way we see the game, it is similar. He shoot from anywhere. I watch him in practice and I think, how you say, this man, he see the goal from the blue line. I see the goal from the blue line too. We just... we understand.

Marcus Bell

You were part of the 2003 run to the Eastern Conference Finals. Your third year, 24 goals, 59 points. What's the memory that sticks from that run?

Martin Havlat

The games, you know. In the playoffs, the games are different. Before, in the regular season, you play, you go home, you forget. In the playoffs, you play and you think about the game for two days before the next one. That is... that is the hockey. That is why you play. I remember the bus rides, the quiet. Everybody think the same thing. The next game. The next game. That is all.

Marcus Bell

Is there a player you line up against who you think, I've got to be at my best tonight? A rival?

Martin Havlat

Jagr. I know everybody say Jagr. But I grow up, I watch him. On the television, before I come here. He make the hard thing look like nothing, you know? Like he is just skating, and then the goal. I play WSH this year, I get a shift against him. He look at you. He look at you before the play. Like he know what you gonna do. I just... how you say... I respect the situation.

Marcus Bell

145 points at 33 years old. Did you ever try to use something of his against him in a game? Try a move and find him already there?

Martin Havlat

Yes. I have the puck, I think I can, how you say, I can make the move. And he is there. Already there. Before I do it, he is there. So I learn: sometimes you just pass. You do not need the move. He know you think you need the move, so he wait for the move. You just pass, you just go. That is what he do, I think. He never need the move.

Marcus Bell

One more thing. You scored 103 points and you missed the playoffs. How do you carry that into the summer?

Martin Havlat

It is hard. You play 82 games, every night you think about April, you think about May. And it is not there. I just... we are young, you know? We are good. Maybe next year. What else can I say? I go home, I skate maybe, I rest. And we try again.

Marcus Bell

Martin Havlat, Ottawa Senators, 103 points this season. Martin, thank you for your time.

Martin Havlat

Thank you, Marcus. Thank you.
