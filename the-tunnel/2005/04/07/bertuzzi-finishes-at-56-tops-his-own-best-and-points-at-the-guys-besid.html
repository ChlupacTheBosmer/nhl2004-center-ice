Bertuzzi finishes at 56, tops his own best, and points at the guys beside him

The Canucks right winger scores his 56th to close the season, surpasses last year's 54, and credits a line that keeps finding each other through a patchwork roster.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelThursday, 7 April 2005

2:30 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Todd Bertuzzi93 closed the regular season with a four-point night, his 56th goal landing as a new career high and putting his season at 128 points. His Vancouver Canucks beat Los Angeles Kings 6-2 in front of a home crowd that got a 6-2 closer to carry into the postseason.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell down here in the tunnel at GM Place with Todd Bertuzzi. Todd, that's your 56th of the year, you just passed your old career high from last season. Four-point night, six-two, season's done. What's the feeling coming off that?

Todd Bertuzzi

Yeah, good, you know, I like, I like going 2-0 to close it out. You know we've been, we've had some games where the last couple weeks the wheels come off a little bit, and tonight we just, we had legs. First period we were on 'em and it kind of just snowballed from there.

Marcus Bell

So 56. Last year you had 54 and that was your best. How does that number sit with you now that you can look at it?

Todd Bertuzzi

Well, I'm, I'm happy it went in, I'll say that much. I didn't want to sit on 55 and just kind of, you know, leave it there. But honestly I think, what does it, it's one goal in a 6-2 game, you know what I mean. The whole line, Mark's got two, Brendan's got two, we've been riding that for a while. The number's nice, the two points for the team is better.

Marcus Bell

You just said the line. Naslund two goals, four points tonight, Morrison two goals, two points. Five of your six goals came from that trio. What keeps it working through all the shuffling around you this year?

Todd Bertuzzi

You know, it's simple, we, we know where each other's at. Mark's, Mark's gonna be open on the back side and Brendan's gonna find him, or Brendan's driving and Mark and I are crashing. We've, we've been doing that since, what, December when Marcus first asked me about us, and it's, it's, the system's simple enough that even if you throw a guy in for three games, he can get the, the reads. So it's not, it's not that complicated.

Marcus Bell

And that's the thing about this room all year, isn't it. You lost Jovanovski and Ohlund on defense back in January, you talked about that with Jan, and tonight Trevor's not in the lineup. Jason King's chipping in up front. How do you keep the top line sharp when the supporting pieces keep changing?

Todd Bertuzzi

Yeah, look, I think that's the one thing I, I give credit to, to our room. Everyone's, everyone's ready when they go in. Jason, tonight he, he gets a point, he's, he's been playing well. The boys on the back end, Sopel and Walker, they're chipping in. You know, we can't, we can't sit there and, and feel sorry for who's not in there. We just, we play the same game, whoever's on the ice, same structure, and it, it works. It's been that way all year, you know. January, February, April, same deal, next man up.

Marcus Bell

You played all 82 games this year. Last year you missed 19. Does that availability change how you think about your own body at 30?

Todd Bertuzzi

It changes everything. You know, last year I'm sitting at 63 games thinking, jeez, I could've been right there for the, the lead in scoring and all that, and this year I get to stay in the lineup every night and, and that's, that's the difference. 128 points, yeah, but without 82 games in there it's not, it's not there. So I'll take it. I'll take the 82 every year.

Marcus Bell

Alright Todd, 56 goals, 128 points, first round starting. Thanks for your time. Back to you up top.
