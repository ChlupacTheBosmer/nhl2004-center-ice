Boris Van Ryswyk on a $13.64M profit and three rooms that never got built

The Panthers' general manager sits down with Marcus Bell to talk about why a club making money has the most neglected player facilities in the league, and what he owes the man behind the bench.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelThursday, 7 April 2005

4:08 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Florida Panthers finished 25-36-16 for 76 points last week and lost their final 11. The club made $13.64M in profit on a $51.07M payroll with tickets at $79, yet its equipment room, workout room and practice rink all sit at level 0, the floor. General manager Boris Van Ryswyk came into the studio to talk about the building, the money, and what comes next.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here in the studio with Boris Van Ryswyk of the Florida Panthers. Boris, seventy-six points, eleven straight losses to close the year. You've been through a stretch like this before in your time here?

Boris Van Ryswyk

Not that long a streak. No. We've had rough patches, but eleven, that's, that's something else.

Marcus Bell

And you're turning a profit. Thirteen million six hundred thousand dollars on the year, sixteen thousand fans through the gate, seventy-nine dollars a ticket. The money is there.

Boris Van Ryswyk

The money is there, yes. I've said that before, the gate is good here, the fans show up. They pay up. And I, what I don't understand from outside, people look at the profit and they think, well he's just hoarding it. He's not putting it into the team. But a payroll of fifty-one million doesn't move on its own. You've got to find the right piece at the right price and I'm not going to throw money at a man and then have him hurt six weeks later.

Marcus Bell

Let me go to the building then. Because this is what people look at when they talk about what the club has paid for. Your equipment room is at zero. Your workout room is at zero. Your practice rink is at zero. The only upgrade you've made that anyone can see is one step on scouting back in December. Every other live track is at the floor.

Boris Van Ryswyk

I know where they sit. I know. You think I don't walk through it every day? The scouting was a decision I made, I needed eyes before the deadline, I needed to know what was out there. That was the priority in December. I get that it looks, it looks bad on paper, three zeros. But the locker room, that's where the boys spend their time and I put resources there.

Marcus Bell

The locker room at four is the highest one on file in the league, but you and I both know that room is a morale thing, it doesn't put a point on the board. The tracks that move the needle for your players, the ones they train in, the ones that make them stronger, faster, those are all zero.

Boris Van Ryswyk

I hear you. And I'm not going to sit here and tell you I've got what the other clubs have. I don't. You look at Toronto, they've built things. They can. They've got ninety-seven dollars on the ticket, seventeen thousand through the gate. We're not in that league yet, the revenue. Seventy-two million eight hundred eighty-eight thousand in the gate, you've got to be careful. But I know what I owe. I know the rooms aren't there.

Marcus Bell

Does the man behind the bench have what he needs? You've got to be hearing this from him.

Boris Van Ryswyk

The coach, he needs the guys on the roster, he needs them healthy, he needs them able. I don't pick the lines, I don't decide who plays Tuesday, that's his. What I can do is give him a room to work in. A practice surface. And right now, I can't. I'm telling you that as plainly as I can.

Marcus Bell

You've got one man on the shelf right now, Nedorost, the leg, back in a couple weeks. That's not what's hurting this club.

Boris Van Ryswyk

No, that's not what's hurting us. What's hurting us is a roster that doesn't have depth enough to survive twenty-some injuries across the league in any season. We've got young guys learning on the fly, we've got veterans trying to hold the line, and we don't have the development structure underneath that the clubs ahead of us have. That's not the coach's fault. That's not the players' fault. That's me. That's the rooms.

Marcus Bell

Talk to me about Aebischer. Contract year, thirty-three games this year, eighty overall, and his morale is sitting at eighty.

Boris Van Ryswyk

Dave's a pro. Thirty-three games is not what he wanted, none of us expected that. His number is still there, he's got eighty on the Bureau's book, and he's a twenty-seven-year-old goaltender with a year left. What I'll say is this: I'm not going to let him walk for nothing if I can help it. But I'm also not going to overpay to keep a backup. You understand what I mean.

Marcus Bell

Sixty-two days to the draft. Free agency after that. You've got the money in the profit column, you've got the scouting upgrade that gives you a better look at the trade market. What are you going to do?

Boris Van Ryswyk

The draft, yes. We've got picks, we've got the scouting now to know what we're taking. Free agency, there's nobody on the board yet, the pool is empty. That changes. What I'm telling you is the rooms come next. I've made the call in my own head, the equipment, the workout, the practice, those have to go up. I can't keep asking players to get better and giving them nothing to work with.

Marcus Bell

And the eleven losses? You've watched that from your seat, what do you take from it?

Boris Van Ryswyk

What I take is that we're down eighteen, that's the live figure, eighteen points behind where we should be on facilities alone, and you feel that in the second half of games. You feel it in March. We're going to build. That's all I can say tonight.

Marcus Bell

Boris Van Ryswyk, Florida Panthers, thank you for your time.
