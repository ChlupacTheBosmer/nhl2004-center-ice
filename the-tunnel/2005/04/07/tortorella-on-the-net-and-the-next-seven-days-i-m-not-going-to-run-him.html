Tortorella on the net and the next seven days: 'I'm not going to run him into the ground'

The Lightning coach leads the Southeast at 107 points with Nikolai Khabibulin at 67 starts, and four games against Boston in the next week puts that number in front of him.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelThursday, 7 April 2005

4:31 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Nikolai Khabibulin92 has taken 67 of Tampa Bay Lightning's 82 games this season, 82 percent of the workload, and the Lightning are in first place in the Southeast at 107 points after a 4-3 win at ATL on April 6. Coach John Tortorella leads the club into a four-game set with Boston that begins Friday, and the net is his call to make.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here in the corridor. Coach, forty-three shots against, twenty for you, and you're walking out of here with two points. Walk me through what that looked like from the bench.

John Tortorella

It looked like a hockey game, Jan. Twenty-seven shots against, twenty-six goals they put in the back of our net, three, and we scored four. That's what the number tells you on the board. But the details, the manage the puck, the compete level in the defensive zone, that's where the two points came from tonight. You don't win on the road in this league unless you're willing to make the play that doesn't show up in the box score.

Jan Kowalczyk

You outshot them, sorry, they outshot you, and yet you won. Did you feel like you controlled the game even without the shot differential?

John Tortorella

Look, controlled is a word I don't use after a 4-3 game. We controlled stretches. The second period, I thought we managed the puck real well, got out of our end with possession instead of just dumping and chasing. The third, uh, we got a little loose. But you play a team at home that's desperate, that's fighting for a spot, and you're going to get pushed. I thought we competed for sixty minutes and that's the bottom line.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're first in the Southeast, a hundred and seven points, you've got breathing room. What's the mood in that room on a morning like this, after a road win?

John Tortorella

The mood's fine. The mood's always fine when you win, Jan, and you know what, I've been around long enough to know that the mood doesn't matter in April if your game's not right. We're playing good hockey right now. We're competing. And we know the four games coming up are, you know, they're going to define where we sit when this is all over. So the mood's good, but it's not, it's not celebration in there. Not yet.

Jan Kowalczyk

Boston four times in seven days. That's a lot of hockey in a short window. How do you approach a stretch like that, the practice schedule, the lines, the rest?

John Tortorella

You manage it. That's what it comes down to. We've got guys who've played a lot of games this year, obviously, and we've got guys like Jassen Cullimore coming back, the tenth, hopefully, and that helps us. But you don't overthink it. You play your games, you get your rest when you can get it, and you show up. I'm not going to sit here and tell you I've got some grand design for four games in a week. I've got a plan for tomorrow's game, and that's where I'm at.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let me go to the one that everyone at home is asking. Nikolai's started sixty-seven of eighty-two. Four games against Boston. Have you talked to him about that?

John Tortorella

Yeah, we talk. You talk to your goaltender, you know, it's not like you wait until it's a problem. And, look, he's sixty-seven games. That's a lot of starts. He knows that, I know that, everybody knows that. But I'm not going to make a call on the television and I'm not going to tell you what Friday looks like until I've looked at practice tomorrow. What I'll say is this: I've got two goaltenders and I'm going to use both of them if I need to. I'm not going to run him into the ground.

Jan Kowalczyk

John Grahame's got nineteen games and eight wins this year. Is he ready to step into a Boston series? How do you prepare a guy who's been the backup for most of the season?

John Tortorella

He's played nineteen games in this league. He's faced, what, eight hundred shots this year? He's not some kid off the street. And look, the way we practice, the way we prepare, every guy in our building knows what the other guy's seeing. I don't need to tell John Grahame how to play this team or any other team. He's ready. Whether he plays or not, he's ready.

Jan Kowalczyk

Cullimore's due back Friday the tenth. What does that give you on the back end, with Bouillon still out until the twenty-fifth?

John Tortorella

It gives me a body. That's what it gives me. We've been shorthanded back there for a while and I think our guys have done a good job plugging holes, that's what you do in this league, you plug holes. But Jassen's a guy who's been here, he knows the structure, and having him out there Friday, Saturday, if the timing works, that's a real plus. And Francis, I think you'll see him when he's ready. The twenty-fifth is the date they've given me.

Jan Kowalczyk

One last one. You've got a five-game win streak in the middle of that last ten, but also two straight losses in there. The ceiling looks like the top of the East, but the consistency question, does that bother you at this point in the year?

John Tortorella

You're asking me about my team's consistency in the last week, Jan. We're thirty-nine wins and twenty-five losses in a long season. Every team in this league has stretches. I think you look at the last ten and you find what you want to find. What I'm looking at is, are we getting pucks out of our zone, are we winning the battle along the boards, are we on the same page in the defensive coverage. If the answer's yes, the wins come. And they've been coming. So I'll take it.

Jan Kowalczyk

Coach, thanks for your time. Back to you.
