Som Richards: We Weren't Going to Mortgage the Future for a Rental

Nashville's general manager on standing pat at the deadline, the core he locked up, and the equipment room he just paid for.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelThursday, 21 April 2005

3:59 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Som Richards carries the league's highest prestige standing at 722, runs a payroll of $46.77M against $81.56M in revenue, and watched Nashville Predators finish 1st in the Central at 101 points before losing four straight to close the schedule. None of his players appear on the top thirty of the contract-year class. He sat down with Jan Kowalczyk in the studio to talk about what he did at the deadline, what he didn't, and what the building means.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here in the studio with Som Richards, who runs the front office in Nashville. Som, thanks for the time. Let me start where the season ended. You win the Central, 101 points, and then the last four go against you. L4 to close. How does that leave you?

Som Richards

It leaves you... it leaves you wanting, that's what it does. Eighty-two games, you're the best team in the division for most of them, and the last week is, it's ugly. The legs are gone, the margin isn't there anymore. And I'm not going to sit here and tell you it's fine. It isn't fine.

Jan Kowalczyk

The deadline was three weeks before that. March 19th. You're sitting in first place. Did you go looking?

Som Richards

You always look. That's the job, isn't it? You're on the phone, you're asking what's out there, what a club would take. But here's the thing people get wrong about first place. They think you have to be a buyer. You don't. You have to be a buyer if what you add makes you a better team in June. Not in March. In June.

Jan Kowalczyk

And what was available didn't make you better in June?

Som Richards

What was moving, the pieces that could have come through here, they were good hockey players in March. By May they're gone. And what do you pay for six weeks of a good hockey player? A young guy who was part of this room. A pick that was part of this room two years from now. No. We weren't going to trade the future for a rental. Not this year.

Jan Kowalczyk

But the numbers are there. Forty-six point seven seven million on payroll, eighty-one point five six in revenue, twenty-seven point one six in profit. People look at that and they say there's room.

Som Richards

Yeah. I hear that. I hear that a lot. Look, the profit is real, I'm not denying that. But a profit doesn't mean I've got money in a drawer with my name on it. The guys on my roster right now, some of them are on good deals. Good deals don't last. And if I spend on a rental at a premium, when my own guy comes knocking for his first real contract, what do I tell him? That I spent it on someone else's six weeks? That's not close.

Jan Kowalczyk

And that's what the contracts tell you. None of your top players are on the top thirty contract-year list. That's by design.

Som Richards

Yeah, that's by design. I won't sit here and tell you I stayed up at night worrying about it, but we did the work. The core is locked. And that gives me something most of these clubs don't have right now. I can wait in free agency. I don't have to panic in July because three guys are walking out the door.

Jan Kowalczyk

Forty-eight days until the draft. What does that look like when your house is in order?

Som Richards

The draft's the draft. You take the best player available. That's not clever, it's just what you do when you can't see the whole market yet. But free agency, that's where having the core on deals actually pays you. I can be aggressive on the outside because my house is where it should be. I'm not forced into anything. That's a position I've wanted to be in for a while.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let me ask you about something smaller. The record shows you upgraded your equipment room between the ninth and the twelfth of this month. After the deadline, after the season's basically wrapped. Why now?

Som Richards

Because we were behind. I'll say that plainly. We were behind on the rooms and we've been behind for a while, and I kept saying next year, next year, and then this year I said no. The equipment room is not glamorous, nobody writes a story about it. But the guys walk into that building every single day. Every day. And if the building says 'we don't care about your work,' they feel it. If it says 'this is a place that's trying,' they feel that too. We needed to get that one up.

Jan Kowalczyk

So the building is where your head is. Not the phone calls.

Som Richards

A deadline is one night. A room is a decade. I know what I'd rather be known for. Grabbing a guy in March who leaves in July, that's not building anything. Giving the guys I have a place to work that's actually good, that compounds. That's the thing that lasts.

Jan Kowalczyk

You sit at 722 on the league's standing. Highest in the circuit. Does that open doors when you pick up the phone?

Som Richards

I don't think of it as opening doors. I know the league keeps the number. And I know if you're calling a negotiation, the other side can see where you are. That's just part of it. But I don't walk into a conversation thinking about a standing. I walk in thinking about what's right for this club.

Jan Kowalczyk

Som Richards, thank you very much for your time.

Som Richards

Thank you, Jan. Appreciate it.
