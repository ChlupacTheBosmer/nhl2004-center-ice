Steve McCarthy: 'I Don't Swing. I Don't Want to Swing.'

The defenseman with the 94 shot in the Book sits down to talk about Trail, the road, who shaped a game nobody can name a single coach in, and why a Fighting 51 is a fair grade he'll carry all the way.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelThursday, 21 April 2005

4:56 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Steve McCarthy81 finished the season with 82 games, 7 goals, 37 assists, 44 points, and 29.9 minutes a night, his best by every measure. The Sweater ran a profile under the title "Shot Power 94, Fighting 51," and the line that stuck was "no armor to play with." McCarthy, 24, sat with Marcus Bell in the studio to walk the road from Trail, British Columbia to the blue line in Chicago Blackhawks, to answer where a shot comes from when you can't point to one man, and to talk about the 16 grades between his base of 75 and his potential of 91.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here in the studio with Steve McCarthy of Chicago Blackhawks®. Steve, thanks for sitting down.

Steve McCarthy

Yeah, thanks for having me, Marcus.

Marcus Bell

The Sweater ran your profile Tuesday. They led with the 94 and the 51 and called you a defenseman with no armor to play with. How'd that land?

Steve McCarthy

Oh, it landed fine. I'd seen the grades, obviously, it's, it's not a surprise to me. But when they put it on a page and say no armor you sort of, yeah, you laugh a little. Because it's fair. FIGH 51, that's where it is. I don't swing. I don't, I don't want to swing. That's just, that's not me.

Marcus Bell

94 on the shot. That's the number everybody stops on. What's the release like? Does it feel different to you than it does to a guy sitting at, I don't know, 75?

Steve McCarthy

It feels normal. That's, that's the weird thing about it. I don't feel it special. You wind up, you go. But, you know, when the puck comes back off the boards hot and the D-man isn't expecting it, you see the look. He flinches. And that's, that's, yeah, that's the fun. You get to see a guy flinch.

Marcus Bell

Endurance 53, though. And you're playing 29.9 a night. How do you get through the last three minutes of a shift on legs that aren't built for it?

Steve McCarthy

You get back to the bench fast. I'm, I'm cooked at the end of a shift, I don't pretend otherwise. And, you know, 29.9 is a lot for these legs. But the coach puts you out, you go, you get the breathing, you go back. You don't, you don't, nobody asks for a shorter shift because they're tired. That's not a thing. You just get back out there.

Marcus Bell

Go back to Trail, British Columbia. Born February 1981. You're drafted in '99, 23rd overall, first round. You're 18. What's that distance like, the one from Trail to where you land?

Steve McCarthy

It's far. It's just, it's far. You're 18 and you step out and, and every building is bigger than the one you grew up in, every kid is a year older and 30 pounds heavier. And you're, you're a kid from Trail in a room full of guys from, from the, the big rinks, the cities with, with four sheets of ice. And you're thinking, do I belong here? Obviously you don't say that. But you think it.

Marcus Bell

Did you? Belong?

Steve McCarthy

Yeah. Because, once you're on the ice, the ice is the same. And I could shoot it. I could skate. And that's what you carry. You can't, you can't fake a 94. You step up and the thing goes, or it doesn't. And if it's going, you're in the room. You don't need, you don't need to be, I don't know, some other guy to be in the room.

Marcus Bell

Who taught you that? The shot, the skating, where does it come from? Was there a coach, a moment, somebody who put a stick in your hands and said, you, you do this?

Steve McCarthy

The first guys, the minor ice stuff, they teach you everything and nothing, you know? You're five, you're six, you're trying to stay on your edges and not hit a post. And then somewhere, I can't, I don't know, early teens, somebody says, keep the stick down, follow through, and it takes. It takes in a way the backhand never did. And I, I wish I could point to one coach. I can't. It was, a lot of them, a little bit, over, over years. And then it just set. And the, the legs came the same way. A hundred small corrections and then one day they're, they're just there. And that's what I've got. The shot and the legs. Everything else I'm, I'm still, still working on, you know.

Marcus Bell

Your skating grades are right there. Speed 92, acceleration 87, agility 87. Is skating the thing that got you 23rd overall?

Steve McCarthy

No. The shot got me 23rd. The skating is why I'm still here. You get drafted on a thing you do well in front of a scout. He sees you rip one in warmups, he writes down the number, you're gone. But 82 games, 29.9 a night, that's the legs. That's showing up in a building you've never been to, on a Thursday, in February, and going. And 92, 87, 87, that's what gets you through February when everybody else is dragging their skates.

Marcus Bell

Let's talk about the jump. Last season: 51 games, zero goals, 14 points, 15.2 a night. This season: 82, 7 goals, 37 assists, 44 points, 29.9 a night. Double the minutes. More than triple the points. Where does that come from?

Steve McCarthy

People want you to point to one thing. A drill, a new routine. And I, I can't do that. The ice time is the answer. 15.2 to 29.9. You're in hockey. You're making reads in hockey. You're not sitting on the bench watching a game you're not in. And when you're in 30 minutes of hockey a night, you find things. A seam you didn't see. A second to hold the puck. And 37 assists, that's, mostly that's the guys in front of me. You've got a guy out there who's gonna bury it from, from anywhere. You just, you put it on his tape.

Marcus Bell

Daze had 57 goals and 127 points. Zhamnov, 111. Sullivan, Fleury, right there on the front end. You're putting pucks on those sticks.

Steve McCarthy

That's the fun part. You step over the blue line and Eric Daze is standing there and you know he's gonna bury it. He's got the eye. And Alexei Zhamnov walks the middle, he wants a tape-to-tape and he just, he walks it in. Theoren Fleury doesn't even need your pass. He takes his own half-second and he's gone. And Steve Sullivan is, he's always open on the far side. You just, you put pucks on sticks, you follow through, and you try not to be the reason we lose it on the other end. Which, with the end of my shifts, that's, that's an ongoing project.

Marcus Bell

The Bureau's Development Index has you up four on your base grade. Morale at 96. That's a man having a good year.

Steve McCarthy

It is. And I'm not gonna sit here and tell you I don't know what those numbers mean. You see 96, you're happy. You're playing thirty minutes, you've got 44 points, you're not hurt, the room's good. What else do you want? You're happy. That's the whole thing.

Marcus Bell

Potential 91, you're at 77 today, base 75. That profile said 16 grades left. Where does a defenseman go when his top attribute is 94 and his bottom is 51?

Steve McCarthy

I go in what's working. I'm never gonna be a fighter. That's 51 and I'll take it, it's fair. The endurance, maybe, maybe I get two or three points on that. I don't know. But the shooting, 94 is not the ceiling. There's a way to get that to 96. Different angle off the rush, a different release point. Small things. And the pass, 86, I can, I can find the fourth guy. The one I keep skipping. So I work the 94. I work the 86. The 51, that stays the 51. And I'm fine with that.

Marcus Bell

Last one. You're 24 now. Drafted in '99. Has the road gotten any shorter? Trail to here, is it any easier?

Steve McCarthy

No. The distance is the distance. You just, you stop marking it. You get comfortable, you know where the coffee shop is in a city you've been to a dozen times. You come off a shift and it's just Tuesday in Chicago. And that, that's when you know it worked. You stop thinking about the drive. You just drive.

Marcus Bell

Steve McCarthy of Chicago Blackhawks®, thank you for the time. Back to you.
