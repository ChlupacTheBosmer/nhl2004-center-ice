Cammalleri on Palffy's hands, a 20-point year, and the 19 grades left

The LA Kings' 22-year-old told The Tunnel that Zigmund Palffy is the best player he has been on the ice with, that his rookie season left him just existing, and that the gap between 79 and 98 is every shift, not one fix.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelFriday, 11 March 2005

3:48 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Mike Cammalleri81 has 48 points in 47 games this season, up from 20 points in 69 games last year. The 22-year-old right wing out of Richmond Hill, Ontario is in his second season with the Los Angeles Kings Kings, and the Bureau's Development Index has him six above his base grade of 79. Marcus Bell sat down with him in the studio.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here with Mike Cammalleri. Mike, thanks for sitting down. I want to start with the obvious: 48 points in 47 games. Last year you had 20 in 69. You're at 20.5 minutes a night now, up from 11.8. What changed?

Mike Cammalleri

Thanks for having me, Marcus. Yeah, the minutes, obviously. When you're out there for 20 instead of 11, you get into games. You touch the puck in the right end and you, you build off that. And the trust helps. Coach puts you on the power play, you're taking faceoffs in the offensive zone, and that, that compounds. You start taking chances because you know the next shift you're still on the ice.

Marcus Bell

You missed the start of the season. The elbow, you were out until December 14th. Did that set you back?

Mike Cammalleri

No. I think it, it helped, honestly. I came off last year kind of, you know, flat. I played 69 games but I was sitting more than I was skating. Getting two weeks off, coming back with some legs, I think that was the best thing that could have happened. I came in and the first game back I felt like I had another gear.

Marcus Bell

Who's the best player you've been on the ice with? I'll throw one at you, the Bureau has Roman Cechmanek at 86 overall, the highest grade in the LA room.

Mike Cammalleri

Cech is great, don't get me wrong. He's got the hands on the puck, he's calm up ice, he's saved us a dozen games. But the best player I've been on the ice with is Ziggy. Zigmund Palffy. He's got 60 goals, Marcus. You don't watch a guy score 60 goals without it getting in your head.

Marcus Bell

What's the thing? You're in practice every day. What do you take from watching him?

Mike Cammalleri

The hands and the positioning. He's not, he's not the fastest guy out there anymore, you know? But he never loses the puck. You think he's gonna make a move and he already made it, what, two seconds ago. And he's always where the play's gonna be before it gets there. I was watching him on TV when I was 15, you know, committing to schools and all that. And now he's sitting next to me on the bus and he's, he's telling me what I did wrong on my backcheck in practice.

Marcus Bell

You played the Quebec Pee-Wee tournament in 1996. Zigmund Palffy was drafted in '91. What's it like being 22 and playing next to a guy who was a pro when you were still in minor hockey?

Mike Cammalleri

It's wild. I remember watching him, I think it was, uh, '96 or '97, and he was the coolest player I'd seen. And now he's passing me pucks in morning skates. He's very, very old school. He'll, he'll stop the drill and tell you your edges were flat. He's not mean about it, but he's not gonna let it go either. And that's good. You need a guy like that in the room who just says what he sees.

Marcus Bell

Let's go back to last year. 20 points, 11.8 minutes. You'd just come from Michigan, where you led the team in goals, you were an All-American, you played in front of 74,554 people at the Cold War game. And you come to LA and you're on the ice for 11 a night. What was that year?

Mike Cammalleri

It was hard. At Michigan I was the guy. I had the puck, I took the shots, everyone ran a play for me. I come to LA and I'm fourth, fourth forward, I'm not even getting into the offensive zone most shifts. And I kept telling myself, you're here, you made it, be grateful. And I was. I was grateful. But I wasn't growing. I was just existing.

Marcus Bell

What did you take from it? Something you carry into this year?

Mike Cammalleri

Patience. And knowing who I am. The Bureau's Book has my fighting at 50, which, yeah, fair. And I spent part of last year trying to be tougher, trying to win puck battles in the corner against guys way bigger than me. This year I just started trusting my hands. Trusting that I can beat a guy with a move or with speed or with a pass before he even gets a body on me. And the points came because I stopped trying to be someone I'm not.

Marcus Bell

The Bureau has your deking at 89, your passing at 89. Those are among the best grades on the LA roster. Your overall is 79 today, the Index has you six above base, and your potential is 98. That's 19 grades between you and the ceiling. What are they?

Mike Cammalleri

I don't think I can name 19 specific grades, you know? But I know what the difference is between me and, uh, and Ziggy. It's doing it 80 times instead of 50. I can have a game where every read is right, every pass is on tape, and I feel like I can play with anyone in this league. And then the next game I'm chasing plays, I'm a half-step behind the puck. Those are the games I have to fix. I think the 19 grades is just, showing up more than half the time and being the guy I can be.

Marcus Bell

One last one. You're 22, one year left on your deal. What does the two-year version of you look like?

Mike Cammalleri

I'd like to be a first-line player. I'd like to be the guy they game-plan for, not the guy they match against the third line. I think that's realistic, I think this year shows it's possible. And I'd like to be in a playoff race. We're not there yet, but I think we're getting close. I'd like to be in it when we get there.

Marcus Bell

Mike Cammalleri, thank you very much. Back to you in the studio.
