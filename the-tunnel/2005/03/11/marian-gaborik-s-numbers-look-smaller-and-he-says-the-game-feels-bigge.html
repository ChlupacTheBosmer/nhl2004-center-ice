Marian Gaborik's numbers look smaller, and he says the game feels bigger

The Bureau's Development Index has him up six from his base, the highest he has been in a Toronto sweater, and he says the reason is not on the scoresheet.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelFriday, 11 March 2005

1:58 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Marian Gaborik99 finished with one assist in Toronto Maple Leafs's 6-2 win at Edmonton Oilers on Thursday, the quietest night of a five-game win streak that has Toronto at 112 points and 49 wins through 70 games. The Bureau's Development Index has him up six from his base grade of 91, putting him at 96 overall, the highest mark of his season. Gaborik has 46 goals and 84 points in 70 games, against 77 and 131 in 82 a year ago.

Yeah, third period, I think. We get in, uh, Pet is on the wall and I see the, you know, the lane open, so I just put it there. Easy one. Chlup does the rest.

Pet Chlup had five points tonight. Two goals, three assists. He is twenty-three, same as you. What is it like playing with a guy your age who is putting up lines like that in your second year here?

Marian Gaborik

He's fast, you know? Fast and he sees the ice like, I don't know how you say, like a center but he plays all over. So when he's out there and I'm out there and Mats is there too, we don't have to think so much. The puck just goes. You see tonight, how many passes in that third period, you know, just one-touch, one-touch.

Marian, I want to ask about something else. The Bureau's Development Index has you at plus six from your base. You're sitting at 96 overall tonight. That's the highest it has been this season. But you've got 46 goals in 70 games, last year it was 77 in 82. What changed in your game that the numbers don't show?

Marian Gaborik

Well, uh, I think, I think before, I was thinking too much about the, you know, the goal. The first thing, I want to shoot. Now, when I see Mats or Alex, I know they finish, so I pass. And then, the other thing is the other end. I play different back there now. Before I would, how you say, I would float. Watch. Now I go back, I help. That's maybe why the number is less and the, uh, the grade is more.

Marcus Bell

You mentioned January when we talked after the Boston game, you said nobody in the room cares about your goal total after a loss. You're on a five-game win streak now, 112 points in the standings. Does the goal thing bother you less, or you just don't notice anymore?

Marian Gaborik

No, I notice. I notice when I go home and I have zero. But when we win 6-2 like tonight, I don't care. I really don't. I look at the scoreboard and six is good. I don't look for my name. Maybe in April I will look. I don't know.

One year left on the contract. Does that sit in your head in March, or are you just playing?
