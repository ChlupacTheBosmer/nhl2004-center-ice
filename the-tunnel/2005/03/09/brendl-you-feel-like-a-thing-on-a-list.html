Brendl: 'You feel like a thing on a list'

The 23-year-old winger on his step from 21 points to 35, three years of control still on his $0.80M deal, and being 20 years old inside the Eric Lindros trade.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelWednesday, 9 March 2005

3:05 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Pavel Brendl81 went from 21 points to 35 this season and from 10.6 minutes a night to 16.1. He turns 24 on March 23, plays for Carolina Hurricanes on $0.80M with 3 years left, and the Bureau's Development Index has him up three. Jan Kowalczyk sat with him in the studio.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here from the studio, and Pavel Brendl is with me. Pavel, you've got 35 points in 69 games this year. Last season you had 21 in 82. That's a step. What's different?

Pavel Brendl

Well, the minutes, you know. I play about 16 now, last year maybe 10. So you see more ice, you touch the puck more, how you say, you're in more plays. And I feel, the good feeling is, I don't try to prove now. I just play. Last year I think: everybody is watching, I must show. This year I just go.

Jan Kowalczyk

The Book has you at 96 for speed and 95 for shot power. Those are up there with the best in the league. But toughness is 52, faceoffs 50. You must notice that people come at you differently when they know that.

Pavel Brendl

Every game. Every game somebody tries to put me in the boards, make me feel it. But I have Rod Brind'Amour on the line, so, you know, I don't need to do that part. Roddy does that part very well. I just skate, I get open, I take the shot. That's my job.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let me bring up the money. You're at $0.80 million with 3 years left on the deal. That's not a contract year. You're not going anywhere in 2005. What does three quiet years do for your head?

Pavel Brendl

I think about the next one. Everybody does. You're 23, you see the older guys, how much they make, how they talk different when the contract is close. But I have 3 years. The league, you know, it changes so fast. Maybe next year I play 20 minutes, maybe 30 goals, maybe nothing. I don't look at year three. Tonight is what I control.

Jan Kowalczyk

You were the fourth pick in 1999. Two years later you were part of the package that sent Eric Lindros to New York. You were 20. Walk me through that day.

Pavel Brendl

I was in the gym, I think. And my agent calls and says, you're going to Philadelphia. And I'm like, what? I just signed, I'm trying to make the Rangers, I'm in training camp almost, and now I'm part of a package. You feel like, like a thing on a list. That's what I remember. You feel like a thing on a list.

Jan Kowalczyk

Does that still sit wrong? Three years later.

Pavel Brendl

No. I'm not angry at it. Eric Lindros, he was a big player, big contract, big situation in Philadelphia. I was a piece. That's how the business works, you know. I don't blame anybody. But you remember the feeling. You remember being 20 and the phone goes and you're not a player anymore, you're a name on the paper.

Jan Kowalczyk

You've got Brind'Amour in there, Sandis Ozolinsh, guys who've been around. Where do you sit in that room? Are you a voice?

Pavel Brendl

I'm not the voice. The voice is Roddy, the older guys. I can, how you say, I can show a young guy where the bus is, how to pack, travel is not easy the first year. I do that. But I'm still building my own game. I don't have the right to talk in the room about how it should be until I'm doing it every night. That's the truth.

Jan Kowalczyk

Your assists went from 9 to 17 this year. Your goals nearly doubled too, but that passing number, what changed there?

Pavel Brendl

I pass more. Before, I have the puck and I think: shoot, I must shoot, I must prove the shot is good. The coach, he tells me, Pavel, people are open. And Sandis on the back end, he sees everything, how you say, the whole ice. I just get open and he finds me. I don't need to carry the whole thing.

Jan Kowalczyk

Three years from now the deal is up. You'll be 26. What does the scoresheet look like when you walk into that negotiation?

Pavel Brendl

I want 30 goals. Seventy, eighty points. That's what a guy with my speed should be doing, I think. I know what I can do. It's the small things, how you say. The wall battle, the draw, things nobody watches on a replay but they take your minutes. I get more minutes, I get more chances. That's the next step.

Jan Kowalczyk

Pavel Brendl, Carolina. Pavel, thank you for sitting down with me.

Pavel Brendl

Thank you, Jan. Good to talk.
