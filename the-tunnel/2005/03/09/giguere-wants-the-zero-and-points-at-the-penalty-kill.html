Giguere Wants the Zero and Points at the Penalty Kill

No shutout in 59 games for the Ducks' goaltender, who says his own team's special teams keep stealing the clean night he can't seem to get.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelWednesday, 9 March 2005

2:15 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Jean-Sebastien Giguere97 stopped 21 of 23 shots and picked up his 32nd win of the season as Mighty Ducks of Anaheim beat Edmonton Oilers 3-2 at home Sunday night. The Ducks have lost 12 games in overtime this season, the most in the Pacific division, and their goaltender carries the number that haunts a 97-grade season: no shutout in 59 starts.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here with Jean-Sebastien Giguere. Three and two over Edmonton, twenty-one saves on twenty-three, and you're a one-game streak team now. You needed this one.

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

I needed it, for sure. Twenty-three... you know, I like when they shoot at me, I stay in it. The puck she was loose tonight, I didn't get her clean a couple times. And two against, no zero. Fifty-nine games, no shutout. That's in my head every night, Jan. Every night.

Jan Kowalczyk

Twelve overtime losses. That's the most in the Pacific division. How much of that comes back to the penalty kill not closing a game down?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

It's the kill, for sure. I've got no size out there, you know? Seventy-three inches, I'm not... I don't push guys off the crease like some goalies. When they park a big one on a two-on-one, I see the shot, I see him, I'm looking around. That's where the goal goes. We have to be better on the kill. That's it.

Jan Kowalczyk

Back in December you told me a good penalty kill quiets your workload, takes the game out of your hands. That quiet hasn't come.

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

December. Yeah, I remember that. I said a lot of things in December. The kill gets a week where it's tight, maybe two, and then someone takes a dumb two minutes and we're back to twenty-five shots. It's, it's... frustrating. The guys try. They do try.

Jan Kowalczyk

Sergei Fedorov wasn't in the lineup tonight. Without him, did Andy McDonald step into that center spot and play the way you need?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

Andy was the man, for sure. One and two, he was everywhere out there. Backchecked, made plays, all of it. And Tuomas with the goal. When you lose a guy like Sergei you can't just put one man in his place. Brown has to take more draws, Rob has to own his end. Everybody adds a little. That's the guys.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're up three on the Bureau's Development Index right now. Ninety-seven overall, your best of the year. Fifty-nine starts and no shutout. Does the grade help at all, or is the zero the only thing that feels right?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

The grade says I'm stopping pucks. I know what I am. But the zero, that's everything coming together. The PK, the defense, sixty minutes of guys giving me a clean look. Ninety-seven doesn't put a zero on the board. Only that does.

Jan Kowalczyk

You told Marcus Bell in January you wanted to talk about two points instead of the scoreboard. Two points tonight. Are we getting there?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

Two points, yeah. That's what I wanted, for sure. But I'd like the zero with it, you know? Maybe next time. I'll keep telling you the same thing, Jan.

Jan Kowalczyk

Thank you, Jean-Sebastien. Good night.
