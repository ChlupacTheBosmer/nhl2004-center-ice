Giguère Gives Three On Eighteen, Still Gets The Two

The Ducks' goaltender owned the three he let past Chicago on 18 shots, named the blue line as the reason the shutout keeps not coming, and said the building carried the overtime win.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelMonday, 14 March 2005

2:12 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Jean-Sebastien Giguère93 stood in the tunnel at Mighty Ducks of Anaheim after a 4-3 overtime win over Chicago Blackhawks on March 12, three goals conceded on 18 shots faced. His season line now reads 34 wins, 26 losses and zero shutouts in 61 games. The Ducks are on a three-game win streak and 24-12 at home. Jan Kowalczyk caught him before the bus.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here with Jean-Sebastien Giguere in the tunnel at Honda Center. Four-three, overtime, twenty-three for your boys and eighteen against. You gave up three on those eighteen. How did it feel?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

The first one, I'm late. I see it and I'm already behind it. The third one, Prossi's in the corner and they come back and it's two-on-one with me, I can't be everywhere. So, they're on me. I should have two. But we won. That's the thing, we got the two points.

Jan Kowalczyk

I asked you back in December about the shutout situation. You were 13-15 then, no zero, and you said it was yours to fix. You're 34-26 now, still no shutout in 61. Still yours to fix?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

Yeah. I said it then, I'll say it now. When Niclas is up at the other blue, when Aaron's in the offensive zone, I'm... how do I say it, the house is open behind me. And Prossi tonight, three goals. Fedorov with the goal and the assist. They give me so much offense. So the trade is, I see more traffic, I see more odd-man things. I like the trade. But yeah, the zero, it's on me.

Jan Kowalczyk

This is your building, 24 and 12 here. It was loud tonight, especially in overtime. What does that sound like from your crease when it's three-all and everything is on the line?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

You hear them. I mean, you can't not hear them. When the building, she's behind you, it's... you want to give them one more save. That's the whole thing. You're not thinking about your stat sheet. You're thinking, they're screaming and I'm the last guy. I've got to make it.

Jan Kowalczyk

And the road is next. You've got three in a row, you're 86 points, and now the travel. Does that wear on this group, the bus and the flights and the hotels, or does a streak like this change how it feels?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

It changes it for sure. When you're losing, the bus is long, the hotel is... the hotel is where you don't want to be, you know? When you're winning, Prossi's making jokes, Andy's got the music going, you don't notice the flight. Sergei's the same every day though. He doesn't change. The bus is long or short, he's there at six in the morning reading something, you know? So you match that.

Jan Kowalczyk

Last one. You said back in January you wanted to talk about two points instead of the scoreboard. Tonight the scoreboard says three on eighteen, the two points say the streak is alive. Which conversation do you want next time?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

The two points, always the two points. I don't want to talk about the three on eighteen. I want to talk about Prossi's third one, the way he got there, the way Fedorov found him. That's what I want the conversation about. The rest I figure out in the morning. Thank you, Jan.
