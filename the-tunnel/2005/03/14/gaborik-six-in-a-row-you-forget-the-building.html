Gaborik: 'Six in a row, you forget the building'

The winger on what it feels like inside a six-game streak, and why the last few weeks have made him comfortable again.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelMonday, 14 March 2005

2:26 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Marian Gaborik99 scored a goal and added an assist as Toronto Maple Leafs beat Calgary Flames 5-1 on the road on 2005-03-13, extending the club's win streak to six. Gaborik carries 47 goals and 85 points through 71 games, averaging 20.2 minutes a night for a Toronto team sitting at 114 points with 11 games left.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here with Marian Gaborik, right after the five-one in Calgary. Marian, six in a row. What does it feel like from the inside when you're in the middle of one of these?

Marian Gaborik

Yeah, it's good, you know. We play good game right now. The puck goes in and the puck doesn't go in our net, so how you say, it's easy to play like this. You come to the rink and you don't think about the loss, you think about tonight.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're 26 and 9 on the road. You just came into Calgary and put five on them and only gave up one on 16 shots against. Does it feel different on the road when you're winning?

Marian Gaborik

Yeah, it does, you know. When you're not winning, the building is loud and the other team, they play good and it's, how you say, every puck is a fight. But now we come here and the building, you forget it. You just play your game. They have a good team, CGY, they can score, but tonight they don't get that many looks. Twenty-six to sixteen, that's, yeah.

Jan Kowalczyk

I talked to you in December after that streak you were on, the eleven, and you told me the numbers don't matter without the Cup. Are you still saying that tonight, with six and counting?

Marian Gaborik

Yeah, I say same thing. The streak is nice tonight, tomorrow it's a new game. I don't sit here and think about fourteen or fifteen. I think about the next game, that's what I say to myself. The regular season is long, we have eleven left, we play good, but yeah, the Cup is what I want.

Jan Kowalczyk

I want to ask you about the structure. I've watched a few of these, and it looks like the coach has changed something, the way you come out of the zone, maybe a different read on the first pass. Has it felt different to you on the ice?

Marian Gaborik

Yeah, yeah. I think, how you say, the first pass is more simple now. Before sometimes we try too much, the first pass, and the puck, it goes away. Now we go, we find, I don't know, Mats or Brad, and they have time. And I go. That's the thing. If I go and I have the space, that's good for me. The speed, you know, if you stop and start, you lose it. But if the pass comes where it should, I just go.

Jan Kowalczyk

Tonight you had a goal and an assist. Walk me through the goal if you can, what you saw.

Marian Gaborik

It's, uh, the puck comes and I have the space, how you say, the defenceman he's late. Maybe half a step, but for me that's enough. I go and I shoot, top corner. Simple.

Jan Kowalczyk

Marian, six in a row, 114 points, the best record in the league, the Cup on the line in April. Is this the room you imagined when you came here?

Marian Gaborik

Yeah. I think, when I come here, I see the guys, I see Mats, I see Ed in net, and I think, we can do something. The young guys, they play good too, Chlup, Gaborik, me, how you say, everyone is ready. The coach, he make the system simple, and we follow it. I think it works.

Jan Kowalczyk

Thanks, Marian. Enjoy the flight home. Jan Kowalczyk from Calgary. Back to you.
