Iginla has 61 goals and a question nobody will answer for him

Calgary's captain talks the gap between 92 and 61, the last year on his deal, and what a man does when the grade book disagrees with how he feels.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelMonday, 14 March 2005

4:23 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Jarome Iginla94 scored 92 goals last season and won the Maurice Richard. Through 64 games this year he has 61, a pace that prints at 132 over 82. His Book overall is 85, against a base grade of 93, and his contract carries 0 years left at $6.50M. Marcus Bell sat down with him in the studio.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here with Jarome Iginla in the studio. Jarome, thanks for sitting down with me. You won the Richard last year with 92 goals, led the league. You've got 61 with, what, 18 games still on the board. What's the difference in how you feel about this year compared to last?

Jarome Iginla

Thanks, Marcus. Well, the goals are the goals, right. You look at the number and you know where you've been. But the thing people miss, I think, is that I feel fine. The game feels fine. I'm skating, I'm seeing the ice, the shot's there. The 92 is behind me and I can't go back and play last year again.

Marcus Bell

But 92 to 61. That's not a small number.

Jarome Iginla

No, it's not. I'll give you that. But you know, I've got 61 goals and 103 points. Most guys in this league would put that on their fridge. I'm not going to sit here and feel sorry about 61 goals. I'm going to feel like I owe this team a few more before April, that's how I look at it.

Marcus Bell

What do you think changed? Is it the way teams are defending you now that you've proven you can score 92?

Jarome Iginla

I think that's part of it, yeah. Last year everybody was, you know, figuring it out on the fly. This year there's a plan. There's a game plan against me every night. A guy gets assigned, there's a coverage call on the forecheck. You know, when you come up and you're hitting 92, everybody starts watching the film. You see what you do and you try to take it away. I expected that, honestly. I just didn't expect it to cost me this much.

Marcus Bell

The Bureau's Book has you at 85 overall right now. Your base grade is 93. That's eight points below where the Bureau says your ceiling should be. How do you read that?

Jarome Iginla

I read it the same way everybody does. It's 85 and I'd like it to be higher. But I can't do much about it tonight. The Index is the Index. You play, the number moves. I've been in this league long enough to know you don't chase the grade, you chase the game and the grade catches up. Usually.

Marcus Bell

And your morale? That's at 87. You sound like a man who's in a good place.

Jarome Iginla

I am. I am. And that's, you know, that's the thing. When I look at the room I'm in, and I look at the guys I play with. We're not the deepest team. You look at our top guy in net, Roman, he's been steady. Dean and Craig and Marty, those are my guys, I know what they give me every night. It's not 16 guys deep like some clubs. I know that. I accept it and I try to find a way.

Marcus Bell

In December Jan Kowalczyk asked you about that overtime loss to Toronto, when you were the league scoring leader. You told him the chances come and the puck finds the other guy. You were at 60 points then. Do you still see it that way?

Jarome Iginla

Yeah. I mean, yeah. Some nights the puck just isn't yours. You know, I had a game the other week, three, four good looks, and I'm shaking my head on the bench and my linemate says, 'You'll get one.' And you know, you hope. You hope you'll get one. But you can't manufacture it. You can't force the puck in. And that's been, maybe, part of the whole year. I've been forcing it a little more than I want to admit.

Marcus Bell

So what do you take from a season that hasn't gone the way you wanted? You're 27, this should be your best hockey. What does it teach you?

Jarome Iginla

It teaches me, I mean... when I was growing up, my grandfather would drive me to every tournament, every rink in Northern Alberta. He didn't ask me how many goals I scored. He asked if I was having fun. And I think this year I lost that a little, you know? I started counting. I started watching the pace. And when you're counting, you're not playing. I learned that the hard way. If I'd just played, I think the number would've been closer.

Marcus Bell

Now let's talk about the other thing. You've got 0 years left on your deal. $6.50M a year, and it expires. How do you keep that out of your head during a season?

Jarome Iginla

I don't think about it. That's the answer everybody wants, right? 'I just go out and play.' But honestly, yeah. I don't. I've never been good at that kind of thing. When I was a kid I wanted to be a two-sport athlete, like Bo Jackson. I wasn't sitting around thinking about my contract with the Raiders, I was trying to hit a fastball. Same thing now. I can't control who calls me or what number they put on a sheet. I can control my shift.

Marcus Bell

But you've got a family, you've got roots here. You've been in Calgary a long time. Does that make it harder or easier, knowing this is a decision year?

Jarome Iginla

It makes it easier, actually. Because I know what I want. I know what this building feels like on a Tuesday in February when it's thirty below. I know my guys. The rest is, that's for my agent and them to talk about. I'm not going to let it make me play scared. I'm not going to take a hit along the boards I usually take because I'm thinking about a number. That's how you get hurt, and that's how you lose games.

Marcus Bell

You had the INJU grade, that's your durability, at 50 in the Book. Lowest of anything you do. And yet no injuries this season. You've played 64 out of 82 and it doesn't seem to have bitten you.

Jarome Iginla

I know, right? And I don't know what to tell you about that. Maybe it just hasn't happened. Maybe it's coming. You know what, I've been lucky. I've taken hits my whole career and got up. You can't take that for granted. You know, my whole game is speed, it's getting open first, getting the puck before the hit. So maybe I've been smart about it. The grade says I should be breaking down, and I'm not breaking down. I'll take it.

Marcus Bell

Eighteen games left. What's the goal for this stretch? Personal and team.

Jarome Iginla

Team first, obviously. We've got to be good in April, and that means getting the structure right, getting Roman some help back there, getting our forecheck honest. Personal? I just want to go on a little run. Two, three games where I feel like myself again, where the puck goes in and I don't think about it. And you know what, the 92 is never coming back this year. I know that. But I've got a shot at something. I can still make this feel like a season, not a footnote.

Marcus Bell

Jarome Iginla, captain of the Calgary Flames® Flames. Thank you, Jarome. Back to you.
