Pavol Demitra: "82 games, every night. That is the work."

The St. Louis Blues® winger on why the games played mean more to him than the 101 points, and why this year's lower number is a different game, not a broken one.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelWednesday, 2 March 2005

4:07 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Pavol Demitra81 turned 30 last November and carries the only 101-point season on the sheet for him: 43 goals, 58 assists, 8 penalty minutes across 82 games in 2003-04. This year he is at 60 points in 63 games, with the Bureau's Development Index two below his base of 89. The Sweater profile "The Long Way Round" traces his road from a soccer ball in Trencin to this chair; the sit-down starts at the ball.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here, studio desk, with Pavol Demitra. Pavol, I was reading the Sweater piece on you, 'The Long Way Round,' and it opens with your father playing professional soccer in Slovakia. You were a soccer kid first. When did hockey become the thing?

Pavol Demitra

Well, I play soccer, I like soccer, I'm good at soccer. My father he played, so this is what I know. But at fifteen, I look at the two things and I think, no. The hockey. That's where I go. I start late, you know. The other boys, they are on skates when they still small. I am fifteen. So I have to work.

Marcus Bell

And you did catch them. You played for Trencin in the premier league as a teenager, you got picked in the 1993 draft, you scored in your first NHL game. But then the road got long. AHL, IHL, a holdout, back to Slovakia.

Pavol Demitra

Yeah. It was, how you say, the long road. The AHL, I learn. The IHL, I learn more. Ottawa they say, we trade you, okay, I go to St. Louis Blues® and then it is good. But those years, the years nobody watch, the small towns, the buses, that is where I become the player. Not the NHL. Before.

Marcus Bell

You were the 227th pick. Eighth round. There's a line people like to say about late-round guys: they're hungry.

Pavol Demitra

No, hungry is not the word. I think... the first round, they go to the NHL, they play, they learn there. I go the AHL, I go the IHL, I learn to be good when nobody look. That is not hungry. That is patience. Maybe. I learn patience from the road.

Marcus Bell

Let's talk about the record. 2003-04. 82 games, 43 goals, 101 points. Nineteen minutes a night. Eight penalty minutes, all season. That is the line you've had your name in the papers for. Is that the one you're proud of?

Pavol Demitra

Eight penalties. I did not want them. I'm not a fighter, you know, I just play, I pass, I skate. But the 82 games. That's the thing. Every night, here. I don't miss. My body, it stay good. I come every day. 101 is nice. But the 82, that is mine. That is the work.

Marcus Bell

You'd take the 82 over the 101.

Pavol Demitra

I take both. But you can have a good year and get hurt. You can be sick and you stay home. Every game I am here. My boys say, Pavol, 101, and I say, I played every game. The 82 is what I want on my sheet when this is done.

Marcus Bell

This year you've played 63 games and you're at 60 points. The Bureau's Index has you two below your base. That's not the same season.

Pavol Demitra

It is not wrong. I don't say wrong. It is different. I play 60, 101 last year, yes, I know the difference. But I play good hockey. I pass to Keith, I pass to Doug, I'm here every day. My morale is 100. I come here and I want to play. The points sometimes they come, sometimes they slow. I don't say it is wrong.

Marcus Bell

What's different about the game right now? Is it what you're doing on the ice or what the other clubs are doing to you?

Pavol Demitra

I think both. Last year maybe I take more chances, more shots. This year I pass more, I play with the team. And the other clubs, they watch me now. They say, this Pavol, he make the play, take it away. So it is harder. I adjust. I always adjust. But it takes time, you know.

Marcus Bell

You mentioned Keith and Doug. You've got Tkachuk, Weight, Pronger. That's a room with names in it. Does having them around change what you're trying to do at even strength?

Pavol Demitra

Keith, he opens everything. You know, when he go to the net, everybody watch him. And I see the other man, I pass. This is easy. Doug, he see the ice like nobody. And Chris behind, he hit everybody. I don't hit. I don't need to hit. Chris do it for all of us.

Marcus Bell

One thing that caught me in the profile. You said the 1998-99 All-Star Game in Tampa was the first time you felt like you belonged. A guy who'd been in the IHL two years before that. What did it feel like in that room?

Pavol Demitra

I score one goal for the World. We lose. But I'm in the room with these guys, the first round, the big names, and I think, I am here. I made it. From Trencin, from the soccer, from the eighth round. I am in Tampa. That's the feeling. Not the goal. The being there.

Marcus Bell

So what would you say to a kid in Slovakia now, fifteen years old, starting hockey late, thinking it's too late?

Pavol Demitra

Fifteen is nothing. I play soccer all day, my skating is bad, everybody laugh. And I say, watch me in two years. So you work. You work on the edges, you work on the hands, every day. And you don't look at the round, who go first, who go second. You just play. That's what I tell them.

Marcus Bell

Last one. The 82 games, you said that's what you want on your sheet. What's the rest of it look like? How do you want the next two, three years to read?

Pavol Demitra

I want the games. Every season, the games. The points, they come and go, that is hockey. But if I play every night, if I am here, if Keith look for me and I am there, that is... that is the job. That is everything. The 101 was the cherry. The games is the cake.

Marcus Bell

Pavol Demitra, thank you for your time. Back to you.
