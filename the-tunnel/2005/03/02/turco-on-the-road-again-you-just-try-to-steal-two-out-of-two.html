Turco, on the road again: 'You just try to steal two out of two'

The Stars' goaltender talks about winning two of two on this road swing, the BUF desperation he felt in the first minute, and what 17 games left means for a man who has been here long enough to know the trap.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelWednesday, 2 March 2005

3:19 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Marty Turco95 stopped 19 of 20 shots as Dallas Stars took a 3-1 win at Buffalo Sabres on Monday, his second appearance and first win of a two-game road swing that opened with an overtime thriller at Ottawa Senators two nights earlier. Dallas now sits at 102 points with 17 games remaining, and the gap between here and April is what Turco has on his mind.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here, post-game at Buffalo. Marty, you asked me in January what I wanted to talk about after the road trip started, and here we are, two games in, four points out of four. How does this stretch feel from where you're sitting?

Marty Turco

You know, it feels like a long trip that went the way you want it to go, and that's not something that happens a lot in this league. We got one in overtime the other night and a 3 and 1 tonight and you just take that. You never want to come on the road and get one out of four.

Jan Kowalczyk

Buffalo's on eight straight losses. You can sense that desperation early in a building like that, the crowd gets nervous, the team gets nervous. Did it feel different in the first period than a normal night?

Marty Turco

Yeah, obviously. You can hear it, the building is, uh, they're pressing for one goal and you can feel every shot has a little more on it. But they only had 20 on me tonight, that's not a lot. The one that went in, it was, I mean, I'd like that back. You know, it was a two-on-one coming off our blue line and I think I was a half-beat slow on the slide and he just, yeah. He buried it. You want those back. But you get back in the next game and you don't worry about it.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let me go back to the Ottawa game, because Tugnutt was in for the overtime there but you started that one and Tugnutt closed it out. What was the feeling on the bench in the third period when it was 3 and 3?

Marty Turco

You know, it was, it was a good game to be in. Both teams were skating, nobody wanted to lose and you could tell. had a couple of chances in regulation and I think their guy made one real save on Jere late and we all know how that goes. But you know, Ronny came in and got us the win in OT, that's what you need. That's the depth thing. You've got two guys and either one can go steal it.

Jan Kowalczyk

Seventeen games left. You've got 102 points, 44 wins. What does the rest of this schedule ask of a club in your position, where the top is locked down and April is the only thing left to prepare for?

Marty Turco

Well, the trap is the complacency. You've got 102, you look up, nobody's going to catch you, and now every road game is a little easier to sleep through. That's the thing, you know. We've been here before, or close to it, and it's, it's hard to keep that edge when you've already won the thing in February. is the guy, he won't let you. If you show up soft, he lets you know about it. That's the beauty of having Mike in that locker room. He still wants to win every practice drill like he's 22.

Jan Kowalczyk

You had 33 wins through 53 games last you were asked about the consistency question, and you're at 33 through 53 now, same number. Does that bother you, or is it just noise?

Marty Turco

It's, look, I don't think it's noise. I think, yeah, the 33 and 18, that's, I've got to be better than that. You know? 33 wins is good but with the team we have in front of me, 162 goals against all year, I should have more. I want to be back at the 45 I had last year, not the 33 I'm at now. That's, that's the number I'm looking at. Not what some guy in the press box says about my consistency. I know what I can do and tonight wasn't, I mean, the first goal I'm not happy with. But I'll take the two points.

Jan Kowalczyk

One more, Marty. You're going into the last stretch with a defence that's allowed the fewest in the league. 162 in 65. What's it like back there, watching that group every night?

Marty Turco

You just, you breathe, you know. Zubov and Sergei, I mean , the way he reads the play, I don't have to move as much. Robidas, he's been playing the best hockey of his career. When the defence is that good you can just square up, track the puck, make the simple save and get off the ice. And you know, I think that's why our numbers are what they are. It's not just me back there. It's all five guys in front of me doing the right thing. You take the credit, sure, but you know it's a team thing.

Jan Kowalczyk

Last one. What's coming, and are you ready for it?

Marty Turco

The last 17, I don't want to talk about the standings. I want to talk about the next game. That's the only way this thing works, you get to the next game and you win the next game and in April you're playing the right way. We'll see what happens in the second week of April. That's the plan. Thanks, Jan.
