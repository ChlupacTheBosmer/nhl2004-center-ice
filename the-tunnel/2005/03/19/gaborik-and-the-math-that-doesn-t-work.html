Gaborik and the math that doesn't work

Toronto won 5-4 on twenty shots against thirty; Gaborik explains how the coach bent the game to fit them.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelSaturday, 19 March 2005

2:28 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Marian Gaborik99 scored once and added an assist as Toronto Maple Leafs beat New York Islanders 5-4 on 2005-03-18, their 8th straight win. Toronto managed just 20 shots against 30 for the Islanders and still put five past the net. Gaborik sits at 48 goals, 38 assists, 86 points in 73 games.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here in the corridor at the Air Canada Centre with Marian Gaborik. You just won your 8th straight, 5-4, and here's the thing that doesn't add up: you had 20 shots, they had 30. How do you win a hockey game when the other team has ten more shots than you?

Marian Gaborik

Well, Ed was good, you know? He made 26 saves and the guys in front of him, they blocked shots. And we, we scored when we had them. Five goals on 20, that is, how you say, efficient. We did not let them get the second one on us too many times.

Jan Kowalczyk

Ed Belfour was sharp, no question. But I want to ask about something else. Gary Roberts had three assists tonight. Three. Mats Sundin, two points. You had two points. Barret Jackman, two points. It looked like everyone touched the puck tonight, and I'm wondering, did the coach change something? A line, a pairing, something before this game?

Marian Gaborik

Yeah, he moved Gary to the second unit with Brad, and that opened things up for me, you know? Gary, he wants the puck in the slot, always, and when Brad has him there, the defense, they cannot cover both. I just, I skate to the back door and, yeah. It is easier when the game is open like that.

Jan Kowalczyk

And defensively? You gave up four goals, thirty shots. Was that a change in how the pairing sits back, or was it just a night where the puck went their way a few times?

Marian Gaborik

The pairing, they play the same. But we lost some, I mean, we lost some puck battles in the corner in the second period and they get, they get two quick ones and you know how that is, you think maybe it is going to swing. But then Bryan scored, McCabe, and it settled. We just need, we need to be better on the board. That is the one thing.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let me take you to special teams. They scored four, you scored five, and the crowd was electric at the end. Was that a special teams game tonight, or was it all five-on-five?

Marian Gaborik

It was, it was mostly five-on-five. They had a couple looks, you know, and we cleared. We did not give them much on the, on the man advantage. That is what the coach wants before the playoffs. He says, special teams win the series, not the first line. So we practice it every day now, the penalty kill. Every day.

Jan Kowalczyk

One last thing. Back on December 7th I asked you about your twelve goals in a row and you gave me nothing. You told me it was about winning. You're at 48 goals now, 86 points, you're sitting at a 97 overall on the Book. Does it feel different now than it did in December?

Marian Gaborik

You remember that? No. No, I feel the same. I feel good because we win tonight. The goals, they come when the team plays right, you know? If we lose tonight I am not sitting here talking about 48. I am talking about what we did wrong. But we won, so, yeah, it is a good night. Back to you.
