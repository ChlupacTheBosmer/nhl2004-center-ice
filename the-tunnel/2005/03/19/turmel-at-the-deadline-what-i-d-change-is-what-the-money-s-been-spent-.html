Turmel at the deadline: 'What I'd change is what the money's been spent on'

The Rangers' general manager answers for $78.14M returning 76 points, an empty-feeling building and the model club down the turnpike.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelSaturday, 19 March 2005

3:29 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Garrett Turmel has run the New York Rangers since the record opened, and the record has him at 76 points on a payroll of $78.14M, the second-largest in the league and the most expensive per point at $1.03M. On the day the calendar has as the trade deadline, he sat with Jan Kowalczyk and said the money was never the part he would change.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk, in the studio, with Garrett Turmel, the general manager of the New York Rangers. Garrett, 74 games in, 76 points, six losses in a row, and what the calendar has as the trade deadline. What does today look like from your chair?

Garrett Turmel

Long. That's what it looks like. The deadline isn't one day, it's the last day of a month of phone calls, and most of the calls don't get you anything. You're just, you're checking on the same names you checked on in February.

Jan Kowalczyk

Your payroll is $78.14M, second in the league behind Detroit. Your cost per point, $1.03M, is the highest in the league. When people say the money is the problem at the Garden, what do you tell them?

Garrett Turmel

The money isn't the problem. We spend. One club in this league spends more than we do. So we're at $78.14M and 76 points, and if that's the number people want to hang on me, hang it. The checkbook is not the part I'd change.

Jan Kowalczyk

Then let me ask it the way you'd answer it. If the money were there, what would you change?

Garrett Turmel

The money's there. That's the part I want people to hear. The money has been there. What I'd change is what it's been spent on. You can put $78.14M on the roster and still leave the room a man walks into every morning at the bottom of the league's ledger.

Jan Kowalczyk

You mean the building. The league's ledger keeps your practice rink at the floor and the travel line near the bottom, and the record shows nothing paid into either of them.

Garrett Turmel

The travel line is the one that gets me. You fly to Vancouver on bad sleep and you play a team that's rested, and nobody in the paper writes about the flight. I have looked at that line. I keep looking at it. Whether it's the number I move this summer, we'll see. That's a different check than a forward.

Jan Kowalczyk

Across the river, New Jersey. Marty Rampton's club: $44.98M payroll, 101 points, $40.19M in profit, 17265 a night at the same $80 you charge. You spend $78.14M for 76 points and they spend $44.98M for 101. What do you make of how New Jersey does business?

Garrett Turmel

What do I make of it? I make that they're the cleanest number in the league and everybody in my chair knows it. 101 points on $44.98M. There's no argument to make. And they draw, that's the thing. 17265 a night. We're at 14708 at the same price. So it isn't the market, Jan.

Jan Kowalczyk

Could you run New York that way? Patient, cheap, draft and wait?

Garrett Turmel

I don't think it's cheap. I think it's a decision about where the money goes and then the discipline to hold to it. That's the part I'd take from them. Not the payroll. The discipline. I've had the money. I haven't always had the discipline.

Jan Kowalczyk

You've got Lindros out with a concussion, back the 13th of April. Leetch, shoulder, the 10th. Carter, abdomen, the 24th. Does that change what you do in the next few hours?

Garrett Turmel

It changes what I can sell. You want to move a body, you need bodies, and three of mine are in the training room. So no, I'm not moving the roster to plug a hole. Different problem.

Jan Kowalczyk

So what does New York do before the phones stop tonight?

Garrett Turmel

We'll see. I'm not going to sit here and tell you we've got something done, because we don't. If it makes us harder to play against, I can make that call tonight. If it doesn't, I'd rather keep the pick and put the money where I just told you it should go.

Jan Kowalczyk

Last thing. You said the checkbook isn't the part you'd change. If you got the piece you want, what does it look like?

Garrett Turmel

A defenseman who can take a shift in the third period of a game we're losing. That's the whole answer. Everybody in this league wants the same man.

Jan Kowalczyk

Garrett, thank you. Back to you.

Garrett Turmel

Thank you, Jan.
