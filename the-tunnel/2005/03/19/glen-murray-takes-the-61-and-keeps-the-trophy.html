Glen Murray takes the 61 and keeps the trophy

The Bruins' captain sat down with Marcus Bell to talk about coming home, a potential grade he finds funny, and playing with Joe Thornton at 32.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelSaturday, 19 March 2005

5:10 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Glen Murray77 came back to Boston Bruins in a trade in October 2001, was traded away and traded back again in his career, and now carries the C at 32 with zero years left on his contract. The Bureau's Book has his potential grade at 61 and his base at 88. His morale grade is 95. He sat down with Marcus Bell in the studio after practice.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here with Glen Murray. Glen, thanks for sitting down with us tonight. Let's start with the arm. You were out until the 15th of January, came back, and now you're 38 games in with 54 points. How is it holding up?

Glen Murray

Yeah, it's good, Marcus. You know, the first week back, maybe the first five, six games, I was checking myself on the board battles. Just, you know, making sure the hit wasn't going to be the thing. And then it's just gone. You get in a game and you don't think about it anymore. So yeah, no worries there.

Marcus Bell

The room around you looks different than last year's. Joe Thornton at center, Sergei Samsonov up there with 111 points through 63 games. What's it like sharing a line with those two when they're rolling?

Glen Murray

It's fun, yeah. You know, Sami's playing the kind of year where everything goes in, everything goes off a stick and lands somewhere good. And Joe is... Joe's a different animal. The size of the guy, the hands, the way he holds onto a puck in a crowd and still finds a seam. I don't think I've seen anybody quite like that. So yeah, I like what we've got.

Marcus Bell

Let's go back a bit. You were traded back to Boston in October of 2001, from Los Angeles. You'd spent five years with the Kings. You were drafted here originally, '91, 18th overall. Do you remember the moment you found out?

Glen Murray

I do, actually. I was in the kitchen, I think. My wife was on the phone, and she handed it over and said, 'You're going to Boston.' And I just remember thinking, well, I guess I'm home. I'd been gone a while. And L.A. was good to me, don't get me wrong. Five years, some good stretches. But you were drafted here, you signed your first deal here, you played your first NHL game here. So yeah. It felt right going back. Even though you're, what, twenty-eight years old and you tell yourself home is wherever you sleep. But it felt right.

Marcus Bell

You signed that two-year contract in July 2002. And you're in the last year of that now. Zero years left. What goes through your head when you think about that?

Glen Murray

I think about it. Sure. You'd have to be lying to say you don't. But I try to keep it, you know, I try to keep it to one hour a day. And the rest of the time I'm just worrying about the next shift, the next game, who we're playing tonight. The contract side is agents and meetings and all that stuff. I've done it before, I'll do it again. I just want to play, Marcus. I've been doing this a long time and I still want to be out there.

Marcus Bell

I want to bring up something from the Bureau's Book. Your potential grade is 61. Your base is 88. You're sitting at 86 right now. That's 27 grades above what the model said your ceiling was, and you're 32. What do you make of that number?

Glen Murray

You know what I make of it? I like the trophy better. No, look, I've been in this league thirteen years. I've been written off more times than I can count. Too small for the next system, too slow for the next coach, too injury-prone for the next GM. You hear it every year, in every market, from every direction. So 61, that's just another number somebody put on a piece of paper. I had 168 points last season. 68 goals, 100 assists. Won the Art Ross. Won the Byng. I'm at 86 now and I feel good. So if the answer's 61, that's fine. I'll take 61 and the hardware.

Marcus Bell

Does it sting at all? Some guys would bristle at that.

Glen Murray

Nah. No. Honestly? If anything it makes me laugh. I remember when I was a kid in Halifax, nobody was giving me grades at all. You didn't know if you were going to make it. You just went out and tried to be better than you were yesterday. So you get to 32, you're the captain of your hometown team, you won a scoring title, and somebody's telling me I peaked at 61. Works for me, buddy. Works for me.

Marcus Bell

You wear the C here. What does that letter mean to you, three and a half years in?

Glen Murray

It means I'm the first one in the room and the last one out, most nights. It means when things go sideways, and they do, guys look at you. Not for answers, necessarily. Just, you know, to see if you've got something in the tank or if you're rattled. And you can't be rattled. Which is harder than it sounds. But it's an honour, Marcus. You don't get many chances to wear the C for your hometown club. I'm not taking it for granted.

Marcus Bell

You mentioned Thornton a minute ago. I'll ask it straight: is Joe the best player you've played with in your career?

Glen Murray

That's... that's a hard one because you've got to think about everyone. But yeah, I think so. I think Joe's the best. The hands, the size, the vision, the fact that he plays that hard for a guy his size. In practice, you watch him and you think, okay, he's in another world right now. And he's young, too, so he's still, you know, he's still figuring out how good he can be. That makes it better for me, playing with him now. But I've been lucky. I've been on some good teams.

Marcus Bell

Halifax, Nova Scotia. You grew up playing there. Does the building feel different to you than it would to a guy who grew up in, say, Alberta or somewhere else?

Glen Murray

Yeah. Yeah, it does. You know, it's not a hockey hotbed. Not like, you know, some places. But we played. We played on ponds and in arenas that smelled like a wet dog and that was fine. And then you come here, you put the Bruins jersey on, and you hear this building, and you remember being a kid in front of the TV watching these guys. It's not nothing. Most guys don't get to do that. Play where you grew up, with the C. So no. It's not nothing.

Marcus Bell

You've got 38 games, 54 points, you're coming back from the arm, you've got no contract for next year, and you're the captain of a team with Thornton, Samsonov, and Rolston. Where do you think this thing goes?

Glen Murray

I think we've got something, Marcus. I do. You know, I'm not the kid anymore who's thinking about his own numbers. I'm thinking about winning a round. Maybe two. And I think this group can do that. Joe's 21 years old and he's going to be better next year. Sami's rolling. The goalie situation is, well, it is what it is, but Andrew's been fine. I just want to get in, get a spot, and see what happens in April. That's all I care about right now.

Marcus Bell

Glen Murray, thank you very much for your time tonight.

Glen Murray

Thank you, Marcus. Good to talk.

Marcus Bell

Back to you.
