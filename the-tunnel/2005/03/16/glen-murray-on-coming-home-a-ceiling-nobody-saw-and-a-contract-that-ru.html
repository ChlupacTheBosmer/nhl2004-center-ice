Glen Murray on coming home, a ceiling nobody saw, and a contract that runs out

The Bruins' right wing talks about five quiet years in Los Angeles, a trade back to Boston that turned him into a scoring champion, and what he does with the fact that people wrote his ceiling at 61 when his base is 88.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelWednesday, 16 March 2005

4:36 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Glen Murray85 sat down with Marcus Bell in the studio after a week in which Boston Bruins tried to find the form that carried them to 168 points last season. He is 32, on an expiring $3.70M contract, and back from an arm injury that kept him out until January 15. This season he has 53 points in 37 games.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Glen, good to have you. Marcus Bell here from The Tunnel. You're 37 games in this year, and we'll get to that, but I want to start on the arm. You missed the first two months, came back January 15, and you've looked like yourself since.

Glen Murray

Yeah, well, the first couple weeks were, you know, you're just trying to get your legs back. You're not thinking hockey, you're thinking, is it gonna hold, is it gonna hurt when I go up the wall. And once it didn't, you know, you just play. I felt good by the second week back. Obviously the timing's, it's not ideal, you miss the chemistry stuff early, but, I'm here now.

Marcus Bell

And you're at 53 points in 37. That's a pace of about 117 over a full season. Nobody in the league is putting up the kind of numbers you put up last year. But that pace, for a man who missed half the year, how do you feel about it?

Glen Murray

I mean, I don't sit at home and do the math, you know. I don't, I look at the, what we need tonight, who's open, where's the puck going. If I sat down and figured out where I'd be at 82 I'd, I'd be doing something else with my afternoon. It's not a good use of time.

Marcus Bell

Let me ask you about something a little uncomfortable. The Bureau's Development Index has your base grade at 88. Your potential, the ceiling they put on you when you were coming up, is 61. You are 27 grades above what people projected. Does that bother you, or does it amuse you?

Glen Murray

You know what, I've had people write me off since I was, since I was a kid in Halifax. First round, sure, 18th overall, but I wasn't the guy everybody was talking about. I wasn't the one they built the poster around. So at 32, someone tells me I'm past my ceiling, I just, yeah, that's funny. That's actually, yeah. That's funny.

Marcus Bell

Let me take you back. Five years in Los Angeles. You went there as a young player and you came back here in October 2001. What were those years?

Glen Murray

Well, I mean, you grow up. You get your body into, into shape, you learn the league. But you know, the word that gets used is, moderate. Some good numbers. And I don't know what they were expecting from me there, I don't know if they ever, if the plan was to build around me, or if I was, you know, a piece. I don't think I was ever the guy. And that's fine, that's the way it was.

Marcus Bell

And then October 24, 2001, you're traded back to Boston with Jozef Stümpel for Jason Allison and Mikko Eloranta. From your side, what was that moment?

Glen Murray

You know, the phone call, you're always half expecting it. You get traded once, you know the league is, it's a business, you don't get, you don't get precious about it. But when they told me it was Boston, and I thought, I'd been drafted here. I was the first pick they took. So yeah, I think, I think something clicked in my head. Like, this is where it started. Let me, let me finish something.

Marcus Bell

And what was different about the room you walked into? You came back to a young team, and the way you played was different from what you'd been doing in LA.

Glen Murray

It was. That's, that's the big one, right. In LA I was a winger who, you know, I'd put up points, I'd play the right side, I wasn't asked to carry a, to carry much. Here, the first thing the bench said to me, and I mean the first meeting, was, we want you on the wall. We want you driving the net. We want you to use what you've got, the size, the shot. And I'd been, I'd been playing a, a softer game. A perimeter game. And someone said, no, you're 225 pounds, you can, you can make people pay. And I thought, nobody ever said that to me before.

Marcus Bell

And you answered. 92 points the first full year back, 168 last season. Art Ross, Lady Byng. Was it just the role change, or was there something else?

Glen Murray

Yeah, it was the linemates. You put me with Joey, with Joe, and, you know, the guy sees plays before they happen. You don't have to, you don't have to think about what he's going to do, you just go to the net and the puck finds you. And Sergei's on the other side. You've got three guys who all want to score and, and can. That's, that's rare. I was lucky. I got to play with two guys who, who made me look like more than I am.

Marcus Bell

Joe Thornton's at 85, Samsonov at 81. You're the captain, you've got the highest overall grade in that room at 79, well, wait, you're at 79 this year.

Glen Murray

Yeah. That, that stings a little. You're 32, you won the Art Ross last year, and your number's below your linemates. But I know, I know what the Index looks at. It's, it's the moment. And my moment is, I was 88 last year. I know what I am. The grade'll, it'll come back, or it won't. I don't lose sleep over it.

Marcus Bell

Your contract runs out. $3.70M, no years left. You're 32, morale 95 on the Index, which tells me you're happy in that room. What's in your head about the next step?

Glen Murray

You know, I've been around long enough to know that, that, you can't, you can't worry about the pen. I mean, I like it here. I like the guys, I like the city. My daughter's in school here. The kid's, she's happy. And I told my agent, I said, don't, don't make me do anything I don't want to do. Let, let the season finish, let's, let's see what the summer brings. I'm not going to sit there and, and force something.

Marcus Bell

One last thing. You said you were the first pick they ever took in Boston, 1991, 18th overall. You've been back here three and a half seasons now. How do you feel about being the guy who came home?

Glen Murray

You know what, I don't, I don't think about it like that. I don't think of it as, as a story. I just think of it as, I'm playing hockey in a building that, that I know. The fans know my name, they've known it a long time. And at 32, that's, that's something. A lot of guys never get that. You bounce around four, five cities and you leave a bunch of half-remembered shifts behind. I got to come back to the one that mattered. So yeah, I'm proud of that.

Marcus Bell

Glen Murray, thank you very much. Back to you.
