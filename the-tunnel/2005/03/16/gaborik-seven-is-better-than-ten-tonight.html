Gaborik: 'Seven is better than ten tonight'

With three regulars out and Ed Belfour in the crease, the Maple Leafs' winger talks through the coaching changes behind a 7-game win streak.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelWednesday, 16 March 2005

2:13 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Marian Gaborik98 logged no points in 20.3 minutes and his Toronto Maple Leafs squad won 3-1 at Vancouver Canucks on 17 shots anyway. The lineup was missing Mats Sundin91, Brad Richards87, and Gary Roberts88, and the crease belonged to Ed Belfour93. Gaborik sat down at the glass afterward, still catching his breath, 7-game win streak and 116 points behind him.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here with Marian Gaborik. Seven straight now, 116 points in 72 games, and you only manage 17 shots on the road. What was the message before the puck dropped?

Marian Gaborik

Play our game. Don't chase them. You know, VAN at home, they push, they get chances. So coach said, we use ours. And tonight three different guys score. That's the way we want it.

Jan Kowalczyk

Ed Belfour in the crease. Roberto Luongo has been your guy most nights. That's a coaching change, isn't it?

Marian Gaborik

Yeah. It's, uh... Ed is calm. You know? Roberto, he's electric. He feeds off it, the crowd gets him going. Great. Tremendous all year. But tonight, 27 against, third period, one-goal lead, Vancouver is on their feet... you look back and Ed is just, how you say, steady. He takes the electricity off the other side. You feel it from the blue line.

Jan Kowalczyk

So the logic is, a calm backstop for a game you're protecting.

Marian Gaborik

I don't tell the coach his job. But yeah. We had the calm one when we needed the calm one. And Ed was there. Twenty-seven shots, one goal. That says enough.

Jan Kowalczyk

I look at the sheet tonight and Mats Sundin isn't there. Neither is Brad Richards. Neither is Gary Roberts. That's three names carrying your top minutes. Who fills that?

Marian Gaborik

When you lose guys like that, everybody shifts. The young guys get more ice, more minutes. And they don't try to be Mats Sundin. They just play. That's the thing. You can't be somebody else out there. And tonight, the defenseman puts one in, a young center scores, Mogilny's got two points. Three different lines. That tells you the room is right.

Jan Kowalczyk

The last time you and I talked, back in January, you said nobody in that room cares about your goal total after a loss. Tonight you're zero again but you win. Does the quiet feel different when the wins keep stacking?

Marian Gaborik

Yeah. Yeah, it does. When we lose and you're zero, zero, zero, you sit on the bus and you think, where was I tonight. But when the puck goes around and the kids are scoring and Mogilny is carrying... I don't care. I want to score every night, you know that. But seven is better than ten tonight.

Jan Kowalczyk

One last one. When Mats and Brad and Gary come back, how does this thing reshape? You've been playing without them.

Marian Gaborik

I don't know, you know? The coach decides the lines. I play where he puts me. I just want the puck when I'm out there. That's all. Back to you, Jan.

Jan Kowalczyk

Thank you, Marian. Jan Kowalczyk, on the boards in Vancouver.
