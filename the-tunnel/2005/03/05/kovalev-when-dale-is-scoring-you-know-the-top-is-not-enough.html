Kovalev: "When Dale is scoring, you know the top is not enough"

The Rangers' alternate captain talked about Thursday's 3-4 overtime loss to the Islanders and what a defenseman's goal says about his own line.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelSaturday, 5 March 2005

2:53 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Alexei Kovalev88 had 1 goal and 2 points as New York Rangers fell 3-4 in overtime to the Islanders at Madison Square Garden on Thursday. The Rangers sit at 26-27-8, 76 points, in the Atlantic Division.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here, rinkside at the Garden. Alexei, you score, you pick up two points on the night, but the Rangers drop this one 3-4 in overtime to the Islanders. What's the feeling right now?

Alexei Kovalev

The feeling is... bad, Marcus. You score, okay, it is good, but what is good? We lose. The game is in our hands, the third period we are there, and overtime. In overtime you cannot make the mistake. We make it. That is everything, the one mistake, and it is over.

Marcus Bell

Take me to your goal. That brings you to 85 points on the season. What did you see?

Alexei Kovalev

The puck comes to me. I have one moment. Two men there, maybe three. So I think, shoot. I shoot. It is through. It is a good feeling, of course, the goal. But I look at the board, we are still one behind. So the feeling, it goes fast. Two points, forty-four goals, it is paper numbers when we lose in overtime.

Marcus Bell

The Islanders outshot you 27 to 23 tonight. You're 26-27-8, 76 points, 13 behind New Jersey in the Atlantic. You've got the skill on this roster. What separates this team from taking the next step?

Alexei Kovalev

We play sixty minutes, sometimes we do. Sometimes we don't. First period, third period, we are there. But the middle, the second period, we are not there. You cannot win in this league when you sleep for twenty minutes. We know this. The coach knows. But the legs, the focus, it goes. And you lose the game. So the skill is there, yes, but sixty minutes is the thing that matters.

Marcus Bell

Dale Purinton scores a goal tonight. A defenseman. Kasparaitis gets a point too. When your blue line is putting the puck in the net, what does that tell you about your group?

Alexei Kovalev

Dale, the goal. I see it, I look at the bench, everybody is laughing. We are happy for him, of course. Dale, he is the workman, he blocks, he hits, he is the glue. But when Dale scores, you know the top is not enough. You know what I mean? Kasparaitis, the same. These men should be doing the work, the blocking, not worrying about points. When they have to score, that means we up front are not giving enough. So it says something.

Marcus Bell

You've got Bure, Nedved, Leetch, Carter. That's a room with skill. Is the problem system, is it the way they play together, or is it just getting every man on the same page?

Alexei Kovalev

The skill, it is there. Bure, Petr, Brian, Anson, of course. The system, we have it. The men, they want. So what is the problem? The legs are tired and the mind goes. It is not that Bure is not good. It is not the coach. It is twenty minutes where we are a different team. And that, is important to fix. But it is not easy, Marcus. If it was easy, we would be fifty and ten, not twenty-six and twenty-seven.

Marcus Bell

You're 32, 19.3 minutes a night. You had 46 goals in 61 games last year, 93 points. This year 44, 85. Are you where you want to be?

Alexei Kovalev

I want fifty goals. The rest, it is the rest. The minutes are less, the coach decides this, I accept. I am 32, the legs are good, the head is good. But I must do more on the ice. When Dale is scoring the goals for us, it means the top line is not enough. This team needs more from me. That is important. I know it.

Marcus Bell

Alexei, appreciate your time. Back to you.

Alexei Kovalev

Thank you, Marcus.
