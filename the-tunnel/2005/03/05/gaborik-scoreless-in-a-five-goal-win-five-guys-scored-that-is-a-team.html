Gaborik, scoreless in a five-goal win: five guys scored, that is a team

The 23-year-old winger logged zero points on the road at Ottawa and said that outcome, five different scorers in a 5-1 win, is the best sign of the season.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelSaturday, 5 March 2005

3:14 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Marian Gaborik99 and the Toronto Maple Leafs Maple Leafs beat Ottawa 5-1 on Thursday, their second straight win, and they lead the Northeast with 106 points, 28 clear of Boston. Gaborik logged zero points on the night, the rare quiet shift from a man the Bureau's Development Index has lifted six spots to 96 overall this season. Jan Kowalczyk caught him in the dressing room after the game.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here in the Ottawa dressing room with Marian Gaborik, number ten. Forty-six wins, twelve losses, 106 points, Marian. You're 28 ahead of Boston with all these games still to play. What does it feel like in there, this room, when it's going?

Marian Gaborik

It's good, you know? First year was, uh, we were maybe finding how to play together. Everybody is, you know, good player, but not everybody know where to go. Now we know. I don't think too much, I just go and the puck, sometimes it's there. It's easy when everybody is working at the same time.

Jan Kowalczyk

Ottawa put 32 shots on goal tonight. You were outshot 32 to 25. You scored five. Ed Belfour was the last man. How do you walk away from a game like that with the win?

Marian Gaborik

Yeah, Ed was the man tonight, you know? He makes the big save, maybe two, maybe three that we don't score on. And the young guys, Rick and Rostislav, they eat the minutes, they block the pucks. 164 goals against in 67 games, that's not me, that's not Mats. That's everybody doing the, how you say, the ugly work. So 32 shots, maybe 10 are nothing, right?

Jan Kowalczyk

The Bureau has you at 96 overall now. Your base is 91, so the Index has you up six. You're 23. Is this the best hockey you've played?

Marian Gaborik

Hmm. Maybe, I don't know. Last year I score more, 77, that's more. But maybe last year I'm, uh, too much alone. I take the puck and I go and I think, this is my goal, my game. Now I pass, and Mats passes back, Brad finds the open man, and I go into space that they make for me. And when you don't guess where to go, you are faster, you know? So maybe now is better hockey even if last year the numbers look bigger.

Jan Kowalczyk

You finished tonight with zero points. Five goals from five different teammates. Rick had two points, Rostislav two, Brad had four points without a goal. Does that bother you, sitting there and not being on the board?

Marian Gaborik

No. You know why? Because five different guys tonight. Rick, Rostislav, Alexander, Stanislav, Jiri. Brad has four points and no goals. That is when you know, you know, that is when you know a team is a real team. If I score zero and five guys score and we win by four, I take that. I take that every single night. You cannot game-plan against five names, you know?

Jan Kowalczyk

Back in January, after the Boston loss, you told me nobody in this room cares about your goal total after a bad one. You said the silence is the problem. Tonight you don't get a point and you're smiling at me. So the silence changed?

Marian Gaborik

Yeah. The silence is the problem, not the zero. When I lose and I have nothing, the bus ride, the quiet in there, that's, uh, very long. But tonight, five goals, the boys are loud, someone's playing guitar maybe, I don't even know who, so the zero is fine. I can have a zero on a good night. A zero on a bad night, that's a different thing.

Jan Kowalczyk

You've got 106 points, you're the defending Cup champions, and you're healthy, you've got no injuries. When April gets here, what does this team do differently from the one that barely got in last spring?

Marian Gaborik

We can play four ways now. Last year maybe we're one way, we run and we hope the speed is enough. Now we can play tight, we can play open, we can beat you with the first line, we can beat you with the young guys. And the young guys are, you know, 21 men are 24 or under. That's, how you say, that's legs. So many legs. When it's late in April and everybody is tired, maybe we are not tired. Maybe that's the thing.

Jan Kowalczyk

Marian Gaborik, thank you for the time. Enjoy the bus home. Jan Kowalczyk, back to you.
