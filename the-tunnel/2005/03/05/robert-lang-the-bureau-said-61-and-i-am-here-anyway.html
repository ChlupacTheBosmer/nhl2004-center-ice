Robert Lang: The Bureau Said 61, and I Am Here Anyway

The Capitals' 34-year-old center, drafted 133th overall in 1990, has put up 82 points in 60 games this season and has a wry answer for the grade that once called him a career .500 player.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelSaturday, 5 March 2005

5:18 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Robert Lang has been with Washington Capitals for three seasons now, long enough that nobody asks him about the road he walked to get here. The Sweater's profile this week called him 23 grades past the ceiling the League Scouting Bureau set for him as a 133rd pick in 1990, and Jan Kowalczyk sat with him to find out what a man does with a number that said he would not be good.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here with Robert Lang of the Washington Capitals. Robert, thanks for coming in. I want to start with something. The Sweater's piece this week called you 23 grades past your ceiling. Your potential was a 61, you're sitting at an 80 overall right now. The Bureau looked at a 133rd pick out of Teplice in 1990 and said: this man will top out around a fourth-liner. You're at 82 points in 60 games. Did you know that number was on you?

Robert Lang

Well. I know now, you know. I did not think about it. When you are young, you are just playing, you do not know what number the men in the office put on your head. And I was 20 when I was drafted, so. What do I care about a 61? I did not even know what a 61 means then. I just knew I wanted to play here.

Jan Kowalczyk

So it never stung? No one said to you at any point, you're a project, you're not going to be great?

Robert Lang

Well, when you go back to Europe you know, twice. When you play in the Extraliga while your friends are in the League, people talk. But that is, how you say, the quiet sting. You go back to Litvínov, you play there, and you say okay, I will come back. Nobody said to me face to face, you are a 61. But I think about it now and I say, good, I showed them something.

Jan Kowalczyk

You were at Pittsburgh in 2000-01 when you had your career year, 80 points in 82 games. You're at 82 in 60 this year. Your ice time is up too, from 19.4 a night last year to 21.1 now. Does it feel like a different job? Are you a different player than you were at Pittsburgh five years ago?

Robert Lang

Yeah. Different. At Pittsburgh I was, you know, I was trying to prove I belong there. I just came from Europe again, Sparta Praha, and then Pittsburgh picks me up as a free agent and I am thinking every night, do not let them see you are not good enough. Now? Now I play, I find the pass. The pass is the thing, you know. I have 55 assists this year because I look for the man, I do not think about myself.

Jan Kowalczyk

And who are you looking for? You're playing with Jagr, you're playing with Bondra. You and Jagr, you came from the same country, you both played in Europe. Does that change how it feels on the ice?

Robert Lang

Yeah. With Jaromir, it is easy. We grew up in the same hockey, you know? The Czech hockey. We see the same things. I look up, I see him there, and I put it on his stick. Sometimes he does not even look at me, he just knows where I will put it. That is, you know, that is special. With Peter, he is Slovak, but, well, the Slovak way and the Czech way, it is close. We both play the game with our heads up.

Jan Kowalczyk

Now, the Development Index has you two points below your base grade right now. So the Bureau thinks that even with 82 points in 60, you have been slightly off your best self. Does that match how you feel, or do you think they're looking at the wrong thing?

Robert Lang

You know, I look at the points and I say, what do they see? I have 27 goals and 55 assists in 60 games. I play 21 minutes a night. I am here every game, no injuries. They say minus two. Maybe the goals. I had 33 last year in 56 games, and this year I have 27 in 60. So maybe that is the minus two. But the assists went up, you know. I make the play more. That is my game.

Jan Kowalczyk

Fair. Let me ask about the body. You're 34. Your grade on toughness and fighting are both a 50. Nobody has ever called you a bruiser. At 34, with those numbers, what do you do to stay out there 21 minutes a night against clubs that are built to hit you?

Robert Lang

You read the book. Okay, yes, I am not the tough one. I do not fight. I do not check into the boards. I use the puck, you know? The puck is my body, how you say. If I have the puck, nobody hits me. That is the plan since I was 18 in Litvínov. I do not need to be the guy who fights. I need to be the guy who has the puck. And I rest when I can. That is the other thing at 34.

Jan Kowalczyk

So when you do rest, when there's no game and no practice, what does a day off look like? You've been in Washington three years now. You have a place here.

Robert Lang

Yeah, yeah. I like the quiet. I wake up late, that is the first thing. When you play at night, the morning is yours. I go for a walk, sometimes with my family, sometimes alone, just the quiet. And I watch the Czech games on television, you know? The Extraliga. I watch Litvínov, I watch Sparta. It is how you say, my connection. I see the boys I grew up with, or their sons.

Jan Kowalczyk

Do you go to any Czech places around here? I've heard there's a couple spots.

Robert Lang

Yeah. I go for the dumplings, the knedlíky. My wife, she cooks at home too, but sometimes I want to just sit somewhere and not think. And I call my brother in Teplice. We talk, ten minutes, twenty minutes, just stupid things. Football, the weather. That is good. You know, when you are in the season, you forget that there is a world outside the hockey.

Jan Kowalczyk

You had that hat-trick last November, your first in the League. At 32 years old. And the Golden Hockey Stick as the top Czech player. Does any of that feel like, at 34, it's the accumulation of all those years people wrote you off?

Robert Lang

I think about it. I think about 1992, the Kings, my first year. I think about going back to Sparta when nobody here wanted me. And then the hat-trick comes and it is just, okay, it happened. I scored three goals. And the Golden Stick is nice, I like it, it is Czech. But you know what I want? I want the wins. I want the Cup. I am here because Jagr and Kolzig and Bondra are all here because we want to win. The personal things, they are nice. They are not the point.

Jan Kowalczyk

You've got three years left on a five million dollar contract. You're 34. If I said to you that the ceiling is 61, one last time, what do you say to that?

Robert Lang

I say, who cares about the number? I have 82 points and three years left and a room that can win. The Bureau, they are smart men, they make their book, fine. But they do not know how many times I got up in Litvínov at five in the morning. They do not know the road. So I play my game and let them grade me however they want.

Jan Kowalczyk

Robert Lang, thank you for your time. He's with the Washington Capitals and he is, by every measure that the man himself would care about, well past his ceiling. Back to you.
