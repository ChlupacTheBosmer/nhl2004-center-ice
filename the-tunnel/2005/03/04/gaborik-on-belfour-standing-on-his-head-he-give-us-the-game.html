Gaborik on Belfour standing on his head: 'He give us the game'

Toronto's top winger talks about the goalie filling in, a quiet night at the plate, and why 31 shots against felt like fewer.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelFriday, 4 March 2005

2:15 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Marian Gaborik98 had no points in Toronto Maple Leafs's 3-1 win over Florida on Thursday, the second night after the trade deadline and the first without Roberto Luongo between the pipes. Ed Belfour, the other goaltender in Toronto's room, stopped 30 of 31 shots in the victory that pushed Toronto to 104 points and a one-point lead in the Presidents' Trophy race.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here at the Air Canada Centre with Marian Gaborik. Marian, three and one over Florida, your 45th win. Belfour in the net tonight. What does the room see differently when he's the guy going out first?

Marian Gaborik

Well, you see him warm up and you know, Ed, he's, he's always the same. Same routine, same stretching, same look on his face. He's been here a long time and he look like it's his first camp, you know what I mean. So the room, we don't think different. We just play.

Marcus Bell

Thirty-one shots from Florida tonight, the most you've faced in a game this week. He stops thirty. Did it feel like thirty-one or did it feel like fewer?

Marian Gaborik

Fewer, yeah. Because when you look back and he's making the second save, the rebound save, you, how you say, it make you relax a little. I see him, the third period, Florida get one good look, maybe two, and he just, yeah, the glove is there. You think, okay, this is fine, I can play my game.

Marcus Bell

You're not talking about Roberto at all tonight. He's not in the lineup, not behind the bench as backup. How does the room handle that, the guy who's been your number one all season just gone?

Marian Gaborik

We, I mean, Roberto is, he's our guy. Everybody know that. You talk to him every day, you see him in the hallway, he's fine, he's positive. But you can't play hockey for a guy who's not out there. So tonight, it's Ed, and we play for Ed. That's just how it is.

Marcus Bell

You yourself, quiet night. No points. Eighty-one on the season and you finish the evening at zero. Does that sit with you after a win?

Marian Gaborik

You know, tonight no. Tonight I go home, I sleep good. Last week after the loss, no, then I can't sleep. But tonight, we win, three and one, and somebody score, Chistov, Kaberle, Fischer, it's all good. I don't need my name on the board every night.

Marcus Bell

Fischer with his first goal of the season tonight. The room must have lit up for that.

Marian Gaborik

Oh yeah, big time. Big time. He's, he's a guy that work, work, work, every day, and sometimes the, how you say, the puck just don't come to him. So when it did, everybody happy. Even me, I'm happy and I don't even know him that well yet, you know? Yeah, we all go to him.

Marcus Bell

One point ahead of Dallas for the Presidents' Trophy, sixty-six games in. You said last time you don't want to talk standings. Still the case tonight?

Marian Gaborik

Marcus, we just won three and one. Tonight I talk standings if you want. But, you know, tomorrow is different. Tomorrow we practice, and then it's next game. The Presidents' Trophy is nice, is good for home ice, but in April nobody remember September. Everybody remember June.

Marcus Bell

Marian Gaborik, thank you again. Back to you at the desk.
