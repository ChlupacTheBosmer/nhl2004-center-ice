Hartley: 'The guys behind the first two lines, they are carrying a weight right now'

After a 3-0 shutout at Anaheim, the Thrashers coach talked about a room that has lost five men in two weeks and the men stepping into those shifts.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelFriday, 4 March 2005

4:15 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Atlanta Thrashers beat Mighty Ducks of Anaheim 3-0 on the road on 2005-03-02, their second straight win, but the coach walked off the ice with more than two wins on his mind. Five Thrashers are on injured reserve, including Sami Kapanen76, signed on 2005-02-28 and hurt before he played a shift in Atlanta. Head coach Bob Hartley spoke to The Tunnel in the corridor at Honda Center.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here, rinkside at Honda Center, with Thrashers head coach Bob Hartley. Coach, 3-0 on the road, 25 shots for you, 17 against, you get a shutout from Byron Dafoe. What was the mood in that room after?

Bob Hartley

Look, it's good to win. 3-0 on the road, the compete level was there all three periods, we managed the puck the way we needed to in the first and third especially. But the mood, you know, it's cautious right now. We've got five guys on the IR. You don't walk into that room and pop the champagne, even after a shutout. The guys know what's missing.

Marcus Bell

You signed Sami Kapanen on Sunday and he was hurt before he took a shift. That's got to be tough.

Bob Hartley

Yeah, obviously. We signed him, we thought he gives us a top-nine winger, someone who's put up 40 points this year in a top-six role. And now I'm looking at the lineup and he's out until the eleventh at best. And Majesky's out, the foot's a hip actually, back on the eighth. So two guys we bring in and they go straight to the trainer's room. That's the way it goes some weeks. You keep working, you don't feel sorry for yourself.

Marcus Bell

Who steps in tonight? I saw Daniel Tjarnqvist in the second pairing, 11 games on the year.

Bob Hartley

Yeah, Tjarnqvist got the nod, he's earned it in practice. And look, I've got a group that has to understand the roles are going to be bigger. Brule is playing 25, 26 minutes now. Kaberle is up in that second pairing, P1 on the power play. That's the detail. I can't get the minutes back from Sutton, from Stefan, I've got to redistribute them. And the guys behind Kovalchuk and Heatley, Savard and V.Kozlov, they've got to give me more even strength offense because the top two lines are going to get doubled anyway.

Marcus Bell

Ilya Kovalchuk has 110 points, Heatley has 102, that's your top two. Do you ever worry about that balance, with five guys hurt behind them?

Bob Hartley

Well, look, I worry about it every single day. I mean it. You look at our third line tonight, it was Tapper, Shantz, Roest or Arvedson depending on the matchup. That's not where I want that line to be against a team like Anaheim when they're trying to get out of a slump. But the compete level, those guys gave me what I can ask for. They didn't create, but they didn't give up a scoring chance either, and the details on our game tonight were good. I just need a little more from those guys over a 30-game stretch.

Marcus Bell

Dafoe has been your man all year, 55 starts. Do you talk to him about the workload, with Nurminen not in the mix and the season stretching out?

Bob Hartley

Byron's been our guy, yeah. 55 starts, 27 wins, three shutouts. He's 34 and he plays like he's got something to prove. You know what, I don't think you protect a guy like that from games he wants. He's been here, he's been in the building all year, the room trusts him. We'll watch it, I've got the kid Nurminen and Hnilicka behind him, but right now Byron is the man. He's earned it.

Marcus Bell

You've got WSH on the road Saturday, then Florida here, Minnesota here, then New Jersey. That's not a soft stretch.

Bob Hartley

No, it's not soft. Look, we're in a fight for a wild card. Washington's 83, we're 68, but that doesn't matter to me on Saturday. What matters is can I roll four lines and have all four lines give me the same effort. If I can do that, we have a chance against anybody in this league. If I lose one of my bottom pairs to another injury, well, that's a different conversation. But I'm not looking at the standings right now. I'm looking at that room and who's available Monday morning.

Marcus Bell

Patrik Stefan back March 14th. Andy Sutton not until April 12th. How do you manage the middle of February when the roster gets that thin?

Bob Hartley

You call people up. You put the kids in. We've got a room right now where guys are playing positions they haven't played. Clarke Wilm, Jeff Cowan, Andre Roy, they're getting 12, 13 minutes because the men ahead of them are hurt. They're not going to put up 30 points. I know that. But they can give me penalty killing, they can give me forechecking, they can give me the dirty areas. And right now that's what I need because the skill is up top with Kovalchuk, Heatley, Savard, and I'm not going to pretend the third and fourth lines can create against a good defensive team. So they have to win me the puck in the corners and let the top guys play.

Marcus Bell

One more thing, coach. You took this team in January 2003. Two full seasons now. What's the difference in the room between the man who walked in that first week and the man tonight?

Bob Hartley

The difference is I know who I've got. In January of '03 I was walking around the room going, this guy can play, that guy can't, this line can work, that one won't. I'm still learning with a few, obviously. But right now? I know what Kovalchuk can do. I know what Heatley can do. I know what Dafoe gives me. The problem is the next twelve guys and the next twelve minutes. That's where you win or lose in this league. And I've got five of them on crutches right now.

Marcus Bell

Bob Hartley, thank you for your time, coach. Back to you.
