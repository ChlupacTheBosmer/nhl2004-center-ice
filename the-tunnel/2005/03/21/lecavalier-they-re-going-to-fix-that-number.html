Lecavalier: 'They're Going to Fix That Number'

The Lightning center, five weeks into a leg injury with an April 1 return, talks about his father, the captaincy he lost, and the contract he says he'll leave to his agent.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelMonday, 21 March 2005

3:57 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Vincent Lecavalier89 has watched Tampa Bay Lightning games from the press box for five weeks, waiting on a return date his general manager told The Tunnel might yield only three or four games. The center sat in the studio to talk about the road from Ile-Bizard to the first overall pick in 1998, and the two years left on a $2.60M deal he says he hasn't looked at.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here in the studio with Vincent Lecavalier. Vincent, five weeks you've been up in the press box now. Your GM told us he's been counting backwards, and he said the number keeps coming out wrong. What's your count?

Vincent Lecavalier

April 1. That's the count. I look at the calendar every morning, I, you know, I mark it down. My father used to do that, when I was small, for his work. You count and the day comes. So I count. But the number he gives you, three or four games, that's not, that's not what I need. But you take what you get, for sure.

Marcus Bell

The team's at 90 points without you. Martin St. Louis is putting up 100 points this year, 41 and 59, and he's 33. You're watching your linemate carry it from up there. What does that look like?

Vincent Lecavalier

It looks like Martin. He's, he doesn't talk, he just goes out and does it. Every night. And I'm up there saying, I should be down there. I should be the one he's passing to. But I'm happy, I'm happy for him. The guys are playing hard. Ninety points, that's good without me. It's just, I want to be in it.

Marcus Bell

Let's go back a ways then. You're from Ile-Bizard, a kid on the North Shore. The Quebec International Pee-Wee tournament in 1994, you were 13. And then Rimouski, the Océanic, two years. Who's the person who taught you this game?

Vincent Lecavalier

My father. He's the one. The Pee-Wee tournament, the North Shore team, my father drove the car, he waited outside, he drove back. Every time. And Rimouski, I was, I was 16 or 17 and I was on my own for the first time, and the coach there, he said, you're a number one center. You play like one or you sit. I was young. I believed him. But my father, he's the one who taught me to show up. The coach taught me the rest.

Marcus Bell

1998, first overall, Tampa Bay. And your new owner Art Williams stands up and says you're going to be the Michael Jordan of hockey. You're 18. A year later they hand you the captain's C. Then before 2001-02 they take it away and say you're too young. How does a kid from Ile-Bizard make sense of all that?

Vincent Lecavalier

You don't make sense of it. You just, you survive it. The Michael Jordan thing, I was 18. I didn't know how to, I don't know, how to buy my own groceries, my mother did everything. And they say you're the next Michael Jordan. So you say thank you, and you go to the rink. And then they give you the C and I thought, this is mine, this is what I do. And then they take it and they say you need to focus. And I didn't argue. I was, I was angry but I didn't argue. For sure I was angry.

Marcus Bell

You've been here a long time now. Martin St. Louis came to Tampa and he's in his mid-thirties. Does he still show you things? After practice, on the bench?

Vincent Lecavalier

He shows me, yeah. He's quiet, Martin. He doesn't say a lot. But he sees, he sees the play two seconds before, you know, before the puck gets there. And after practice he'll grab me and say, Vince, did you see the space? And I say no, I didn't see it. And he shows me the same thing every day until I see it. That's, that's what I get from him. He's, he's patient like that.

Marcus Bell

2004 World Cup of Hockey. Canada wins the whole thing and they name you MVP. What's that feel like, in the room?

Vincent Lecavalier

That was the best room I've been in. Mario, everybody, all the guys together for Canada. And we won and they said, you're MVP, and I just, I said, no, no, I was just trying not to embarrass myself, for sure. But it was, it was something. Playing for Canada, you don't get that every year.

Marcus Bell

You've got two years left on a $2.60M deal. You're not in a contract year. But you turned 24 in April, you had 138 last season, and the next one is going to be a big number. Does that ever run through your head?

Vincent Lecavalier

No. No, I don't, I don't think about the money. My father, he didn't teach me to count dollars. He taught me to show up. So I show up. When the time comes, my agent, he talks to the club, that's, that's his job. My job is, is the shift. The next shift. That's all I need to think about.

Marcus Bell

The Bureau's Development Index has you at 84 today. Your base is 90. And the Book, your durability, it's at 50. That's the lowest thing on your report card. The foot in 2001, now this leg. Does that get to you?

Vincent Lecavalier

It gets to me. Yeah, it gets to me. I know what the number says. Fifty. I know. The foot, now the leg. People talk. But I can't, I can't change what the body does. I can change what I do when the body is there. And I'm coming back and I'm going to play and they're going to fix that number. That's all I got. They're going to fix it.

Marcus Bell

Vincent, thank you for your time. Vincent Lecavalier in the studio, back to you.
