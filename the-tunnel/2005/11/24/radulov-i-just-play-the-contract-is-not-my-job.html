Radulov: 'I just play. The contract is not my job'

The 23-year-old left wing on an expiring deal talks about the pace he's running, Nizhny Tagil, and why he doesn't read the Index.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelThursday, 24 November 2005

4:35 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

Igor Radulov80 has 8 goals in 19 games for Chicago Blackhawks, a jump from last season's 8 in 79 at 11.4 minutes a night. The Bureau's Development Index has him at +11 on a base of 71. His contract, at $0.60M, expires in 0 years.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here in the studio with Igor Radulov. Igor, thanks for sitting down. You're 23, you've got 8 goals in 19 games this year, and last year you had 8 in 79. That's the line everyone's writing. Does it feel different on the ice?

Igor Radulov

Yeah, it feels different. I play nineteen minutes now, before eleven. When you play eleven, you... you touch the puck once, maybe twice, and you go back to the bench. You are happy with the shift, you sit down, you drink water. Now I play more, I have more... ...more of the game in my hands. So the goals come. It is simple.

Marcus Bell

Simple. But you went from 12 points in 79 games to 12 in 19. Same production, quarter the games. You didn't just get more ice. You got better.

Igor Radulov

I get better every day, of course. No, but, you know... the head is different. Last year I play with Fleury on one line and I am... I try to stay in my spot, I don't make the mistake. Now Daze is on my wing, and he talks to me, he says 'Igor, you go there, I come here.' So I play freer. The puck goes where I want.

Marcus Bell

The Bureau's Development Index has you up 11 from your base of 71. The profile called it a 33-goal pace on the league's worst team. Do you read those things?

Igor Radulov

No. I don't read it. My friend sends me the internet thing, the page, and I say... ...I say, okay, and he closes the laptop. The Index is a number somebody writes. I play the game. We must play sixty minutes, not thirty-three goals. The goals come from the sixty.

Marcus Bell

Let me push on that, because it's unusual. A 23-year-old, first real taste of regular minutes, morale 91, and you tell me you don't look at your own grade?

Igor Radulov

Morale is... ...morale is good because we lose, but I am young, I know tomorrow is another game. You know? When you are 23 and you lose four in a row, you go home, you sleep, you come back. When you are 33 and you lose four, you go home and you think, this is my life. I am still here. So yeah, the morale is high because... ...because I don't know yet how much it hurts. Maybe next year I know more.

Marcus Bell

You've been in Chicago since before the record opened. Nobody here seems to remember how you got here, exactly. The draft was 2000, 74th overall. What happened between Nizhny Tagil and the United Center?

Igor Radulov

In Nizhny Tagil I play for the small team. It is a city of metal, you know? Factories. The rink is... ...the rink is not what you see here. My father, he skate with me when I am six, seven. He is not a player. He is a worker. But he say, 'Igor, go outside, put the blades on, come back when you can stop.' That is the whole lesson. Stop on one foot. You come inside.

Marcus Bell

That's the whole foundation? Your father at the rink?

Igor Radulov

My father, and the man who coach the boys' team, Kovalyov, not the player, different Kovalyov. He make us skate before we touch a puck. Forty minutes skating. Every day. I think that is why my... ...the puck feel, the edges, that is good. I am 86 on puck handling in the book, or whatever the number is, and I think it comes from the ice in Tagil where the holes, where you must... ...you must find your balance because the ice is bad. So the bad ice made me good on good ice.

Marcus Bell

And the draft. 2000, round 3, 74th. Were you surprised someone picked you?

Igor Radulov

I didn't know I was drafted until my friend at the factory call me. He say, 'Igor, you are in the paper.' I say, what paper? He say, the hockey paper, the list. Seventy-fourth. I think... ...I think it is a joke. A friend is playing with me. No, it is real. I come here after some years. I play in Russia, I play some American hockey, and I am in Chicago. The rest is... ...the rest is the record. You know more than me how I get here.

Marcus Bell

Fair enough. Let me put the contract in front of you. $0.60 million, zero years left. You're 23, you're scoring at a 33-goal pace, your grade is up 11. What do you want?

Igor Radulov

Everyone ask this. 'What do you want, Igor.' I want to score goals tonight. I want the next game. You know the contract, it is... ...it is important, of course. Money is important. But I think, if I play like this, the money come. If I play like garbage, no money, no years. So my job is the sixty minutes. The agent, he does the paper. I do the skating.

Marcus Bell

But there's a version of this where you're here and it's a bridge year. You show them what you can do, and then you walk. Is that in your head at all?

Igor Radulov

No. Maybe. I don't think like this. I think: I play here, this is my club. The room, Fleury is here, Daze, Sullivan, Bell. These guys, they show me where to be on the ice. It is a good room, even with the wins not... ...even with the wins not coming. I stay, I leave, the management decide. I just want the ice time to stay.

Marcus Bell

One last thing. You're 23, this is 19 games of real hockey in this league. What do you still need to learn? What's the thing you don't have yet?

Igor Radulov

Fighting. No, I mean, the... ...I don't finish. The last fifteen minutes, when the legs, when the head is... I have 51 on fighting in the book. You know what that means? I don't... ...when they push me in the corner, I give the puck away. I must learn to hold it. That is the next thing. Hold it sixty minutes, not fifty.

Marcus Bell

Igor Radulov, thank you very much. Appreciate it.

Igor Radulov

Thank you, Marcus. Good game tonight, I hope.
