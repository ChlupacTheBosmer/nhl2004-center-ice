Turco: the third period didn't lose this one

After a second straight overtime loss, the Stars' goaltender said the game at Detroit was settled before the third period and pointed to the shot clock.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelSunday, 6 November 2005

2:28 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Marty Turco95 has won one and lost one over his last 2 starts, both losses coming in overtime, the latest a 3-2 setback at Detroit Red Wings on November 3. Turco faced 23 shots and the Stars managed 19 the other way, a gap he said was the story long before the extra period began.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here in the tunnel at Joe Louis with Marty Turco. Thirty-two, two, overtime, your second straight one-goal loss on this road trip. You told Jan last December, after Philadelphia, that the game wasn't lost in the third. Tonight, it went to overtime. Different feeling?

Marty Turco

Well... I mean, yeah, I remember that one, Philly. And I still think the same thing about that game. Tonight though, uh... no. Tonight I think the third period was the best period we played. We had our chances. The game was, you know, it was done before that.

Marcus Bell

So where does it break for you? You're outshot 23 to 19. That's a four-shot gap on the board.

Marty Turco

That's the thing, yeah. Twenty-three to nineteen. We just, we didn't get enough pucks there. Modano had two great chances in the second, you know, the one off the crossbar and the other one where the D got a stick on it, and we never, I mean, we never built on it. You get those chances against a club like Detroit and you have to bury them because they're going to come at you all night and they did. They just, they kept coming.

Marcus Bell

Two overtime losses back to back. Columbus on Tuesday, tonight here. That's two points off the board you probably feel you should have six.

Marty Turco

Yeah, look, obviously. You look at the standings, we've got eighteen points, that's not a bad start. But yeah. Columbus, that one I was thinking about all night. We got outshot there too. Two games, both one-goal margins, both on the road, you know, that's, that's two points. That's a lot right now in this division.

Marcus Bell

Is there one moment tonight, one save or one goal, that you'll watch back tomorrow and think I could've done something different?

Marty Turco

Probably the third one. In the third, when they tied it. I know I was a little, I wasn't set on that one. I was late across and the puck went over my shoulder and you know, those are the ones that keep you up. But look, I made the saves I needed to make until then. Nineteen shots for my boys. I can't ask them to win a game and then sit here and complain about the one I let in.

Marcus Bell

You're a year left on your deal, the Book has you at 97, morale 97. You're thirty years old, Sault Ste. Marie. Does the way these games go, the ones you lose, does that weigh differently now than it did a few years ago?

Marty Turco

You're asking if I'm feeling it. No, no. I mean... the first year or two, yeah, you go home and you watch every replay and you want the earth to open up. Now it's more like, fine, tomorrow we watch it, we fix it, we get ready for the next one. You know, I've been in enough games where the third period didn't matter because the first two were already gone. That's what I'll remember tonight. Not the OT. The first thirty minutes.

Marcus Bell

Jere Lehtinen's got five points in his last four, Morrow's been solid too. What do they need to do to pick you up on the road?

Marty Turco

More pucks there. That's, that's the whole answer, you know. Jere's been great, Mike's been great, they've done their job. I just need them to get me a few more looks so I'm not watching twenty-three pucks all night. That's all. That's all I want.

Marcus Bell

Marty Turco, thank you. Back to you.
