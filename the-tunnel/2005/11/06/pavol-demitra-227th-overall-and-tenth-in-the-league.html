Pavol Demitra: 227th Overall, and Tenth in the League

Demitra sat down with The Tunnel and traced the road from a bus in PEI and a 1996 hold-out to an 89-point season, saying the year nobody watched him is the year he keeps.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelSunday, 6 November 2005

4:13 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Pavol Demitra83 went 227th in the 1993 draft and spent four seasons between the AHL and Ottawa before a trade sent him to St. Louis Blues on 27 November 1996. At 30 he has 8 goals and 13 points in 11 games this season, with the Bureau's Book at 71, his base of 89 down 4 on the Index. He sat down in the studio with Marcus Bell.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Pavol, thanks for the time. Marcus Bell, The Tunnel, sitting down with you in St. Louis. 9 October 1993, your first NHL game, and you score on Curtis Joseph. Today you play for the club he was in net for. Take me back to the start. Trencin.

Pavol Demitra

Yeah, Trencin. Born there, 1974, November. My father, he play soccer, professional, so for me first the ball, you know. Not the puck. I play soccer until maybe twelve, thirteen. Then fifteen, I start hockey. Only hockey after that.

Marcus Bell

Fifteen is late. Most of the men in this room were on skates at four.

Pavol Demitra

Late, yeah. But in Slovakia the ice is there, everybody play, you know. So it is not so strange. I go, I learn, I keep going.

Marcus Bell

Where do you learn the game, Pavol? Dubnica?

Pavol Demitra

Dubnica, yeah. ZTS Dubnica, second league. Senior hockey, men. I am seventeen and I play against men, 28 games, 23 points. Thirteen goals. That is where the game, how you say, where it becomes real for me.

Marcus Bell

A lot for a boy in a men's league.

Pavol Demitra

Maybe. When you are young you don't think, you just play. But the older guys, they help. They talk to you on the ice, they tell you where to stand, you know. That is how I learn. From them.

Marcus Bell

Then Trencin and the Extraliga at 18, 46 games, 11 goals, 28 points. And then the draft.

Pavol Demitra

1993. Eighth round, 227. So, you know, nobody waits for me. I know that number. I know it all my life.

Marcus Bell

Twelve games with Ottawa that year, a goal and two points. And then PEI, 41 games, 41 points. What was that first year like?

Pavol Demitra

Bus. Long bus, small rink, cold, you know. PEI. I am nineteen, I don't speak much English then. But in PEI I play every night. In Ottawa, twelve games, I sit and watch. So I learn on the bus, not in the NHL.

Marcus Bell

Next year, 74 points in 61 games and 16 games with Ottawa. Then 81 points in 48 games, and a permanent call-up, 7 goals and 17 points in 31 games. Two leagues in one season. Which one are you?

Pavol Demitra

Both, you know. I am the guy on the bus and the guy in the NHL, same week. That is the hardest part. One night you play twenty minutes, and two nights later you watch from the top row.

Marcus Bell

And then the hold-out, 1996.

Pavol Demitra

Yeah. I stay home. I play one game in Trencin, with Dukla, one goal, two points. Then Las Vegas, the IHL, 22 games, 21 points. I wait, and I wait, and then Ottawa, they trade me. 27 November 1996. St. Louis. For Christer Olsson.

Marcus Bell

How did you take the trade?

Pavol Demitra

Hurt, sure. But I look at it now, the trade, it saved me. Grand Rapids, IHL, 42 games, 50 points, 20 goals. Then March, I get the call. 17 March 1997, my first game here. Phoenix, we lose 3-2.

Marcus Bell

Two weeks later, 3 April, you score twice on Tommy Salo. The Islanders, 5-5.

Pavol Demitra

Two goals, yeah. That night I think, okay. Maybe I stay.

Marcus Bell

Then the playoffs. Detroit. Your first playoff point, an assist, in a 2-0 win. And 22 April, you beat Mike Vernon, a goal and two assists, 4-0.

Pavol Demitra

Detroit, the best team then, yeah. We win 4-0. You keep those nights, you know. In the summer you take them out and look at them.

Marcus Bell

1998-99. 82 games, 37 goals, 52 assists, 89 points. Tenth in NHL scoring. From 227th.

Pavol Demitra

That is the one. That year everything is there at one time. My hands, my legs, everything. 89 points, tenth in the league. Who expects that from 227? I keep that one.

Marcus Bell

Then the All-Star Game in Tampa, 1999. You score for the World team.

Pavol Demitra

Yeah, 8-6, we lose. I score, but we lose. Still, All-Star, with the best guys in the world. From Dubnica, from the bus in PEI, to that. That is something for a boy from a second league.

Marcus Bell

Last season, 82 games, 31 goals, 46 assists. This year, 11 games, 8 goals, 5 assists, 13 points, 18.8 minutes a night. The Bureau's Book has you at 71 today, your base of 89 down 4 on the Index. At 30.

Pavol Demitra

The Index goes down when you are 30, sure. That is normal, you get older. But I feel good. 13 points in 11 games, the legs are there. I don't look at the number. I look at the puck.

Marcus Bell

The room, then. Tkachuk, Pronger, Weight, Osgood, Boguniecki. What is this group in November?

Pavol Demitra

Good room, yeah. Keith is loud, always talking. Doug, he talks on the bench. Prongs, he says nothing, he just plays. Old guys like me, we know what we have here. It does not last forever, you know. So we want to win now.

Marcus Bell

You are 30. Three years left on the deal. Where is the end of this road?

Pavol Demitra

I don't know. I play. When the legs don't come, then I know. Not this year. Not yet.

Marcus Bell

One more. What do you tell a kid in Dubnica, in the second league, waiting to hear his name late in the draft?

Pavol Demitra

Skate, you know. And be patient. The bus is long, but the bus takes you somewhere. For me it takes me here. So. Keep skating.

Marcus Bell

Pavol Demitra, St. Louis Blues. Marcus Bell, The Tunnel. Thanks, Pavol.
