Gaborik: The Kill Gave Us Our Legs

Marian Gaborik says the penalty kill in the second period of Saturday's 3-2 overtime win over Tampa Bay is what kept a 6-game streak alive on the third night of a three-in-five.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelSunday, 6 November 2005

2:24 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Marian Gaborik99 scored twice in three games as Toronto Maple Leafs held off Tampa Bay Lightning 3-2 in overtime on Saturday night, extending the Maple Leafs to 9-2-0 and 22 points, the most in the league. The win came on the third game of a three-in-five, against a team that outshot them 36 to 33. Jan Kowalczyk caught him in the tunnel.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here with Marian Gaborik. Thirty-two minutes of overtime, thirty-six shots against, thirty-three for, and you still found a way. Six in a row. What was it tonight?

Marian Gaborik

Yeah. The game was, how you say, back and forth the whole night. TB they shoot a lot tonight, thirty-six, and our boys in front, we block, we clear, we just stay in it. And in overtime you get one chance, maybe two. We take it. That's hockey.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let me ask about special teams. A game that tight, 3-2 all the way, TB outshooting you by three. Did a power play or a penalty kill decide this one?

Marian Gaborik

The kill, I think. In the second period we get one bad penalty and we spend too much time defending. But the four guys, they just, they keep it outside, no second chance. And that is what we talk about before every game, no second chance. So when we come off, it gives you legs. You kill two minutes like that and nobody scores, you come off the bench and you feel good. You feel fast.

Jan Kowalczyk

Three in five. Thirty-one, two, five. You've been out there every night. How are the legs?

Marian Gaborik

I think I am good. The third game is always the game where your legs say something, you know? Tonight in the third period I say, okay, now I feel it. But you play 82 games, man. You don't think about it too much. You think too much, your legs go slower. So you just go.

Jan Kowalczyk

Two goals in three. Is something clicking right now in your game, or does it come when the team clicks?

Marian Gaborik

I don't know, man. I try to be open every shift and sometimes the puck come, sometimes not. We win 6 in a row, everybody looks good. That's the truth. I just skate and I try to make a play. When the team play like that, the puck find you.

Jan Kowalczyk

Same answer you gave me in December when I asked you about twelve. I'm not asking about twelve tonight. I'm asking what the next stretch asks of you, because you're thirteen games in and six straight and the road is coming.

Marian Gaborik

Well we don't look at number. Not now. We look tomorrow and we see who we play and we prepare. The schedule, I know it is tight, three in five, games in a row. But you just rest when you can. Eat good. You sleep. And you go to the next one.

Jan Kowalczyk

In January I asked you what the room does after a loss and you said nobody cares about your goal total. Six straight. Is anybody caring a little tonight?

Marian Gaborik

Tonight, maybe, we care a little. A little. But tomorrow we watch the video and we say, okay, the second period was not good, we fix it. The win is nice. But the video, you know, the video always say something. So we go home, we watch, we fix.

Jan Kowalczyk

Marian Gaborik, thank you for your time. Jan Kowalczyk, back in the tunnel in Toronto.
