Eric Lippmann and the cheapest 18 points in the league

The Pittsburgh GM says the number that matters to him is $2.72M a point, and the deadline talk starts with Martin Straka's contract year.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelSunday, 6 November 2005

4:05 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Pittsburgh Penguins sit 5-2-0 with 18 points after 7 games, the best per-point payroll in the league at $2.72M. GM Eric Lippmann sat down with The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk to talk about what that buys him before the deadline, the locker room he just paid for, and whether the man behind the bench has the roster he needs to do something with it.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here with Eric Lippmann of the Pittsburgh Penguins. Eric, five and two and nobody beat you yet, riding a four-game streak. Does it feel like October is going to plan?

Eric Lippmann

Uh, it feels like seven games. We've got 33 goals for, 27 against. That's... the first period is where we've been loose, you know? Two of those two losses we gave up the first one and chased. The guys are finding it. I'm not going to sit here and pretend we've solved anything.

Jan Kowalczyk

You've got $48.92M on the books and 18 points. That's $2.72M a point, best in the league. Toronto pays $4.09M, New York $4.65M. Does that number tell you something the standings don't?

Eric Lippmann

It tells me we haven't overpaid for the wrong people yet. I mean, look, I'd rather have 30 points and a bigger payroll, sure. But right now, the money's in the right spots. Martin Straka is 87 in the Book, he's got 21 in 11 games, he's on $4.20M. You find me another 33-year-old doing that for less. That's what $2.72 looks like on the ice.

Jan Kowalczyk

That's a contract year. One year left on Straka. The deadline's 133 days out, March 19 what the calendar has as the trade deadline. Are you the man at the table buying or selling, come March?

Eric Lippmann

You know, everybody wants me to pick a side in November. We're five and two. We're not selling. I'll say that. But buying? What am I buying? I need... the locker room we just moved up in October, that was a start. But we've got equipment at 1 and practice at 1 on my building sheet. The coach, he doesn't need me to trade for a winger if the guys can't get their legs back in a practice rink that's... that's not what it needs to be. So the question for me in March might be what I do in May and June with what I have left, not what I do in March.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're talking about the building, not the roster.

Eric Lippmann

I'm talking about what the coach can work with. His live sum is minus 12 on our building sheet. That's the practice track, the travel track, the workout track, the equipment. We moved the locker room up this month, fine, morale helps. But the room you skate in, the room you lift in, those are at 1 and 2. You put a good coach in there and he's fighting the building. I own that. That's my line, not his.

Jan Kowalczyk

But if you could make one move before March 19, one player who fills the gap you're describing, who is he?

Eric Lippmann

I can't... I don't know the name. I know what the roster needs. Depth on the back end, maybe. We've got Chartrand out till November 23 with the ankle, Fata till January with the abdomen. That's two bodies gone and the coach is short a line sometimes. But I'm not going to stand here in November and tell you I'm chasing a name in March. I'll tell you this: we're not a seller. We're 18 points in 7. The window's open.

Jan Kowalczyk

The coach, Eric. You said he's fighting the building. Does he have what he needs to stay competitive through March?

Eric Lippmann

The coach picks his lines and he picks the net and I don't touch that. What he needs is bodies and he needs a building that doesn't tax them every night. I just said I own the building. So does he have what he needs? Not yet. I'm getting there. The locker room upgrade is October, that's one piece. Equipment, practice, those come next. They take time and they take money and I've got profit at $20.87M, so I've got something to work with.

Jan Kowalczyk

Straka's 21 points in 11 games, morale at 100. If he keeps this pace, are you extension-ready come the spring?

Eric Lippmann

He's 33. He's playing like he's 23. I've got a conversation to have with him, yes. But right now I want him at 100 and scoring and not thinking about a number in a folder. You know how that goes. You put a contract in front of a man in November and suddenly he's a different player. We'll see in the spring. He's got my number.

Jan Kowalczyk

Last one. Five and two, division lead in the Atlantic, W4. You said you'd rather have 30 points and a bigger payroll. At what number do you feel like you've arrived, and not just outperformed?

Eric Lippmann

Ninety-five points. That's the number. That's the Cup run. We're on pace for... look, I don't do the math on that in November. I'll know in April if we've arrived. And if we haven't, I've got $20.87M in the bank and I'll spend it somewhere, in the building or on a name I can't say yet. Eric Lippmann, thank you Jan.

Jan Kowalczyk

Appreciate the time. Back to you.
