Tortorella on five overtime losses: 'That's not luck, that's coaching'

The TB bench boss says the structure breaks down in the last five minutes, not the effort, and that Svitov and Sutherby need the reps before the production arrives.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelSunday, 6 November 2005

3:19 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

John Tortorella's Tampa Bay Lightning lost 2-3 in overtime to Toronto Maple Leafs on 2005-11-05, their fifth overtime defeat in 13 games. TB outshot Toronto 36 to 33. Tortorella told The Tunnel's Marcus Bell the problem is decisions in the closing minutes, not compete level, and addressed why Alexander Svitov and Brian Sutherby stay in his lineup with 1 point and 0 points respectively.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here in the corridor at the Air Canada Centre, standing with John Tortorella after that 3-2 overtime loss to Toronto. Fifth overtime loss in thirteen games, 36 shots on your side of the rink. What's happening in those last five minutes?

John Tortorella

You know, I've been asked about overtime now what, five times this month? And I'll tell you the same thing I told the last guy. Five overtime losses is not a coincidence. You can put one down to a bounce, you can put two down to a hot goalie. Five? That's your team making the wrong play at the wrong time, and that's on me. That's coaching.

Marcus Bell

You outshot them 36 to 33 tonight. That's not a team that looked tired or outworked. Is this an effort problem or a structure problem?

John Tortorella

Effort. No. I'm not, I won't stand here and say these guys aren't competing. 36 shots on the road against a team that's 9 and 2. Effort's fine. What's not fine is the last five minutes. You're making a read, you're carrying it in where you should dump it, you're not the first man to the puck on the forecheck when it's one on one in OT. That's a structure thing. That's a decision thing. And like I said, those decisions get made because of what we practice, what we prepare. So yeah. It's coaching.

Marcus Bell

Let me pivot to the bottom of your lineup. Alexander Svitov, 1 point in 13 games. Brian Sutherby, no points, 13 games. They're regulars. They're 23. What do you see when you watch them on a nightly basis?

John Tortorella

What I see is two young guys getting the reps. They're 23. Svitov's in there killing penalties, he's in there on the third line, he's winning some of those battles. Sutherby's the same. They're not... look, the numbers don't show it yet, I'm not going to pretend the numbers aren't thin. But if I pull them every time they don't produce, what am I teaching them? I'm teaching them to be scared. And I need them not scared in February and March. So they're in my lineup, and they'll be in my lineup for a while.

Marcus Bell

Is there a timeline in your mind, though? At some point you need those points from those lines because your top six is carrying a lot of it. Stillman and Lecavalier with 17 each, that's a lot of the load.

John Tortorella

You want me to put a date on it? You know what, I can't. I genuinely can't. What I can tell you is I'm not going to bench a 23-year-old because he's got zero through thirteen. I'm not going to put that on a kid in front of a camera. The production will come. And if it doesn't, we'll have a different conversation. But that's in here, not out here.

Marcus Bell

You haven't turned from Nikolai. Thirteen starts, the only goalie you've used all season, he's 32. John Grahame played one game. Does the workload factor into those fifth-minute stretches at all?

John Tortorella

No. I'm not going to make it about that. He's my best goalie. I'll play him until he tells me he can't go, and he hasn't told me that. We outshot them tonight 36 to 33, the work in front of him was fine. You want to ask about goaltending? Ask me after he's given up four or five cheap ones. That's not tonight. That's not been the story all year.

Marcus Bell

Three in a row, and you get Pittsburgh Sunday at home, then Chicago Tuesday. Four and oh in your building so far. What's different when you play at Home Depot Center?

John Tortorella

You know, I wish I had the answer. Four and oh at home, two and seven on the road. It's not the travel, we're in this league, the travel's the same. Something... something settles when we play in front of our own people. The structure holds. The last five minutes, we make the right read at Home Depot Center and we don't on the road. And that's what I'm trying to figure out. That's my homework tonight.

Marcus Bell

Coach, thank you for your time.

John Tortorella

Yeah. Thanks.
