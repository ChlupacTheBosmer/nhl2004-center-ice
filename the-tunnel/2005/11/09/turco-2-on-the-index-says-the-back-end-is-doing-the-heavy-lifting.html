Turco, +2 on the Index, says the back end is doing the heavy lifting

After a 4-2 win over Toronto that capped a two-game stretch with a shutout, the Stars goaltender credits four different defensemen who scored in the span and calls his own play 'catching up to the grade people talk about.'

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelWednesday, 9 November 2005

2:17 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Marty Turco95 stopped 31 of 33 shots as Dallas Stars beat the reigning Cup champion Toronto Maple Leafs 4-2 at home on 2005-11-08, snapping a one-game losing streak with his second straight win and a shutout two nights prior. The Bureau's Development Index has him at +2 above his base grade, the highest it has put him this season. Four different Stars defensemen had points in the two-game stretch.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here with Marty Turco after a four and two win over Toronto. You're on a two-game win streak, a three-nothing shutout two nights ago, and tonight you hold the Cup champions to two on 33 shots. What does that kind of night feel like?

Marty Turco

It feels good, you know. Obviously you look at their lineup and you've got, what, Luongo in the net, McCabe on the point, every piece is there. You don't get nights like that very often. We just wanted to come out fast and, you know, ride it. Four and two, you'll take it.

Marcus Bell

The Bureau's Development Index has you at plus two above your base, 95 overall today. Two wins in two, a shutout, 31 saves tonight. Is something different in your game, or are you just catching a rhythm?

Marty Turco

Yeah, well, I mean, the rhythm's there, I'll give you that. But honestly I think the guys in front of me are doing more of it than I am. You look at the last two nights, four different defensemen with points. Four. Erskine, Zubov, Boucher, Descoteaux. When your blue line is scoring, the whole game, you're not fighting, you're not, you know, chasing pucks the way you have to when you're down. It just gets easier to see the puck.

Marcus Bell

That's the thing I wanted to ask about. I've noticed the pairings have been shuffled, different looks on the back end lately. Who's stepped up for you when the lineup has been different than what you expected?

Marty Turco

You know, the thing about a room this deep is, you don't really, I mean, you don't feel the gap. Boucher's been up the ice more than he has in a while, and Erskine, the kid, he's got size and he's not afraid to block a shot and get right back up. You see that from the crease, you trust it. You lean on it. I don't have to worry about who's next to who, I just, I just have to see the puck and make the stop.

Marcus Bell

Thirty-three shots is a lot in a win. In January Jan asked you about the home-ice edge and you told me it's legs and sleep, nothing mystical. You're four and two at home now, six and three on the road. Legs holding up either way?

Marty Turco

Yeah, you remember that one. Well, legs, sleep, it works both ways, you know what I mean. We've been fresh on the trips this year, that's, that's the truth of it. And tonight, TOR came at us in waves in the second, I think 15 shots in the period, but when you've got the legs, you stay square, you stay set. If you're tired you open up and those guys, Morrow, Arnott on the front side, they don't miss. They didn't tonight either.

Marcus Bell

Fifteen shots in the second. Was there a moment in there where you felt the game could turn?

Marty Turco

Yeah, there was a scramble, I think it was, maybe five minutes in, we had a bad change and they ended up two on one and, I made the first one, I'm not going to tell you what it was, I just remember the glove hand came up and the puck went over and I thought, okay, that's the game right there. If that goes in it's 2-2 and you're chasing. But it didn't. We killed the next penalty and the crowd got loud and, you know, that's the one.

Marcus Bell

Marty Turco, thank you. Back to you.
