Martin: 'Aebischer's our guy and he stays our guy'

Two losses, two three-goal games, two more shots than the other team: the Florida coach says the process is close and the net is settled.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelWednesday, 9 November 2005

2:29 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

The Florida Panthers Panthers lost 4-3 in overtime at Washington Capitals on Monday night, their second straight defeat despite outshooting the Capitals 24-19. Head coach Jacques Martin spoke to The Tunnel in the tunnel afterward about the week's results, what they hide, and the goaltending call for Saturday's game against Calgary Flames.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here in the tunnel at MCI Center. Coach, you outshot them 24-19, scored three, and you're on the bus with nothing. What did the first two periods look like from the bench?

Jacques Martin

I thought we were good. I thought we were the better team for two periods, you know? We had our chances. We managed the puck well in the neutral zone, we got to the net, we had traffic. They get one on a bounce, we get back even, and then the third one, uh, the third period we got a little loose with pucks in our end and they capitalized. That's the game in a sentence.

Jan Kowalczyk

And it's the same story as Thursday here at home against Pittsburgh. Twenty-one shots, three goals, nothing in the column. Two games, six goals for, eight against, zero points. What is the one thing between those three and two?

Jacques Martin

The one thing. I'd say it's the puck going the other way on two or three plays. You look at the shot totals, we're generating, we're getting to soft ice, we're competing. But the one thing that separates winning from losing right now is a puck that rings a post or a deflection off a shin pad or a two-on-one that isn't a two-on-one. You want me to name something structural and I don't see it yet. The details are close.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let me ask about the net then, because Saturday you've got Calgary in Sunrise. David Aebischer, twelve games, five and seven, one shutout. You lose two in a row, do you go back to the same man or does Jani get the start?

Jacques Martin

Aebischer starts Saturday. I'm not going to, you know, I don't think that's, uh, I don't think that's the answer. The shutout he had, the games we've played well in front of him, the record reflects the team in front of the goalie. He's a good goalie, he's a big goalie, he's seen the puck tonight on nineteen shots and I thought he was fine. So, Aebischer.

Jan Kowalczyk

You mentioned the group in front. You've got Branislav Mezei back on the thirteenth, Eric Messier on the eighteenth, Krajicek not until December. Does that depth change how much you ask of the goalies in the meantime?

Jacques Martin

It changes who dresses. Mezei gives us a seventh defenseman, so that's a body, that's a rotation we've been short on. Messier gives us a forward, he's played some third line for us, he's good on the penalty kill. You lose bodies and you lose depth and then the minutes get longer on guys and the compete level drops at the end of the night. That's not a goalie problem. That's a body problem. And bodies are coming back.

Jan Kowalczyk

One last thing. Calgary's two points behind you in the race for fourth in the division. You've got three of your next four at home. What does Saturday night have to look like differently from Thursday?

Jacques Martin

It has to look like a team that takes the three-goal lead and protects it. We haven't had one. We've had the lead in both those games, we've been up, and we haven't managed it for the last twenty minutes. That's the one thing, and I'm not putting it on one line, I'm putting it on all four. You protect it, you don't chase, you get pucks deep, you make them get it back out. That's the difference between a two and a zero. That's the difference.

Jan Kowalczyk

Coach, thank you. Back to you.
