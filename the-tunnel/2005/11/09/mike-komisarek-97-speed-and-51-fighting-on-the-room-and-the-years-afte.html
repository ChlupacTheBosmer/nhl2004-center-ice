Mike Komisarek, 97 Speed and 51 Fighting, on the Room and the Years After

Komisarek says the Book has him fast and does not have him a fighter, that the tone in Montreal comes from the men around him before it reaches his seat, and that he wants to coach kids once the playing stops.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelWednesday, 9 November 2005

3:37 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Mike Komisarek Mike Komisarek87 is 23, in his third season on the Montreal Canadiens blue line, and the Bureau's Book grades him 84 overall with speed at 97. He plays 29.5 minutes a night on a $1.10M deal that has nothing left on it after this season. He came into the studio this week to talk about the room he plays in, from his seat, and about what he'd do when the skates come off.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk, in the studio, sitting with Mike Komisarek. Mike, thanks for coming in. Third season, 13 games, 12 points. The number that jumps at me is the ice time. 29.5 minutes a night.

Mike Komisarek

Yeah. Uh, it's, you know, I'd rather play that than sit. That's what I wanted when I got here, to be a guy they could leave out there. It's a lot of hockey. I'm 23, though. I can take it.

Jan Kowalczyk

The first thing people say about you is the size. 225 pounds. The Bureau has your checking at 91 and your aggression at 86. Is that who you are?

Mike Komisarek

See, that's the part, I'm not a fighter. My fighting's 51, that's the worst number on my card, and it should be, you know? I'm not out there dropping them. Speed's 97. I want to be a guy you can't get past, and I want to get to the puck first. That's the game now.

Jan Kowalczyk

Fair. And you know where the low grades are without me reading them out. Do you look at them?

Mike Komisarek

Every summer. I look at the ones I don't like. The Bureau, they watch everything, so they're not wrong. I know what I've got to be better at. I don't need to say it out loud, I just go work on it.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're 23 and that room is older than you are. From your seat, who sets the tone in there?

Mike Komisarek

Jose, uh, Jose Theodore, he does it every day. He's 91 in the Book and he still acts like he's got something to prove out there. And then you've got guys like Richard Zednik and Michael Nylander, they've been around, they talk. But it's, leadership isn't one guy with a letter, you know? It's the guy who's there early when nobody's watching.

Jan Kowalczyk

Where do you fit in that?

Mike Komisarek

I'm getting there. I want to be the guy they count on. When it's going bad out there, I want the puck on my stick and I want to be the one who fixes it. That's the leadership I care about. The letter, that's for other people to decide.

Jan Kowalczyk

You were seventh overall in 2001 out of Michigan, signed in July of 2002, first game that season. What did you think you were walking into?

Mike Komisarek

I didn't know anything. I was a Long Island kid, I played two years at Michigan, and then all of a sudden I'm here and everything's faster. Two years at Michigan I had 46 points, and I fought a lot more there than I do now, 145 minutes in 80 games. The game is different up here.

Jan Kowalczyk

You've got 46 penalty minutes in 13 games this season. That's a lot for a man who just told me he isn't a fighter.

Mike Komisarek

That's, that's the aggression number, I guess. It's the stick, it's the after-the-whistle stuff, it's me being in the wrong, being in the wrong spot. I've got to pick my spots better. My coach tells me that. He's right, you know?

Jan Kowalczyk

You played all 82 games last year. Never out of the lineup.

Mike Komisarek

That matters to me. More than the points, honestly. You want to be the guy the room can count on to be in the lineup every night. That's, uh, that's a reputation I'd like to have, you know, when it's all done.

Jan Kowalczyk

You go back to Long Island in the summer. West Islip. What's there for you?

Mike Komisarek

Family. The rink I grew up in. I skate with the kids there sometimes, the, uh, the PAL program, that's where I started. You go back and you're 23 and they look at you like you're, like you're somebody, and you're the same guy, you know? I like that.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let me ask you something I don't get to ask much. When the skates come off, and you're 23 so that's a long way off, what's the plan?

Mike Komisarek

I think about it. I do. I had Gary Dineen when I was a kid, he ran the junior team, and I had coach Nikiforov, and those men, they gave me the game. So I'd want to give it back that way. Coaching. Maybe the national program, USA Hockey, that's where I came up. I don't know the shape of it yet. But I don't want to be a guy who just disappears when he's done.

Jan Kowalczyk

Coaching, would you want the pro side or the kids?

Mike Komisarek

Kids, probably. With kids you can still teach them something. By the time they get up here, they know it all already. I'm joking. Kind of.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're on $1.10M and the Bureau's Contract Ledger has nothing left on the deal after this year. Do you think about that?

Mike Komisarek

You'd be lying if you said no. But that's my agent. I'm not doing my own deal in a studio in November. I want to be here. I like this room. That's all I'll say about it, you know?

Jan Kowalczyk

One more, then. It's your third year and it's a long season. What do you want to be by April?

Mike Komisarek

Better. That's it. Same guy, better player. I don't have a number for you. I just want to be somebody the room leans on.

Jan Kowalczyk

Mike Komisarek, thanks for your time. Jan Kowalczyk, back to you.
