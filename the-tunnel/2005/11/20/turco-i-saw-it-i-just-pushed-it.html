Turco: 'I Saw It, I Just Pushed It'

After 22 shots and overtime, the Dallas goaltender describes the save that kept them alive and what sitting first in the Pacific asks of a room this deep in November.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelSunday, 20 November 2005

2:24 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Marty Turco95 and Dallas Stars beat Columbus Blue Jackets 4-3 in overtime on 2005-11-17. Turco faced 22 shots and stayed in it long enough for the extra-period goal to land. The Stars sit at 11-4-3, first in the Pacific.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here with Marty Turco, after a four to three overtime win over Columbus tonight. Twenty-two shots on your net, and you're in the corridor with the pads still on. There's a stop in the third, late, where you got a piece of it and the building came down. Take me to that one.

Marty Turco

Yeah, yeah. That's the one, that's the one I was thinking about coming off. You know, we're tied, the clock's running, and they're pressing, and I just... I saw it come off the stick and I was already committed short side, you know, so I just... let it hit me. If you watch it back it's not, it's not a highlight-reel save. But I needed that one.

Jan Kowalczyk

You've been in net 17 times this season, 67.1 minutes a night. Does it feel like a lot, this deep in November?

Marty Turco

No. No, I don't think so. I had stretches at Michigan where I played every weekend back to back, you don't think about it. You think about it in, in February maybe, when the legs are gone. Right now I feel good. If they asked me to go tomorrow I'd go. And they won't, because Jason's been... he's been good. So it's fine.

Jan Kowalczyk

First in the Pacific, three straight. What does that stretch ask of you that the first two months didn't?

Marty Turco

The schedule asks the same thing, every night, you get the two or you don't. What's different is... what's different is you're on the board now. People look up, they see Dallas, and the next team comes out wanting to be the one that knocks you off. Columbus tonight, they were there every minute. That's a different kind of night when you know everybody's playing their best game against you.

Jan Kowalczyk

Three wins, the last one in OT. Finding something, or a run that corrects itself?

Marty Turco

You're gonna get me to pick a lane in November, aren't you. I don't know, man. The third period, the OT, we're winning those. That's real. The start, the first period, we've been slow a couple times, you know, we chase. I can't tell you what January looks like from here. I'm not the guy you ask about January in November.

Jan Kowalczyk

Lemieux had two tonight. Messier had one. What's it like in that crease watching them go up and score while you're trying to hold the other end?

Marty Turco

You know what, it's the best part. You're just... you're just sitting there going, 'yeah, get it in, get it in, don't need me right now.' And then you get the puck back and you're the one with the pressure. Claude, he's been... he's always somewhere he shouldn't be. You look up and he's right there, you know. You can't coach that.

Jan Kowalczyk

Last time we talked, January, you told me the home edge was physical. Legs, sleep. Is that still your read?

Marty Turco

Yeah, it is. You don't change a theory after one game. We're nine and four at home for a reason. The guys sleep good, they get on the ice in a routine they know, and they skate. On the road it's a different hotel, a different breakfast, a different bus ride. That's it. That's the whole mystery. No magic, just... just routine. And the guys at home have the routine.

Jan Kowalczyk

Marty Turco, thank you. Back to you.
