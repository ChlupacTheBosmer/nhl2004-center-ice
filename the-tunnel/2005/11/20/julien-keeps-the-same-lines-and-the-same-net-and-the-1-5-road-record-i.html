Julien keeps the same lines and the same net, and the 1-5 road record is the one he cannot coach around

MTL's coach talks about structure through a last-place start, why he has not shuffled his lines, and the back-to-back in Pittsburgh tonight.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelSunday, 20 November 2005

3:42 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Claude Julien took the microphone in the tunnel at the Bell Centre after Montreal Canadiens beat Pittsburgh Penguins 3-2 on 2005-11-18, a win that snapped a two-game losing streak but left the Canadiens at 5-8-3 and last in the Northeast. Julien is 1-5 on the road this season and 6-6 at home, the widest split in the division. He has made no line changes in the past two editions.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here in the tunnel with Claude Julien. Coach, 3 and 2 over Pittsburgh, who came in at 8 and 4. Twenty shots to their eighteen. You get the result. Walk me through what you liked tonight.

Claude Julien

Yeah, the compete level, you know, that's what I wanted to see. We won the puck battle in the corners, we moved it out of our zone with some poise. I thought, uh, the first period we were a little tentative, we were carrying the puck too long, trying to make the perfect play. After that we just started dumping it in and going to work, and that's, that's more us. You know, Pittsburgh's got skill, they've got Straka, they've got Crosby on the power play, and I thought we managed them well enough in our own end.

Jan Kowalczyk

You mentioned being tentative early. You gave up the first goal, I believe, and then you had to dig out of it. Is that a pattern you're seeing, this slow start?

Claude Julien

It's been a pattern, yeah. Not every game, but more than I want. And I'll tell you, uh, we talk about it. Practice, video, the morning skate. We talk about coming out and winning the first ten minutes. Some nights it clicks, some nights guys are, they're waiting for the game to come to them instead of going to get it. Tonight we were a little flat in the first and then we tightened up. I think that's, that's a growth thing. You know, you look at this group and we've got guys who are still figuring out each other's game.

Jan Kowalczyk

Claude, you're five and eight and three. You're last in your division. I haven't seen a line change from you in at least a fortnight. Same four lines, same pairings. Is that by design?

Claude Julien

It's by design, yeah. Look, I'm not going to, you know, change something every time we lose a game. If I start moving guys around every three days, nobody knows where they're supposed to be. I need these guys comfortable. Zednik's got thirteen goals, he's earning his spot and then some. Nylander at thirty-three is, he's our guy down the middle. Komisarek is fourteen points from the blue line at twenty-three, the kid's earning every minute he gets. So no, I don't change what's working. What I change is, uh, the details. The structure around it. That's where you win games.

Jan Kowalczyk

That's fair. But I have to ask about the split. Six and six at home, one and five on the road. That's the widest gap in the Northeast. What is happening when you put on the white sweater?

Claude Julien

Yeah. You know, I don't have a magic answer for that one. We play, uh, we play our game at home, we've got six and six, that's not great either, but it's better than one and five. I think on the road, and this is, this is a hard thing to put your finger on, I think we get outworked in the first period. We show up and they hit us first and we spend the whole game chasing. At home the crowd gets into it, we get a couple of bounces, the game opens up. On the road you're fighting the building and you're fighting yourself. And we've got to find a way to bring the, the house energy with us. That's, that's the coaching job. That's my job.

Jan Kowalczyk

Jose's started fifteen of eighteen. Six and nine, no shutouts. Roloson's got three starts. Is there a conversation about giving Jose a breather, or is he your guy regardless?

Claude Julien

He's our guy. You know, and, and I know the six and nine looks a little heavy, but look at what he's doing behind us. Forty-one goals for, forty-six against, we're, we're close every night because he's keeping us in it. Dwayne's in there when Jose needs a night. I'm not going to, you know, manufacture a controversy where there isn't one. Jose is our starter, that's been clear since camp, and he's going to play a lot of games.

Jan Kowalczyk

One last thing, and it's immediate. You're back in Pittsburgh tonight. Same opponent, same building, less than twenty-four hours apart. What are you telling these guys in the morning?

Claude Julien

I'm telling them it's a different game. That's the lie every coach tells, right? But it is, it's genuinely, uh, a different game. They're going to come out angry, they're at home, they lost one they feel they should've won. And we're going to be tired. The travel, the, the little sleep you get between games. So what you do is you simplify. You make it a twenty-minute game, you win the first one, you see where you are after forty. I don't have a big speech for a back-to-back. I just say, 'here's what we need to do tonight, let's do that.'

Jan Kowalczyk

Claude Julien, thank you. Back to you.
