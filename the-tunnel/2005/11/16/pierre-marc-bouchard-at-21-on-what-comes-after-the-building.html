Pierre-Marc Bouchard, at 21, on what comes after the building

The Wild's 21-year-old center on three years in the Midwest, the room he plays in, and a life he cannot yet see past the glass.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelWednesday, 16 November 2005

5:28 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Pierre-Marc Bouchard79 sat down with The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk in the studio after a week in which his ice time reached 17.7 minutes a night, his third season with Minnesota Wild. He is 21, from Sherbrooke, Quebec, drafted 8th overall in 2002, and his contract at $1.20M has 0 years left on it.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here with Pierre-Marc Bouchard. Pierre-Marc, three years now in Minnesota. When you landed here, what did you think you were walking into?

Pierre-Marc Bouchard

Three, yeah, almost four now. And I thought, okay, it's far from home. That's the first thing. Sherbrooke to here, it's, you know, a long way. I was 18 when I started here so everything was new. The league, the travel, the cold. But the cold, actually, we have cold in Quebec. It's different cold. Here it's flat, the wind comes and it goes right through you. In Sherbrooke you're in the hills, it's, I don't know, sheltered.

Jan Kowalczyk

You played Pee-Wee there, College-Français Rive-Sud, back in '98. Does that kid have any idea where you're sitting right now?

Pierre-Marc Bouchard

No, no. No idea. I was so small. You could see the next guy through me, you know? And now I'm still small but at least I'm here. But that kid, he just wanted to score a goal in a Pee-Wee game. He didn't think about this. He couldn't.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're in your third year here. This is home now?

Pierre-Marc Bouchard

Yeah. Yeah it is. I have my places, I know where to go. The guys, when they come to my house, they know it's, okay, this is the French guy's house. But yeah, I feel settled. It took a year maybe. You don't feel settled in a new city when you're 18. You're in a hotel in your head.

Jan Kowalczyk

And the building. You skate in it every day, you play in it every other day. What is it?

Pierre-Marc Bouchard

It's good. When it's full, the noise, it comes down on you. The people here, they're quiet people, you know? Not like Montreal, not like Quebec where they yell and they talk and they touch your arm. Here people are polite, they nod, they say hey, and then they go. But in the building, the building opens up. When you've got a good game going and it's loud, it's loud. You feel it in the second period, when it gets going, you feel the floor a little bit.

Jan Kowalczyk

You mentioned the guys, and I'll get to them. But first: Richard Park is third in the league right now, 25 points in 18 games. He's in your room. What is it like watching that from the next locker?

Pierre-Marc Bouchard

Oh, he's unbelievable this year. Yeah. He's, you know, he's been good before, everyone knows that, but this year the puck just goes to him and he puts it in. And in the room he's quiet. He doesn't say a lot. But you watch him and you think, okay, that's what it looks like when it's working. I watch. I try to do my version of it, you know? I'm not as big, I'm not as strong. I've got to be different.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're 70 inches, 165 pounds, and the league's weakest numbers on you are the fight, toughness, checking. Does that define how you feel in the building when you're playing?

Pierre-Marc Bouchard

No. I don't think about it like that. I think, okay, I've got my speed, I've got my hands, I use that. If I go looking for the hit I lose the puck or I get the hit. So no. I stay on my side of it. The coach knows what he's getting when he puts me out. I'm not going to change. You can't be someone else in this league. You'll get exposed.

Jan Kowalczyk

Your ice time went from 11.6 last year to 17.7 this year. That's a big jump. Did the coach say something to you?

Pierre-Marc Bouchard

Not much. He just, yeah, he started giving me more. And the more you get, the more you want. When you're at 11 you think, okay, I'll take my shift, I'll go to the bench. At 17, 18, you're tired in the third but you want the puck. You want to be on it. It changes you, more minutes. You see the game different from out there when you're on longer.

Jan Kowalczyk

So let's talk about the room. Saku Koivu is in there. Wes Walz. Manny Fernandez. You're one of the youngest in there.

Pierre-Marc Bouchard

Yeah, the youngest. Saku, he's done everything, you know? He's the captain, he's the man. And Wes, he's old, he's been everywhere, and he's funny, the guys laugh. But they don't, I don't know, they don't treat you different because you're young. They treat you the same on the ice. If you lose a puck, you lose a puck, you're 21 or 33. That's good. That's how it should be.

Jan Kowalczyk

And your contract. $1.20M, 0 years left. That's a word people use around you now, unrestricted. Do you hear it?

Pierre-Marc Bouchard

I hear it. Everybody hears it. But I don't... I don't sit and figure out my plan, you know? My agent talks to them. I play. If they want me, okay. If they want somebody else, I go somewhere else. The game is the same in every city. It's not... it's not the worst problem I have in my life.

Jan Kowalczyk

Fair. But you're 21. When do you start thinking about the rest of your life? Because you're going to be playing this for a while and then you won't.

Pierre-Marc Bouchard

I think about it. I mean, not every day. But when you're hurt or when you're tired, you think, okay, what else. And I think, maybe I go back. Sherbrooke is, you know, it's home. And there's things you could do there, small things. Business, maybe. I don't know what. My father, he always worked. He doesn't talk much about hockey anymore but he worked his whole life. So I'd find something. I'd figure it out. But not now. I can't. I'm in it.

Jan Kowalczyk

Would you miss the building specifically? The city?

Pierre-Marc Bouchard

The guys. I'd miss the guys. The building, it's a building. The city is cold and flat and it's fine, I live here. But the guys, you spend so much time together. The travel, the bus, the... You're with them more than your family some months. So yeah, I'd miss the guys. The rest I can get back.

Jan Kowalczyk

Thirty-five. Can you see yourself at 35?

Pierre-Marc Bouchard

Thirty-five. That's old man. I don't know. Maybe I'm playing somewhere. Maybe I'm done. Maybe I'm in Sherbrooke watching the pee-wees. That would be good. That would be a good life. But I don't, I can't tell you where I'll be. I'm 21. I'm trying to get better right now, that's all I can do.

Jan Kowalczyk

One more thing. You're from Sherbrooke, you speak French, you live here in the Midwest. Do you feel like an outsider in this room or this city?

Pierre-Marc Bouchard

No. Not anymore. At the beginning, yeah. You walk around, nobody speaks French, you miss it. You want to call your mother in French. But now, no. And the guys, they try. Saku's from Finland so he gets it, being from somewhere else. Walz he just... he says a word wrong and laughs about it. It's fine. I'm from Quebec, everyone knows that. I don't hide it.

Jan Kowalczyk

Pierre-Marc Bouchard, thank you very much for sitting down with us.

Pierre-Marc Bouchard

Thank you, Jan. Good to be here.
