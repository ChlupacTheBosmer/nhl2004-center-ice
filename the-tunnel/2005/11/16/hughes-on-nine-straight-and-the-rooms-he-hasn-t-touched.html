Hughes on nine straight and the rooms he hasn't touched

The Chicago GM tells The Tunnel where $25.79M in profit goes while the building record shows nothing spent on it.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelWednesday, 16 November 2005

3:04 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Chicago Blackhawks is 2 and 8 and 4, on a nine-game losing streak, last in the Central with 10 points. The club posted $42.36M in revenue and $25.79M in profit, yet the building record shows no upgrade from Chicago since December. Equipment at 0, practice at 1, travel at 1, live sum of minus 17. Jason Hughes sat down with The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk in the studio to talk about the deadline, the money and the rooms.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here in the studio with Jason Hughes. Jason, two and eight and four, nine in a row, 10 points, last in the Central. The goals against is 47 in 15. What's the mood inside?

Jason Hughes

Not good. You know, you can't dress that up. We're getting outscored and we're losing enough to make the point over and over. The first period especially, at home, 1 and 8 in our own building, and that's... that's a room where something isn't right. Nine straight, I've been here long enough to know you don't fix that in one press conference. We're just, we're short right now.

Jan Kowalczyk

What the calendar has as the trade deadline is 123 days out. When that day comes, what are you? A buyer, a seller? Does the question apply at all to a club at two and eight?

Jason Hughes

Yeah, it applies, it just applies different. Look, everybody wants you to pick a lane in March. Buy, sell. Here's the thing, and I mean this, I'm not trading bodies off a roster that's already thinned out. Quint is out till November 24 with the ankle, Henry is back, the back end is... we've got four guys we trust and that's it. So no, I'm not moving a defenseman just to fill a box on the wire. If I'm at that day, I'm trying to add. But adding what, from whom, that's the part I don't have figured out yet.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let me put a few numbers on the table. Chicago took in $42.36M last season. Profit was $25.79M. Payroll sits at $48.74M. And the building record since the first of December shows zero upgrades from this club. Equipment at 0. Practice at 1. Travel at 1. Live sum of minus 17, third worst in the league. The money is there. Where is it going?

Jason Hughes

It stays in the club. That's the answer you don't want, I know. You want me to say, here's the new weight room, here's the new practice sheet. I'm not there. And look, you want me to say we should've done equipment two years ago, fine, I'll sign off on that. Probably should've. But I've watched clubs throw money at a building while the roster's still no good, and they lost the same games. The building is behind. I'll say that, I'm not arguing with the minus 17. But the reason we're losing nine in a row isn't the travel budget. It's what's on the ice.

Jan Kowalczyk

If you could move one track tomorrow. Equipment, practice, travel. Which one?

Jason Hughes

Practice. And I'll tell you why, because 1 and 8 at home. Those guys are skating on their own ice and we're losing. That's the practice rink. That's the room you prep in, the room where the lines come together. If they're not sharp when they step on it, something's wrong with the room they came from. But I can't give you a date. I can tell you I've been talking about it and I know what I'd do first.

Jan Kowalczyk

And what would you say to the fan base looking at 10 points and a building they know is old?

Jason Hughes

I'd say I see it too. I see it every morning when I walk in. I'm not, I don't get a different set of numbers than they do. The 10 points, the minus 17, all of it. I just... I've got $25.79M in profit and I've got a roster that's not good enough and a building that's behind. The fix is the same, it's time. And I know time is a terrible answer when you're nine straight down.

Jan Kowalczyk

Jason, thank you. Appreciate you sitting down.

Jason Hughes

Thank you, Jan. Back to you.
