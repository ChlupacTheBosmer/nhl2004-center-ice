Martin Won't Name His Starter For Saturday

The Panthers coach says his lines are under review for the first time this season after a 3-1 loss in Ottawa, and leaves his goaltender for the STL game unannounced.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelWednesday, 16 November 2005

2:34 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Florida Panthers dropped its second straight, losing 3-1 on the road at Ottawa Senators on Monday night to fall to 5-8-3. Head coach Jacques Martin spoke to The Tunnel's Marcus Bell in the tunnel after the game, addressing the lack of lineup changes this week and the goaltending decision looming for Thursday's home date with St. Louis Blues.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here, Jacques, thank you for staying. A 3-1 loss on the road, shots even at 21 apiece. What did you see in that one that kept you from getting out of the building with something?

Jacques Martin

You know, we had our looks. Twenty-one shots on the road, that's respectable. But the details, you know, we had two, three rushes where we didn't manage the puck out of our own zone and they made you pay. That's the difference. You get to the NHL and the other team takes what you give them.

Marcus Bell

You're two and eight on the road now, five and three at home. That's the widest split in the division. What's different when you're in someone else's building?

Jacques Martin

Well, I'll tell you what's different. You don't get the crowd behind you, you don't get the flow of the game going your way on those fifty-fifty calls. But I don't want to make excuses for it. We've got to be more structured on the road, we've got to compete for sixty minutes and we haven't done that away from home. That's on me and it's on them.

Marcus Bell

You've made no changes to your lines this week. No call-ups, no swaps in the top six. Is that coming?

Jacques Martin

It's coming if it needs to come. I'm not going to sit here in November and blow up four games because I lost two. But I watch every shift, I watch practice, and if a guy's not earning his spot, the spot changes. I'll make the calls I need to make. We'll see Thursday.

Marcus Bell

Talk to me about the net. Aebischer has started 15 of your 18 games, he's 6 and 9 with one shutout. Hurme is 0 and 2 in three starts, Potvin won the one game he played. Who's your guy for STL on Thursday?

Jacques Martin

You know what, I'm not going to answer that right now. I've got a game Thursday, I'll make the decision based on what I see tomorrow and Wednesday. David's played a lot of hockey for us, he's been our guy, and I appreciate that workload. But I'm not going to commit on a microphone.

Marcus Bell

Lukas Krajicek is out until December 9 with the abdomen. That's 11 games and five assists in 11 starts, he's been your third-pairing guy logging heavy minutes. Without him, who steps up on the back end?

Jacques Martin

Andreas Lilja and Kristian Kudroc have got to give us more. They're not the guys I'm pointing at, I just mean the math, you lose a body, the others get more ice and they have to answer for it. Kudroc's got six assists in 18 games, he's skating fine. We just need the pairings to hold together when the calls get tight.

Marcus Bell

Eric Messier's listed to return on the 18th. Will he be ready for Saturday?

Jacques Martin

The 18th means the 18th. He won't play Thursday. Ottawa's next, that's the first he could be in the mix. I'll know more after a couple practices.

Marcus Bell

One more thing. Your room has the second highest average morale in the league. The guys seem to be fine with you. Is that something you lean on, or does it bother you that the results don't match the mood?

Jacques Martin

You know, I've been around long enough to know that high morale and five and eight don't sit well together for long. You can like your coach, you can like your teammates, and you can still be a bad team. We need to put points on the board and that changes the mood for all the right reasons. Until then, it's noise. I like that they're together. I don't like that they're losing.

Marcus Bell

Jacques Martin, thank you again. Back to you.
