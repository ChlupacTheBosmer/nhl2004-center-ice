Adam Oates on the number they gave him at 19 and the number he put up at 43

The Bureau's Development Index had his potential at 53. He is 43, playing 18.8 minutes a night, and he still knows who told him he was too slow.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelWednesday, 30 November 2005

5:10 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Adam Oates77 sits 43 years old in the Colorado Avalanche room, the oldest man on a 28-man roster that averages 30.0 years. The Sweater's profile this week calls it 'The Long Way Round,' pointing at the 27-grade gap between the Bureau's potential figure of 53 and his base grade of 80. Marcus Bell sat across from him in the studio.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here with Adam Oates from Colorado Avalanche®. Adam, good to see you. The Sweater ran 'The Long Way Round' this week, and the title leans on that 27-grade gap between your potential and your base. You saw the piece?

Adam Oates

Yeah, I saw it. Twenty-seven grades. I'll tell you what, that number don't mean much to me anymore, you know. The Bureau puts a number on you when you're 22, 23, they figure out your ceiling and they write it down and that's that. And I've spent, what, 20 years trying to prove a number wrong. So no, it don't bother me. It never did.

Marcus Bell

But it's a low ceiling for a guy who won the Masterton last season and is still dressing for a Cup contender. 80 base, 77 today. What do you think they saw wrong?

Adam Oates

Well, you go back to where they saw it. Scouts looked at me at Markham, 159 points in a season, and the word was too slow. Same word at RPI. And look, I won't argue the skating. I wasn't fast. I spent a whole summer with a skating coach after my freshman year just trying to keep up at that level. But the hands, the vision, the read... you can't teach that in a summer. And maybe that's what they didn't put in the box score of the kid from Markham.

Marcus Bell

Before Markham you were in the OLA, the Etobicoke Eclipse. 181 points in 19 games in 1981, the Bobby Allan as leading scorer, one game with 19 assists. You left Senior A lacrosse in '84 to go to hockey. Most people would call that a detour.

Adam Oates

No, no. That's backward. Lacrosse was the thing. I was good at it. I loved it. Hockey was the detour I fell into because somebody needed a centre on a Tuesday night. You know what lacrosse teaches you? You can't stop your feet. The crease is in your face, a guy is on your back, the ball's hot, and you still gotta make the pass. That's a two-on-one in the slot with a defenceman on your hip. I've been doing the same play since I was 16, you just changed the stick for a shorter one and the ball for a puck.

Marcus Bell

And you were a gas station attendant at 19. You'd dropped out of school. You called yourself a punk in that profile interview. What happened between the pump and RPI?

Adam Oates

You know, I was a punk. I won't dress it up. I liked the beer, I liked the noise, I didn't like school. And at 19, sitting in a station in Toronto, pumping gas, you look up and you think... this is it? This is the plan? And then you get a call. Paul Allen was at an OPJAHL game, he's there to watch some other kid, and he sees me make a play, and he comes over and says, 'You wanna go to school?' And I'd just dropped out of school. So you can imagine how I answered that.

Marcus Bell

And that becomes 85 wins, three straight ECAC titles, back-to-back All-American seasons, an NCAA championship in '85. You led the team in assists three years running. By the time you signed with Detroit, five teams had tried to get you. What changed?

Adam Oates

Everything changed. And nothing changed. I'm still the guy who's not fast enough. Paul Allen just gave me a place to prove it didn't matter. And the skating coach that summer, yeah, I got faster. Not fast. But fast enough that when you pair it with the hands, all of a sudden the scouts had to look twice. I think that's the whole story right there. The hands were always there. The legs just had to catch up.

Marcus Bell

Let me pull you forward to now. You're 43. You're in a room that averages 30, seven guys 33 or older, two kids under 24. You're the oldest man in there by a stretch. Who's the captain?

Adam Oates

Joe Sakic. Joe's the captain. He's been the captain. That's settled, you know, that's not, I don't need to talk about that. But you asked who the leader is in there and that's a different question. Peter is the best player in our room. 95 on the Index, he doesn't need to say anything, you watch him practice and you get it. Paul is the same, quiet, but the man shows up every single day and the kid at 24 sees him and he gets it. What I do is... different.

Marcus Bell

Tell me what different looks like. You're in your 23rd game, 10 points, 18.8 a night. What's your job in there right now?

Adam Oates

My job is to be there. That's it. That's the whole thing. I'm 43, I've been doing this since before half these guys were born, and I come in at the same time every day, I do the same warm-up, I do the same faceoff work, I sit the same chair. And the young guy, he walks past me and he sees a 43-year-old man doing the same boring stuff he has to do. And that's worth something. You can't lecture a kid into caring. You just gotta do the thing for long enough that he can't look away from it.

Marcus Bell

The Masterton you won last season, that's a perseverance award. You scored 71 points at 41 and the league said that meant something. Does the award sit in a place where you can see it?

Adam Oates

It sits in a closet. I'm not being modest, it's a nice trophy, but it's in a closet in the house and my wife uses it for the remote controls. No, you know what, I'll keep that. It means something because it's the one thing that wasn't about the hands or the assists. It was about being here. About doing the thing when it's not fun. And I think that's... yeah. I think that's the one I'd want on the shelf if I was picking.

Marcus Bell

You're on a three-year deal, $4.52M. You'll be 44 in August. What does 45 look like? What does the end of this road look like?

Adam Oates

I don't think about that, you know. I think about tomorrow's practice and whether Peter and Paul and I can work the power play a little sharper. The body's good, the morale's good, the legs are where they need to be for the minutes I get. And I've been here long enough to know you don't decide when it's over until it's over. I had people telling me at 20 that I'd never play professional hockey. I'm not going to make up my own retirement date now.

Marcus Bell

One more. You came up through lacrosse, through Junior A, through a college nobody's recruiting for. If you could sit across from the 19-year-old at the gas station, what would you say to him?

Adam Oates

I'd say stay in school. No. No, I'd say... I'd say the guy at the pump is gonna get a phone call from somebody named Paul Allen. And he's gonna not believe it. And he's gotta believe it. And then he's gotta spend a summer skating when he doesn't want to. And then he's gotta go play centre next to Joe Sakic and Peter Forsberg and not be embarrassed. And that's it. That's the whole pitch. Don't quit between the gas station and the phone call. Just... hang in there for the call.

Marcus Bell

Adam Oates from Colorado Avalanche®, 43 years old, still putting points up in the middle of the order. Thank you, Adam. Back to you.
