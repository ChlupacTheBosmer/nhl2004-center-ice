Sundin, Maple Leafs win on 12 shots in overtime at Ottawa

Mats Sundin says Roberto Luongo's absence meant nothing tonight because Maxime Ouellet stood on his head for three periods and a quarter of a fourth.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelSaturday, 26 November 2005

2:02 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Mats Sundin92 had one assist as Toronto Maple Leafs won 4-3 in overtime at Ottawa Senators on Friday night, a road game they finished with 12 shots on goal against 58. The Maple Leafs are now 6-1 away from home and sit first in the Northeast Division with 32 points.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here with Mats Sundin, thank you for stopping. Twelve shots. Fifty-eight the other way. And you walk out with two points and a win. How do you make sense of that on a whiteboard Monday morning?

Mats Sundin

I don't think you make sense of it, no. Ouellet was... he was the game. You know, the first twenty minutes, they're in zone and we're, uh, we are trying to get out and he just, of course he stops everything. And after that you just, you play for him. You try to keep it simple. For sure.

Jan Kowalczyk

And you were 3-3 after three. You only had about ten or eleven shots through regulation. Was there a moment in the third where you felt the legs going, where you thought this one might break the other way?

Mats Sundin

Well, I think... no, I don't feel that. We had four guys score tonight, four different guys. Safronov, Lundmark, Rick, Jackman. When different names go on the board, the room feels... I don't know, it feels like there's more than one way to get the point. So even on twelve shots, of course, it feels okay.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're 6-1 on the road now, Mats. Six and one. This is a building that is 5-6 at home and they're pushing, 58 shots. What is it about playing away that suits this group?

Mats Sundin

I think we play a little, uh, a little more free. At home there is a little more, I don't know, the first shift, you try to do too much. On the road it is easier to just... I think we played good tonight. Simple. Get it out, get back, let the goalie work.

Jan Kowalczyk

The crowd here, they got loud by the second period, I could hear it from the press box. Does that help, does that make you sharper?

Mats Sundin

It helps, for sure. You know, when they're loud it is easy to focus. You know what they want and you know what you want. I think it is harder when your own building is quiet, that you have to... to make the energy yourself. Here they give it to you. You just try to play the other way, of course.

Jan Kowalczyk

Last time we talked, in January, you mentioned playing ahead of the play, getting into the slot before the puck crosses the line. Did that feel like part of the assist tonight, or was it something different?

Mats Sundin

It is similar, yeah. I was... the play comes to me and the man is already going, so I just put it where he is going, not where he is. That is, that is all it is. I don't take credit. He scores, I get a point. That is fine.

Jan Kowalczyk

Two on the board, you'll want the bus home. Thank you, Mats. Jan Kowalczyk, from the tunnel in Ottawa. Back to you.
