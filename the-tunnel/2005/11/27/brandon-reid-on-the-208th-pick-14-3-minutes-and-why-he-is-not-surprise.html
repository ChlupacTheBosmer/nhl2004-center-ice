Brandon Reid on the 208th pick, 14.3 minutes, and why he is not surprised by 29 points

The seventh-rounder with the expiring $0.60M deal and the 108 pace says the hard season taught him more than the good ones, and he wants the minutes in the tough spots.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelSunday, 27 November 2005

4:45 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Brandon Reid79 came to St. Louis Blues on March 4 of last spring and has put up 29 points in 22 games on 18.9 minutes a night, a 108 pace that has him on the league's scoring sheet for the first time in his career. His contract pays $0.60M with zero years left. He is 24, from Kirkland, Quebec, 68 inches tall, the 208th pick of the 2000 draft. Marcus Bell sat with him in the studio on Sunday.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here with Brandon Reid, who's been a problem for everyone in the league these first six weeks. Brandon, 29 points in 22 games, 108 pace. You're on the scoring sheet for the first time in your life. Did you see that coming?

Brandon Reid

Yeah, I mean... you work, you hope. I'm not, I don't think about the sheet. I think about the next game. But yeah, it feels good. The guys around me make it easy, you know? Keith and Doug and Pavol, they're finding me and I just got to put it in.

Marcus Bell

Let's talk about the profile, the Sweater piece. They called it 'Seventh Round, Zero Years.' You're the 208th pick, you're making $0.60M, and you've got zero years left. Does that framing bother you?

Brandon Reid

Nah. For sure it's true. That's the facts. I'm not going to, you know, I'm not going to be mad at a number someone wrote down in 2000. That's just what happened. I'm here now. The money is what it is. I'm playing, I'm scoring, and we'll see what we see at the end of the year.

Marcus Bell

You're from Kirkland. Walk me back there. How does a kid from Kirkland end up on a sheet at 208th?

Brandon Reid

Well, I mean, everybody in Kirkland played hockey. You grow up, it's, it's the whole world. You go outside, you're on the pond or you're in the rink, and that's the day. My father, he put me on skates when I was small, you know, very small. And I just, I was always going fast. That's what I could do. I was never the biggest. I was never the strongest. I was the one going the fastest and making the other guys turn around.

Marcus Bell

Did he play? Your father.

Brandon Reid

A little, yeah. Nothing, nothing serious. But he played, and he knew, you know, he knew what the game was. He taught me to, what do you call it, to protect the puck with your body. That's funny now, you know? He was trying to teach me to be tough and I just, I'm not. But he taught me to keep it away from them. I did that part.

Marcus Bell

You're 68 inches, 165 pounds. Your speed is 99, your acceleration is 99. Your toughness is 50. You know what those grades say.

Brandon Reid

Yeah, I know. Yeah, that's, that's honest. I'm not going to pretend I'm going to hit anybody. I'm going to go the other way. That's the thing, you know? Everybody has, everybody has one thing. My one thing is I can't stop. You put me in open ice and, and good luck to you. That's the job I got.

Marcus Bell

The 2003-04 season. 58 points, 73 games. But 14.3 minutes a night. That's a fourth-liner's minutes on a guy putting up 58 points. How hard was that?

Brandon Reid

That was... yeah. That was hard. I scored when I got the ice. I did. But I wasn't out there enough. You know, you're on the bench and the game is going and you're thinking, I could help you right now. I could. And he didn't, I mean the coach, he didn't trust me in those minutes. And that's, that's his right. That's his job. But it's, it's a hard thing to sit and watch. You're scoring in practice, you're scoring in games when you get the chance, and you're still sitting.

Marcus Bell

Did you think it was over? That you weren't going to get another shot?

Brandon Reid

No. No, I don't think I ever thought that. But you think different. You think, what am I doing wrong? What do I have to show them that I'm not showing? And the answer is... you can't show it from the bench. You can't prove it sitting down. That's what I learned. You need the ice. That's it. Everything else is just noise.

Marcus Bell

And then March 4, you're on a plane to St. Louis. What's that conversation like? The trade comes down and you get the call?

Brandon Reid

They called me into the room, they told me. And I was, honestly, I was relieved. That sounds bad, you know, leaving a place. But I needed a new room. I needed somebody to look at me and say, okay, play. That's what I needed. And here they did. And I've got the minutes now. 18.9, yeah. It's not 22 minutes. It's not nothing.

Marcus Bell

You missed time this winter with an abdomen thing. Back on February 9. Did that slow your start here or was it behind you by then?

Brandon Reid

It cost me some time, for sure. You're coming back and you're not sharp for a little while, maybe three, four games. You're skating, you're not, you're not in the play yet. But by the time you play your first real game here in the fall, I mean it's done. It's done. The guys were good to me when I came back. Doug especially. He's been in the league a long time, he knows how it is, you get hurt, you come back, you need the puck to come to you for a shift or two. He did that.

Marcus Bell

You've got Keith Tkachuk on the wing, Doug Weight at centre, Chris Pronger back there, Pavol Demitra on the other side. What does it feel like to put up points next to those names?

Brandon Reid

It's, you know what, it's funny. Keith, he just goes to the net and they can't stop going there. And Doug sees everything before you do. I just have to be where I should be. That's the whole thing. I don't have to make the play. I just got to be open and go fast, and the puck is there. Pavol too, he's, he's a magician with the stick. I just show up and, and they give me the chance.

Marcus Bell

Zero years on the deal. You're 24. You've got the pace. What do you want from the next four months?

Brandon Reid

I want the minutes in the tough spots. That's, that's what I want. I want him to put me out there when we're killing one, or when it's the last two minutes and we need a goal. That's what makes you a player in this league. Not the 29 points. The tough minutes. I'm not there yet. I'm getting closer. And, and the rest, the contract, that's, that's for my guy. I just play.

Marcus Bell

Is there anything you'd want to say to the guy who picked you 208th in 2000? Any message?

Brandon Reid

No. For sure not. He was right too, in his way. I mean, 50th would've been nice, the money would've been better. But no, honestly no. He picked me at 208 and here I am and I'm playing. That's, that's fine. That's all I need.

Marcus Bell

Brandon Reid from St. Louis. Thanks very much, Brandon.

Brandon Reid

Thank you, Marcus.
