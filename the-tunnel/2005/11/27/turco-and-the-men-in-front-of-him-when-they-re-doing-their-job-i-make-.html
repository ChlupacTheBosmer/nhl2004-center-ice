Turco and the men in front of him: 'When they're doing their job, I make the save'

The Dallas goaltender credits a quiet night against Phoenix to a defensive group he says has tightened since December, and calls Jere Lehtinen's two-goal game the kind of gift a goalie lives for.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelSunday, 27 November 2005

1:57 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Marty Turco95 turned aside 17 of 18 shots as Dallas Stars beat Phoenix Coyotes 2-1 at home on November 25, moving Dallas to 14-5-3 and first in the Pacific. Jere Lehtinen84 scored twice for the Stars, his second multi-goal game of the season. Turco spoke with Marcus Bell after the game about the defensive group in front of him and staying sharp on a quiet night.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here with Marty Turco. Marty, two and one, the Stars move to fourteen and five, first in the Pacific. Jere Lehtinen had two goals tonight. What does that look like from your end?

Marty Turco

Oh it's, you know, it's a treat. Jere's been finding soft spots all year. Tonight the puck just found him twice and he buried both. You know when your right winger's scoring you sleep better at night. That's the honest answer.

Marcus Bell

Phoenix only managed 18 shots tonight. Your group in front of you, the defensemen, the back-checking, how is that unit finding its shape?

Marty Turco

Yeah I mean, it's been clicking. You look at Des and Zubov, those two are always talking, they know where everybody is. And the forwards, Morrow's been relentless down low, he doesn't give guys a free pass in the slot. When you've got guys that committed to the defensive side, the numbers take care of themselves. That's what happened tonight.

Marcus Bell

Seventeen saves on eighteen shots though. You weren't called on a whole lot. How do you stay locked in when the volume's that low?

Marty Turco

That's the trick, isn't it. Every goalie's answer is stay in the moment and you just, you hate saying it because it sounds like a cliché, but it is. You can't let your mind go to the third period when it's the first and you haven't seen a shot in six minutes. I make myself talk to the box, wave guys back, do whatever I can to stay present. You know, the quiet nights are the ones that get you if you let them.

Marcus Bell

You and Jan talked after the Philadelphia loss back in December, about owning that first goal. You've got a different kind of night tonight. Does it change how you process these, when you're not the one with a bad goal on you?

Marty Turco

Yeah it does. I mean obviously the one and five was tough. But I think that was a long time ago. We've grown as a group since then. The structure's tighter, the communication's better. I don't beat myself up the same way I used to because I can see the guys in front of me are doing their job. And if they're doing their job, I'm going to make the save. That's the whole thing.

Marcus Bell

Modano had two assists tonight. He's been feeding that left side all season.

Marty Turco

Mike's been Mike. You know, he sees the play before it happens, always has. I'm just happy I'm on the right side of it tonight instead of the wrong one. He makes everybody around him better, I don't care what anybody says.

Marcus Bell

Marty, thank you. Good night. Back to you.

Marty Turco

Thanks, Marcus. Hey, enjoy the rest of your night.
