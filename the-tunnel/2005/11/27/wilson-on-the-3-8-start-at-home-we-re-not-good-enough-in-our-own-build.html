Wilson on the 3-8 start at home: 'We're not good enough in our own building'

The Sharks' coach explains the goaltending switch back to Nabokov and frames a four-game week as the reset his room needs.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelSunday, 27 November 2005

4:22 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Ron Wilson's San Jose Sharks lost 2-3 at home to St. Louis Blues on November 25, dropping to 3-8 on their own ice and into a two-game skid at 20 points in the Pacific Division. Wilson addressed the home-ice struggle, the decision to scratch Evgeni Nabokov87 in favor of Jeff Hackett83 for that game, and a stretch that puts San Jose Sharks in Colorado Avalanche tonight before home games against Calgary Flames and 6-0-on-the-road New Jersey Devils.

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here, in the corridor at the Pond, with Ron Wilson. Ron, two straight, seven and eight and four, twenty points. You're three and eight at home. What's the mood in there right now?

Ron Wilson

Not good, obviously. You don't win three of eleven in your own building and feel great about it. I think guys are frustrated, they should be. We're not good enough in our own building, and that's the one that sticks with me more than the two losses. The two losses I can look at and find the detail, tonight was, I think, our fourth line had a bad period and that's where the third goal comes from. But three and eight, that's not one bad period. That's a pattern.

And you're five and four on the road. So it's the same men, different ice.

Ron Wilson

Yeah, I've thought about that. You know, I've thought about that a lot actually. I think there's something about, when you're on the road, you're in a hotel, you've got nothing to do but come here and play, and the edges are, they're a little sharper because you don't have, I don't know, you don't have the comfort. At home you sleep in your own bed, you drive to the rink, you think you've got it handled. And then the other team comes in and they're hungry and you're just, you're flat. That's what three and eight looks like to me.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let me ask about the net. Evgeni Nabokov wasn't even dressed tonight. Jeff Hackett started. He's back tomorrow against Colorado. What was that decision?

Ron Wilson

Well, you know, I had you up in January and you said get back to me on the goaltending once we finish that road trip, and I, I think I owe you a longer answer than I gave you then. But here's the short version. Jeff's two and two. He hasn't lost. Two and two, four games. And Geno's, Geno's been working hard, but two and two in the back of your mind says start the guy who hasn't lost. That's how I saw it. Simple as that. And Geno handles that fine, he knows it's not a statement, it's a, it's a four-game thing.

Jan Kowalczyk

But Geno's played sixteen of your twenty. Eighty percent of the workload. Is there a plan here, or are you just, going game to game?

Ron Wilson

There's a plan. The plan is, Geno plays when he's the guy. Jeff plays when he's earned the start. And right now, two and two, he earned it. That's how I've handled it since I got here. You can't get cute with two goalies in this league. You put your number one in when his number two is beating him in practice and in games. Right now Jeff's beating Geno in that sense. Geno'll bounce back, he's good enough, he's, he's one of the best in this league. But I've got to ride the hot hand and tonight it was Jeff's.

Jan Kowalczyk

Tonight's game. Three and two, you gave up two goals on eighteen shots. Twenty on the other side. That's a lot of chances and you don't get enough out of it.

Ron Wilson

Yeah. Obviously. Twenty shots is, it's not nothing, but we didn't, we had some good looks and Jeff's not going to apologize for making them. But I think the, the, we had maybe three or four two-on-ones where we didn't, we didn't make the right play. And that's a, that's a compete thing, that's a details thing. You've got to bury those. And the third period we were, I mean, we were pushing, but we were pushing without a plan and that's, that's a different problem than not pushing.

Jan Kowalczyk

You've got Colorado tonight, who've won four straight. Then Calgary Sunday, New Jersey Monday. New Jersey's six and oh on the road. Then Boston on the third. That's, what, four in six days against teams that are all playing good hockey.

Ron Wilson

Yeah, it's a run. It's a good run. You know what, I'll take that. If somebody told me in October I'd have four games against those four clubs in a week, I'd say good, that tells me where I am. Colorado's got the four straight, they've got confidence, they've got Adam Foote playing like himself again. New Jersey's, New Jersey's got the best record in the Eastern Conference and Brodeur's in there. But here's the thing, we're three and eight at home. We've got nothing to lose in this stretch. And sometimes, , sometimes you need to lose a couple more before a group says enough is enough. And I'd rather that be this week than January.

You mentioned the fourth line's second period tonight. You've got a few new faces in the lineup. Marcel Hossa played, Mike Rathje's still out. How are you sorting the bottom six?

Ron Wilson

Well, Hossa is, he's working. Four points in twenty games, that's not where I want him, but he's, he's earning ice. He's getting it because he's competing, and that's how I see it. The fourth line's got to, it's got to give you forty-eight seconds of, of honest hockey, and some nights we get it and some nights we don't. Tonight's second period, we didn't. That's all I can say about it.
