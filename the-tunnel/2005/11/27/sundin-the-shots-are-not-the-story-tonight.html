Sundin: The shots are not the story tonight

After a 4-2 win over Washington, the Leafs captain pointed to the men in front of the net and the one behind them, and said the shot column is not the way to read this game.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelSunday, 27 November 2005

2:17 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Mats Sundin92 had one assist and no goals in Toronto's 4-2 win over Washington on Saturday, his 16th point in 22 games this season. Toronto outshot by nine, won by two, and moved to 34 points and first in the Northeast with a three-game win streak. The 34-year-old captain spoke about goaltending depth, the road, and what he does now that he did not do a few seasons ago.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here with Mats Sundin after a four and two win over Washington. Twenty-one shots for you, thirty against. Three in a row, thirty-four points, first place in the Northeast. How do you win that game with fewer shots?

Mats Sundin

I think it is not... it is not about the number of shots. We have the chances we need and we put them in. Thirty for them, yes, but many are from the outside, from the point, the kind you do not worry about as much. The big thing is we scored four and they scored two. That is the game.

Jan Kowalczyk

Thirty shots your goaltender had to face tonight. What does that look like from your side of the blue line? What do you see that the shot column does not show?

Mats Sundin

I think you see the traffic. You see the bodies in front, the guys who block the one-timer and then get up and go over the boards. That is... people see thirty shots and they say the goaltender had a good night. Maybe. But the team in front of him had a good night too. That is where the saves come from.

Jan Kowalczyk

Roberto Luongo sat tonight. Your Conn Smythe man, last year's Jennings winner, watching from the bench. How does a room keep that competition when one of them is not in the net?

Mats Sundin

For sure. In practice it is the same every day. Everybody wants in, you know. Roberto pushes... the other guy is better because Roberto is there. And the one who plays tonight, he plays because of that competition. That is how it should be. Everyone gets a chance.

Jan Kowalczyk

Four different names on the scoresheet tonight for the goals. Rostislav Klesla among them. How much does the room value that spread, versus one line carrying it?

Mats Sundin

Yeah, we want that. You know, if one line scores every game, the other team knows what to do. When it goes around, all four lines, it is very hard to prepare. Klesla scores tonight, maybe the next game is somebody else. That is how it should go.

Jan Kowalczyk

Six and one on the road. That's the best record in the league away from home. What do those games ask that the ones at home do not?

Mats Sundin

I think on the road you play simpler. You take care of the puck because if you turn it over the building gets loud and they get the momentum. At home you can take a little more... you know, you can play a little more open. On the road it is wait, be patient, find your moment. And we have found it six times.

Jan Kowalczyk

You told me back in January you were playing ahead of the play more, keeping pace with Marian Gaborik on short shifts. Still the approach at thirty-four?

Mats Sundin

Yeah, of course. I am thirty-four so I do not chase the same way I did. I let the puck come to me. I wait in the right spot. I think that is why I still have some points. I save the legs for when it matters.

Jan Kowalczyk

Mats, thank you. Enjoy the three in a row. Jan Kowalczyk for The Tunnel.
