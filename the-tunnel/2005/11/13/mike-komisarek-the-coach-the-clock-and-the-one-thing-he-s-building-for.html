Mike Komisarek: the coach, the clock, and the one thing he's building for after

The Canadiens' seventh-overall pick on why a Lithuanian coach matters more than his contract year, and what a speed-95 defenseman from West Islip sees on the other side of the glass

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelSunday, 13 November 2005

4:59 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Mike Komisarek81 sits down with Marcus Bell after a 14-game stretch that has him at 13 points and 46 penalty minutes in Montreal Canadiens blue. The Bureau has him at 82 overall, up 10 on the Development Index, with a base grade of 77 and a speed grade of 95. His contract pays $1.10M and expires after this season.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Mike, thanks for sitting down. Marcus Bell here from The Tunnel, and we've got you in the chair instead of the corridor, which I think everybody prefers. You're fourteen games in, two goals, eleven assists, thirteen points. That's a different pace than last year. What's it feel like from inside?

Mike Komisarek

Yeah, it feels, uh, it feels like the right amount. Last year was, look, I don't know if you get those back. Eighty-two points in eighty-two games, that's not, you know, that's not a thing you bank on. I'm playing the minutes I can handle and the games are where they're supposed to be.

Marcus Bell

The Sweater ran a profile on you with a title that's hard to miss: 'Seventh Overall, Zero Years Left.' It talks about what speed-95 costs at a $1.10M contract. Does that framing bother you? The money side?

Mike Komisarek

No, man. No. Obviously you look at the number and you go, okay, that's what I'm getting. But I'm not the one writing the cheque, you know? I've got zero years left and I just, I want to be good at the thing I'm doing. The number takes care of itself if I get to the thing right.

Marcus Bell

So the speed, you're 6'5" and 225 and you run a 95 on the Bureau's speed grade. What does it feel like on the ice? You're the fastest man out there at your size?

Mike Komisarek

I mean, not the fastest, you know. There's guys who are quicker through a gap. But when I get it going, uh, I can cover a lot of ice. A lot. And guys start turning the other way when you've got that on your side plus, plus the shot. They don't want to block that.

Marcus Bell

That shot power grade is 93. You get those opportunities with the room you've got in front of you? Zednik is sitting there at 82, Nylander at 78, you've got Kilger and Bulis too.

Mike Komisarek

Yeah, and look, those guys, they make the first read easier for me. I come in and I'm, I'm trying to, you know, I don't want to over-handle it because my, my breakout with the stick isn't exactly where it should be. So I get it to Zed or Michael and they hold it and I can step in. That's the, that's the whole thing. I'm a one-touch guy right now.

Marcus Bell

You've been open about that. Your puck handling grades, the breakout at 52, the offensive awareness at 55. That's unusual for a guy who put up 82 last year. How do you square that?

Mike Komisarek

Yeah. Yeah. Look, I had a, a year where everything went in. That happens. And I'm not, I don't sit around going, well why didn't I make the play there, I'm going to get better at it. I just, the speed lets me get out of bad decisions. That's what it is. I make a bad read, I can skate out of it before someone punishes me. Right now that works. I don't think that works forever.

Marcus Bell

Let me bring you back to the background sheet. You're a student of Aleksey Nikiforov, the Lithuanian coach. You're still working with him. What did he change? Because the kid who came out of Michigan was already a big fast shot, and this guy added something.

Mike Komisarek

He made me shut up and think, honestly. You know what I mean? At Michigan I was, uh, I was the loudest guy in the room. Everybody's talking, everybody's laughing, and Aleksey, he just sits there and watches and then he says one thing. One thing about where your weight is, or about the angle of your stick, or about, you know, about why you went the other way. And it's, it's always about the why. Not the what.

Marcus Bell

He's a Lithuanian coach teaching an American kid from West Islip. What's the culture gap, if there is one?

Mike Komisarek

No, no, there's no gap, man. The game is the game. You know what, I grew up on Long Island playing in Suffolk PAL and then going to St. Anthony's and then the Jr. Coyotes with Gary Dineen, and then the NTDP. I've been around guys from everywhere. Aleksey just, he doesn't talk like me. He's, his English is, it's fine but he says things like, 'you are fast but you are not quick.' And that's, that's the whole thing. That's the difference. That's what I work on.

Marcus Bell

Let me stay on Nikiforov for a second because the title of this is 'the coach who changed you.' Is he the one who changed you, or was there someone else earlier, Dineen, Michigan?

Mike Komisarek

Dineen made me a hockey player. Michigan made me a professional. Aleksey made me think like one. They're all, they're all different pieces. But yeah, he's the one who changed the most in the shortest time because I came to him already, you know, already the tools were there. He just rearranged the order I use them in.

Marcus Bell

Forty-six penalty minutes in fourteen games. That's over three a night. Is that part of the rearranging or is that just you?

Mike Komisarek

That's me, man. That's the body thing. My checking is at 89 but my fighting is at 51. You do the math on that. I hit people and then I get in trouble because I shouldn't be there after the hit. That's, you know, that's a discipline thing. That's, maybe that's the next thing Aleksey fixes.

Marcus Bell

Let's go forward. You're 23. Your contract's done in May. The Development Index has you up 10. But nobody plays forever. Have you thought about what the other side looks like?

Mike Komisarek

Yeah. I think about it. I think about it more than a 23-year-old probably should. But when you've got zero years left, you kind of, you're forced to look at it, you know? I'm not going to be playing at 38. I don't think.

Marcus Bell

What is it? Coaching? Something with the game?

Mike Komisarek

I think it's, I think it's the same thing Aleksey does. The watching. The one thing at the right time. I don't want to be, you know, I don't want to be the loud guy in the room telling guys to hit harder. That's not, that's not the thing. I want to be the one who says 'you are fast but you are not quick' and means it in a way that actually lands. You know what I'm saying?

Marcus Bell

So you'd follow that path. Teaching, developing guys.

Mike Komisarek

I don't know yet. I'm 23. But yeah, if the game takes me somewhere after, it'll be that. It's, it's the part I like watching. The part that makes the kid from Suffolk PAL into the kid at the NTDP into whatever this is. That chain, that's what I want to be a link in.

Marcus Bell

One last thing. You came in seventh overall in 2001. The Michigan year, you were an All-American, you had the CCHA title, you went to two NCAA tournaments. Did that two years at Michigan, with Eric Nystrom next to you, did that make the transition easier? Coming to Montreal?

Mike Komisarek

Yeah. Yeah it did. You know, Eric's from the same place I'm from. We went through the same stuff. And Michigan, it's, it's a big program, the building's big, the expectations are big. So you come to Montreal and the building's big and the expectations are, well, you know, they're Montreal. So it's not like I walked in and I was overwhelmed. I'd been somewhere where people expected you to be good every night. That helped.

Marcus Bell

Mike, I appreciate you sitting down with us. Good luck with the next fourteen games and the rest of the year. Marcus Bell, The Tunnel. Back to you in the studio.
