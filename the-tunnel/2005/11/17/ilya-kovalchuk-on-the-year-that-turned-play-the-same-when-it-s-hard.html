Ilya Kovalchuk on the Year That Turned: 'Play the Same When It's Hard'

A season after 138 points, Kovalchuk has 16 in 16 games, and he told The Tunnel's Marcus Bell the easy year taught him not to trust the easy year.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelThursday, 17 November 2005

3:38 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Ilya Kovalchuk89 scored 138 points for Atlanta Thrashers a season ago and took the Lady Byng with it. At 22 he has 16 points in 16 games this season, 22.1 minutes a night, and a room that stopped going in for him. He sat with Marcus Bell in the studio to talk about the year that turned, and about the days when there is no game.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Ilya, Marcus Bell, The Tunnel. Thanks for the time. Let's start with last year, because it's the tape everything gets measured against. 79 games, 63 goals, 75 assists, 138 points, second in the league behind Jagr, and the Lady Byng at the end of it. What was that season like from the inside?

Ilya Kovalchuk

You know, that year, everything I shoot is goal. I don't know how. I don't think, and puck go in. When I think, no goal, is simple like that. So I play, I don't count, and after, my friend call me, he say 138. I say, hmm, ok. But then the summer, is done. New year, everybody starts at zero.

Marcus Bell

It hasn't been zero, though. Sixteen games, 10 goals, 6 assists, 16 points, 22.1 minutes a night. The goals are there. The points aren't.

Ilya Kovalchuk

No. Six assists, 16 games. For me, that is nothing. You know, I like to pass. Last year I give 75. So something is, uh, not right. Maybe I hold the puck too long. Maybe the guy is not in the spot. I watch it, the coaches watch it.

Marcus Bell

Which one is it?

Ilya Kovalchuk

Is both. Honest, both. I can pass better. But you need somebody in the spot too. Danny, when he's there, he's there. He can finish. Some nights we're not on the ice same shift, or we are and I don't see him. That last one, that's on me.

Marcus Bell

Our profile this month calls you a man who's outplayed his ceiling and run out of room. Is this the season that went wrong?

Ilya Kovalchuk

Wrong? I don't use that word. Wrong is when you stop working. I don't stop. But is different, yes. Last year was easy. This year I must find the game again. That is what I take: when it go easy, don't believe it. Never easy.

Marcus Bell

So what does finding it look like, on the ice?

Ilya Kovalchuk

Simple. Everybody say play simple, coaches say play simple, and is true. When it's not going, don't try the between-the-legs. Skate, shoot, go to the net. First period, one shot, two shots, you feel the puck, and the rest comes. I know this. When I forget it, I lose two, three games.

Marcus Bell

There was the shoulder, back in the spring.

Ilya Kovalchuk

Is nothing. Two days there, I come back same week. You tape it, you go. Shoulder is fine.

Marcus Bell

All right. A day off. What does Ilya Kovalchuk do with a day off?

Ilya Kovalchuk

You want the good answer. I sleep. Ten hours, maybe more. The body, after the travel, it needs it. That's the real answer. Nobody likes it.

Marcus Bell

That's the whole day? Sleep?

Ilya Kovalchuk

No, no. I eat. I call my mother, she's in Tver, you know Tver? That is where I grow up. She ask, did you eat, did you sleep. Every time. We don't talk hockey. She don't know what is a power play. Is good for me, that.

Marcus Bell

Do you watch the league on a day off?

Ilya Kovalchuk

Yeah, I watch. Is my job, but I like it too. I watch the good players. You take one thing, next game you try it. You are never too good to watch.

Marcus Bell

You mentioned the Book. The Bureau has you at 89. The people who watch you every night say there's nothing left to add.

Ilya Kovalchuk

Nothing to add? I'm 22. You know what I add? The same game every night. Not three points, then nothing. Same. If I do that, the number, up, down, I don't care about the number. I care about the Cup. That's the only thing on my list.

Marcus Bell

When it's going like this, who do you lean on?

Ilya Kovalchuk

The guy next to you, he feels the same, so you talk. You know, when you lose, first thing, you want to go home, don't talk to nobody. That's wrong. That's when you must talk. So we talk, we skate, we go again. It's not fun right now. It's the job.

Marcus Bell

What's the hardest part of a day off?

Ilya Kovalchuk

The quiet. The room is loud, the rink is loud, then a day off and there's nothing. First two hours, I don't know what to do with my hands. Then I sleep, and it's fine.

Marcus Bell

So the season the profile's about. What's the one thing you take out of it?

Ilya Kovalchuk

The one thing? Play the same when it's hard. That's it. Before, I think, if I score, I am good. Now I know, if we win, I'm good. Last year I score 63. This year I want something different. I want the spring.

Marcus Bell

Meaning?

Ilya Kovalchuk

Meaning April. Meaning May. You know what I mean.

Marcus Bell

I do. Ilya, thanks for the time. For The Tunnel, Marcus Bell in the studio. Back to you.
