Ilya Kovalchuk: the shoulder, the Byng, and the coach who said again

Kovalchuk gives Marcus Bell the rookie year a shoulder cut short, the Lady Byng he keeps on the shelf, and the Spartak drills that made him quick.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelSaturday, 15 October 2005

2:16 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Ilya Kovalchuk90 has 5 points in 2 games for Atlanta Thrashers, 3 of them goals, at 20.0 minutes a night, and the Bureau's Book has him at 92 overall, five up on the Development Index. The 22-year-old left wing won the Lady Byng Memorial Trophy in 2003-04, the season he scored 63 goals and 138 points. He sat with Marcus Bell in the studio to talk about the rookie year a shoulder ended after 17 games missed, and the coach who taught him the only drill that matters.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell, The Tunnel, in the studio with Ilya Kovalchuk of the Atlanta Thrashers®. Ilya, good to have you. Two games, 3 goals, 5 points, 20 minutes a night, and the Bureau has your pace at 205. What does this start feel like from inside it?

Ilya Kovalchuk

Yeah. Two games, five points. Is nothing, Marcus. Is October. In October, everybody score, you know? Ask me in March, maybe then is something.

Marcus Bell

Then let's go back instead. June 2001, you're the first pick in the draft. First Russian ever taken at number one. You come over as a kid. Take me to that first season.

Ilya Kovalchuk

First year... Everything new, you know. Ice, rink, food, language. I score 29 goals, I have 51 points. But I miss 17 games. Shoulder. Season finish too early for me. And it come back, you know. In March, last season, same shoulder. Small, I come back. But you remember. Every time, you remember.

Marcus Bell

The shoulder ends the year for an 18-year-old. What do you take out of a season that stops like that?

Ilya Kovalchuk

Is important, that year. You learn the game is not only the hands. You learn the body, you know. Next year I score 38 goals, 67 points, and hands are the same. Head is better.

Marcus Bell

And then the year after that. 63 goals, 138 points, and they hand you the Lady Byng.

Ilya Kovalchuk

Sixty-three goals and the trophy is for the manners. I take it, of course. My mother, she like it. But is not why I play the game.

Marcus Bell

Who taught you? You come over as a kid, and somebody had to change how you play. Which coach was it?

Ilya Kovalchuk

When I was young, in Spartak Moscow, there was coach. He... he never let you be comfortable, you know? Every day, same thing.

Marcus Bell

What did he do?

Ilya Kovalchuk

He stop the drill. He say, 'again'. You skate it again. You tired, legs are gone, he say, 'again'. Is not talent, Marcus. Is you do it when nobody want to do it.

Marcus Bell

So he taught you the extra hour. The work nobody sees.

Ilya Kovalchuk

No, no. He teach me skating. Skating is everything. If you can skate, you can play. If you cannot skate, good hands, no help. You know?

Marcus Bell

Do you still do it? The drill, the again?

Ilya Kovalchuk

Every day. Before practice, after practice. Is how I am quick. Is how I am still quick, you know.

Marcus Bell

One more and I'll let you go. The deal pays you $1.10M this season and there is nothing on it after. People write about that number. Does it follow you into the room?

Ilya Kovalchuk

People write. I don't read. I have one job. My job is hockey. Everything else is after the season.

Marcus Bell

Ilya Kovalchuk of the Thrashers. For The Tunnel, I'm Marcus Bell. Thanks for the time.

Ilya Kovalchuk

Thank you, Marcus. Thank you.
