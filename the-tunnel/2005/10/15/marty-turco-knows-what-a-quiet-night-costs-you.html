Marty Turco knows what a quiet night costs you

The goaltender on the mental weight of a sixteen-shot game, a one-goal road lead, and why his January theory about travel still holds after two wins away from Texas.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelSaturday, 15 October 2005

2:18 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Marty Turco95 stopped 15 of 16 shots as Dallas Stars won 2-1 on the road at Phoenix Coyotes on 2005-10-12, moving to 2-0 away from Texas this season with a 4-point lead at the top of the Dallas Stars division race. Neither side saw more than 17 shots. Turco says the thin nights are the ones that stay in your chest.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here with Marty Turco in the tunnel after a two and one win in Phoenix. You saw sixteen shots tonight. That is as close as a hockey game gets to a chess match.

Marty Turco

Yeah, you know, a win is a win and we'll take it. But those quiet ones, they get under your skin more than the busy ones. You've got to be sharp on every single one. You can't coast through two of them and figure you earned a breather. There is no breather when it's one-goal on the road.

Jan Kowalczyk

Take me to the third period. Up one on the road, twenty minutes to go. What is that like for you in the net?

Marty Turco

You want traffic. I mean, honestly, you do. A body in front, a screen, a deflection, something that makes it obvious what's happening. A clean look from the blue line with nobody else's stick around, that is the one that haunts you. And in the third, legs start to go, guys get a half step lazy, and those clean looks start showing up more. You're praying for one of your guys to get a stick in the lane.

Jan Kowalczyk

Back in January we talked about the road. You said it was legs and sleep, not something mystical. You're two and oh away from Texas right now.

Marty Turco

Yeah, I remember. Legs and sleep. I'll take credit while it lasts. Two games, Jan. Two games. You get into November, the back-to-backs start stacking up, the sleep doesn't come easy in a hotel room and the legs don't bounce back the same way. Then you find out if I was smart or just got lucky.

Jan Kowalczyk

You guys had seventeen shots of your own though. You weren't just sitting there protecting the lead. Messier and Arnott each had a goal, Messier added the assist.

Marty Turco

Yeah, and that's what you need, right? Two goals on the road, that's plenty. Modano's been good about settling the puck in the neutral zone, taking the time, not forcing the play. You don't need five, six goals to win a road game. You need two and a goalie who doesn't give up the cheap one.

Jan Kowalczyk

You didn't get the cheap one. Did the coach change anything between periods tonight? A pairing, a matchup, something to get the legs back?

Marty Turco

Not that I could see from out here. The structure was there all three periods. I think he just kept saying the same thing we've been saying all camp: protect it, protect it, protect it. You know? It's almost boring how simple it is. But the games you lose on the road are the ones where you get greedy in the third and try to score the empty-netter before they pull the goalie.

Jan Kowalczyk

You had that in January too, the idea that this room doesn't beat itself. You think that's still true after three games?

Marty Turco

Three games. You know what, I'm not going to build a philosophy on three games. I'll tell you in February if we're still this sharp. But yeah, tonight felt like the team I remember from camp. Everybody was where they were supposed to be. That's all I can ask.

Jan Kowalczyk

Marty, good game. Thanks for stopping. Back to you.

Marty Turco

Thanks, Jan.
