Gaborik on 24 shots, one goal, and what the room does after that

TOR outshot PIT 24-22 and lost 1-3; Gaborik says the special teams gave them nothing and calls Mogilny's goal the only thing that worked

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelSaturday, 15 October 2005

2:03 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Marian Gaborik99 had no points in Toronto Maple Leafs's 1-3 home loss to Pittsburgh Penguins on October 14, a night where Toronto outshot Pittsburgh 24-22 and lost by two. The 23-year-old right wing spoke to The Tunnel's Marcus Bell after the game about the gap between chances and goals, Alexander Mogilny's single-goal night, and where this room goes from a first real home defeat.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here with Marian Gaborik in the corridor at the Air Canada Centre. Marian, twenty-four shots, you lose 1-3. Pittsburgh had 22 and put three past you. What is the difference between those two numbers?

Marian Gaborik

Yeah, well, uh, they scored. You know, we shoot and shoot, and the puck go in for them and not for us. I think the first period we were okay, we were there, but then, how you say, they get the bounce and we don't. You know, it is one game.

Marcus Bell

One game, right, but you are down a goal with a home crowd and you cannot find the second one. When does that frustration creep in?

Marian Gaborik

I don't think it, I mean, we keep going. We have, what, twenty-four shots, so we have chances. Maybe we rush, maybe we take, uh, the first thing we see. But we don't stop, no. You know, we play the whole sixty.

Marcus Bell

Alex Mogilny scored your only goal tonight. He's got one and one on the night. What do you see from him in those moments?

Marian Gaborik

Sascha is, you know, he is quiet but he see everything. The pass go to him and he don't think, he just put it in. I play with him a long time, how you say, we know where the other man is. Tonight is nice for him, one and one. He deserve it.

Marcus Bell

Special teams tonight. You got nothing from the man advantage, and Pittsburgh found a way on three of their chances. Was that the special teams or was it the legs?

Marian Gaborik

The special teams is not good, yeah. We know that. The PK, we give them too much room, how you say, they get the second chance and the third. And the power play, we move it around but we don't shoot enough. You can't score if you don't shoot. Tonight the special teams lose the game for us, I think. Yeah.

Marcus Bell

Jan Kowalczyk asked you last January about how this room resets after a night where the shots don't produce. You outshot them 24-22 tonight. Same question, same room. Where does it go?

Marian Gaborik

Well, we go to the next one. You know, the room, we talk, we watch the video, we fix the PK, we fix the first shot. That is it. We don't sit here and feel sorry. Next game is important, this one we put behind. But the special teams, that we fix first.

Marcus Bell

And you, zero points tonight. You said last January nobody in this room cares about your goal total after a loss. Still true?

Marian Gaborik

Yeah, I mean, you can score four and we lose, it is the same feeling. You know, I want to score, sure, but I want to win more. Tonight I don't score, fine, we fix it. The team is what matter. You know, the table don't care about my points either.

Marcus Bell

Marian Gaborik, thank you. Back to you at the desk.
