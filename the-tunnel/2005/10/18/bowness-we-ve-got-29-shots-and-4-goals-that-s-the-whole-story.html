Bowness: 'We've got 29 shots and 4 goals. That's the whole story'

The Coyotes' head coach on a team that is outworking opponents and outscoring nobody, a goaltending rotation stuck at zero, and a road trip that does not get easier.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelTuesday, 18 October 2005

3:34 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Phoenix Coyotes is 0-5-0 after dropping a 6-4 decision at Toronto Maple Leafs on 2005-10-17, outshooting their hosts 29-26 and leaving with nothing. Rick Bowness spoke to The Tunnel in the corridor at the Air Canada Centre about the gap between what he sees in effort and what lands on the scoresheet.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here with Rick Bowness, head coach of Phoenix, after a 6-4 loss on the road at Toronto. Coach, you outshot them 29 to 26 tonight and you're down 0-5. What did you see out there, effort-wise?

Rick Bowness

Yeah, well, the effort was there. I think that's the frustrating part of it. You can't coach a guy into wanting it, and those boys wanted it tonight. We had 29 shots, we had rush chances, we had the puck in good areas. What we didn't have was the structure behind it. You get a lead, you get 2-1, and then you start trying to play with it instead of playing the game we talked about. That's on me.

Marcus Bell

You said that's on you. Is there a specific breakdown in the structure you can name?

Rick Bowness

Yeah, the blue line. We're getting through the neutral zone, we're getting there with numbers, and then the support isn't there. The second man and the third man are, I mean, they're late. And when you're late against a team like Toronto, a team that has guys who can make you pay in transition, you end up 3-on-2 the wrong way. That's how the third and fourth ones came. We got caught going, not coming back.

Marcus Bell

You mentioned you got a lead and then started playing with it. Who scores your goals tonight?

Rick Bowness

We've got four tonight, I'll tell you that much. Four different guys. That's the thing I don't mind. I'd rather win 6-5 where five different guys chip in than have one guy score four and still lose. The goals are spread out. It's the other end that's killing us.

Marcus Bell

Let's talk about the other end then. Brian Boucher in tonight, he gives up six. Sean Burke was in two of your first four and gave up five in each. Neither man has a win. Are you going to change the call at Buffalo Thursday?

Rick Bowness

You know, I'm not going to stand here and, uh, put it on the goaltenders. Sean is 38 years old and he's been playing this game a long time, he's got the respect in that room, and Brian's a young guy that needs to get confidence somewhere. Right now it's not going to come from the outside. I've got to make the decision based on, you know, practice habits, what I see, where I think we've got the best chance to, to, stop the bleeding. I'll make that call tomorrow.

Marcus Bell

You're not putting it on them. But 16 goals against in 5 games is over 3 a night. Is it that the defense can't stop them, or the team structure isn't holding in the defensive zone?

Rick Bowness

It's the team. It's always the team. You ask me, is Paul Mara responsible? Is Ossi responsible? Yeah, obviously they're responsible. But when we're not supporting them in our own end, when the forwards aren't back in the right lane, a good team is going to carve you up. And most of the teams we've played are good teams. We're going to find out, though, because Buffalo Thursday, they're not as deep as Toronto, but they've got speed and they've got a young team that wants to get it done. So, you know, it's the same problem waiting for you.

Marcus Bell

Looking at the schedule, it's one game this week, Buffalo on the road, then Colorado at home on Sunday. Is there a comfort to fewer games, or does this stretch of losses make you want every opportunity you can get?

Rick Bowness

Honestly? I want every game. I want to get back out there and fix it before the thinking starts. That's the danger, right? You get a day off, you get two days off, and a locker room that's 0-5 starts reading, starts talking, starts looking at the standings before it's even October. I don't want that. I want them on the bus, I want them on the ice, I want them thinking about tomorrow night's shift, not about where they sit. So the short week, I'll take it, but I'm not grateful for it.

Marcus Bell

One more thing. Mike Sillinger has been your top center, you've got him at every spot, even-strength, power play, and he's got zero points in five games. Is that something you're trying to solve, or is it early enough that you're patient?

Rick Bowness

Mike's playing well. He's not producing, sure, I'll give you that. But he's making the plays that don't show up on a scoresheet, he's winning faceoffs, he's giving us that end-to-end shift, you know, the shift that lets your defensemen make a clean first pass. The goals will come. I've seen it before, I coached guys that went 20 games without a point and then scored four in a week. I'm not going to start shuffling a 34-year-old center after five games because the goals haven't landed. He's earning my trust. I just need the puck to go in.

Marcus Bell

Coach Bowness from Toronto tonight. Back to you.

Rick Bowness

Thanks, Marcus. Yeah. Go fix something.
