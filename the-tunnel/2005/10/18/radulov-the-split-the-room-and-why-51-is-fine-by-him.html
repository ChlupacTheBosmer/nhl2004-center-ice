Radulov: the split, the room, and why 51 is fine by him

The Sweater put a 39-grade gap between his puck and his fists; the 23-year-old in a 30-year-old room says he knows exactly who he is.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelTuesday, 18 October 2005

4:41 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Igor Radulov80 sits in the second chair at The Tunnel, the first time anyone from this desk has put a microphone in front of him. The Sweater ran his profile this fortnight: a Chicago Blackhawks winger whose puck handling reads 86 and whose fighting grade sits at 51, whose current overall is 76 and whose ceiling, by the Bureau's Book, is 79. He came to talk about what that number means in the room and who he watches on the other side of the glass.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here with Igor Radulov from Chicago. Igor, thanks for sitting down. I'll be honest with you, the first thing people ask me when I mention your name is about a number from the Sweater. They want to know if you saw it.

Igor Radulov

I saw it. Everybody saw it. My phone, I wake up, forty messages. 'Igor, is it true, you fight nobody?' Yes. Forty messages. I read one and go back to sleep.

Jan Kowalczyk

Eighty-six in puck handling, fifty-one in fighting. The profile called it the 39-grade split. They put you at 80 on the ice right now and a ceiling of 79. Does that gap between your best number and your worst number bother you, or is it just what it is?

Igor Radulov

It is what it is. I play hockey, I do not play... what is the word... boxing. I am a hockey player. I have the hands, I have the speed, I have the pass. The fighting, that is for other guys. The 51, of course I see it. You know, in Russia when I was small, the coaches said, 'you will get hit, you will get hit, learn to keep the puck.' I kept the puck. The fighting, that never was in me. It is not a weakness to me. It is just a number.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let me put this to you differently then. You are in a room that dresses thirty men, average age thirty. League average is twenty-eight. Nine guys are thirty-three or older. You are one of four that are twenty-four or under. How does a twenty-three-year-old whose game is... unconventional... find his seat in a room like that?

Igor Radulov

You ask how I sit. I sit where they tell me to sit. No, no. I think what you mean is, do I feel... small? No. Alexei , he talks in the room, he is quiet most of the time, but when he talks you listen. That is how the room works here. Nobody tells me to be louder. Nobody says, 'Radulov, you are young, be quiet.' They play, I play. I learn from watching. Theoren , I watch his feet in practice, I watch how he makes the first move. You do not need to talk to be in the room.

Jan Kowalczyk

Does anyone lead differently than you expected? You came from Russia, you had a certain idea of what a veteran looks like. Did the reality match?

Igor Radulov

In Russia, the old man, he hits you in practice and that is your leadership. Here, Eric shows you the angle on the board. He says, 'here, the defenceman, his hip is late, you go here.' He does not hit you. He draws the picture. That was, I will say it, that was new to me. I had to learn that a man can teach you with a whiteboard and it works better than a hit.

Jan Kowalczyk

Your contract has zero years left. Sixty thousand... six hundred thousand dollars. Does that sit in the back of your mind when you're watching all those veterans play, knowing none of this is guaranteed for you next October?

Igor Radulov

Of course it is in the back of my mind. I am not stupid. I see the numbers, everyone sees the numbers. But you play from that place or you do not play. If I wake up and think, 'Igor, you are here today, gone tomorrow,' then I am slow on the ice. So I do not. I go, I play, I keep the puck. The room, the contract, that is one thing. The shift is another thing. They are separate.

Jan Kowalczyk

You played two games last season after seventy-nine the year before. Twenty-one minutes a game in those two. Then the head, and you were back in April. You have not played yet this season. What did those two games tell you about where you stand in this lineup?

Igor Radulov

They told me I can do it. Twenty-one minutes, of course I can. The problem was I was not ready, I had not played since... a long time without the puck, you lose the timing, you lose the legs. The two games, they were good, the goal, the assist, I felt fine. Then the head came and... that is done. This year I am ready. The rest is the coach's.

Jan Kowalczyk

Who do you watch? Not a teammate, someone on the other side. A guy whose game you study and think, 'yes, that is the way.'

Igor Radulov

Ilya . Everyone knows I watch Ilya. He is from here, from Russia, we are close in age. He is the #1 pick, I am the 74th. We are not the same story. But the game, the way he shoots, the way he goes from the corner to the net in two seconds, that is... that is real hockey. He does not fight either, I think. Maybe a little. But he wins with the puck, same as me. When I watch him I think, 'yes, this is how it should be, fast and simple.'

Jan Kowalczyk

People on this desk have been talking about leadership all fortnight. Bullard told Marcus he'd build the workout room first because he needed it first. Is there something you would build if you were in that chair, if you were the captain?

Igor Radulov

No. I would not be the captain. I know who I am. I am the guy with 51 in fighting and 86 in the puck. You do not put me in front of thirty men to talk. I would go find the young guy who is scared and sit with him and say, 'hey, the first hit is bad, the second is fine, the third you forget.' That is what I would do. The rest of it, Alexei and the old guys, they do it right.

Jan Kowalczyk

One last thing before I let you go. The ceiling is 79. You are at 76. That is three points. What has to happen for you to get past the number people think is your limit?

Igor Radulov

The number, the ceiling, that is for your magazine. You write 79, I play tomorrow and maybe I am 77, maybe 80. I do not know. What I know is the 21 minutes I got last season, I want every night. I want 21 minutes and I want the puck on my stick in the zone and I want to make the play. If I do that every night, the number goes where it goes. I cannot control the ceiling.

Jan Kowalczyk

Igor Radulov, Chicago winger. Thanks for sitting down, Igor. Back to you.
