Bryan Murray: 'Two goals in two games, that's not a mystery'

The Ottawa head coach took 29 shots tonight, gave up 4, and says nothing in the record is hidden by the shot count.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelThursday, 13 October 2005

2:18 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Ottawa Senators sit at 0 and 2 with 2 goals for and 7 against after a 1-4 loss to Toronto Maple Leafs on 2005-10-12. The Senators managed 29 shots on goal against 36 from the hosts. Head coach Bryan Murray spoke to Marcus Bell in the tunnel afterward.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here in the tunnel at Toronto with Ottawa Senators® head coach Bryan Murray. Coach, you're 0 and 2 to start the season. 1 and 4 tonight. Where do you start?

Bryan Murray

You start with the scoreboard. That's where you start. We scored one, they scored four. I don't dress it up for you and I don't dress it up for the guys in that room. We're not good enough right now. That's for sure.

Marcus Bell

You put 29 pucks on net tonight. They had 36. That's not a blowout in terms of possession. Does the record hide what the game looked like from your bench?

Bryan Murray

No. No, I don't think it hides anything. You want to know the truth? Twenty-nine shots, sure, that's a number. But half of those were from the outside, no traffic, no second chance. They scored on the other end because they managed the puck in our zone and we didn't. The result says exactly what happened.

Marcus Bell

You've started Ray Emery in both games. Patrick Lalime came in one of them. Is that going to stay the same?

Bryan Murray

Ray's our starter. I've told him, I've told the room. He's 23, he's playing well enough to be out there, and I'm not going to change it after one game where the team in front of him gives up four. That's not his loss. That's everyone's loss. Pat came in and was fine, but it doesn't change the plan tonight.

Marcus Bell

You've got Steffen Prommersberger in there, he's 20 years old, and he's played both of these. What does he bring when you put him out there against NHL competition?

Bryan Murray

What does he bring. He brings his feet, he brings a body that wants to hit somebody. I don't put him out there to score 40 goals for me. I put him out there because he competes, and that's the word I like tonight because I need everybody competing. The rest will come. He's 20, he's got time.

Marcus Bell

One thing the record does show: Daniel Alfredsson and Martin Havlat, your top two scorers from last year, together they have one point between them in these two games. Are you talking to them about it?

Bryan Murray

I talk to all of them every day. I'm not going to stand here and say it's fine. Dan has not had his good chances yet, and Martin, he's got one assist, that's not the Martin I know either. But the defense, she was not sharp enough in front of them. You ask them to produce when the puck is coming to them clean. Right now it's not coming clean. You fix that, they fix themselves.

Marcus Bell

Phoenix is up on the 17th. Three days between games. What has to be different?

Bryan Murray

Details. I know that sounds like the answer every coach gives, but it's the true answer. We turn pucks over in our own zone, we're not finishing our checks, our third period she's not good enough, and I think the first period of the last game was the same problem. Three days is enough to talk about it and drill it. It's not enough to fix everything. We manage it one night at a time.

Marcus Bell

Coach Murray, appreciate you. Back to you.
