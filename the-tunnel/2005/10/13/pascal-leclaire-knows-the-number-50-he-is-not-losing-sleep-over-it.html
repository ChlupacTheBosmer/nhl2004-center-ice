Pascal Leclaire knows the number 50. He is not losing sleep over it.

The CBJ goaltender, whose Book split is the widest in his position, talks about leading a room of men a decade older and what he does when the crease is empty.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelThursday, 13 October 2005

3:47 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Pascal Leclaire93 sits in Jan Kowalczyk's chair with the Bureau's Development Index at +9, a Book overall of 91, and a consistency grade of 50. He is 22. He is the first goaltender the Columbus Blue Jackets drafted, eighth overall in 2001, and his contract has 0 years left at $1.10M. The Sweater's profile called his split the widest in goaltending. He is not bothered by the title.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here at the studio with Pascal Leclaire. Pascal, thanks for coming in. The Sweater ran a profile on you this week, the title was Speed 99, Consistency 50. You saw that.

Pascal Leclaire

Yeah, I saw it. My agent sent it to me, so. I read it, I thought, yeah, that's fair enough.

Jan Kowalczyk

That's fair enough. You don't argue with the 50.

Pascal Leclaire

No, I mean... look, I know what the games look like. I know when I make the save that's not a save, and I know when I let one in that I shouldn't. The 50 is not telling me something I don't already know, you know? It's just, it's one number. And I'm 22. I'm going to fix it.

Jan Kowalczyk

But how do you fix it? Speed you can coach a little, glove you can drill. Consistency, what does that even look like in practice?

Pascal Leclaire

It's the, uh, it's the boring stuff. It's the routine before the game, it's the same warm-up every night. Some nights I'm flying, I feel good, and some nights I'm not there in the first period and then I wake up for the second. So. The fix is I need to be ready at puck drop, not at minute eight. That's it.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're in a room, by the record, that averages 29.9. Eleven men 33 or older. You're one of four guys 24 or younger. Does that affect you, being young in there?

Pascal Leclaire

No, no. I like it, honestly. The guys, they've been around, they know when to shut up and when to say something. Marc, Marc Denis, he's been a starter for years. He was 90 in the Book last I checked. So I watch him, you know? How he gets ready. How he handles a bad goal, a bad period. He doesn't... he doesn't spiral. That's the thing I need. I don't spiral, but sometimes I drift. He just resets.

Jan Kowalczyk

Can a goalie lead a room though? In a practical sense. You can't wear the C.

Pascal Leclaire

No, no, I can't. But the crease, that's the loudest spot on the ice, you know? I'm telling guys where to go, I'm talking the whole time. And in the room, I'm the first one in, I'm the last one out. That's my way of leading. I don't need to make speeches. The guys see me working and they know I care, for sure.

Jan Kowalczyk

Todd Marchant, he's 82 in the Book, he's been a pro a long time. Tyler Wright the same. Do they treat you different, being the young guy, the high pick?

Pascal Leclaire

No. Marchant, he'll hit you with something, yeah, if you slack off in practice. But not because I'm young. Because I slack off. And Tyler, he's funny, he's always joking. It's a good room. It's an old room but it's a good room. Nobody's looking at my age. They look at whether I made the stop or I didn't.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let me take you away from the rink. A day off, nothing on the schedule. What does Pascal Leclaire do?

Pascal Leclaire

Oh, it's not exciting. I, uh, I sleep in. That's the first thing. And then I go to the range, shooting, just for fun. Not hockey shooting, just, you know, guns. I like that. And some days I drive, I just drive around. No plan. The guys, they know, I'm gone by 9 in the morning and I come back whenever.

Jan Kowalczyk

You drive around. Columbus? You don't get tired of driving around Columbus?

Pascal Leclaire

I've been here a while, you know? No, I mean, it's quiet. That's why I like it. After a game, especially a bad one, I need quiet. If I go to a restaurant and there's fifty people and someone recognizes me, I just, I can't do that. So I drive. I go out on the highway and I don't think about the game. And by the time I come back, I'm okay again.

Jan Kowalczyk

Does it help, the quiet? After a game where you let in three that you feel you shouldn't have?

Pascal Leclaire

Yeah. Yeah, it's, it's the only thing that works. If I stay in the room and everyone's talking and laughing, I feel like I'm, I don't know, I feel exposed. Like I should be laughing too but I can't. So I leave. I drive. I go somewhere with no signal, maybe. And I just sit with it. And then tomorrow it's a new day.

Jan Kowalczyk

The contract. You're at $1.10M and 0 years left. How much do you think about that?

Pascal Leclaire

I don't. I mean, the agent handles it. I know it's there. I know this year matters. But if I'm thinking about money when I'm in the crease, I'm going to be slow, I'm going to be... I don't know, I'm going to be thinking about next year instead of the next shot. So I don't. I play, and we see.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're 22. You've got 49 games last year, and the Index has you up nine. Does the word starter feel settled?

Pascal Leclaire

No. Nothing's settled. Marc is right there, he's 90, he's been doing this longer than me. I have to win it every camp, every game. And that's fine. That's good, actually. I don't want to be comfortable. Comfortable is where the 50 comes from.

Jan Kowalczyk

Pascal, thanks for the time.

Pascal Leclaire

Thank you. It's good to talk about something besides the game, for sure.
