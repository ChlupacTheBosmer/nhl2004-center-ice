Erat: The 191st Pick Knows Where the Road Came From

The seventh-round pick talks Milwaukee, Trebic, and a year that taught him patience.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelWednesday, 12 October 2005

4:11 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Martin Erat85 sits at 85 overall in the Bureau's Book, 6 points up on the Development Index, with 45 assists to his name from last season's 53-point campaign and a contract that reads $0.60M with 0 years left. Marcus Bell sat down with him in the studio after a 29.0-minute shift in Nashville Predators's one game so far.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here with Martin Erat of Nashville. Martin, good to have you. You are a seventh-round pick, 191st overall, 1999. Do you still remember the number?

Martin Erat

Yeah. 191. I remember. When you are the 191st pick, everybody remember, you know? The reporter in the room, they all look at you, 'you are the 191st.' And I say yeah, I know. I am here.

Marcus Bell

You've been in Nashville since your first NHL game in 2001-02. That is the vowel line, right? You, Orszagh on the right, Arkhipov in the middle. First point a game winner against Calgary, October 11, 2001. What do you remember about that shift?

Martin Erat

I remember the bench, you know. I remember the bench because I am thinking 'oh my God, I am in the NHL and I just scored the winner.' And Orszagh, he grab me so hard, my shoulder, I thought it fall off. And then the rest is fast. I remember nothing else. The celebration, the crowd, it is all... after. You know how it is when you are 20 and you think this is normal, that you play here.

Marcus Bell

It wasn't normal, though. Because the next year you are in Milwaukee, most of the season. What happened in 2002-03?

Martin Erat

Well. I think, I think I thought that when you play one year good, you are a player. And then the game, it slow you down. You know, you are not fast enough anymore, everybody know what you do, and you are... I don't know the word, how you say... I am lost. I go to Milwaukee and I am thinking, okay, maybe I am just AHL player. Maybe this is my life.

Marcus Bell

And then you came back. You changed your number, your play jumped. 53 points last year, 45 assists. What did you do in Milwaukee that you couldn't do in Nashville?

Martin Erat

I worked, you know. In Nashville you play the game, you go home. In Milwaukee you are... there is no pressure, no TV, nothing. And I watch my game, every game, I sit and I watch. And I see, oh, I skate good but I make bad decision. And I fix the decision. Slowly, every night. And when I come back, I feel I have good feeling about myself again. That is important, you know. You need to trust your game.

Marcus Bell

So the ceiling people talk about, the 191st pick, the grade at 85 now, that doesn't bother you?

Martin Erat

My grade. I know my grade. No, it don't bother me. I think, when you are 191 and you play NHL, you are already, how you say, you already beat the number. You understand? The number say you should not be here. So 85, 86, what is the difference. I play tomorrow and that is what matter.

Marcus Bell

You played 29.0 minutes in that one game this season. Last year you averaged 20.8. Is the job different now? Are they asking more from you?

Martin Erat

Yeah, yeah. The coach, he trust me more. Before, I am the young guy who skate fast and make the play. Now he say, 'you are here, you are the player, you bring the puck.' And Legwand, he is good to play with, you know? David, he always is open, I look up and he is there. It is easy to play with a guy like that.

Marcus Bell

Let me take you back further. Trebic. You are 19 or 20 when you leave for North America? What is the road look like from there in your memory?

Martin Erat

Trebic. It is small, you know. Small city. And you leave and you think, I go to America, I play hockey, it is simple. But it is not simple. The flight is long, and then you land and nobody speak your language. I come to the locker room and they say things, joke, and I understand nothing for maybe three months. I sit and I smile and I don't know what they say. That is... lonely, you know? Even when you are 20 and you think you are strong.

Marcus Bell

Is family still back there? What do they say when they see you play?

Martin Erat

Yeah. Family in Trebic. I talk to them, the phone, every day. Czech, you know, so I can speak and think in Czech, and that is... it is good. When you play 82 games and everything is English, you need one voice that is home. My mother, she calls me after every game. Even the bad ones. Especially the bad ones. She say, 'Martin, you play good game.' And I know it was terrible. But I say 'thank you, mama.'

Marcus Bell

Your contract is $0.60M with 0 years left after this season. Does that sit anywhere in the back of your head when you are skating on the second shift?

Martin Erat

No. I mean, I know. My agent, he call me, and I say, 'not now.' Last year I play 79 games, I play the playoffs, I score 5 points in the playoffs. I know what I can do. If the team want me, they call. If not, I go somewhere and I play. I don't worry about it now. My morale is... you see it, yes? It is high. I am here, I play, I skate. That is enough.

Marcus Bell

Last one. The vowel line. Orszagh, Arkhipov, you. Do you still talk?

Martin Erat

Sometimes. Not every day, you know? But when we play each other, I call him in the morning. 'Hey, today we are opponents, but yesterday we are brothers.' And Arkhipov, he is in Moscow now, I think, or close. He call me too, sometimes late, when he drunk a little. He say 'Martin, remember when you score against Calgary?' And I say, yes Vladko, I remember for the rest of my life. You know?

Marcus Bell

Martin Erat, thank you very much. Back to you.
