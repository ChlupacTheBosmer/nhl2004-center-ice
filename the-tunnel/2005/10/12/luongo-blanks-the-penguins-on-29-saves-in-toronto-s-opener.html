Luongo blanks the Penguins on 29 saves in Toronto's opener

The goaltender says the quiet start he had last fall is behind him, and credits the structure in front of him for a clean first night.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelWednesday, 12 October 2005

2:18 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Roberto Luongo95 stopped all 29 shots he faced as Toronto Maple Leafs opened the season with a 4-0 win in Pittsburgh on Saturday. Four different Leafs scored. The shutout is his second of the last 46 games, counting the regular season and the Conn Smythe run last spring.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here, outside the visiting room at the Mellon Arena. Roberto, congratulations. Twenty-nine shots, nothing through. A clean way to open a season. How does that first one feel when you get the empty net at the end?

Roberto Luongo

It's a good feeling, for sure. You know, the first one is always, uh, the first one is where you find out where you're at. And tonight, I felt good. I felt, I don't know, I felt ready for it.

Marcus Bell

The last time we spoke was December, your first game with Florida. You were 8 and 11 and you told me wins matter more than shutouts. A shutout in your first game here in Toronto. Is that a different thing?

Roberto Luongo

Yeah, you remember that one. Well, I still think the same thing. The win is the thing. But you know, zero on the board, it's, it's a nice start. I'll take it.

Marcus Bell

You mentioned feeling ready. Your last season here was a short one, 25 games in the spring, and you were sharp in all of them. What's changed for you this fall? You look different in your movement tonight, I'm not sure how to put it.

Roberto Luongo

Uh, I think, you know, the summer was good. I spent time on my, on my edges. My feet. And tonight I could see it, I could see the puck when it was behind bodies. That's the, that's the big thing for me. When my feet are good, I track everything. When I'm not, I lose it behind a screen and I'm on my back. So, yeah. The feet.

Marcus Bell

The coaching staff, what have they changed in what you see in front of you? You've been here since January. Does this fall feel structured differently from the spring?

Roberto Luongo

Yeah. Yeah, I think so. In the spring we were, uh, we were getting a lot of traffic in front of me. A lot. And this fall, in practice, the first thing was, stay between me and the net. That's it. Don't be fancy. And tonight, I looked in front of me and there was nobody. Maybe one time in the second period I couldn't see, that's it. Otherwise, clean lanes. Kaberle was, uh, he was pinching but he was getting back. The guys in front of me were good.

Marcus Bell

Four different goal scorers. Chistov, Afinogenov, Chlup, Spacek. What does that do for you back there?

Roberto Luongo

It takes the pressure off me, for sure. You know when you win one-nil, one-nil, and you're the one who has to be perfect every night. Four different guys, it means everyone's in it. Everyone's got a job. And it's easier to be, to be locked in when you know the other guys are doing their thing too.

Marcus Bell

Pittsburgh put up twenty-nine shots but four of them were, I don't know, I didn't see you make one you'd call desperate. Was that the structure you just talked about, or was that you tonight?

Roberto Luongo

I think, uh, a little of both, you know. I made the saves that I should make. That's the job. But the team gave me a lot of, a lot of clean looks. No, no scramble plays. And that, that makes me look better than maybe I am, you know what I mean?

Marcus Bell

I'll take that as a compliment to your defense. Roberto, thank you for your time. Roberto Luongo, in the corridor after a four-nil start. Back to you.
