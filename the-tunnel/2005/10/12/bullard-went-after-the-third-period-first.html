Bullard went after the third period first

Montreal's general manager explains why the workout room got built twice before anything else, and says the travel room is next.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelWednesday, 12 October 2005

2:50 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Stefaan Bullard sits down with Marcus Bell in the studio to talk the money, the gate, and the rooms at Montreal Canadiens. Montreal lost its opener 3-2 in overtime on the road and sits at 1 point. The payroll is $54.89M, the seat is $79, and the bottom line is $17.76M in profit.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here, studio sit-down, and I'm joined by Stefaan Bullard, the general manager of Montreal. Stefaan, thanks for sitting with me. One game in, a 3-2 overtime loss on the road. How do you take a start like that?

Stefaan Bullard

You take it. You take the point. It's one game, we were there, we didn't close it. I'd rather be 0-0-1 with a point than 0-1-0 with none. But you don't sit here and smile about a loss, no.

Marcus Bell

Let me put you on the business side. The seat here is $79. Toronto is at $112, Columbus at $83. You brought in $26M in revenue. Does $79 leave money on the table?

Stefaan Bullard

I don't think you price a seat by what another club charges. You price it for the people who come through the door. $79, it's what this market holds. And I'll tell you, $26M in revenue, we cleared $17.76M on the bottom line. That's the number I look at. Not the ticket, the profit.

Marcus Bell

And the payroll is $54.89M. That's the seventh-highest in the league. You're spending at the top and charging at the bottom.

Stefaan Bullard

You put it like that, sure. But the payroll, that's the product on the ice. That's the guys you're paying to be here. The ticket is what the fan pays to watch it. Those are two different decisions and I don't mix them.

Marcus Bell

Fair enough. Let me move to the rooms. Your workout room, you've upgraded it twice. It's at level 4 now. That's par, that's where most clubs want to be. Why did that one go up before anything else?

Stefaan Bullard

Because I lost games in February. I lost games where we were the team that got run over in the third period. And the workout room, that's where you build the strength, the checking, the endurance. You don't get that in October. You get it in January when your legs are gone and you've got 4 minutes left. I built that room first because I needed it first.

Marcus Bell

I want to be fair to you. Your travel room is at zero. That's the floor of the whole league. Practice is at one. Your live sum across the four rooms that count is minus 14. When does the next one go in?

Stefaan Bullard

You're right, and I'll say it straight. Travel is next. It's next because we spend half the year on a plane, and right now we're taking that penalty every road trip. Every single one. I know what it costs us. I know exactly what it costs us. And I'm telling you, it's next.

Marcus Bell

The trade deadline the calendar carries is 158 days away. Does the travel room wait until after that, or do you try before?

Stefaan Bullard

I don't build rooms on the deadline. I build them when I can. The deadline is for the roster. The rooms are something else. One at a time.

Marcus Bell

Practice at one, you said. Is that after travel, or does it wait too?

Stefaan Bullard

Practice waits. That's the honest answer. I'd like to do both. I can't. That's what the $17.76M tells you, I can do one and I'll do it right.

Marcus Bell

Stefaan Bullard, general manager of Montreal. Thank you for your time.

Stefaan Bullard

Thanks, Marcus. Good to see you.
