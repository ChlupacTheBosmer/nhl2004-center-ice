Kovalchuk: 'I don't need to be there when the hit comes'

The Thrashers' 22-year-old left winger talked speed, Markus Naslund, the Vysshaya Liga, and why $1.10M is not his problem tonight.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelSunday, 16 October 2005

4:28 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Ilya Kovalchuk90 walked into the studio with a Lady Byng on his shelf, five 99-grades in the Bureau's Book, and a $1.10M contract with zero years left. Jan Kowalczyk sat down with him to talk about what changed between a rink in the Vysshaya Liga five years ago and this week: 2 games, 5 points, 20.0 minutes a night. The shoulder is fine.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here, sitting with Ilya Kovalchuk from Atlanta Thrashers®. Ilya, the Lady Byng. You won it last season, 79 games, 63 goals, 75 assists, 138 points, and 18 minutes in the penalty box. What's the first thought when they call your name?

Ilya Kovalchuk

First thought, I think is mistake. I look around, I say, is this for me? But yes. I keep it. The goal, that is what everybody know I do. The trophy for... how to say. The gentleman's trophy. That is something different. So yes, I keep it on the shelf and sometimes I look at it and think, yes, okay.

Jan Kowalczyk

18 penalties in minutes with 63 goals. That's a number people point at. Does the contrast between the two surprise anyone in your room?

Ilya Kovalchuk

No, no. I think... if you are fast, you don't need to reach for a man. You go around. The stick, sometimes, yes, is important to keep down. I think about that. Not about the box. About the stick.

Jan Kowalczyk

The Bureau's Book has you at 99 in acceleration, 99 in agility, 99 in speed, 99 in shot power, 99 in heroics. Five 99s. Your overall is 92 and your Development Index is up 5. Do you feel that difference on the ice this year?

Ilya Kovalchuk

I don't think about the Book. I think about the game. Is the first step there. Is the shot there. If the number goes up, good. If the number goes down, I go back to work. I try to play sixty minutes. The number, that is not sixty minutes.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let me ask about Markus Naslund. Same position, left wing. Last season you both had 138 points. He won the Hart, you won the Lady Byng. He's 32, you're 22. Do you watch his games?

Ilya Kovalchuk

Yes. I watch. Vancouver Canucks®, I look at him always. He is... how to say. He play the left wing like me. The speed, the release. But 32 years old and he score 74 goals last year. That is not easy. The shot, the way he go behind the defense, that is beautiful. I watch that.

Jan Kowalczyk

What do you take from him? Is there something in his game you've tried to put in yours?

Ilya Kovalchuk

He play simple. Simple is hard. Everybody want to be fancy, want to do the trick. Naslund, he go, he shoot, he score. No trick. That is what I try. I try to not be fancy. I try to go, shoot, score. Maybe sometimes I am fancy. But I try not.

Jan Kowalczyk

Five years ago you were 17, playing for Spartak Moscow in the Vysshaya Liga. Before that you were at the Quebec Pee-Wee tournament in '97. That's a long road to sitting in this chair as the first Russian picked first overall. Do you think about the distance?

Ilya Kovalchuk

Yes. I think. In the Vysshaya Liga, you play against men. Big men. Not... not the top men in Russia, but strong. I am 17, I am 74 inches, 202 pounds. The man is older, he hits you, you go to the boards. So you learn to not be there when the hit comes. You learn to be fast because you must be fast. The Vysshaya Liga teach me that. Not the speed. The speed is there. The reason to use it, that is there.

Jan Kowalczyk

What would the 17-year-old in that locker room think if you told him where you are now? Lady Byng, 92 overall, 138 points in a season.

Ilya Kovalchuk

He think I am lying. No, he think this is... the dream. But when you are in the Vysshaya Liga, the NHL is for the television. You don't think it is for you. You play, you go home to Tver, you eat, you sleep. So he think I am crazy. But I tell him, come on, we are here.

Jan Kowalczyk

Number 17. That's for Valeri Kharlamov, the Soviet player from the seventies. Your father showed you those films.

Ilya Kovalchuk

Yes. Kharlamov. The speed, the way through the zone. Not the same body, maybe. Not the same... the era, it is different. But the way he shoot, the way he decide. When I take the number, I think, okay, I can carry it. Maybe not every game. But I try.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're at $1.10M and you have zero years left on the contract. You're 22. How do you think about July?

Ilya Kovalchuk

I think about tonight. The contract, that is for my agent, my people. I play hockey. What comes in July, July will come. I am not a man for planning too much. Tonight I go, tomorrow I practice. The rest, we see.

Jan Kowalczyk

But does the number bother you? You put up 138 points last season, won the Lady Byng, you're 92 in the Book, and you're at $1.10M with no term. That's a gap.

Ilya Kovalchuk

You ask this to everybody, I think. No. I score for the team. The money is important, of course. But I don't sit and count. The money, that is not on the ice. On the ice is the puck, the speed, the shot. That I control. The contract, I don't control. So.

Jan Kowalczyk

One last one. The shoulder, late March. You were back by the 30th. How is it now?

Ilya Kovalchuk

It is fine. March, yes, it was there. But I am back the same day, basically. A small thing. Now it is gone. The shoulder is not in the sixty minutes. So I play.

Jan Kowalczyk

Ilya Kovalchuk, thank you. Back to you.
