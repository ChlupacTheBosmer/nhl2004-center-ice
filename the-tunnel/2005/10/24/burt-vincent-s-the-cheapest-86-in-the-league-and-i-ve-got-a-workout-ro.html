Burt: 'Vincent's the cheapest 86 in the league, and I've got a workout room at zero'

The Tampa Bay GM on his center's contract year, why travel came before the gym, and a deadline he'd like to beat.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelMonday, 24 October 2005

4:39 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Vincent Lecavalier90 has 7 points in 5 games at 25 years old, an 86 overall and a contract at $2.60M with one year left. His general manager, Michael Burt, has the fourth-highest prestige in the league at 1008 and a workout room sitting at zero. Jan Kowalczyk sat down with him after two straight losses.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here in the studio with Michael Burt of Tampa Bay Lightning®. Michael, you're top of the Southeast with 8 points through 5 games, but you've dropped two straight. How do you feel about where this sits right now?

Michael Burt

It sits... look, I'd take it in October. Eight points in five games, division lead, the record is there. But you're right, the streak is two and both of those were on the road. 18 goals for, 13 against. We score, sure, but we don't close. That's what two losses in a row tells me.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let me go to the player your room is built around. Vincent Lecavalier, 7 points in 5 games, 86 overall, one year left on $2.60M. What do you see night to night?

Michael Burt

You know what I see? A guy who's done proving himself. People still talk about him like a project. He's 25. He's got 7 in 5 and his morale is 100. I watch him and I think this is the year the number jumps again. The question isn't whether he's the guy. The question is what he is next year when the Bureau puts him somewhere north of 86 and that contract reads the way it does.

Jan Kowalczyk

So you're thinking about extension.

Michael Burt

Every day. You've got a 25-year-old center at $2.60M, an 86 in the Book, and a year on the clock. The moment that number moves up six, the moment the League Scouting Bureau edition changes and he's in the high 80s, the money changes a lot. So yes. I think about it before I have my coffee, if I'm being honest.

Jan Kowalczyk

Do you open that door before the trade deadline, or let him play through the year?

Michael Burt

I let him play through it. You don't sit a man down in the middle of a run like this and put numbers in front of him. He's got 7 in 5, he's got his legs, morale is 100. I want that staying exactly where it is through March. But it's on my list. Top of the list.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let's talk about what you've been building. The travel bump, that was filed between October 18 and 20. Your road record this year is 1-2. Did it come a week too late?

Michael Burt

A week, a month. I'm not going to pretend. 1-2 away tells you the -4 is still too big. And we were at -6 for the whole back half of last season. That's the heaviest penalty you can carry on a road trip. Moving it to -4, that's the right direction, but you're right that the 1-2 says it wasn't enough, fast enough.

Jan Kowalczyk

Your workout room is still at zero. That's the bottom of the league. Five on strength, five on checking, five on endurance, all gone.

Michael Burt

I know what it costs. I look at it. But here's what I'd push back on. Stefaan told this desk he built his workout room first because he lost games in February, because that's where the legs come from. And I respect that logic for his club. But look at what I've got: 18 goals in 5 games, Vincent with 7 points, we score. I'm not the team getting out-muscled in October. I'm leaving points on the road, every trip. So I fixed the road first. The gym is next. It's next.

Jan Kowalczyk

When, though? Before the deadline?

Michael Burt

Before the deadline. I know, I know. Different man, different building, different math. I'd like it done by March. Whether it actually gets done by March, that depends on what the books look like after the break. I'm not in a spot where I can't do it. I'm in a spot where I have to do it in the right order and I can't do two at once.

Jan Kowalczyk

Can it come out of payroll? You're at $59.79M and you turned $17.57M in profit on $28.92M in revenue. That's a building that pays for things.

Michael Burt

You read my ledger back at me. Fine. Yes, $17.57M profit, that's a cushion. 16398 through the turnstiles at $81, the gate is holding. So when I say I'd like it done by March, I'm not saying we can't afford a gym. I'm saying if I cut payroll to do it early, I'm asking Vincent what 86 feels like on $2.30M instead of $2.60M, and then I'm losing the run he's on. That's not a trade I make.

Jan Kowalczyk

Final one. You're fourth in prestige, 1008. You've got zero Cups and your last run was 2. How does that standing sit with the state of the building?

Michael Burt

It sits like a question I don't have the answer to, Jan. The prestige number, I've got 1008 and a workout room at zero. That's the standing and that's the room. You put them next to each other and it looks strange. I don't pretend I understand how the league keeps that score. What I know is the bottom line says we're fine, the gate says we're fine, and the kid on $2.60M is scoring 7 in 5. I build what I can when I can, in the order that keeps us on the ice tonight. That's my whole job.

Jan Kowalczyk

Michael Burt, general manager of Tampa Bay. Thanks for your time.

Michael Burt

Thanks, Jan.
