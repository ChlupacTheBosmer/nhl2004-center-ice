Forsberg: 'Ten in six is not a step down'

After the Avalanche's second straight overtime loss, Forsberg corrected the premise that his production has fallen off and said the team must give Jamie Storr more in front of his net.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelMonday, 24 October 2005

2:27 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Peter Forsberg89 had one assist on 24 shots as Colorado Avalanche lost 4-3 in overtime to Edmonton at home on Saturday, dropping to 2-1-2 on a two-game losing streak. Forsberg is at 10 points in 6 games this season, averaging 24.8 minutes a night. The Bureau's Development Index has him at minus one below his base grade of 97.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here with Peter Forsberg, right after that 4-3 overtime loss to Edmonton. Two and one and two, two straight losses, twenty-four shots on your own ice. Peter, when the close ones stop turning your way, what is it like out there?

Peter Forsberg

It is frustrating, of course. I think we played good tonight. We had the chances, we had the puck in their zone, and overtime, it is fifty-fifty. But two losses now, so, no. You are not happy.

Marcus Bell

Jamie Storr faced twenty and gave up four. From where you were tonight, was he getting screened? Was there traffic he could not see through?

Peter Forsberg

I think we did not help him enough. The whole team, not just one line. The first goal, maybe a screen, I am not sure exactly. But you know, Jamie gave us a chance to win in the third, and we did not close it. That is on us. We have to be better in front of our own net, clear the middle, do the small things. It is not on him.

Marcus Bell

Let me put something to you. You are at ten points in six games, twenty-four point eight a night. But after one hundred and twenty-nine last season, some people look at ten in six and think the wheels have come off a little.

Peter Forsberg

No. That is not true. Ten in six is, you do the math, it is more per game than last season. So it is not, I do not know what you call it, a step down. It is the same pace or better. I am playing the same game, the same minutes, the same lines. For sure, if we had won tonight it would be a nice night. But the number itself, it is not less than before.

Marcus Bell

The Bureau's Development Index has you below your base grade right now. Minus one. Do you follow that, or is that the kind of thing you ignore?

Peter Forsberg

I ignore it. I do not look at these things, no. I watch the video, I talk to the coaches, I know how my legs feel. And my legs feel fine. So, the Development Index. I do not think about it.

Marcus Bell

You told Jan in December that you would trade every goal you scored for two team points. You are on a two-game slide now. Does that feeling change at all when the losses start stacking up early?

Peter Forsberg

It is the same, but more. When you win and you score, the goal is, it is nice. You feel good. When you lose and you score, it is nothing. So of course it is harder now. The points mean nothing when the record is not right. You know? It is just a number in a book.

Marcus Bell

In January you were twenty-three and nine. Now you are two and one and two, and two of those losses went to overtime. Is anything different in how you close these games now, compared to then?

Peter Forsberg

No, I do not think so. The games are still close. We are still in them, we still have the chances. The difference is in January they went our way every night, and now two have gone the other way. That is hockey. You cannot win them all in overtime. But of course we want to close them. We want to win. That has not changed.

Marcus Bell

Peter, Jan asked you a few times if you would sit for a one-on-one about the Lindros trade, and that never got set up. Care if I try after the next home date?

Peter Forsberg

We will see. After the next home game, we talk. Maybe. I am not making promises.

Marcus Bell

Peter Forsberg, thank you again. Back to you.
