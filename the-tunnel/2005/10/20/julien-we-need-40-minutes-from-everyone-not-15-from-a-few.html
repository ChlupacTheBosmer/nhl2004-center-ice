Julien: 'We need 40 minutes from everyone, not 15 from a few'

The Canadiens' head coach on the third straight loss, the 9 goals in 5 games, and why the goaltending is not the problem.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelThursday, 20 October 2005

3:38 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

The Montreal Canadiens Canadiens lost 2-0 to the Toronto Maple Leafs at home on 2005-10-19, their third straight defeat, leaving them at 1-3-1 with 9 goals for and 14 against through 5 games. Claude Julien spoke to The Tunnel in the corridor after the final whistle about the scoring drought, the goaltending split between Jose Theodore and Dwayne Roloson, and the 10 healthy scratches his 29-man roster forces him to carry every night.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here in the Bowring Park corridor with Claude Julien. Coach, two-nothing, third straight, no goals scored. What did you see tonight?

Claude Julien

We had our looks. You know, we had, I mean the first period I thought we were solid, we had three or four good chances. And then after that we got flat. You can't get flat against a team like Toronto, they're, they're structured, they're deep, and they take the ice away from you. So you get your chances early and you have to bury them. We didn't tonight.

Marcus Bell

Thirty-one shots on your end, you had 26. That's not a team that was shut out of scoring chances.

Claude Julien

Exactly. And that's what frustrates me, right? 26 shots, that's not nothing. That's not nothing. But when you get 26 shots against a team that's, that's got a 67-grade back end, you need to convert some of them. And the 9 goals in 5 games is, it's not enough. It's not enough to win hockey games.

Marcus Bell

Jose Theodore started and took the loss. Dwayne Roloson is 0-2. How do you manage that net right now?

Claude Julien

You know, people look at the goaltending first. And I get it. You get shut out, the first finger points at the goalie. But I'll tell you what I told the guys: we gave up two goals tonight, that's not a goaltending problem. That's a problem in front of them. We had two, three breakdowns where we opened the middle of the ice and Toronto made you pay. Jose played well tonight, he had some good save on the two-on-one late, and that's the one that could have been three-nothing. We're not putting this on the goaltenders.

Marcus Bell

You have 29 men dressed and nobody on the injured list. Ten healthy scratches every night. Do you go to a different look, try someone different up front?

Claude Julien

Well, look. When you've got nobody hurt and you've got 29 guys, that's, that's a problem in itself. Everybody's good. Mike Ribeiro's a guy, obviously we'd like him in there, he's a skilled player. But then you've got guys like Chad Kilger, who gives you size, gives you a presence. You've got Magnus Arvedson who's solid defensively. So when you're 1-3-1 and you haven't scored, you start thinking about it. You think about a change. But I'm not going to blow up something that's, I mean, we're not bad. We're not. We're flat. There's a difference.

Marcus Bell

Richard Zednik has 6 points, 4 goals in 5 games. Is he the guy who has to carry this offensively?

Claude Julien

You put it on Richard, sure, he's had a good start. 4 goals, that's real. But I can't ask him to carry it every night, he's not that kind of player. He's a 20-foot guy, he needs service. And right now we're not getting him service enough. Mike Komisarek's got 4 points from the blue line and he's a defenceman. That tells you something about where our offense has been coming from. And it shouldn't be coming from there.

Marcus Bell

Three in four days starts with Pittsburgh on Saturday, then Philadelphia on Monday. Does that urgency change how you line up?

Claude Julien

It changes, you know, it changes the urgency in the room, obviously. You talk to them about it. Three in four, Pittsburgh is hot, they're 2-1-0 and they're winning. You've got to be ready to go from the opening whistle. You don't get to start slow in these games because there's no time to recover. So yes, it weighs on the preparation. You shorten things. You focus on the details, you don't try to install a new breakout at 11 o'clock in the morning.

Marcus Bell

One last one. The dressing room, you've got guys on 92 morale on average. Is there frustration, or is it more confusion about what's not clicking?

Claude Julien

It's frustration, sure. These guys want to win. Nobody came here for training camp and said, yeah, let's lose our first three. They're frustrated because they know they're better than the record shows. And the, the thing with a young group, you know you've got, you've got guys here who are 21, 22 years old, they're going to look at the standings and worry. But 28.4 years of average on this team and they've got some maturity in there. We'll get it fixed. It's not complicated. You compete for 60 minutes, you manage the puck, you get pucks to the net. We've been, we've been 30 minutes and change a game. We need 40. That's the whole thing.

Marcus Bell

Claude Julien, thank you very much, Coach.

Claude Julien

Thank you. Back to you.
