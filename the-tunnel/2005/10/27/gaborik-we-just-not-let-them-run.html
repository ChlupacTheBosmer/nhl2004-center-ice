Gaborik: 'We just not let them run'

After a 2-0 win over New York Rangers®, Marian Gaborik talked about the building, Tellqvist's shutout, and why his three points in eight games do not keep him up at night.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelThursday, 27 October 2005

1:59 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Marian Gaborik99 had one assist in Toronto Maple Leafs's 2-0 win over New York Rangers on 2005-10-26, a night that saw Mikael Tellqvist stop everything for the shutout and the home club outshoot the visitors 34 to 18. Toronto Maple Leafs now sits at 7-2-0, 14 points, on a two-game win streak. Gaborik spoke to Marcus Bell in the tunnel.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here with Marian Gaborik. Two-nil, Tellqvist with the blank sheet, thirty-four to eighteen in shots. From the ice, walk me through it.

Marian Gaborik

Yeah, it's good game tonight. You know, Mikael was, he was just... he takes everything. We shoot thirty-four and he stops, I think, all of them. And the room, we play good tonight. We not let them have the speed. That's the thing, you know? They want to open it up and we just, we stay in the middle and they have nowhere to go.

Marcus Bell

Now New York came in tied with you on fourteen points. Did that register at all before puck drop, the two clubs at the top of the table?

Marian Gaborik

No, no. We don't look at that. We know they're good, we know they're right there in the standings, but you can't play a game with the standings in your head. And look, they play different on the road. One and three away, they're, how you say, more clogged. They drop back, they wait. At home they come at you more. Tonight they wait and we have to find the space. So that's, that's what the thirty-four shots says. We had to work for it.

Marcus Bell

Your own night: the one assist. What did you see there?

Marian Gaborik

I see the lane. The D-man, he step up and there's the space behind, and I just push it through. Barret is there, he puts it in, good shot. So, yeah, one point. I like it.

Marcus Bell

You're three-nil on the road this year. In December you told me the shorter shifts keep your legs at full and open ice helps. Does this building change what you do out there, with this crowd behind you?

Marian Gaborik

It's different, yeah. On the road you want to go fast, you get the first puck and you go. Here you know, the crowd, they push you. You make a hit, they are up. You get one, they are up. So you play a little more with them, how you say? You play with the energy in the building. But you still need the speed. You can't slow down just because people cheer.

Marcus Bell

Three points in eight games. I know how you feel about individual numbers, I've heard you say it before, but you're at 20.4 minutes a night, you're the best player in this room, and the rest of the league is counting those goals. Does it not itch a little?

Marian Gaborik

You always ask me this. Yeah, three points in eight, it's, I know what you mean. But I'm healthy, I feel good, I play twenty minutes and the team is winning. That's what matters to me. The goals, they come. They always come. You just stay in it, you keep pushing. Seven-two right now, that's what I look at. Not my number.

Marcus Bell

Marian Gaborik, thank you. Back to you.
