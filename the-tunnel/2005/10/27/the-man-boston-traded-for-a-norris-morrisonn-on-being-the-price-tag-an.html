The man Boston traded for a Norris: Morrisonn on being the price tag and coming home

Shaone Morrisonn tells The Tunnel what Sergei Gonchar taught him, why the Development Index jumped 11 grades, and what he does with 0 years on a $1.10M deal.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelThursday, 27 October 2005

5:18 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Shaone Morrisonn79 was 19th overall in 2001, the Boston defense prospect the club sent to Washington in March 2004 for Sergei Gonchar. Twenty-two months later he is back with Boston Bruins, sitting at 74 in the Book — 11 grades above his base of 70 on the Bureau's Development Index — on a contract with 0 years left at $1.10M.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Shaone Morrisonn, good of you to sit down with us. You're the subject of a profile this week, the Sweater's piece, 'The 99 That Boston Gave Away.' Does that headline bother you?

Shaone Morrisonn

No. Honestly? No. I read it and I laughed, because, you know, it's... it's true. They gave me away. That's the trade. You can't really argue with it. You trade Shaone Morrisonn for Sergei Gonchar and you write whatever headline you want after that.

Marcus Bell

Were you surprised when it happened? March 2004, the Gonchar deal?

Shaone Morrisonn

A little. I mean, I wasn't playing a ton, so, you know, I saw the writing on the wall a bit. Thirty-one games the year before, ten minutes a night. I knew I was the fourth or fifth D. But when your name actually comes up, when they call you into the office, it's different. You go, 'oh, this is actually happening.'

Marcus Bell

And you went to Washington, played there, and then you're back here. What changed?

Shaone Morrisonn

Ice time. That's the whole thing. I went to Washington and I wasn't the fourth guy anymore. I was the second or third. And when you're out there nineteen, twenty minutes a night, you figure out what you are. You figure out, 'oh, I can actually move the puck' — I don't mean scoring, I mean the breakout, the first pass. You get reps and it comes out.

Marcus Bell

Let me ask you about Sergei Gonchar, because he's the reason you were dealt, and I think the interesting question is what you took from watching him. What did Gonchar teach you?

Shaone Morrisonn

The simplest thing and the hardest thing. He joined the rush on a read, not a gamble. I used to step up because I thought, 'I've got speed, I've got the shot, I'm gonna go.' And he'd just... wait. And then the moment the guy's head came down, the pass came, and he was through. I'd been doing it the wrong way my whole life. You know? And he's a Norris winner. And he's teaching me how to play D.

Marcus Bell

He won the Norris that year. You don't get a guy like that by accident.

Shaone Morrisonn

No. You don't. And that's the thing about the trade, right? They traded me for a Norris winner. I wasn't bitter about it. I thought, 'that's fair value. I'd trade me for him too.'

Marcus Bell

And now you're in a room with Joe Thornton. Eighty-six in the Book, nine goals and thirteen points out of seven games. Who's the best player you've shared a locker room with?

Shaone Morrisonn

It's Thornton. It's gotta be. I played with Gonchar, yeah, and Gonchar was... he's special, the way he sees the ice. But Jumbo is a different animal. You put the puck on his stick and he just... he doesn't need a second look. He knows where you're going before you do. And he makes the guy around him look ten grades better. I've got an 83 on the pass in the Book. I think some of that's him giving it to me.

Marcus Bell

The Bureau's got you at plus-11 on the Development Index. Base is 70, you're sitting at 74 overall. That's the biggest jump I've seen on a young defenseman this edition. What did the coach do differently here?

Shaone Morrisonn

The coach here told me something I hadn't heard before. He said, 'you don't have to be the best player on the ice, you have to be the most difficult player on the ice.' And I thought that was... that was kind of obvious. But then you actually play it, you know? You just use the body. You're 6'5", you're 182, you take that space. I was trying to be a skill guy. I've got 51 in fighting, I've got 50 in hero, those aren't... I'm not that guy. But 'difficult' I can be.

Marcus Bell

Your shot is 92. That's the highest grade on your sheet. Does that number mean something to you?

Shaone Morrisonn

Yeah. Obviously. When you're a big defenseman and your shot is 92, you want to use it. I just... the issue is getting into position, you know? Last year I had one goal in 31 games. This year in five games I've got... zero goals, two assists. So I'm not putting that 92 on the scoresheet yet. But the puck comes off different for me. You know when you hit one clean and it sounds different? That's every day now.

Marcus Bell

You missed time last spring. Elbow, sometime between February 13th and 16th, back April 27th. That's about ten weeks. How did you come back from that?

Shaone Morrisonn

Slowly. That's the honest answer. Slowly. An elbow, it's not a knee, it's not a shoulder, you can't just... you can't test it in a game the way you can a leg. So for the first two weeks back I was just... passing it around. I didn't take a shot for two weeks. And that's hard when your shot is the thing, you know?

Marcus Bell

You've got zero years left on your deal. A million one, I think the number is.

Shaone Morrisonn

Yeah. One-ten. And look, I'm not going to stand here and, you know, play games with it. I know what's happening. The Index goes up eleven, the profile runs, everyone's talking about the 99 potential, and now the money changes. I get it. I'm not... I don't want to be that guy who's always on TV going 'I want more.' I just want to get an extension before January.

Marcus Bell

Who handles that? Do you have an agent, or are you in the room with Peter Chiarelli and talking?

Shaone Morrisonn

I've got someone. I don't want to get into the details of who's calling whom. But I'll tell you this — I don't want to leave here. I was traded away once and I didn't like it. Washington was fine, the people were fine, but I wasn't home. Vancouver to Boston is a long way, but this is... I was drafted here. I want to finish here, I guess.

Marcus Bell

The room is young. Ten guys twenty-four or under out of twenty-nine. Does that change how the leadership works on a blue line where you're 22 and Bryan Berard is 77 in the Book?

Shaone Morrisonn

Berard's funny. You'd think, 'oh, the veteran's gonna talk a lot.' He barely says anything. He just plays. And you watch him and you learn more from the way he positions his body on a breakout than from any meeting. So I don't know if leadership in that room is... it's more like, the guys who've been around just do their thing and you copy them. That's how I learned from Gonchar anyway.

Marcus Bell

Last one. The Sweater's profile calls you 'the 99 they gave away.' The Bureau's Development Index has you at plus-11 right now. If the Book gets to its next edition and you're sitting at 80, what does that number mean to you?

Shaone Morrisonn

Then I'm not the 99 anymore. Then I'm the 80, which is a lot less sexy for a headline. Honestly though? Eighty is... I've been a 70 guy my whole life. I was a 70 guy in Kamloops, I was a 70 guy in Boston's system, I was a 70 guy at the trade. If the Bureau puts me at 80, that means I stopped being the 70 guy. And that's... yeah. That's what I want.

Marcus Bell

Shaone Morrisonn, thank you for your time. Good luck this year. Back to you.
