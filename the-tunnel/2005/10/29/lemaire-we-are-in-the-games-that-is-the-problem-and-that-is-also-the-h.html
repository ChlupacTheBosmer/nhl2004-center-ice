Lemaire: 'We are in the games. That is the problem, and that is also the hope'

Minnesota's coach makes the Pelletier call official, welcomes Pihlman back to the top line, and refuses to call it a season that has slipped away.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelSaturday, 29 October 2005

2:59 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Minnesota Wild sits at 1-5-2 through 9 games after losing 3-4 at Colorado Avalanche on 2005-10-27, their 6th straight without a win. Head coach Jacques Lemaire spoke at the bench afterward about the goaltending change, the return of Tuomo Pihlman78, and a team that keeps finding itself inside games it cannot finish.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Jacques Lemaire at the bench here in Denver after that one. Marcus Bell, The Tunnel. Six in a row without a win. Three-four to Colorado. How do you take it tonight?

Jacques Lemaire

You take it like you take all of them. You look at what you did right and what you did not, and you go again tomorrow. Tonight we scored 3, that is not nothing. We had 23 shots, they had 24. We were in it.

Marcus Bell

You say you were in it, but you are 1-5-2. The first one came four weeks ago. What does the room look like in there right now?

Jacques Lemaire

Quiet. Not defeated, I want to be clear about that. Quiet. The guys know what they are doing wrong. They are not confused. They are, I mean, they feel the weight of it, for sure. But quiet is not the same as giving up.

Marcus Bell

You made a move tonight. Jean-Marc Pelletier in net. Manny Fernandez on the bench. That is the third change at that position this season. Walk me through that call.

Jacques Lemaire

It is not three changes. It is one change, made once, and we stick with it. Manny has given us everything. 6 games, 2 wins. He is our guy when he is sharp. Tonight I wanted a different look. Jean-Marc had been good when he came in, 3 games, and I wanted to see what that gives us.

Marcus Bell

Manny's morale is at 75. That's the fifth-lowest in the league. Is he hurt by this?

Jacques Lemaire

I do not talk about morale numbers in this room. I do not. He is a grown man, he is a competitor, he knows what the coach has to decide. I talk to him. We talk every day. You think I put him in a corner and walk away? No. We have a relationship, you know? And I respect him for how he handles it.

Marcus Bell

Fair enough. Tuomo Pihlman is back on the first line tonight, top power play unit, penalty kill. He was not in that spot last week. What changed?

Jacques Lemaire

He earned it. That is all there is to it. Tuomo came in and in practice he was the guy winning battles. The Calder last year was nice, sure. But last year is last year. Right now he has to be the best player on that shift or he sits, and this week he was. So he plays.

Marcus Bell

He has 4 assists in 5 games since he came back in. Does he see more ice going forward?

Jacques Lemaire

If he keeps competing like he did this week, yes. I am not going to tell you how many minutes, that is between us. But he has legs, he has vision, he is 21 and he does not think about his contract, he thinks about his next shift. That is what I want from everybody, actually.

Marcus Bell

Pierre-Marc Bouchard moved up to the top power play unit too. That is a 21-year-old on your first PP. What does he see that you like?

Jacques Lemaire

He sees ice that is not there yet, you understand? The kid, he is patient with the puck in a way that makes me nervous sometimes. But on the power play that patience is a weapon. He does not force it. He waits for the seam. And if you have 5 on 4 and a guy who waits, that is dangerous.

Marcus Bell

So the young guys are coming in, the lines are shifting, the net is different. Is there a structural thing you want to change, or is this about execution inside the system?

Jacques Lemaire

It is execution. I will not change the system after 9 games. The system works. When we manage the puck, when we are responsible in the neutral zone, we are hard to score on. But we are taking chances we do not need to take. Turnovers in our own end that become goals against. That is a decision the player makes, and it is not what we practice. That is what I can fix.

Marcus Bell

You have Colorado here tomorrow. A quick turnaround. Does that change anything about how you look at tonight?

Jacques Lemaire

It changes everything about how I look at tomorrow. We get on the bus, we sleep, we get here, we practice, we are ready. I cannot feel sorry for tonight. The guys know that too. One game, then the next one. That is all there is.

Marcus Bell

Jacques Lemaire, head coach of Minnesota, thank you. Back to you.
