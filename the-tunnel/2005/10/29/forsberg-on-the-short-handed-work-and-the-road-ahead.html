Forsberg on the short-handed work and the road ahead

Two goals in the win over Minnesota, but Forsberg spent his time after the game on the penalty kill and what the next week asks.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelSaturday, 29 October 2005

1:50 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Peter Forsberg95 had two goals and two points as Colorado Avalanche beat Minnesota Wild 4-3 on 2005-10-27, his second multi-goal game of the season. The 32-year-old left wing spoke to The Tunnel about the special teams work that has been a talking point since January and about what the schedule asks of a team sitting 3-2-2.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here, in the corridor at the Colosseum, with Peter Forsberg. Peter, 4-3 over Minnesota, two goals from you tonight. How did that one feel from inside?

Peter Forsberg

Yeah, it was, I think we played good. Not good, maybe not all sixty minutes, but we found the way to win. That is what matters.

Jan Kowalczyk

You told this desk back in January that the special teams were 4 on 18, that the gap was the gap. I looked after tonight and I want to ask, is the unit in a different place now?

Peter Forsberg

Well. The penalty kill, yes, it is... we have more discipline in it now. Before, we were, I think, too aggressive, we came out of the box and then we were not back in position. Now we stay, we wait for the puck. And when the puck comes, Joe or Paul can move it out. So yes, it feels more, for sure, it feels like a system and not just four men trying to survive.

Jan Kowalczyk

You mentioned Paul. Three points for Kariya tonight, his third multi-point game in this stretch. He is 34, he is playing like that. What does that say about him?

Peter Forsberg

I think it says he is good. He has always been good. You know, people sometimes forget because he has been here a while, but his hands, his read on the play, that does not go away. I see it every shift on the power play. He gets open and the puck finds him. That is all of us trying to find him, but he makes it easy.

Jan Kowalczyk

Peter, you are three and two and two after eight. The Northwest is tight, Vancouver right behind you, Calgary, everyone. What does the next stretch ask?

Peter Forsberg

Everything. You look at the schedule and it is, it is no rest. You go, you play, you travel, you play again. In October it does not feel so bad. Your legs are there from camp. But we know where this goes. If we want to be, if we want to be the team that plays in April and not the team that watches it, we have to take points now. You cannot catch up in March.

Jan Kowalczyk

Two goals tonight. Does the personal stuff, the points, the Book number, does it mean anything at 32 with one year left?

Peter Forsberg

No. I mean, yes, it is nice to score, for sure. But I would trade two goals for a two-nil win. I said that before. I say it again. The wins are what matter. Everything else is just... paper.

Jan Kowalczyk

Peter Forsberg, thank you as always. Back to you.
