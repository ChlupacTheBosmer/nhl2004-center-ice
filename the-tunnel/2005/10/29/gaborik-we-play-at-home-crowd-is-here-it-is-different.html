Gaborik: 'We play at home, crowd is here, it is different'

The right wing scored his third of nine games in a 3-1 win over Anaheim, then talked about what the coming road stretch asks of a line that feeds off speed and open ice.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelSaturday, 29 October 2005

2:22 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Marian Gaborik99 scored once in Toronto Maple Leafs's 3-1 win over Anaheim on Saturday night, giving the club its third straight and moving Toronto to 8-2-0, first in the Northeast with 16 points. Gaborik has 3 goals and 4 points in 9 games this season, a quieter start than his 99 overall and the +11 on the Bureau's Development Index would suggest. Toronto outshot Anaheim 21 to 30 and won anyway.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here in the tunnel, Marian. Three straight, eight wins in ten, and you get one on the board tonight. How does the room feel after that one?

Marian Gaborik

Yeah, good, we play good game tonight. They shoot 30, we shoot 21, and we win, so, you know, the goalie was big. Mikael was big. That is how we do it. We do not need the most shots, we need the right ones and he makes the saves on the rest. Good feeling.

Jan Kowalczyk

You've been quiet by your own standard, three in nine. Did tonight's goal come at the right time for you personally?

Marian Gaborik

For me, no, I do not think about when. I think about the team and I think about what the line does together. If I score tonight or next week, it is the same. We are winning. The goals, they come, you know, the game opens up and the speed is there. I am not, how you say, pressing it.

Jan Kowalczyk

You told me back in January, after a loss in Boston, that the result was on the club and not on you personally. You're 8-2 now. Does it feel different when the wins are coming?

Marian Gaborik

Yes, it feels different, how you say, lighter. But I said the same thing because I believe it still. We win together, we lose together, I was not wrong, I was just losing. Now I am losing less. That is the difference.

Jan Kowalczyk

You are 5-2 at home, 3-0 on the road. Tonight the building was loud. Does that crowd tonight help a line like yours that feeds off speed and open ice?

Marian Gaborik

Yes, at home the crowd is here, they push, and when we get the puck in our end and I turn, the noise goes up, you know, it gives me energy to go the other way. On the road it is different. The crowd is against, the ice is theirs, the bus is long. You have to bring the same speed into a building that does not want you to have it. That is harder. But we are 3-0 so, you know, it is okay.

Jan Kowalczyk

What does the road ask of you that home does not? Is it the travel, the building, the crowd, all of it?

Marian Gaborik

It is the legs, I think. When you travel you get on the bus or the plane, you get to the hotel, you sleep strange, you wake up and the body is not the same. At home I sleep my bed, I eat what I want, I come here and the legs are fresh. On the road you have to take care, you have to, you know, I do not know how you say, I have to be good with my body between games. It is not so easy.

Jan Kowalczyk

Hainsey had a goal and two points from the blue line tonight. You and him, does that open things for your side of the ice, him joining up like that?

Marian Gaborik

Yeah, when Ron goes in like that, the other team, they have to watch, and that opens a little space for me in the neutral zone, so, you know, I can find a little more ice on the first step. He does it well. Good for him.

Jan Kowalczyk

Marian, thank you. Back to you.
