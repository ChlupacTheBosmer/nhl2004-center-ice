Gaborik on the Index at 95: 'Less Is More'

The winger is averaging 19.4 minutes a night, down from 21.9 last season, and the Bureau's Development Index has him up six. He says the coach came to him with the idea.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelWednesday, 9 February 2005

2:18 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Marian Gaborik98 scored as Toronto Maple Leafs beat Montreal Canadiens 5-2 at home on Tuesday night, his 42nd of the season in 55 games. The winger sits at 95 on the League Scouting Bureau's Development Index, six above his base grade, while averaging 19.4 minutes a night this season against 21.9 last year.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here in the tunnel at the Air Canada Centre with Marian Gaborik. Marian, 5-2 over Montreal tonight, and your 42nd on the year. You're up six on the Development Index, sitting at 95 overall, but you're on 19.4 a night, down from 21.9 last season. What's different about your game?

Marian Gaborik

Yeah, I feel good, you know. Shorter shift, I come on, my legs are, how you say, fresh. Before, 21, 22 minutes, and the last five minutes I am... I am not me. Now I go short, I come off, I rest, I go again, full speed every time. It's better for me.

Marcus Bell

What changed on the bench? Did the coach move your line?

Marian Gaborik

Yeah. Brad. We play now, I think four, five games. Before I play with Mats and it's good, but it's different tempo, you know. Brad, he... he see the play before it's there. I go, and he put it right on my stick. He know where I am. So that is, how you say, that is a different game for me.

Marcus Bell

You had your goal tonight and four other TOR guys scored. Mogilny, and three defensemen as well. Five names on the board. Does that change how you play?

Marian Gaborik

Yeah, it change. Before in Minnesota Wild, everybody know, they play for me, everybody look for me. Here, everybody can score. The D score, everybody score. So I don't force. I wait. If I have the chance, I take it. If not, I give to somebody, and somebody else make it. Five names tonight. That's good hockey.

Marcus Bell

You're 144 goals against in 55. Lowest in the league. Does knowing that behind you change what you try up ice?

Marian Gaborik

Yeah, big. I know behind me, the defense is good, and Roberto, you see him tonight, he make the save. So I can, how you say, I can take the chance. I can go around, I can try. Because if I lose it, they fix it. Before I think, if I lose it here, they score. Now I trust the guys. That's different, you know.

Marcus Bell

Tell me about the goal. What do you see?

Marian Gaborik

I get the puck, I go, the D he step up, I go around, I shoot short side. And maybe the goalie he didn't see it. You know? Sometimes is just, you go, you shoot, and it go in. I don't think too much, you know.

Marcus Bell

Back in December I asked you about first-shift intensity and you said the shorter shifts keep your speed at full. Did the coach come to you about the ice time, or did you go to him?

Marian Gaborik

No, no, he come to me. After a couple games, we talk, and he say, Marian, less is more. And I think, yeah, you're right, because my legs, when I come on, they're fresh every time. And my job is go fast and score. Not play 22 minutes and be tired. So I say, okay, 19, that's fine.

Marcus Bell

Gaborik, good game. Thanks for stopping. Back to you.
