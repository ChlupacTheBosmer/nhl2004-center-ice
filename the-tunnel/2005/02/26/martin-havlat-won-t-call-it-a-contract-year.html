Martin Havlat Won't Call It a Contract Year

Havlat says he plays for the room and not for July, and he keeps the spring of 2003 ahead of the 80 points he has now.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelSaturday, 26 February 2005

4:36 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Martin Havlat has 80 points in 63 games for the Ottawa Senators, 31 goals and 49 assists at 20.7 minutes a night, with one season left on a $0.90M contract. He sat with Marcus Bell this week to talk about the small rink in Mlada Boleslav where the game started, the run to the conference finals in 2003, and a word he will not use about himself.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell, The Tunnel, sitting with Martin Havlat of the Ottawa Senators. Martin, thanks for the time. Start easy. Where does hockey start for you? Before the Czech league, before any of this.

Martin Havlat

Yeah, Mlada Boleslav. Is small town, you know, near Prague. I start there, on the ice outside, when I am maybe 4, 5. Small kid. Always cold. I don't remember not playing.

Marcus Bell

And who put you on the ice?

Martin Havlat

My father. He put me on the skates, first time. My ankles, they go like this. But he, uh, he skates with me, and I don't want to stop, so I keep going. Is simple, you know. Somebody takes you, you stay.

Marcus Bell

Then the Czech league, and then 1999. 26th overall. Do you remember that day?

Martin Havlat

I don't know the number that day, no. I know my name and I wait and I wait. Then Ottawa picks me. 26, okay. I stay one more year home after, and that is good for me, I think. I am not ready yet.

Marcus Bell

Then 2000-01, you come over. Rookie year. How hard is that?

Martin Havlat

Everything is different. The rink smaller, the guys bigger, the language, I don't have it, you know. On the ice, hockey is hockey, that I know. Off the ice, I learn. First year I learn a lot.

Marcus Bell

Your third year, 24 goals, 59 points, and the run to the conference finals. What do you keep from that spring?

Martin Havlat

The building, when we win the series. I never hear noise like that. Then we lose and it is quiet. Both. I keep both of them.

Marcus Bell

This year the sheet says 63 games, 31 goals, 49 assists, 80 points. Is that the record you are proudest of, or is it 2003?

Martin Havlat

Proud, that is a big word, Marcus. The 80 points, I don't count them. I count the games we win. But 2003, that run... when you are young and you go that far, you think, okay, this is every year. Is not every year. Now I know better.

Marcus Bell

The Bureau's Book has your speed at 99. Top of the scale. Agility 95, acceleration 94. When did you know you were fast?

Martin Havlat

I always am fast. No, I am serious. When I am small I am fast, but everybody is small and fast. Then you come here and everybody is fast too. So the speed, is not enough. You need the hands, the head, how you say, the timing.

Marcus Bell

One year left at $0.90M. The profile on you calls this the contract year, the one where a man plays for July. Is that how it works in your head?

Martin Havlat

No. I hear this and I don't like it. I don't play for July. I play for the room. The money, I don't know about the money, my agent knows. I know the next game. That is my job. The rest is not my job.

Marcus Bell

Then what are you playing for this spring?

Martin Havlat

The playoffs. This team. Daniel, Marian, Radek, we have the group here, you know. Last year, 4 games, 0 wins, out. That stays with you all summer. I don't want to feel that again. Nobody in that room wants it.

Marcus Bell

Last year you had 48 games, 25 goals, 25 assists, 50 points, and then the four playoff games, 2 and 2. Then the sweep. Did the summer change something?

Martin Havlat

I skate, I think. You lose 0 and 4 and you look at yourself, what you do. I come back and I have the 80 points, but the points don't fix it. Only the spring fixes it. So.

Marcus Bell

Ken Hitchcock had something to say about you that year, that "somebody is going to make him eat his lunch." You were suspended twice, 4 games total, once for kicking, once for high-sticking.

Martin Havlat

I know what he says. Is his job to say things. My job is to play. The suspensions, I don't like it. 4 games, the team pays for that, not me. So I try to be smarter. But I don't change how I play. Maybe a little, here and there, not who I am.

Marcus Bell

Alfredsson is your captain. What does he give you?

Martin Havlat

Daniel, he talks to me, and not always about hockey. When I am young and I do something stupid, he says, Martin, come here. Not loud. Just, come here. That is the captain, you know. You remember it.

Marcus Bell

Chara behind you, Redden beside you, Lalime in the net. Does that change how you attack?

Martin Havlat

With Zdeno, you skate, you don't look, he is there and is fine. Wade, same. And Patty, when he stops the puck like that, we all feel it on the bench. So yes. Maybe you take one more chance, because they cover you. Is a good feeling.

Marcus Bell

The Book has you at 84 overall, up 3 on the Development Index, and the Morale Scale has you at 92. Does it feel like that in the room?

Martin Havlat

I don't know the index, Marcus. You know the numbers better than me. But the room, yeah, the room is good. We win, we laugh, we eat, we play cards, the same guys. That part is true.

Marcus Bell

Back to the start. Small kid, cold rink, Mlada Boleslav. Does that kid know he gets to here, 80 points, conference finals?

Martin Havlat

No. He knows nothing. He just wants to play and not go inside. Maybe that is still the same, you know. I still want to play. Everything else is extra.

Marcus Bell

You said proud is a big word, and you still have to pick one. What do you want on your file?

Martin Havlat

The spring of 2003. The conference finals. Because you never know you go back, and then 4 games, 0 and 4, you see. So I keep that one. The 80 points is nice, is nice. But the spring, that one I keep.

Marcus Bell

Martin Havlat of the Ottawa Senators. Thank you for the time. Back to you.
