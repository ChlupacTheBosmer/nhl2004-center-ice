Farkas: Smyth stays, the rooms get the money

Edmonton's general manager says the club is a buyer at the deadline and that Ryan Smyth's contract is not a problem he will solve by trading him.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelSaturday, 26 February 2005

4:46 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Paul Farkas has run Edmonton Oilers through 24 wins, 19 losses and 8 overtime losses this season, good for 78 points and third place in the Northwest. The oil is there: a $36.81M payroll against $64.62M in revenue produced $29.23M in profit this year. The rooms are not. Farkas sat with Ryan Smyth82's expiring deal, a four-game slide and 21 days until the deadline, and told Marcus Bell where the money goes.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here from the studio with Paul Farkas, who runs the hockey side for Edmonton. Paul, thanks for sitting down. You're at 78 points, third in your division, and you've dropped four in a row. What does this club look like to you right now?

Paul Farkas

Thanks for having me, Marcus. Look, we're in a playoff spot. I'm not going to sit here and pretend four losses feels good, because it doesn't. We've scored 214 and given up 221, that's the whole story of us right now. We're a one-goal team in a tough division. But 78 points with 20 games left and we're still second in this race by one point, one behind Colorado at 79. I know what the streak looks like on paper. I'm not selling short where we are.

Marcus Bell

You made $29.23M in profit this year. That's the fourth-highest number in the league. The payroll is $36.81M, which is fourth-lowest. A lot of your peers are spending twice what you spend and getting fewer points. When you look at what this franchise is worth as a business, what do you see?

Paul Farkas

I see a building that's full. 15,027 a night, 79 dollars a ticket, people showing up. I see a club that can pay its bills and still make a profit. And Marcus, I'll tell you what that profit is worth to me. It's worth getting the practice rink up from where it is. That's what I'm thinking about. Not what the number is on a balance sheet for somebody in a boardroom somewhere. What's it do for the guys on the ice Tuesday night? That's the question I ask myself when I look at the money.

Marcus Bell

And you moved on the equipment room last month. I understand it went from zero to one, first time since December.

Paul Farkas

Yeah, right around the sixth or seventh. And I'll be honest, that was the first one that felt like we could breathe. Because the practice rink is at zero, the workout room is at one, travel is at two, none of those are where I want them. Equipment was the one I could actually see the effect on, the skating, the edges, the little things. You put a guy on a blade that fits his foot versus one that doesn't, and it's hockey. It's not the same game. So that went first. The practice rink, that's next. That's where the dollars go.

Marcus Bell

Let me put a name on the question everyone's asking you. Ryan Smyth. Eighty points in 55 games, a Hart Trophy to his name from last season, 84 overall, and $2.50M coming off the books in July. What does he do at the deadline?

Paul Farkas

He plays here in April. I mean it. I'll tell you right now, Marcus, I am not shopping Ryan Smyth. He's 29, he's playing the best hockey of his career, morale is 94, the man loves this town. You want to know what I think he's worth? I think he's the heart of this thing. I think a club that trades its Hart Trophy winner in February while it's still in the race is a club that's told itself a lie about where it's going.

Marcus Bell

So you're re-signing him. You're committing the money.

Paul Farkas

I'm going to make him an offer. What he does with that offer, that's his call and his agent's. But the club's side of it is yes. We want him here. I'm not going to let a man put up 80 points in a half-season and then just wave him out the door because the math looks cleaner at the end of June. That's not how you build something that lasts in this league.

Marcus Bell

That leaves the deadline itself. You've got 21 days. You've got that $29.23M in profit sitting there. Are you buying?

Paul Farkas

I'm telling you right now, we're not selling anything. If you're calling me about Steve Poapst, I know he's out with a hand and we're thin back there, but I'm not dumping someone to get picks. I want a piece that makes us harder to play against in April. That's what I'm looking at. We're good enough to be a problem for somebody in the first round and I want to take the swing that makes us better. Not worse. Not a future draft slot. Better.

Marcus Bell

You said you're not a future-picks club right now. Walk me through what that means operationally when you've got the lowest payroll in the division and the cheapest hockey per point in the league.

Paul Farkas

Okay, I hear you, I get the math. But Marcus, we're not in a fire sale, we're not in a rebuild, we don't have to pretend we're something we're not. What we are is a club that's been careful with its money because we had to be. The market is what it is. The revenue is what it is. We found a way to make $29.23M while spending 36. That doesn't mean I have to be cheap to be smart. I spent some of that profit in February. I'm going to spend more in March and more in the summer. The rooms come first, then a player or two. That's the order.

Marcus Bell

The four-game slide, Paul. You're watching it from the office. Does it change how you think about the next three weeks?

Paul Farkas

Of course it does. You're going to look at it and you're going to ask what's wrong. 214 for, 221 against. We're seven goals underwater on the season. The four losses, some of those we were flat. Travel's been rough, we're 16 and 15 on the road. I'm not going to sit here and make excuses, but the travel budget is at two and that costs you in the body. So you see, it comes back to the rooms. Everything comes back to the rooms. I fix the rooms, maybe we're not dropping four in a row in February.

Marcus Bell

Paul Farkas, thank you for your time.

Paul Farkas

Appreciate it, Marcus. Back to you.

Marcus Bell

Back to you.
