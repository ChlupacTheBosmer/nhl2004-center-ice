Gaborik: 'January We Shoot 32 and I Score, Tonight We Create Less'

After a 2-0 home blanking to the Islanders, the 23-year-old winger distinguishes this goalless night from the one Jan Kowalczyk asked about in January and says the 19-game run to April means nothing different to the room.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelSaturday, 26 February 2005

2:05 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Marian Gaborik98 and the Toronto Maple Leafs Maple Leafs lost 2-0 at home to the New York Islanders on Friday night, outshot 33-28. Gaborik, who carries 44 goals and 80 points in 63 games this season, was held without a point. Toronto leads the Northeast Division with 100 points through 63 games, 19 games remaining.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here with Marian Gaborik in the corridor. Marian, 28 shots, zero on the board, and the Islanders came in with 64 points and left here with two. What did that feel like inside?

Marian Gaborik

Yeah. It's, you know, it's frustrating. We had, maybe one or two in the first, I think I had one and maybe Gabor, I mean, we had chances. And the puck is, it don't go. You play a game where you don't give up much and you score zero, it's, how you say, it's the game is not fair tonight. That's what it is.

Jan Kowalczyk

I asked you on January the twelfth, after a game in Boston, about how the room resets when the shots don't produce points. You told me the result is on Toronto, not on Marian. Is this the same reset, or is something different here?

Marian Gaborik

This time, it's different. In January we shoot 32 and I score, so it feel okay. I can go home and watch the game and say, okay, the team didn't win, but we create. Tonight we shoot 28 and they shoot 33. We create less and they create more. That is, you know, that is not just the puck not going in. We didn't give ourselves enough chances. I think that's the, the bigger problem.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're at 100 points in 63. Boston is 70 with two games back. Nineteen games left. Does the room feel the weight of that lead, or does it not change anything?

Marian Gaborik

Everybody looks at the table. I look at the table. I think Brad looks at the table. But we don't change how we play. We practice the same, we prepare same. You cannot say, oh, we have 100 points, let's play soft tonight. That's not, that's how you lose in April. And I don't think anybody in this room wants to talk about April when we lose 0-2 at home. You know?

Jan Kowalczyk

And for you personally, 19 games, 20 minutes a night, 80 points on the board. Does this stretch ask something different of you?

Marian Gaborik

It ask the same. I want to win. If I score and we win, perfect. If I don't score and we win, also perfect. Eighty is good, sure. I won't say it's not good. But it don't help in April if we play these 19 games for points instead of for, for the 60 games we need to be ready. So I think tonight is maybe, how you say, maybe a good thing. I don't like it. I don't like lose zero-two at home. But maybe we need it.

Jan Kowalczyk

Marian Gaborik, thank you again. Back to you.
