Stirling: one night is one night, and DiPietro made it a good one

The Islanders' head coach took the win in Toronto for what it was, then pointed at the young guys in the bottom six and the 25-28-4 that sits behind it.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelSaturday, 26 February 2005

3:26 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

New York Islanders beat Toronto Maple Leafs 2-0 on the road on 2005-02-25, the Islanders' second win in a row and a statement against the best club in the league at 100 points. Steve Stirling's team is 25-28-4, 64 points, 3rd in the Atlantic division. Rick DiPietro89 made the saves; the young lines carried the minutes Stirling has been looking for.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here at the Air Canada Centre with Steve Stirling. Coach, 2-0 on the road against a hundred-point team. What does that tell you about this group?

Steve Stirling

It tells me we played 60 minutes right. That's what it tells me. We didn't do it for 60 minutes in Buffalo, and we didn't do it last week against Philly, and tonight we did. You know, a hundred-point team is a hundred-point team, they're not going to get worse tomorrow because we beat them tonight. But we managed the puck, we competed, and DiPietro was standing on his head at the end there, so the details lined up.

Marcus Bell

Rick's got one shutout on the year, 46 games in. Does this one come in a bigger spot than any of the other ones he's had?

Steve Stirling

Every game matters at this point. You know, I don't rank them. I told him in there, I said, 'Ricky, that's a good one,' and he said 'yeah, okay.' Twenty-three wins is 23 wins. I'd like him to have a couple more before the break, that's just the arithmetic of where we are.

Marcus Bell

You're 25-28-4 right now, coach. You beat the best team in the league and you're still under .500. What's the gap between what you saw tonight and what the standings show?

Steve Stirling

The gap is 20 some-odd games where we didn't play like we played tonight. That's the gap. I look at the schedule, I look at where we are, I know what we are. We're a team that can beat anybody on a night where everything clicks, and we're a team that can lose to anybody when it doesn't. Tonight was a click. I want to see it for a week. Three games, four games, that's what I want.

Marcus Bell

Kristian Timander is 21 years old, 27 games, 17 points. He's the youngest man in your room. How much are you asking of him right now?

Steve Stirling

I'm asking him to play his game. He's got, he's got hands, he's got speed, and what he needed was confidence that he wasn't going to get pulled after two bad shifts. So I keep him out there. The kid's earned his minutes, he competes, and he's not going to stop. Seventeen points in 27 games at 21, you know, you don't tell that kid he's too young to be out there. He's telling me he's old enough.

Marcus Bell

And the other young guys. Justin Papineau's in 42, Mapletoft's played all 62, Weinhandl all 62. Is that your fourth line or is that a development project?

Steve Stirling

It's both, and I don't apologize for that. Look, the fourth line is supposed to be the easiest minutes to give. If you're developing a guy, you're developing him in the easiest minutes, not the hardest. They give me a shift, two shifts, a PK shift if it fits. They're earning their ice time the way everybody else does. I don't hand out minutes because of a birthday.

Marcus Bell

Let me ask about the schedule. You've got New Jersey at home Friday, then Buffalo Monday, then you're at the Rangers, then Tampa Thursday. Four games in six days, three of them divisional.

Steve Stirling

I know what it is. New Jersey's a good team, they're playing good hockey, 85 points, they're going to push us every night. We'll deal with it. I think the thing that gives me a little comfort, if you want to call it that, is we're healthy enough to have a rotation. Garth is ready when we need him, DiPietro's playing well, and I've got enough bodies up and down the lineup to run four lines. That's what I need out of this stretch.

Marcus Bell

You mentioned Philly earlier. You dropped three straight to open February. What was that about?

Steve Stirling

The details weren't there. You lose your structure for two, three games and the goals go in the wrong end. I'm not going to sit here and name a man. It was us, the whole group. We lost games we should have managed better, and we got a couple of breaks tonight that we didn't get the week before. That's the difference between 2-0 on the road at Toronto and losing to Buffalo.

Marcus Bell

One last thing. Your room's on the lower end of the morale scale, 87.1 as a club average. Is that something you're working at, or is that just where it sits?

Steve Stirling

You know, I can't control a spreadsheet, Marcus. What I can control is whether those guys get out of here feeling like they competed. Tonight they will. Monday I don't know. I'm trying to make every night a night where the effort matches what we're capable of, and when it does, the mood takes care of itself. That's the whole job, really.

Marcus Bell

Coach Stirling, thank you. Marcus Bell from Toronto, back to you.
