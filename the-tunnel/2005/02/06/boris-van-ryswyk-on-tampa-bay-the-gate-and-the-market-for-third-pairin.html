Boris Van Ryswyk on Tampa Bay, the Gate, and the Market for Third-Pairing Defensemen

Florida's general manager does not flinch at the comparison with Tampa Bay, does not apologize for $13.32M in profit, and says his rooms are at the floor of the league.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelSunday, 6 February 2005

3:32 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Boris Van Ryswyk runs the Florida Panthers with a payroll of $51.07M that has returned 45 points and a six-game losing streak. At $79 a seat and an average attendance of 15,051, the Panthers are turning $13.32M in profit on a season that has them last in the Southeast. Jan Kowalczyk sat down with him in the studio.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here in the studio with Boris Van Ryswyk of the Florida Panthers®. Boris, thank you for sitting down. Seventeen wins, twenty-seven losses, forty-five points, six straight. You've got $51.07M on the books. Forty-one days from what the calendar has as the trade deadline. What does February look like from your chair?

Boris Van Ryswyk

It looks like forty-one days of phone calls. I'm not going to pretend this is a February where we're shopping for a playoff piece. Forty-five points, I mean, you look at the standings and you know. We're looking at what we've got and asking what it's worth to somebody else, and what comes back. That's the season.

Jan Kowalczyk

A seat costs $79. You're averaging 15,051. And the profit figure for the season is $13.32M. You're making money while the team is the worst in the division. Does that bother you?

Boris Van Ryswyk

No. I'll tell you what bothers me. Fifteen thousand people are buying a ticket at seventy-nine dollars and watching a team lose at home nine and eighteen. That bothers me more than the profit. I'd rather be in the standings and losing money. But we're where we are.

Jan Kowalczyk

Tampa Bay. Same division, same ticket price, $79. They spend $45.79M, you spend $51.07M. They've got sixty-seven points, you've got forty-five. Their profit is $29.40M. Do you watch that?

Boris Van Ryswyk

I watch every club's books. Every one. And I'll tell you where the gap shows. It shows in what we've built and what we haven't. Equipment room, nothing. Workout room, nothing. Practice rink, nothing. Travel is the only line item we've put money against and it's not even where it should be. You can see it. You can see it in the record. I can't tell you what it costs to fix any of that. I can tell you where we are. Where we are is not enough.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're still getting fifteen thousand in at seventy-nine dollars despite all of that. What does the building itself say to you?

Boris Van Ryswyk

It says people in this market are patient. I don't know how patient. Fifteen thousand for a team at seventeen and twenty-seven, that's not nothing. But patience has a shelf life and I don't know where ours runs out. I keep thinking, how many more forty-five-point seasons before they stay home. That's what keeps me up.

Jan Kowalczyk

You've got one man on the shelf, Igor Ulanov, back in late February. Shortest injured list in the league. Does that change how you think about the next forty-one days?

Boris Van Ryswyk

The healthiest team in the league is last in the division, so the injuries aren't the story, I'll give you that. Ulanov's a depth defenseman, he's back in three weeks, it doesn't move the math. What moves the math is the question you started with. What do we sell, what do we hold. And I'll be honest with you. You're not shopping a franchise guy at forty-five points. You're shopping third-pairing defensemen and fourth-line wingers, and the market pays late picks for those. That's the truth of it.

Jan Kowalczyk

One number before I let you go. You're $1.13M per point. Montreal Canadiens®'s at $1.21M. Is that any comfort?

Boris Van Ryswyk

You want me to feel good about being marginally better on the per-point line than a club at forty-four that's also last in their division? I don't want to be compared to Montreal Canadiens®. I want to be compared to Tampa Bay Lightning®, to Carolina Hurricanes®. Somebody winning in the Southeast. That's who I study.

Jan Kowalczyk

Is there anything you'd want to say to the fifteen thousand?

Boris Van Ryswyk

I'd say we know. We see the same numbers you see. We're working on the rooms, we're working the phones. This isn't the plan from October. But it's February and we're here. And I'm going to keep trying to fix it. That's what I owe them.

Jan Kowalczyk

Boris Van Ryswyk, the general manager of the Florida Panthers. Boris, thank you.

Boris Van Ryswyk

Thank you, Jan.
