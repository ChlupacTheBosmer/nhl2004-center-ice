Jason Arnott on Dallas, a Contract Year, and the Man He Was Traded For

Arnott says a city that fits is its own kind of security, and that a $3.80M contract year stays out of his head only as long as he keeps his eyes on the next shift.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelSunday, 6 February 2005

3:01 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

Jason Arnott85 has 12 points in 16 games for Dallas Stars this season, and the 30-year-old center is in a contract year: $3.80M, nothing on the books past the spring. He won the Cup with New Jersey Devils in 2000, on the goal at 8:20 of double overtime in game six against Dallas, and Dallas has been home since the 2002 trade. He sat down with Jan Kowalczyk to talk about the city and the year.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here, in the studio with Jason Arnott of the Dallas Stars. Jason, thanks for sitting down. You were born in Collingwood, you grew up in Wasaga Beach, and you've been in Dallas since the spring of '02. What's that city to you now?

Jason Arnott

Yeah, it's home, you know. People hear Dallas and they think football and heat and, uh, that's about it. But it's a hockey town more than you'd guess. The building's loud. The people know the game. I've been in a few places now and this one, this one fits.

Jan Kowalczyk

You keep coming back to the building. What is it about playing there, night to night?

Jason Arnott

I don't know if it's one thing. You walk in and you know where you sit, and the guys in the room, you know them. When you're new somewhere you're always looking for the light switch. Here I don't look for the light switch. That's a good feeling, honestly.

Jan Kowalczyk

It's a strange thing, though. You won the Cup with New Jersey in 2000. The goal was 8:20 of double overtime, game six, and it was against Dallas. Then two years later you're in Dallas. Did that ever sit odd with you?

Jason Arnott

Yeah, when I got here, it's the first thing out of every person's mouth. Eight-twenty. Double OT. Against these guys. And then you get in the room and, uh, nobody cares. These guys, they don't care what you did to them before. They care what you do next shift. That part I liked right away.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let's do the one you know is coming. You're 30, it's a contract year, $3.80M, and there's nothing on the books past this season. How do you keep that out of your head?

Jason Arnott

Some days you don't. But you have to, you know? Everybody in this league is playing for their next deal or their last one. If I'm thinking about July in February, I'm not, uh, I'm no good to the guys in here. So I show up and I play.

Jan Kowalczyk

But the noise is there. Friends, your agent, the papers. Does any of it get in?

Jason Arnott

Not from the room. That's not what guys talk about in here. And I don't read the papers, not that stuff. I've been traded twice. Twice is enough for me. But that's, uh, that's not mine to decide. What's mine is the next shift. So that's what I've got.

Jan Kowalczyk

So what keeps a man level through a year like that? The routine, the room, the guys around you?

Jason Arnott

All of it, I guess. You come in and you do the work, and then you go home. I've got Mikey in here, you know, you watch a guy like that and you learn what to sweat and what not to. And then you just play. That's the day.

Jan Kowalczyk

There's one more circle in this, and it's the reason we wanted you. In January of '98 you were traded to New Jersey, and going the other way was Bill Guerin. You got a Cup out of it. Now you're in the same room as him again. What's that like, coming the long way round with a guy you were once traded for?

Jason Arnott

Yeah, that's a funny one, you know. We got traded for each other, basically. And you don't, you don't think about it, and then one day you look across the room and there he is. Same big guy, same shot. It comes all the way back around.

Jan Kowalczyk

He's got 99 points, 50 goals, in 53 games. At 34. What's he doing that's working?

Jason Arnott

Everything, apparently. No, he's, uh, he's a horse. He gets to his spot and the puck finds him, and my job is just, you know, get him the puck and get out of the way. 99 points at 34, that's not an accident. That's a guy doing the work every day.

Jan Kowalczyk

One last thing. You're 30 now, you've been through the draft, two trades, a Cup. What does the kid from Wasaga Beach know now that he didn't?

Jason Arnott

That it's a job, you know, but it's a good one. When you're young it's all about you, your points, your next deal. Then you get traded and you win one and you, uh, you lose a few, and you figure out it's the guys in the room. I didn't know that at 18. I know it now.

Jan Kowalczyk

Jason Arnott, thanks for your time. For The Tunnel, I'm Jan Kowalczyk.

Jason Arnott

Thanks, Jan. Appreciate it.
