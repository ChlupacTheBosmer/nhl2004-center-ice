Quenneville on a 20-year-old on the blue line and a power play that doesn't explain all 220

The Avalanche coach pushed back on the idea that one unit drives the league's highest goal total, and talked about why Ondruska and Stephens are in his lineup and not on the bench

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelSaturday, 12 February 2005

4:09 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Joel Quenneville's Colorado Avalanche sit third in the Northwest at 29-18-3, 69 points, with 220 goals for, the most in the league. A 4-2 home win over Calgary Flames on February 9th followed a 3-2 loss in Detroit the night before, and it made the club 18-8 on the road. Quenneville spoke to The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk in the tunnel after the game, with Jamie Storr84 credited for 44 games this season and no Avalanche skater on injured reserve.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here, in the tunnel at the Pepsi, after a 4-2 win over Calgary. Coach, you dropped the one to Detroit 3-2 the night before, then come back and put four on the board. What did you like about this one?

Joel Quenneville

Well, I thought the first period was the best we've played in, uh, I don't know, a couple of weeks. We had the puck. We moved it with purpose. And I think that's the thing that jumps out, we weren't forcing it, we were just finding the open man and letting them go to net. And Jamie was sharp when we needed him, early in the second there was a scramble, he handled it clean. So, good win.

Jan Kowalczyk

That bounce-back from Detroit, is it the kind of thing you want a coach to see, a group that doesn't carry the previous night?

Joel Quenneville

Yeah, that's what you want. You know, we talk about compete level and the details and all the clichés, you know, the room's vocabulary, but the truth is you get one night off your game and the next night people want to show you something. That's healthy. I like it. I don't like the Detroit game, I didn't like how we started, but the response tonight, yeah, that's good.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let me go to the scoring side. 220 goals on the season, that's the most in the league. And obviously a power play with Peter Forsberg, Joe Sakic, Adam Oates on it, that unit has to be part of that number. What's that group doing right?

Joel Quenneville

I appreciate the question but I'd push back a little on the premise. Obviously our power play is important, you've got those guys, they see things, they make plays, that's what they do. But 220 goals, that's not one unit. That's five-on-five hockey. That's Milan and Teemu finishing, that's Derek Morris walking the blue line and putting it on net, that's Alex Tanguay driving the middle of the ice. I think if you sat in front of the tape tonight, you'd see our 5-on-5 creating more than our power play is. And I'm happy for the power play numbers, don't get me wrong, but it's a group thing.

Jan Kowalczyk

Fair enough. Let me stay on the special teams side then. Your penalty kill, who are the guys eating those minutes, who's doing the blocking and the pressure?

Joel Quenneville

Dan Hinote, Bates Battaglia. Those guys will sit in the box all night and not complain. And obviously our forwards up top, Peter, Joe, they're good at getting into the shooter, disrupting the first pass. And on the back end we've got guys who'll get in shooting lanes. Adam Foote, he just puts his body there. It's not glamorous. Nobody wants to be on the kill, that's the truth. But those guys, they accept it as a job.

Jan Kowalczyk

You mentioned a job, and that makes me think about your young players. Marcel Ondruska is 20 years old, 29 games already. Charlie Stephens is 23 and he's played all 54. Jordan Krestanovich, 23, 46 games. Those are real minutes for young guys. How does that happen?

Joel Quenneville

They earned it. I'll say it again, they earned it. I don't hand minutes out in this room. You come to camp, you show me you can play, you get in. Marcel, he's 20 but he doesn't play like 20. He's physical, he's not, he's not shy with the puck, and he reads the play better than I expected, honestly. Charlie, 54 games, that's because you can count on him every night. You put him out there, he's going to do the job. He's not going to make the highlight play but he's going to be where he's supposed to be.

Jan Kowalczyk

Is there a side of this where you're thinking about their development, their growth, or are you just trying to win the next game? Where's the line for you?

Joel Quenneville

That's, that's a good question. I think the answer is both and neither. I'm trying to win every game. That's, that's the obvious answer. But I'm also watching a 20-year-old learn how to play in this league in real time, in a building, and that's the best classroom there is. I'm not going to put him in the press box so he can think about his posture. He plays. He makes mistakes. He gets better. That's the fastest way.

Jan Kowalczyk

Martin Skoula, 25, 25 games. Is he in the same bucket, a young defenseman you're developing?

Joel Quenneville

Martin's a little different. He came in and he had to, uh, work his way back into the lineup. He's a 74 overall guy in a league, you know, that's a real player. He just needed the reps. And 25 games, that's not enough but it's coming. He's healthy, he's skating well, and when he gets the opportunity he takes it.

Jan Kowalczyk

Last one. You're at Vancouver on the 13th, home to Minnesota the 15th, Chicago on the 17th. Two of those three are on the road and you're 18 and 8 there. How do you approach a stretch like that?

Joel Quenneville

One game at a time. That's not a cliché, I mean it. Vancouver's fast, they've got a big surface, they make you defend the whole width of the ice. Minnesota's desperate, every game with them is, is a war. And Chicago, well. I know Chicago. I was there seven and a half years. So that one's a little different for me personally. But the team's not going to get fired up because I've got a few friends there. We'll be ready.

Jan Kowalczyk

Thank you, coach. Jan Kowalczyk, back to you.
