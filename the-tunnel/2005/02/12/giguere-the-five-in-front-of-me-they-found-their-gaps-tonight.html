Giguere: the five in front of me, they found their gaps tonight

After a 4-3 overtime win that broke a stretch of OT heartbreak, Jean-Sebastien Giguere talks about the defense pushing the blue line and what that does to the last man back.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelSaturday, 12 February 2005

3:08 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Jean-Sebastien Giguere97 made 18 saves on 21 shots and earned his 25th win of the season as the Mighty Ducks of Anaheim Ducks beat Carolina Hurricanes 4-3 in overtime at Honda Center on Tuesday. Anaheim sits at 21-16-11, sixth in the Pacific, with 11 losses in overtime, the second-most in the league. The win moved them past Los Angeles Kings in the division and gave the room something it has not had much of this February.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here with Jean-Sebastien Giguere. Forty-eight games, no shutouts, and tonight it takes overtime to put one away. How does the building feel when you're the one still out there after sixty?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

You know, it's loud. It's really loud. After the third goal, I'm looking at the clock and the guys are on the bench and the noise just, it keeps going. It's not like when we play in Montreal, it's not that kind of loud, but it's, what do you call it, it's steady. It's a hum. And when you're the last guy back and you can hear that through your mask, you know, you don't want to let it down.

Jan Kowalczyk

You've got eleven losses in overtime this season, more than any team except Carolina. Tonight you finally get one. Does that number sit on you in those extra minutes?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

Eleven, yeah. I see it, you know? I'm not going to pretend I don't see it. But tonight, tonight we had the puck. We had it more in their zone than in ours, and that's the difference. If we're clogging it in our own end for five minutes, I'm going to make a mistake. I'm human. But if the guys are out there and they're working and they're not just dumping and chasing, it's, it's a different game for me.

Jan Kowalczyk

Last time we sat down, in December, you told me the coach's changes were going to give you speed and quiet your workload. You've got 21 shots tonight, your lowest all month. Did they deliver?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

Yeah, they did, they did. And, you know, I think what I meant that night was that the D, they're pushing up. Havelid, Carney, they're joining the rush and they're making plays with the puck, and that means fewer guys coming back at me on the rush. And yeah, tonight Havelid's got a goal, Carney's got a goal, that's offense from the blue line. The only cost is, the only cost is if they lose it, I've got a wide-open man coming the other way. So it's a trade. But I like the trade.

Jan Kowalczyk

You said in December that the changes gave you speed. Fedorov's got two points tonight, Cole's got two assists, Prospal's got one. Do you feel that speed from the back end, or is it a different feeling for you than it is for the guys on the wings?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

It's different, for sure. I see the game, I see the whole ice, so when Serge gets the puck in the neutral zone and Cole is already going, I see it happen, you know? I see it and I think, okay, here we go. But I can't, I can't make that play. I can only, I can only give them the puck on the clear and hope they get it. So I watch them, I just watch them and I'm happy when it works. And tonight it worked.

Jan Kowalczyk

You mentioned the D pushing up. When they do that, are you talking to them, or is it, is it silent out there?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

Oh, I'm talking. I'm talking the whole time. You can probably hear me on the broadcast, probably not, I don't think they put a mic on me. But I'm saying, 'Hav, Hav, you're too far in.' I'm saying, 'Aki, Aki, you're behind the play.' They know. They know. It's just, the problem is when you're winning 3-2 and the D want to join, they want to join. And I don't blame them. It's fun. But I'm out here and it's, it's a little less fun when the other guy's one-on-one with me.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're 17 and 10 at home this season. Does it matter to you, home and away, or is a game a game when you're the one in the net?

Jean-Sebastien Giguere

It matters. People think, you're in the crease, you can't see the crowd, so it shouldn't matter. It matters. In here, the building, you feel the floor when they're stomping. In here I know when we score. In here I know when we need a stop. On the road, especially on a back-to-back, the first ten minutes I'm, I'm feeling for the ice. Is it fast? Is it slow? Where's the, the bounce off the boards. So yeah. It matters. And we're good here. Seventeen and ten, that's, that's something.

Jan Kowalczyk

Jean-Sebastien, thank you. Enjoy the win. Back to you.
