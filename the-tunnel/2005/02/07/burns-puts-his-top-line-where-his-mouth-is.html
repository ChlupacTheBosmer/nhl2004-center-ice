Burns puts his top line where his mouth is

The Devils' coach explains the four-slot reshuffle that sent Langenbrunner to the top line and dropped two men from the first power play unit.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelMonday, 7 February 2005

3:20 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

New Jersey Devils coach Pat Burns spoke to The Tunnel in the tunnel at Washington after a 3-2 overtime win on 2005-02-05, his seventh in ten games. He discussed the return of Patrik Elias86 (5 goals, 5 assists in 7 games), the promotion of Jamie Langenbrunner86 to the first line, and the two men he is playing without until mid-February.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here with Pat Burns, right after the game. Coach, 3-2 on the road, overtime, seventh win in ten. You outshot them 24 to 22, but it went to the extra frame. How did that one feel from the bench?

Pat Burns

It felt good, for sure. You come to this building, you know, Kolzig's been playing 52 games, he's not going to make a lot of mistakes. So you get your chances and you take them. Twenty-four shots, we managed the puck better in the second and third than we did in the first. That's the detail that wins you road games.

Marcus Bell

You brought Patrik Elias back into the lineup tonight and he's got five goals, five assists in seven games since he returned. What does that change about what you're trying to do?

Pat Burns

It changes everything, honestly. You put him in the top six and the top six gets better. He's, I mean, he's been our best player by a mile when he's in there. Five and five in seven games, you don't coach that. You just give him the ice and get out of the way. He makes the guys around him better. That's, that's what an eighty-eight does.

Marcus Bell

Jamie Langenbrunner moves up to the first line tonight, H1 and line one right wing. And John Madden takes over centering that line. What drove that change?

Pat Burns

For sure, Jamie's scoring at a point and a half a game when he's played. Twenty-nine goals in thirty-nine games, that's, what, a forty-goal pace? I like my center. I like the way he wins draws, the way he manages the puck on the forecheck. So I put them together. Brylin moves over to right wing on the fourth line and we get more balance down there. Sergei's a big body, he can use his frame there.

Marcus Bell

On the power play, Steve Guolla and Grant Marshall are both off the first unit tonight. Guolla was on the first before, Marshall was on the first. What are you trying to say to those two, or to the group?

Marcus Bell

Sorry, go ahead.

Pat Burns

I'm not trying to say anything to them. I'm trying to put the right five guys out there. Guolla's a good player, but right now the PP's not scoring enough and I need more traffic, more net presence. So I shuffle. Grant moves to the second unit, he's fine there, he'll get his time. It's not a punishment, it's, it's a lineup decision. These guys are grown men. They understand the details.

Marcus Bell

You scratched Bryan Muir tonight. He's healthy, forty games played, and Sean Brown went in instead. Is that a matchup call or a message?

Pat Burns

Matchup. Sean's stronger on the wall, he's 6-foot-4, and we knew they'd dump it in and try to grind us in our own end. That's a Washington team, that's what they do. Muir's a good player, but he's a 270-pound guy who's better in transition than he is in a battle along the boards. So I dress the guy who wins the battle. That's the job.

Marcus Bell

Now, Turner Stevenson is out with a knee, back the 22nd. Michael Rupp is out with a shoulder, back the 18th. Two guys gone, and you've just gotten Elias back. How do you manage that middle of the lineup between now and the 22nd?

Pat Burns

You manage it by trusting the guys who are here. Craig Darby moves down to the fourth line center, he's 32, he's been in this league a long time, he knows how to play a role. Erik Rasmussen drops a line. Guolla's there. We have depth, it's just, it's not the depth I want. Stevenson gives us size and faceoffs on the third line, Rupp gives us a body and a net-front presence. So when they come back, we get them back. Until then, you ask the guys to play a little more than they're used to and they do it. They've done it all year.

Marcus Bell

Martin Brodeur's played 43 of your 52 games. You went with Ari Ahonen as the backup tonight instead of Corey Schwab. Is that a plan for the rest of this schedule, or was it just tonight?

Pat Burns

It's a plan. Martin's our guy, he's the best in the league, that's not in question. But 43 games, he's a human being. Ari's played nine games and he's 2 and 3 but he's been sharp, his save percentage has been there. So I dress him, I give him the opportunity, I keep Schwab fresh. It's not a knock on Corey. Corey's been fine. But Ari's 24 and he's got the rest of his career in front of him, and I want him ready. That's the whole thing. You prepare the young one while the old one's still carrying you.

Marcus Bell

Coach, you've got Atlanta tomorrow at home, then you get four games in six days. Do you expect to keep these line changes, or are you going to mix it again?

Pat Burns

We keep what works. Tonight worked. Four lines playing, everybody competing, the puck management was there. So I keep it until someone stops doing their job, and then I change it. That's not complicated. That's coaching.

Marcus Bell

Pat Burns, thank you as always. Back to you.
