Jason Arnott, the Circle, and the Year He Can't Look Past

Dallas' center talks about a 16-game season so far, Collingwood on the phone, and a contract that runs out in June.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelMonday, 7 February 2005

5:30 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Jason Arnott85 has played 16 games for Dallas Stars this season, 4 goals and 12 points in 18.0 minutes a night after a groin cost him the opening weeks. His contract carries 0 years left and $3.80M. Jan Kowalczyk sat down with him in the studio after practice on a Monday afternoon.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here with Jason Arnott of the Dallas Stars. Jason, good afternoon. You're sixteen games into your season, four goals, twelve points, and we saw the Sweater's profile this week on you and Bill Guerin. Do you want me to open there or do you want to talk about something else?

Jason Arnott

No, we can open there. I read it. I read it twice actually, you know, because the first time I was in the lobby at the hotel and I'm thinking, is this real? Because I remembered getting traded to New Jersey for him, right, and I remember thinking at the time, well, that's a young guy with a big body and a good shot, he'll be fine. And now he's sitting next to me at practice. So yeah, the circle's real.

Jan Kowalczyk

Did you know when Dallas got you in '02 that Guerin would end up here too?

Jason Arnott

No. Not even close. When I came over for Nieuwendyk and Langenbrunner, I mean, the Devils needed a center and we needed, I don't know, a different look, and Guerin was somewhere else entirely. He was in New York then I think. Or maybe he was in Boston by then. I lost track of him for a couple years.

Jan Kowalczyk

He's sitting right next to you on the top line now. Eighty-nine grade, ninety on the shot power, you've got the ninety-nine yourself. Does it feel strange, playing beside a guy you were once traded for?

Jason Arnott

No, I don't think so. Hockey's small. You play against guys, you play with them, somebody trades them, somebody trades you. The thing about Guerin is, he's, I mean, he's the same guy. Big body, goes to the net, doesn't care who's on the other end of the pass. You know, he doesn't give a damn about the story. He just wants the puck and wants to put it in. I respect that.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let's talk about your season. Sixteen games in, four goals, eight assists. You're back from the groin and you're logging eighteen minutes a night. How are you feeling physically?

Jason Arnott

Good. The groin is fine. That's the main thing. I missed, what, the first stretch of the season and, uh, I'm back, I'm moving well, the legs are there. The points will come. I'm not worried about it. You know, last year I had sixty-four in fifty-one games. I'm not forty years old. I think I can find that again.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're thirty. That's not old. But you've been playing since you were nineteen. Does your body talk to you differently now than it did in New Jersey?

Jason Arnott

Oh, every day. Every morning. You get up, you're stiff, you figure out what you can do, what you can't. The groin thing was a wake-up call in that sense. Like, okay, you're not going to walk off a hit like you did at twenty-three anymore. You've got to take care of it. So I'm doing the extra stuff, the stretching, all that boring stuff. And it helps.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're from Collingwood, Ontario. That's not a short drive to Dallas. Do they make the trip?

Jason Arnott

My family? Yeah, my wife comes down when she can, the kids come down in the summer, that's when it's the hardest, obviously, but in the winter we get them here for the holidays and some long weekends. You know, my mom's the one who really tries. She's, what, sixty-something now, and she still makes the flight. Every year she's here a few times. She just wants to watch me play. You don't say no to that.

Jan Kowalczyk

Was Collingwood always the plan? Was there a moment where you thought, I'm going to be a hockey player?

Jason Arnott

No. Collingwood's a small town, you know, it's, what, twenty thousand people? You grew up thinking you'd work at the marina or something. I was on the Stars, the Wasaga Beach Stars, DD's, that was the whole world. Then you go to Stayner, you go to Oshawa, and suddenly the whole world gets bigger every year. By the time you're in the draft you realize, oh, this is actually happening. But Collingwood's still home. My parents are there. That's where I hang my hat.

Jan Kowalczyk

The road is part of it. Dallas is not the easiest trip from anywhere. You're on planes a lot. Does the family part get harder when you're flying into somewhere cold for three nights and coming back to ninety degrees?

Jason Arnott

Yeah. Yeah, it does. That's the part nobody talks about, you know, people say, oh, you're on the road, it's fun, you're in hotels. But the hard part is the phone call. It's eight o'clock at night in some arena hotel and your kid's trying to show you a drawing or whatever and you can barely hear them and you're tired and you've got a game tomorrow. And you know they need you to be present and you're not there. That's the thing. That's the part that gets to you.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let me ask you about the contract. You've got no years left. It's an expiring deal, $3.80 million. How do you keep that out of your head when you're out there?

Jason Arnott

You don't. You can't really. I'd be lying if I said I don't think about it. But, uh, what do you do? You show up, you play your shift, you try to help your team win. If I sat here and worried about next June, I'd play worse and then I'd get a worse deal anyway. So you put it in a box. You put it on a shelf. You deal with it in March, April, whenever.

Jan Kowalczyk

Is there a temptation to think about it more when the numbers aren't there? Right now you're at twelve points in sixteen games. Does that change the math?

Jason Arnott

Well, yeah, it does a little. You think, I should be better, and if I'm not better, what's that worth at the table? And that's, that's a bad thought. That's a thought that makes you press. And pressing makes you play stupid, makes you take bad plays, and then you're worse, and it's a cycle. So you have to, I don't know, you have to trust that if you play your game the points come. The groin took time away. Give it another month, another six weeks, I'll be fine.

Jan Kowalczyk

You were in Dallas when you scored that Cup-winning goal. Game six, double overtime. Do you remember what that felt like, and does it change how you think about this locker room now?

Jason Arnott

I remember the feeling of the puck, I remember the net, I remember the noise. But I remember it from the other side of the bench. I was a Devil. I was scoring against them. And now I'm in this room and Marty Turco is in net and Modano's on the wing and Guerin's on my other side and it's a different feeling. I'm not coming here as an opponent anymore. I'm trying to help these guys. And this room, this room has a chance. You can feel it in practice. They work hard.

Jan Kowalczyk

Mike Modano, Marty Turco, Bill Guerin, you on center. That's a first line. What's it been like building that?

Jason Arnott

You know, it's still building. Sixteen games isn't a long time. Modano's Modano, he sees the ice better than anybody I've played with. Marty's Marty, he makes the save you can't explain. And Guerin, he just goes. He doesn't think, he goes. You just give him space and he'll find the net. So yeah, it works. We just need more time together.

Jan Kowalczyk

Last one. Collingwood. Your mom's sixty-something, she still flies to Dallas. When you're on that ice, eighteen minutes a night, what does that mean to you?

Jason Arnott

It means everything. It means, I don't know, it means she never gave up on the whole thing. The Wasaga Beach Stars, the Stayner Siskins, the Oshawa Generals, all of it, and then the draft, and then all these cities. And she's in the stands in Dallas. Watching number forty-four. You know, you try not to get emotional about that. You don't want to think about it before a game. But yeah, it's, that's why you play. That's who you play for.

Jan Kowalczyk

Jason Arnott, thank you for your time. Back to you.
