Fedorov on the Profile's Fall: 'I Am 62 Goals. You Choose That or the 61'

The Ducks' 35-year-old center dismisses a 61 potential grade, credits the first NHL season rather than any coach for rebuilding his game, and names Mogilny and Giguere as the best players he has played with.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelThursday, 24 February 2005

3:26 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Sergei Fedorov90 has 62 goals in 61 games for Mighty Ducks of Anaheim this season, on a pace of 121 points over 82, at age 35. The League Scouting Bureau's Development Index has him at 86, one point below his base grade of 94, with a potential number of 61, the spine of The Sweater's recent profile on him. Marcus Bell sat down with him in the studio after practice.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here, in the studio after practice, with Sergei Fedorov of the Ducks. Sergei, 62 goals in 61 games at 35 years old, 90 points on the season. Thank you for sitting down with me.

Sergei Fedorov

Thank you, Marcus. Good to talk.

Marcus Bell

I want to start with the other number though. You had 55 assists last season. This year it is 28. That is a big drop. You see that?

Sergei Fedorov

I see it every game, yes.

Marcus Bell

You are playing 22.9 minutes a night, up from 21.1 last year. More ice, fewer helpers on the scoresheet. How does that happen?

Sergei Fedorov

I play simpler now. In Detroit, everything come through me. Carry, pass, create. Here, I go to the net. I am there, I wait, the puck come. You look at my goals, many of them are one-timers, are rebounds off the pad. I am not making the play from behind the net. That is a different game. At this point, you find the thing that work.

Marcus Bell

The Sweater ran a profile on you this week. The title is 62 Goals, 61 Potential: Sergei Fedorov at 35 and the fall the Bureau sees. Your Development Index is one off your base of 94. Your potential number is 61. What do you say to that?

Sergei Fedorov

I read it. I read it and I think, this is very interesting. 61 potential. At 35 years old. What is potential? I am not potential. I am here. I am 62 goals. You write the 62 or you write the 61, Marcus. You choose.

Marcus Bell

The Development Index is about form, not the future. It says you are not quite at 94 right now.

Sergei Fedorov

I know the numbers. 86 against 94, minus one. I know. But I play 22.9 minutes, I score 62 goals. That is not a fall. That is a man playing his game in a different way.

Marcus Bell

Let me take you back. Pskov, the national team, the defection, Detroit. Was there a coach that changed you? Somebody who took the hockey you grew up with and rebuilt it?

Sergei Fedorov

No. People want me to say this. A coach took me, changed me. No. The first game here changed me. I step on the ice, this guy hit me, I am on the boards, I get up, another one come. In Russia you have time. You see the play, you think, you make the pass. Here, no time. You think you are dead. That was the teacher. Not a man in a suit.

Marcus Bell

So the game itself was the transformation.

Sergei Fedorov

Of course. Every game is a lesson. The speed, the body, the sixty minutes. In Russia, maybe you save something for the next shift. Here you save nothing. Everything on the ice, every night, every game. The game teach this. No coach teach this.

Marcus Bell

Gretzky said after watching you against the Rangers that you were the best player in the game at that point. You stayed at his house for two weeks. But best player you have played with? Not against. With.

Sergei Fedorov

Alexander Mogilny. Before he go to Buffalo. We are on the national team together, before I come here. You play with him, you understand the game different. He see it all before it happen. You make a move, he is already where you want to put the puck. It is like, how do I say, you play with your mind. Not your feet. Your mind.

Marcus Bell

And in this room, now? Day to day?

Sergei Fedorov

Jean-Sebastien Giguere. 93. You know the number. But the number is not what it is. You play behind him, you hear nothing from the crease. He just is there. The young guys, they see that. It is calm. That is important. Very important for a room.

Marcus Bell

Does knowing he is behind you change how you play? Do you take more of a chance going to the net?

Sergei Fedorov

A little. You push the blue line higher, you crash the net harder. You lose the puck, he is there. You play different. Not a lot. But different.

Marcus Bell

Sergei Fedorov, 62 goals at 35, and the word potential, he says, is for another man. Thank you, Sergei. Marcus Bell, back to you.
