Trotz, 33 shots and no goals: 'You still get the point'

The Predators' coach on a dry night in Toronto, a league-leading clean bill of health, and keeping a 1st-place room honest after two losses.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelThursday, 24 February 2005

4:03 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Nashville Predators lost 1-0 in overtime to Toronto Maple Leafs on 2005-02-23, firing 33 shots to their 22 and leaving a point in Toronto that matters in the race at the top of the West. Barry Trotz's club sits 1st in the Central at 35-16-6, 84 points, and has nobody on the injured list heading into a three-game home stand. He spoke with The Tunnel's Marcus Bell at the rink the morning after.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here with Barry Trotz of Nashville Predators®, the morning after a 1-0 overtime loss to Toronto Maple Leafs®. Barry, you fired 33 pucks on net and got nothing for it in regulation. How do you go about dressing that up for a room that's been winning all month?

Barry Trotz

You don't dress it up. You just tell the truth, which is we had our chances, we had some real good ones, and their goalie was the difference in the first two periods. That's the hockey part. The point part, you know, we get a point on the road against the best team in the league. I'll take it. Doesn't feel good tonight, but the standings don't care how it feels.

Marcus Bell

You're 35-16-6 now, 84 points, first in the West. And you've got two losses in a row after that long run. Does a two-game slide in February feel different from February when you're 30 back?

Barry Trotz

Well, obviously it's different in the sense that you're not chasing anymore, so every point has a little more, a little more weight to it. But I think, I think the way we talk about it in here is we still want to be 1-0 every night. We don't, we don't do the point math in the room. The guys are, you know, they're competing for it every night and that's what I've asked them to do since '98. The two losses, the first one we didn't play well enough to win. Last night, the details weren't there in the offensive zone. Different problems, and you fix different problems.

Marcus Bell

Let me ask about the net. Tomas Vokoun's played 49 of 61. That's 80 percent. Jan Lasak and Brian Finley are on the bench. Are you comfortable with that workload as you go into the last two months?

Barry Trotz

You know, I've had to have that conversation with myself more than once. He's been available. He's been good. The kid's 90 overall, you can't really sit him when he's giving you what he gives you. And Jan's in there when we need him, he's got seven wins, he's solid. I manage it, I watch the back-to-backs, I look at the travel. We haven't had a stretch where we've had to ask him to do something stupid. He's earned every minute of that 49.

Marcus Bell

You're one of the few clubs in this league right now with nobody on the injured list. Seven guys out in Tampa, five in Chicago, five in Detroit. Is that luck, or is there a structure behind it?

Barry Trotz

You know, I think it's a little of both, and I don't want to be too proud of the structure because I know how fast that goes. We've got a staff that watches, that pushes back when I'm pushing ahead too hard. We've been lucky, obviously. Guys stay out of the bad bounces. But I'll tell you what, when you look around the league at who's on the shelf, it makes you appreciate who you've got in the lineup every night. You don't get to take a guy for granted. Not in this schedule.

Marcus Bell

And when someone does go down, you're deeper than most. You've got Greg Johnson at 26 games, Chris Gratton, Vernon Fiddler in the middle six. Does the depth get tested when it's not needed, or does it get stale?

Barry Trotz

It's a real concern, yeah. You keep the guys engaged by, you know, putting them in situations where it matters. Kill shots, penalty kill, the fourth line's got to block shots every night. Fiddler's not scoring 20 goals for us, but he's out there eating minutes, he's doing the dirty work, and the other guys see that and they know their number could be called tomorrow. I think the room stays sharp because the roles are clear. Nobody's confused about what they're doing.

Marcus Bell

You gave up 176 goals this season, the fewest in the West. What does that say about the way you've built this thing since '98?

Barry Trotz

You know, it says we've been boring for a long time and we like it. That's the compliment I take. The truth is, our structure is the one thing that doesn't change whether we're winning nine in a row or losing two. The first guy back, the middle of the ice, all those things, that's the detail work. Offensively we've got the talent, Legwand's got 78 points, Walker's got 59 in 47 games. But the thing that keeps us at the top of this division is we don't make the other team pay for our mistakes. That's a coach's win, I'll take it.

Marcus Bell

Three home games coming up: Columbus Friday, Pittsburgh Sunday, Chicago Tuesday. Does the schedule matter this late?

Barry Trotz

Everything matters. Columbus is fighting for a playoff spot, Pittsburgh's the same, and Chicago's going to bring everything they've got because they've got nothing to lose. You can't look ahead. We've got to be ready to play a hockey game tonight, not on Tuesday. I'll tell you that much. The guys know. After a night like last night, you want to play again. You want to get it right.

Marcus Bell

Last one. You took this club to its first playoffs last year. Now you're first in the West. Does this room believe it can go deep?

Barry Trotz

You know, I don't think about April yet. I genuinely don't. I think about Columbus on Friday. These guys, they've got 84 points, they're not going to get excited about a number in the standings. They want to win the next game. That's the belief I want in that room, right now, in February, not in April. We'll talk about April if we get there.

Marcus Bell

Barry Trotz of the Predators, thank you as always. Back to you.
