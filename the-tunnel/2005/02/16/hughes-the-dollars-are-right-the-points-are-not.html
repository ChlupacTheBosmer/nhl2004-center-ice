Hughes: the dollars are right, the points are not

Chicago's general manager defends a payroll at $39.27M and a per-point figure of $0.79M while sitting 25 points back of the division leader, and says every expiring contract is on the table before the deadline.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelWednesday, 16 February 2005

4:15 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Chicago Blackhawks sits 18-29-8 with 50 points, fifth in the Central, with a payroll of $39.27M and a profit of $24.32M. Jason Hughes, the club's general manager, sat down with Jan Kowalczyk in the studio to talk about the arithmetic of spending less and winning less, and what he means to do in the 31 days before what the calendar has as the trade deadline.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here with Jason Hughes, general manager of Chicago, in the studio tonight. Jason, your payroll is $39.27M, fifth-lowest in the league. You're sitting at 50 points. What does the number tell you when you look at it every morning?

Jason Hughes

It tells me we're efficient, Jan. Per point we're at $0.79M. That's better than Detroit at $1.24M, better than New York Rangers at $1.16M, better than Philadelphia at $1.20M. We're getting more hockey per dollar than clubs spending twice what we spend. I know that doesn't put you in the playoffs. I know what the table says. But the operation is sound.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let me push on that. The table says 9 and 18 at home. You're making $24.32M in profit and playing in front of 15,101 people at $77 a ticket who watch you lose at home two out of every three nights. Do you look at that and think, the efficiency isn't enough?

Jason Hughes

I look at it and I think the money is in the right place for right now. We have a profit. That profit, that's what keeps us able to do the things that are slow, the rooms, the depth. You don't build a club in a season. I'll say that once and then I'll stop saying it. The record is what it is. The 9 and 18, that's, that belongs to the bench more than it belongs to me. I hand the coach the roster. What he does with it on a Tuesday at home, that's his chair.

Jan Kowalczyk

So the deadline is 31 days away. What the calendar calls the trade deadline. You're 25 points back of Nashville. What is the posture in your office?

Jason Hughes

We're listening to everyone. Every call that comes in gets returned. I'll tell you this, Jan, I'm not sitting back waiting for somebody to call me with a fire-sale offer on a piece I want to keep. But we're at 50 points. You and both know what that means. I've got contracts coming up in the summer, I've got guys who might be worth something to somebody else now. I'm not going to name my own chips on your set.

Jan Kowalczyk

I won't make you name them. But Eric Daze has 96 points, he's on $3.00M with one year left, morale at 96. Jocelyn Thibault, $2.80M, one year left, 86 overall at 30. Those are the two that jump out at me. Does the math on those two look like, we keep them and go again, or do you think the time is now to get something for them?

Jason Hughes

You know, I think... I think the answer is both. I think the answer is, I'm not ruling it out either direction. Daze is playing at a level right now, 96 points, the highest he's ever been. If we're talking about a window where we're actually competitive, I want him here. But I'm not competitive right now. I'm 25 back. So if somebody puts something in front of me that moves us forward faster than keeping a guy on an expiring deal and hoping we improve next year, yeah, I'm listening. Same with Thibs. He's been steady for us, 48 games, 86 overall. Goalies are hard to find. But if the return is right, the return is right.

Jan Kowalczyk

You upgraded the locker room in late January. You've got the equipment room at zero, dead level, the lowest the file shows. What's the next room, and can the profit actually buy one?

Jason Hughes

You want me to tell you what's next and when. I can tell you the locker room was the right call. We needed it. Morale's where it is for a reason, Daze at 96, Thibault at 96, that's not nothing. The equipment room, yeah, it's zero. I know it's zero. We'll get to it when the numbers tell us we can get to it. I'm not going to spend money we don't have and end up with a nice room and a team at 50 points next year too. That's a worse version of the same problem.

Jan Kowalczyk

You have three guys out. Poapst back March 20th. Does that change anything for the deadline?

Jason Hughes

Poapst's a depth defenseman. It doesn't move the math the way Daze or a goaltender would. I appreciate you asking. The depth is thin, sure, Steve's out, but the things that would actually change the shape of this roster are the names you just read me.

Jan Kowalczyk

Last question. A man in this market, 15,101 a night, $77 a ticket. He's watched you finish 50 points, 50 points, and here we are at 50 again. What do you say to him?

Jason Hughes

I say I see it. I see the same number. I say the money's clean, the profit's real, the rooms are getting better, and I'm going to make a decision before March 19th that I think makes us harder to play against by next October. And if I don't, then you people will be right to ask the same question you asked me tonight.

Jan Kowalczyk

Jason Hughes, general manager of Chicago. Thank you for your time tonight. Back to you.
