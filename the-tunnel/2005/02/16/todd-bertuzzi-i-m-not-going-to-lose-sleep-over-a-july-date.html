Todd Bertuzzi: 'I'm not going to lose sleep over a July date'

The Canucks' right winger on his day off in Vancouver, the coach who once passed on him, and the one year left on a $2.60M contract.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelWednesday, 16 February 2005

4:12 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Todd Bertuzzi93 sits at 98 points in 57 games for Vancouver Canucks, a pace of 141 over 82, on a $2.60M contract with one year remaining. This week's Sweater profile put the question to him directly: what does a man at 30 with a base grade of 94 and an expiring deal actually think about on a Tuesday with no skate. Marcus Bell asked him the same thing, in armchairs, at the studio.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here with Todd Bertuzzi of the Vancouver Canucks for The Tunnel. Todd, thanks for coming in. Did you see the profile this week in the Sweater? '37 Grades Past the Ceiling.'

Todd Bertuzzi

Yeah, I saw it. And I'll be honest, I sat with the title for a minute because I don't, you know, I don't sit around watching my overall grade. I know where it's at. 89 today. But I don't go there. I go to the rink and I try to play and whatever the Bureau decides to print is what they decide to print.

Marcus Bell

Your base is 94 though. The Bureau's got you two spots up on the Development Index, morale sitting at 100. That's a man who looks content on the page.

Todd Bertuzzi

Well, I am. And I think that comes through. I like the room, I like the city, I like the way it's set up here. So yeah, morale's high. I don't think I could fake that, you know. Morale is just, are you glad to show up. And I am.

Marcus Bell

I want to take you back though. You're 30, you grew up in Sudbury, you were the 23rd pick in '93 out of New York. You've been here a good long while. How did the move from Long Island to Vancouver happen, from where you were sitting?

Todd Bertuzzi

Man, that's a long time ago now. I don't remember the exact phone call. I remember the feeling of it. You've been on one road, and it's not a bad road, but you're on it, and then the ground shifts under you and it's, okay, new city, new room, new everything. I remember driving to the airport and thinking, I don't know anybody here. And then you show up and Naslund's there, and Trevor Linden's there, and, yeah. It goes quick. It always goes quick.

Marcus Bell

You told me in December that first week, you felt the line with Markus and Brendan coming together quick. You two are still out there together. What makes a line work?

Todd Bertuzzi

Trust, basically. You know, Markus does what he does, he sees the ice different than most guys. And Brendo, he's got those hands. I've gotta be honest, I just, I try to get open and get out of the way some nights. I'm the big body, I go to the front, I tip the thing. And when they're feeding you like that, you'd have to be doing something real wrong not to produce.

Marcus Bell

41 goals, 57 assists. You had 101 in 63 games last season. Do you watch the number?

Todd Bertuzzi

I mean, obviously. I'm not gonna say I don't. You see what you had last year and you want to get back to it. But 98 in 57 right now, I think that's, yeah, that's fine. The games add up. They always add up. I just gotta stay healthy and keep the line going.

Marcus Bell

Let me take you away from the rink for a second. You've got a day off. No skate, no meeting. What does a Tuesday look like for Todd Bertuzzi in Vancouver?

Todd Bertuzzi

You know, if the weather holds, I'm on the water. I love that. I'll just take the boat out somewhere nobody can find me. And it sounds cliche, I know, every guy says that. But it's, it's the quiet. You've got 20, 25 guys in a dressing room screaming and banging and there's noise all the time. And then you're out there and it's, nothing. Just water and the engine. I grew up around lakes and rivers so it's in me. You know?

Marcus Bell

Sudbury to Vancouver. Two places with a lot of trees and a lot of water. Is there a connection?

Todd Bertuzzi

There is. And I think that's why I like it here. I grew up, you know, northern Ontario, you're surrounded by bush and rock and lakes. And Vancouver is different, obviously, it's got the mountains, it's got the ocean. But the feel of it, the quiet of it, the gray sky in winter, that's, yeah, that's familiar. It's like a bigger, greener version of what I grew up with.

Marcus Bell

Now here's something. Marc Crawford was the general manager of Cornwall when you went through the OHL Priority Selection in '91. He passed on you. Called it maturity issues, said you hadn't grown into your body. And now he's your head coach.

Todd Bertuzzi

Yeah, I remember that. And I remember when he told me he said it, and I said, yeah, he wasn't wrong. I was a mess. Big kid, no idea what I was doing. So I don't hold any, any grudge about that. And look, he's simplified our game and it's working. He's a straight shooter. You know where you stand. He'll pull you over and say, Bert, that's not good enough, and then he'll pat you on the shoulder. I like that.

Marcus Bell

You don't feel like you've got something to prove to him?

Todd Bertuzzi

Not anymore. Maybe when I was 19, 20. But I'm 30 now. I don't gotta prove anything to Marc Crawford. I gotta prove something to myself every night, and I gotta get the job done for my line, for the team. Whatever he thought at the draft was 14 years ago.

Marcus Bell

Let's land the plane. One year left on your deal. $2.60M, it comes up in July. Do you think about that?

Todd Bertuzzi

Yeah. Everybody does. You'd be lying if you said you didn't. But I'm not gonna, I'm not gonna let it, you know, take up space in my head in February when we're in the middle of a season. I've got a job to do right now. I'm playing the best hockey I've played in a while, I think. And whatever happens in the summer happens. I trust the people around me. And the fact is, I like it here. So yeah, I hope it gets sorted out. But I'm not losing sleep over a July date.

Marcus Bell

Todd Bertuzzi, thank you very much for your time.

Todd Bertuzzi

Thanks, Marcus.
