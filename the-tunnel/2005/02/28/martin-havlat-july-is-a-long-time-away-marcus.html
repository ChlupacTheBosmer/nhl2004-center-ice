Martin Havlat: 'July is a Long Time Away, Marcus'

The Senators' 23-year-old left winger has 81 points on a $0.90M salary with one year left on his deal, and says he is thinking about April and nothing else.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelMonday, 28 February 2005

3:40 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Martin Havlat84 has 81 points in 64 games for Ottawa Senators this season, his assists rising from 25 in 48 games last year to 49 now. He is 23, he earns $0.90M, and his contract has one year left. The Sweater's contract-year profile caught him in the studio.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here in the studio. Martin Havlat of the Ottawa Senators®, good to sit down with you, Martin. You've got 81 points through 64 games, your assists are up from 25 to 49, and you're playing the last year of a deal at $0.90M. Is it strange, putting up these numbers and knowing what they're worth?

Martin Havlat

It is... yeah. I know everyone is talking about it. But I play the hockey first, you know. I don't think about the contract every day. I think about tomorrow's game, the next shift. Then, how you say, when it is the time, we talk. My agent talks. I play the hockey.

Marcus Bell

You were drafted 26th overall in 1999, out of Mlada Boleslav. What do you remember about the first time you put on the Ottawa Senators® sweater?

Martin Havlat

I come here in 2000, and I was very young, you know. I'm 19 years old. And the buildings, the travel, everything is big. I remember the first game, the guys, they hit different here. The ice is smaller, so you don't have the time. I was thinking too much. I should just play, but I was thinking a lot, you know, about the game, about the hit that is coming.

Marcus Bell

You spent one more season in the Czech Republic before coming over. Were you ready after that year?

Martin Havlat

Nobody is ready, you know. But I think the year was good for me. I play men's hockey in Czech Republic, and the body gets a little more. If I come right after the draft, maybe I am too small for the ice here. I don't know. But I think it was the right thing.

Marcus Bell

49 assists in 64. You never had more than 25 in a season. What changed?

Martin Havlat

The confidence, maybe. And the linemates. I play with Daniel and Marian, you know? They are smart. I see them in the practice every day and I know where they are. I go there, and the puck comes. You don't think. You just go there.

Marcus Bell

So when the line is set, you don't ask who is on it?

Martin Havlat

No, I go there, I don't care. But when it's Daniel and Marian, the game is slower, you know? I have the time. I can look up and see where Daniel is cutting and I put it there. Without them, maybe I just skate and shoot. With them, I see the game more.

Marcus Bell

Let's talk about a Sunday. You're 23, you're in Ottawa, you've got the day off. What does Martin Havlat do?

Martin Havlat

Sleep. That is number one, number two, number three. I sleep, and maybe in the afternoon I go to the store, get the food. And then, I don't know, I watch something. But mostly the sleep, you know. The body needs it.

Marcus Bell

You mentioned the food. Is that a hard thing, being here?

Martin Havlat

Yeah. There is no good Czech food here, you know. I miss my mother's cooking. When you are 19, you think, no, I don't care, I am here, the hockey is the thing, the money is good. But when you are 23 and it is Sunday, and you are hungry, and there is nothing that tastes like home... you think about it, you know. About going back. About the Czech house.

Marcus Bell

Do you and Marian Hossa spend time together? You, him, Zdeno Chara. The language, that piece of it?

Martin Havlat

Yeah, we go for the coffee, maybe. Zdeno is Slovak, Marian is Slovak, I am Czech, but the language is close, you know. Three guys talking the same words in a coffee shop in Ottawa. It is good. It is a little piece of the Czech house. And the Slovak house too. But the same.

Marcus Bell

You made the Eastern Conference Finals in your third year here. Last year, four games and gone. What do you want from April this time?

Martin Havlat

I want to be there longer, you know. Last year is... I don't want to talk about that. But this year the room is good. Daniel is playing the best hockey, the guys are working, and I think we are a problem for somebody. I want to be good when it matters. The points in February, they are good. But I want April.

Marcus Bell

And July? You've got one year left on that deal.

Martin Havlat

July is a long time away, Marcus. I love this place, you know. Daniel, Marian, the guys in the room, the fans, they are good to me. I don't want to go. But I am 23 and I play the hockey, and, you know what the other guys are making. So we sit down, I talk with my agent, and we see. But that is July. Right now it is the game.

Marcus Bell

Martin Havlat, thank you for the time, Martin. We'll see you in July. Back to you.
