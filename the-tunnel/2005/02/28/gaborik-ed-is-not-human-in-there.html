Gaborik: 'Ed is not human in there'

The Toronto right wing on Belfour stopping 45 of 47, what a 21-shot night looks like from up ice, and why 44 wins still feels like nothing without the Cup.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelMonday, 28 February 2005

2:29 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Marian Gaborik scored one of Toronto's three in a 3-2 road win at Detroit on February 27, helping the Maple Leafs improve to 44-11-4 and 102 points while Ed Belfour93 turned aside 45 of 47 shots in the crease. Gaborik sits at 81 points on the season, his 45th goal of the year. The Maple Leafs have surrendered just 160 goals in 64 games, the lowest total in the league.

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here in the tunnel at Detroit, with Marian Gaborik. Marian, forty-seven shots against, two of them got past Ed. What was he doing in that net tonight?

Marian Gaborik

Yeah, Ed was, you know, he was not human in there. I look back on the bench and I see him make the save and then I see another one come right after. Forty-seven, that's, uh, a lot. But I think in front of him, we make it easier. Svehla is, you know, he eats the puck out there. And Klesla, he's not scared to block. So Ed sees more clear lanes than maybe the number say.

Jan Kowalczyk

Twenty-one shots for, forty-seven against. That's the biggest gap I've seen from you all in a while. Did it feel like you were under pressure out there, or did the game feel different than the number says?

Marian Gaborik

No, no, it's not, I don't think siege. They shoot from, how you say, outside, and the point, and the rebound, so the number is high but it's not, uh, the chances are not all good ones. And when we get the puck, we have speed, so they have to back up. My goal, that's, we take their puck in their zone and I just, you know, I go. Mats make the pass, I shoot, that's it. We don't need forty-seven. We need three.

Jan Kowalczyk

You've got forty-four wins. One hundred two points. That's the best record in the league by a long stretch. From inside that room, what does that feel like? Do you walk in and feel the weight of it, or is it just another Tuesday?

Marian Gaborik

You know, it's... nobody talks about the number. You come in, guys are joking, the music is loud, and you just play. I think, I think the weight comes in April. Right now we just, we just try to play the way. And 102 points, yeah, it's good. But 102 and no Cup, that's, that's nothing. I said this before to Marcus, I say it again. You want the thing at the end. Not the number in February.

Jan Kowalczyk

You've said the same thing before, I'll give you credit for consistency. But you're playing at a level that maybe nobody expected at 23. Forty-five goals, eighty-one points, 96 on the Development Index. Does that feel like the best hockey of your life?

Marian Gaborik

Well, you read me the number. I just know that, you know, I feel good. The room, everybody works, so the game opens for me. Mats, he makes me better. Brad, Gary, everybody back checks, so I can, I can just go. And yeah, I feel good, my legs feel, uh, the speed is there. That's all I need.

Jan Kowalczyk

One hundred two points, the lowest goals-against in the league, a man who's not human in the net. You have everything you need. What's left to fix?

Marian Gaborik

Everything. No, I think, you know, we fix nothing. We just keep doing it. Tonight, twenty-one shots. That's not enough. We could shoot more. But we get two points. So tomorrow, we talk about why we shoot twenty-one and not more. That's the thing. You win and the coach still is not happy.

Marian Gaborik, thank you. Back to you in the studio.
