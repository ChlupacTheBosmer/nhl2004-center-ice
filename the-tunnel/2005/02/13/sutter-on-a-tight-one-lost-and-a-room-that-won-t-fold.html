Sutter on a tight one lost and a room that won't fold

The Chicago coach says his group is closer than 50 points, and he's not letting anyone pick apart his goaltender.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelSunday, 13 February 2005

3:57 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Chicago Blackhawks lost 3-2 at home to Toronto Maple Leafs on 25-25 shots on 2005-02-12, dropping to 18-28-8 and 50 points, last in the Central. The room carries a 93.9 average morale, above the league median. Head coach Brian Sutter spoke with Jan Kowalczyk at the bench after the game.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here in the corridor at the United Center with Brian Sutter, thirty minutes after a 3-2 loss to Toronto. Twenty-five shots each way. What did you see tonight?

Brian Sutter

Yeah, we saw them. Look, you give Toronto twenty-five shots and you lose by one, well, you're in the game. I thought we managed the puck well enough for the first two periods. Third period, we got a little loose with it, they got the extra man, and that's hockey. They're forty and nine, they're going to punish you. But I didn't think we were outplayed. I thought we made the, you know, the one more wrong play at the wrong time.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're nine and seventeen at home now. Twelve and nineteen on the road. You play better in other buildings.

Brian Sutter

I know what the numbers say. I'm not going to sit here and tell you it doesn't bother me. It does. You want to win in front of your own people, and we haven't. But I'll tell you what bothers me more: the margins. We've been in a lot of these games at home. They're one-goal games, they're OT games, and we've been on the wrong end of the OT coin flip more than most. That hides the fact that we're not being outplayed at home. We're just not finishing.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let me ask about the room itself. You're in last place in the Central. I know that's not the only way to look at your season, but there's a mood question. What's the feeling in that dressing room right now?

Brian Sutter

The feeling in that dressing room is that we're not playing as bad as the points column says. You watch , he's got ninety-five points, he's not moping. , , those guys know where they are, they know it's not good enough, but they also know we've been in this game three nights in a row where we're good and the result isn't there. The mood is, I mean, it's not despair. It's frustration. And frustration, you can work with. Apathy you can't. I don't have apathy in my room.

Jan Kowalczyk

Jocelyn Thibault, eighteen and twenty-nine in forty-seven starts. He's in eighty-two percent of your games. When the goals are going in against him in close ones, how do you handle that, both the conversation with him and the one with yourself?

Brian Sutter

You don't bench a man because he's got twenty-nine losses. That's not how this works. Jocelyn's been good for us. You look at tonight, twenty-five shots, three go in. One of them's a broken play in transition that nobody's covering, one's a point shot from a bad angle that he probably stops eight times out of ten. And we've scored two. Two is not a lot. You're not going to win a lot of hockey games scoring two. That's on us, not on him.

Jan Kowalczyk

You've got three on the shelf. Poapst, Korolev, Thornton. Korolev back March 2, Poapst March 20, Thornton March 15. Does that change your lines, or are you managing around them?

Brian Sutter

Well, Steve's our seventh defenseman or eighth, depending on the night, so that's not the biggest hole. Igor's been in and out and I don't see him changing our structure when he walks in. Shawn is a guy who plays the fourth line, kills penalties, does the stuff that doesn't go on the scoresheet. Losing him, you feel it in the fourth line shifts, in the penalty kill. We've got , we've got , is out there giving us what he gives us, which is, you know, he's not a scoring guy but he competes and he's tough on a man. The bottom line is we make do. Everybody's got injuries. I'm not going to lean on that.

Jan Kowalczyk

The schedule. Columbus Friday, Colorado Sunday, Phoenix at home Tuesday. Columbus is right behind you in points, three more than you. Is Friday a must-win?

Brian Sutter

No game's a must-win in February. You know what I mean by that? You're going to play sixty-some games regardless, and if you start thinking of any one of them as the one that decides your season, you've already lost your focus. What I'm thinking about is Friday in Columbus is a game we can play well in. They've got some of the same issues we've got, they're young in spots, they've got three of their own on the shelf. We go there, we play our game, and we see what happens. If we worry about Colorado on Sunday, we lose in Columbus.

Jan Kowalczyk

Last one. , fifty-three games, forty-one points. Is he the player you expected when you had him in camp?

Brian Sutter

You're trying to get me to grade a man on television. He's thirty-six, he's got a bad back or he's got whatever he's got, I don't know, I'm not the trainer. But I'll tell you this: he's out there, he's competing, he's making plays. Forty-one points for a thirty-six-year-old man who's been through what he's been through, that's a contribution. You want me to stand here and say he should have fifty? Sure, we could always use more goals. But Theo's not our problem tonight. Losing by one at home is our problem tonight.

Jan Kowalczyk

Brian Sutter from the United Center. Thank you, coach.
