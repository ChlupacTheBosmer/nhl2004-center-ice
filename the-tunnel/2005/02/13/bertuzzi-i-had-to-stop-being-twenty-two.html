Bertuzzi: 'I Had to Stop Being Twenty-Two'

The Vancouver right winger, projected at 58 and now graded at 95, told Jan Kowalczyk he had to dismantle the heavy game he built his identity on, and that a day off means fishing alone because he's had enough of rooms full of guys.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelSunday, 13 February 2005

3:29 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

The Sweater's profile this week put a number on it: Todd Bertuzzi89 is 37 grades past his ceiling. The Bureau's potential grade on him is 58. His Book grade today is 95, plus 2 on the Development Index, with 96 points in 56 games for Vancouver Canucks. He sat down with Jan Kowalczyk in the studio to talk about how a kid from Sudbury ended up past every number anyone wrote down.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here with Todd Bertuzzi of the Vancouver Canucks, in the studio. Todd, The Sweater ran a profile on you this week. Thirty-seven grades past the ceiling. Your potential on the Bureau's books is 58. You're at 95 today. Does that number land?

Todd Bertuzzi

Fifty-eight. You know, my first year in junior I got suspended fifteen games for kicking a guy during a game. So. I get the fifty-eight. That was not a crazy number for me. But ninety-five. I don't know. I didn't see that. My coaches probably didn't see that either.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let me take you somewhere quieter. You got a day off this week. What do you actually do with it? You're in Vancouver, it's February.

Todd Bertuzzi

I fish. That's, that's the one thing. I get in the car, I drive out, I get on the water. Alone. You know, I'm around twenty-nine guys ten months a year, so when I get a day off I just, I don't want to hear a voice. I want the line and the quiet. Don't care if I catch a thing. Don't even care if the water's good. I just want to be somewhere nobody needs me on the ice.

Jan Kowalczyk

So you've found the thing that settles you. Did you have that at twenty-five, or were you a different person on a day off?

Todd Bertuzzi

No. No, I didn't fish. I couldn't sit still back then, you know? I was restless. And I didn't know what I was good at. I thought being 75 inches, 225, that was the whole thing. Get to the net, get hit, hit somebody back, score ugly. That was, I mean, that was the only trick I had.

Jan Kowalczyk

You're still 75 and 225. The frame hasn't changed. So what did?

Todd Bertuzzi

Knowing what my hands can do. That's the difference. At twenty-five I'd get the puck and I'm thinking about the one play, the big play, the thing that makes the highlight. Now I'm, I already know where the next guy's going. I see the second play. I see the third. And I've got guys who go. So I just put it where they're going and hope they bury it.

Jan Kowalczyk

The numbers back it up. 56 assists this season. That's up from 47 last year. You're making more people better than you were making yourself look.

Todd Bertuzzi

Well, I don't know about making people better. But yeah, that's the work. It's, it's watching tape, it's knowing where Brendan's going to be before he gets there, where Markus is going to go. And then you just make the pass. And the simple thing is, that's harder to do than people think. The simple thing.

Jan Kowalczyk

Marcus Bell caught you back in December, after the New Jersey game, and you were still finding your feet. You and Markus and Brendan up top. Did that line take a while to find its rhythm?

Todd Bertuzzi

A while, yeah. You know, when I first got here I was trying to show I still had the legs. Show I could be the heavy guy, go in the corners, do all the, all the old stuff. And Markus is not, he's not that guy. He's a hockey player first, always has been. So I had to, I had to stop being twenty-two. Once I stopped trying to be that, it opened up. Everything about it opened up.

Jan Kowalczyk

Did you feel like you needed to prove something, coming in?

Todd Bertuzzi

Not prove. Find. I don't think it was, I mean, nobody was saying go prove yourself. I think I was saying it to myself. You know, the new room, the new system, the new city. You don't know where you fit. And now I know where I fit. Twenty-one a night, the puck's on my stick, I make the play. That's home.

Jan Kowalczyk

You've got to get asked this one. Your contract is up in July. $2.60 million, one year left. How are you sitting with that right now?

Todd Bertuzzi

I'm not. That's, that's just not how I work. You know, I play hockey, my agent handles the other stuff. Always has. I can't, I don't, I don't know how to sit here in February and worry about July. I got a game Thursday. That's what I can hold in my head.

Jan Kowalczyk

One last one. Your twenty-five-year-old self is watching this interview. He knows nothing that's coming. You get one thing to tell him. What is it?

Todd Bertuzzi

Be patient. And, you know, maybe don't kick that guy in the third period. Fifteen games is a long time to sit. But mostly, just be patient. It's going to take longer than you think. But it gets there.

Jan Kowalczyk

Todd Bertuzzi, thank you very much.
