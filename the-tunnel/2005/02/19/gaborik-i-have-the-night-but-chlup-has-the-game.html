Gaborik: "I have the night, but Chlup has the game"

A zero from the 43-goal man, and Toronto wins 3-1 anyway; Gaborik on a lineup that bent and held.

Marian Gaborik98 went quiet for the first time in a while on Saturday, 0 points in a 3-1 win over Carolina Hurricanes at home. The Toronto Maple Leafs winger carries 43 goals and 77 points through 59 games this season. But Toronto Maple Leafs won anyway, and he was the first one smiling about it in the corridor.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here with Marian Gaborik at Scotiabank Arena. Marian, 3-1, another one in the column, but you come off the ice 0 for 0 on the night. First game where that happened for a while? What was it out there?

Marian Gaborik

Yeah, uh, I don't know. I had some looks, I think in second period maybe one or two, but the goalie, the first one, he makes a good one. And the rest, you know, I just have the night. But we win, three and one, so it is good. I play bad, the boys still win. That is how you know the team is okay.

Jan Kowalczyk

And that's what stands out to me tonight. Your top six has a different shape, I noticed that early, and Chlup scores, Spacek scores. Two guys who are, what, twenty? They fill that spot and they put the puck in the net. How do you see it from next to them?

Marian Gaborik

Petr, yeah, he plays, he plays so free, you know? No, he doesn't think too much. He just goes and he shoots and sometimes it goes in, like tonight, and he doesn't worry about it. And Vaclav, Spacek, he is quiet but he is there at the right time. He just gets the puck and puts it in. For the young guys, this is how you learn, right? Not from the video. From the night. You are in the spot and you have to do it.

Jan Kowalczyk

Carolina put 39 shots on goal tonight. Thirty-nine. You held them to one. Was that a structure thing, or did Ouellet just steal the game?

Marian Gaborik

Well, both, I think. Maxime, he is the last one, and he makes the big save, the one after their second goal, maybe the power play, where if he scores it is three and two and the building gets nervous. He doesn't let it in. But also we, we blocked so many shots tonight. You know, I look on the bench and I see guys on the ice with sticks everywhere, trying to get in front. They had 39 but maybe, I don't know, 10 or 12 were blocked or wide. So the structure is there. That is the whole thing, the 154 goals against, that doesn't happen by accident.

Jan Kowalczyk

It does not. And you're sitting here at 94 points with, what, 21 games to go. You guys are 41 and 10 with 4 losses in regulation and overtime. That's not normal. That's not what anyone expected from Toronto in December when you showed up, or rather when you were already here and people started to look at this room.

Marian Gaborik

Yeah, I remember December. You ask me about the room, I say the same thing now as then. We are, how you say, we are a family. Not because we say it. Because when, like tonight, I don't score, Petr scores and I am happy. When the third line gets a power play goal, okay that one is and , but when the young guys block a shot in the last five, you see it in their face. They want it. And the veterans, Mats, Gary, Ed, they show them how. So that is why we are 41 and 10. You play for the guy next to you, you know?

Jan Kowalczyk

One last one before they get you. You're 43 goals in 59. You're 97 in the Bureau's book, that's the best grade in this building. And tonight you were invisible. Does the grade mean anything to you after a night like that?

Marian Gaborik

You know, I told you before, Jan, I think in January, we talk about the grades. I say the same. I don't care about 97. If I play the game right and we win, that is the job. Tonight I play okay, I just don't score. That is fine. The team wins. So I go home and I sleep good. The grade is for you guys, right? You write it, you talk about it. For me it is the next game.

Jan Kowalczyk

Marian Gaborik, thank you. Back to you.
