Iginla: 'I don't ask about the phone'

The Flames alternate captain keeps the contract year out of the rink, and tells Jan Kowalczyk why Jaromir Jagr at 33 is the one he watches.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelSaturday, 19 February 2005

4:40 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Jarome Iginla89 sat down with Jan Kowalczyk in the studio after the Sweater profile 'Sons of St. Albert: where Jarome Iginla learned to score 92' ran this week. The Calgary Flames right winger has 53 goals and 89 points in 54 games, his second season as the league's most dangerous finisher. He is in the last year of his $6.50M contract.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here, in the studio, with Jarome Iginla of the Calgary Flames®. Jarome, thanks for sitting down with me. We ran a profile on you this week, 'Sons of St. Albert: where Jarome Iginla learned to score 92.' That's a long title for a short kid from Alberta. Did you read it?

Jarome Iginla

Yeah, I read it. You know, St. Albert. That's where it all started, but I think people think it was all hockey. It wasn't. I was a catcher. I played baseball until I was, what, seventeen? So it was, you know, summer ball, winter hockey, and my grandfather driving me to both. Every practice, every game. My mom was working, my dad was in school, and Grandpa just, he showed up. If he hadn't done that, I don't think any of this happens.

Jan Kowalczyk

He's the reason you're in this chair?

Jarome Iginla

Obviously. You know, that's the cliché, right, 'I couldn't have done it without my family.' And everybody says it. But with me it's, it's literally true. A kid in St. Albert without a ride, without a grandfather who got up at five in the morning to take him to an ice rink, that kid doesn't make it to eleven overall in the draft. He doesn't make it to Kamloops. So yeah. That's him.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let's talk about the number in that title. 92 goals last year. You had 140 points, you won the Richard Trophy. And Calgary Flames® won 26 games. What do you remember about that year when you look back at it now?

Jarome Iginla

You know, in the moment, I was scoring and I wasn't really looking at the standings. I was trying to, uh, you know, get the next one. And then the season's over and you, and you see where you are, and 26 wins, and you're watching April from your couch. So I remember the goals, sure. But what I remember more is the feeling after. Like, it wasn't enough. The 92 wasn't enough. And that kind of sticks with you, you know?

Jan Kowalczyk

You told me after that Toronto game in late December, the 2-3 in overtime, you said the looks were there, the chances were coming. You were the league leader on 60 points. You're at 89 now and you've dropped to 17th. Are the looks still coming, or has something changed?

Jarome Iginla

No, the looks are there. And I think the difference is, this year the movement around me is better. Craig Conroy back there, he's, he's seeing things a half-second before guys did last year. Martin Gelinas, he's been finding me on the back door. And Dean McAmmond, when he's out there, he makes the play to the open man, not the safe man. So the looks are there. I've got 53 goals in 54 games, so something's working. I just, you know, I gotta bury the other ones.

Jan Kowalczyk

And the people who passed you. Jaromir Jagr is at 113 points, he's 33 years old, playing for Washington Capitals®. Markus Naslund at 110. Do you watch them? Do you watch what they're doing?

Jarome Iginla

Well, I don't sit and watch other guys. I watch our own clips, trying to figure out why a shot missed the net by two inches. But you see those numbers. You can't not see them. 113 at 33. With Jaromir specifically, what I respect is, he doesn't rush anything. He'll wait. He lets the play come to him and then he's got that half-step, that little move where he gets a step on a defenseman and then he's in position and he's shooting where it counts. Most guys at 33 are hanging on. He's still adding. And I think, you know, I think that's the hardest thing to do in this league. Get better when you're supposed to be getting older.

Jan Kowalczyk

Let me ask you about something you don't control on the ice. You've got zero years left on your deal. $6.50M is on the books. This is a contract year. How do you keep that out of the building?

Jarome Iginla

You know, you focus on the next shift. That's the only thing that works. You can't, you can't go out there thinking about October and agents and, you know, phone calls and all that other stuff. You just, you get out there, you play the game, you do what you can for the room. That's, that's it.

Jan Kowalczyk

Is that easy, though? On a cold night, the puck's not going in, the bench is quiet, does your mind wander to, I don't know, what's next?

Jarome Iginla

Sure. Of course it does. I'm not made of stone, Jan. You sit in that, in that quiet, you start thinking, well, what am I worth, where does this go, who's, you know, who's calling. And then, you catch yourself. You come back to what you do. And what I do is shoot the puck. That's the one thing I can control. The shot, the release, the spot on the ice. I try to live there. But yeah. It creeps in.

Jan Kowalczyk

Do you call your agent? Do you ask where things stand?

Jarome Iginla

I don't ask. That's the thing. I don't pick up the phone and say, 'where are we, who's calling, what's the market.' I just, I don't want to know in the middle of February. If I know something, it's going to sit in the back of my head on a breakout, on a forecheck, and that's not, that's not good for me or for the room. So I let it be what it is. Whoever handles it, I trust them to handle it. I just play.

Jan Kowalczyk

One more on last year. 140 points, 26 wins. Some people look at that and say, that's a guy carrying a team. Is that a fair thing to say?

Jarome Iginla

No. And I don't like that word, 'carrying.' It sounds like everyone else was just standing around while I skated by. That's not what that room was. Roman Turek was in net every night. Craig Conroy was doing things with his stick that you don't see on a stat sheet. Martin Gelinas, everybody in that locker room was working. One guy can't win 40 games. You know what I mean? I scored goals. That's my job. I did it. But I didn't carry anybody. We all went through it together, and we all came out of it knowing it wasn't good enough. And I think that's what made this year different. I think the guys in this room want it more.

Jan Kowalczyk

What does breaking through look like to you? What has to happen between here and April?

Jarome Iginla

I think we're closer than people think. Roman Turek can steal you games, he did it all year. The structure is there. We just need to, you know, we need to stay healthy and we need to put pucks in the net when the looks come. And I think the belief is different this year than last year. I felt that on the bench. When you're killing a penalty in the last minute and everyone's locked in, you know, that's something. That's not nothing. And I think we're building on that.

Jan Kowalczyk

Jarome, thank you for your time tonight. Appreciate you sitting down.

Jarome Iginla

Thanks, Jan. Good to see you. Back to you.
