Babcock: three forwards out, same game

The Ducks coach explains how a team missing Cole, Gosselin and Smirnov is still first in the Pacific after a 3-2 win over the Islanders.

Jan KowalczykRinkside reporter and studio host · The TunnelSaturday, 19 February 2005

2:39 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Mighty Ducks of Anaheim beat New York Islanders 3-2 on 2005-02-17 at home, holding on despite being outshot 22 to 20. Head coach Mike Babcock spoke to Jan Kowalczyk85 after the game about how the Ducks keep winning with three regular forwards in the trainer's room.

Transcript

Jan Kowalczyk

Jan Kowalczyk here in the corridor at the Arrowhead Pond, with Mike Babcock. Mike, you win it 3-2, but they outshot you 22 to 20. What does that tell you about the way the game played out?

Mike Babcock

Well, it tells me we managed the puck better than we did the last week or so. You look at the shot number and you think, okay, we sat on our hands a bit. But the 3-2 games, they're won in the details. The first one to two, the second one to three, and we had those tonight. They didn't.

Jan Kowalczyk

You've got Cole out, Gosselin out, Smirnov out. Three regular forwards in the room. And you're first in the Pacific. How do you fill that?

Mike Babcock

You put other guys in and you say, this is your job now. Erik's a good player, you don't replace him one for one. But Andy McDonald's been playing the 1C spot and doing well there, 35 points in 59 games, he's been solid. And the young guys step up. Tuomas is 20 years old, he's got 23 points in 53 games this year, you just... you trust him and you go. You don't overthink it.

Jan Kowalczyk

And the special teams tonight? You didn't give up much, did you?

Mike Babcock

The penalty kill's been our bread and butter. We've been, you know, the structure's there. Ruslan, Keith, those guys back there, they're not going to lose their gaps. And Aki Berg, 59 games played, every night, he's a wall. You put him on the PK and he's not going to take a step backwards. That's the whole thing. You don't need to be creative. You need to be honest.

Jan Kowalczyk

Last time we spoke, back in December, you said Giguere was your guy. You're 27-24 on the year with him in 52 games. Still the answer?

Mike Babcock

Yeah. Yeah, he's the answer. You look at the number, 52 games, 88 percent of ours, he's carrying it. And Martin Gerber when he goes in, he's ready. That's what you need from a backup, you need him to be ready and not to think he's owed something. We've got 71 points. Jean-Seb's had a hand in most of them.

Jan Kowalczyk

Gosselin's back the 21st. Erik the 23rd. Does the lineup change a lot when those two come back?

Mike Babcock

It changes, obviously. Erik gives us a second scorer, a left wing who can actually, you know, put the puck in the net. And Gossie gives us another body on the wing. But I'm not going to blow things up. You don't fix what's working. We win tonight, you don't sit here and rearrange the furniture. You wait, you get healthy, and then you make the adjustments. The schedule doesn't wait for you, though.

Jan Kowalczyk

You've got Columbus coming tomorrow, and the Rangers on the 21st. Four games in five nights. Does the injury list force your hand on the fourth line?

Mike Babcock

It forces it every night this year, you know? Curtis Brown, Sam Pahlsson, Matthew Stajan, those guys have been playing fourth line minutes all year because we've been short. They're not going to blink. You put 'em out there and you say, kill penalties, don't make a mistake, let the top nine handle the rest. That's hockey. You don't need four lines scoring. You need four lines competing.

Jan Kowalczyk

Mike, thanks for your time. Back to you.
