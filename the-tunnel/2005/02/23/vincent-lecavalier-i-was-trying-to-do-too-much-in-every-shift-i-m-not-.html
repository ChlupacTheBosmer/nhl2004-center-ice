Vincent Lecavalier: 'I was trying to do too much in every shift. I'm not anymore'

Out with a leg injury until April 1, the Lightning centre tells Marcus Bell how a coaching conversation reset his game, why the 2001-02 season taught him to put down a nickname carried since 1998, and what he wants from the last stretch.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelWednesday, 23 February 2005

3:15 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Vincent Lecavalier90 leads Tampa Bay Lightning in scoring with 47 goals and 58 assists for 105 points in 59 games, a pace that projects to 146 over a full season and would top his 138 from 2003-04. He has been out with a leg injury since sometime between February 16 and February 19, with a firm return date of April 1. Marcus Bell sat with him in the studio tonight.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here, in the studio at Tampa Bay Lightning® with Vincent Lecavalier. Vincent, good to see you. How's the leg?

Vincent Lecavalier

Thanks Marcus. It's, it's not great. I felt it right away. The guys on the bench they looked at me and, you know, nobody had to tell me. And the doctors, they say April 1. Six weeks. I think, okay, at least I see the last games. At least I can come back for that part.

Marcus Bell

Before we get there, I want to talk about what you were doing before this. 47 goals, 58 assists, 105 points in 59 games. That's a 146-point pace. You had 138 last year in 82. Whatever the injury says about the rest of it, you are playing the best hockey of your career. What changed?

Vincent Lecavalier

The pace, yeah, but what is a pace if I'm not out there? I'd trade the pace for ten games right now. But yeah, to answer you. I know what my job is now. I play center. I drive the play with Martin St. Louis on one side, Cory Stillman on the other. That's the job. Before, two years ago, three years ago, I was trying to be everywhere. I was playing wing and center and first shift and, I don't know, doing four things at once. Nothing was working because I was doing too much.

Marcus Bell

Was there a moment? Did someone sit you down and say, here's what we want from you?

Vincent Lecavalier

Yeah. There was. I don't want to name the man because I respect everybody that worked with me here, everybody. But there was a conversation, yeah. They said, your job is to drive the play, that's it, stop trying to be six guys on the ice. And after that the game, it opened. I wasn't fighting myself anymore.

Marcus Bell

Let me take you back further. You get drafted first overall in 1998, the owner says you'll be the Michael Jordan of hockey. You wear the C young. Then before 2001-02, they take it off. And that season doesn't go the way you want. Was that your worst year?

Vincent Lecavalier

That year. Yeah. I was 21. The foot was broken, I missed 14 games, and then they take the captaincy. And I thought, who am I? Every game I was forcing it. Pass when I should shoot, shoot when I should pass. Everything was too hard. It was not good. Not a good year at all.

Marcus Bell

And now? 47 goals at 24. World Cup MVP last summer. Did you put that weight down?

Vincent Lecavalier

The Michael Jordan thing. Yeah, I carried that around. You're 18, the owner says that, you believe it, for sure. You think, okay, I'm supposed to be somebody else. And after a couple years of not being that, you learn. I'm not him. I'm me. I'm a center. I play with Martin and Fredrik Modin and the guys and I do my job. That's who I am.

Marcus Bell

The World Cup. You were the MVP of the Canadian team. Does a summer like that follow you into the season?

Vincent Lecavalier

Oh, for sure, for sure. You put on that jersey, you play with those guys, you win. I came back and I thought, I belong here. I belong with the best players. That helped me in September, in October, when the games got tight. I didn't doubt myself. Not like before.

Marcus Bell

You and Martin St. Louis are the best players on this team. What's the thing between you two?

Vincent Lecavalier

He's, uh... Martin is, she's the first one in the building every day. Sorry. He is. He's the first one in, the last one to leave. He scores 92 points and he doesn't say a word about it. You look at him and he just wants to win. I want to win. That's the whole conversation. We don't need to, you know, have the big talk. We just go out there.

Marcus Bell

Last one. April 1. That's maybe a dozen games back after that. What do you want from the rest of this year?

Vincent Lecavalier

I want to be back when it counts. I want the playoffs. The goals are there, 47, I know I can add more. But I'm tired of watching from the side. I want to be in the shift. I want to be with the guys. That's it.

Marcus Bell

Vincent Lecavalier, thank you for your time tonight.

Vincent Lecavalier

Thank you, Marcus. Good talk. Back to you.
