Eric Daze: 'I am not a piece in a book, for sure'

The left winger behind Chicago's 99-point season talks about the pick that came from Hašek, the year that broke out and the year that fell back, and what it sounds like inside when your GM says he is ruling out nothing.

Marcus BellRinkside reporter · The TunnelMonday, 21 February 2005

5:35 · synthetic voices, produced from the record · download

ShareCopy link

Eric Daze87 sits down with Marcus Bell in the Center Ice studio. The Chicago Blackhawks left winger has 99 points in 60 games this season, well below the 153 he posted last year at 74 games. His contract has 1 year left at $3.00M, and the club sits at 50 points, 25 back of a playoff spot.

Transcript

Marcus Bell

Marcus Bell here, studio desk, with Eric Daze of the Chicago Blackhawks. Eric, the profile we are running this week is titled 'The Pick That Came With Hašek.' You were the pick that came back from Ottawa for Dominik Hašek. Third round, 90th overall, 1993. That trade made you a Blackhawk. How old were you when you found out?

Eric Daze

Yeah, I was seventeen, maybe eighteen, something like that. I was in Beauport, you know, in the QMJHL. The trade was already done, the pick was already Chicago's, and then the scout calls my agent, my agent calls my father, my father calls me at the rink. And I am thinking, okay, fine, third round, that is not nothing, that is a chance. But I did not know it came from Hašek. Nobody tells you that at seventeen. You are just happy somebody drafted you, for sure.

Marcus Bell

You broke out at Beauport the year after. 59 goals, 107 points in 66 games. First All-Star team. You must have thought you were going to be a top pick the next summer, but you were already drafted.

Eric Daze

Yeah, yeah, exactly. That is the funny part. I am having that year, everybody is talking about me, and the Blackhawks already have me. I signed in March of '95, the three-year deal. I played four games at the end of that lockout year. Four games, one goal. And then I went back down and I worked. Nobody was waiting for me up there.

Marcus Bell

From '96 to '03 you scored 20 goals every single season. That is eight straight years at 20, through line changes, through injuries, through coaches coming and going. You were never the first name on the scoresheet in Chicago, but you were always there.

Eric Daze

I mean, that is what I was. I was the guy who scored twenty, twenty-five. Never thirty in a full year maybe, maybe a year here and there. But the guys, the guys needed someone on that wing who would show up and put it in the net. I lined up with a lot of centers, you know. Gilmour one year, then Amonte on another line, then it changes again. And I just tried to be there, for sure.

Marcus Bell

And then last year you score 70 goals, 83 assists, 153 points. Third in the league. The whole story changes. What happened?

Eric Daze

I think the thing is, when you are twenty-nine and you have always been the second guy, the third guy, and then suddenly you are first line, you are playing twenty-two minutes a night, the puck goes in and you think, okay, why not, I can do this. The first three games I had six goals and I was thinking, this is the dream, this is what you work for since the QMJHL. It just went, for sure.

Marcus Bell

This season you are at 99 in 60 games. That is 135 pace over 82, still top ten. But it is not 153. Is the season going wrong for you, or is it just a smaller year in a smaller team?

Eric Daze

Well... the team is not good. I think you know that. The team is not good. And when the team is not good, the ice gets long, the chances are not the same, the defensemen cannot get you the puck clean because they are busy trying not to give it up. But 99 is 99. I am not going to sit here and tell you I am unhappy. I can do more, yeah. I know I can do more. But it is not, it is not a bad year. It is a year where I am thinking about the team first.

Marcus Bell

You said twenty-two minutes a night last year. You are at twenty and a half this year. You are still the number one left winger in that room. Theoren Fleury is on your side, Zhamnov down the middle. That is still a line that can score.

Eric Daze

Yeah, Flo is... Flo is thirty-six and he plays like he is twenty-two, for sure. And Z has the hands, you know, Z can see the play before it happens. We can score, that is not the problem. The problem is the game is sixty minutes and we are not good for sixty. We are good for twenty, twenty-five, and then the other team puts pressure and we crack. That is what I took from it. You cannot win in this league with twenty-five good minutes.

Marcus Bell

So what do you do, from your seat? You have been in this building longer than most of them. When the team is twenty-five points out, what does leadership look like in that room?

Eric Daze

You know, I do not know if leadership is the right word. I think it is just showing up. Showing up and being the same guy. The guys can smell it if you check out, if you are thinking about your own numbers when the team is losing. I try, I try to talk to the young guys, Mark Bell, Kyle Calder, the kids who are learning the league. But it is not a speech thing. It is a, you do your job, you play your shift, you go back the next night. That is it. We have to be better, that's it.

Marcus Bell

There is a captain in every room. Not you, but there is one. From where you sit, who is carrying that?

Eric Daze

I think Z carries a lot of it. Just by, by how he is. He does not say much, you know? But when he talks in the room, everyone listens. And Flo, Flo is loud, Flo is the energy. And me, I am... I am the guy who has been here a while. That counts for something. Not a speech thing. Just, they know if I am doing well then maybe the team can do well. That is the role.

Marcus Bell

Jason Hughes told this desk a few days ago that he is not ruling out either direction. He has got your contract coming up, one year left. What does it sound like in the room when the GM says a thing like that?

Eric Daze

It sounds like, it sounds like you are in a book. You are a number. He says, either direction, and you think, okay, I am a number in his ledger. But listen. I am not... I am not saying I am angry. I am twenty-nine, I have one year left, the team is where it is. I understand it. But it sounds, how do I say, it sounds strange to hear a man say they are not ruling out moving me, and then you see him on the bench the next game saying good game, Daze. For sure. That is the job. But it is strange.

Marcus Bell

Would you go somewhere that is winning?

Eric Daze

Yeah, of course. I want to win the Cup, everybody wants that. But I have been here since... a long time. I am not going to say I love this city, I am from Montreal, that is different. But these are my guys. I know where they are going to be on the ice. That takes years. You cannot, you cannot do that in a week. So yeah, I would like to win. But I would like to win here, you know? If they call me and say, you are going somewhere else, okay, I go. But I am thinking about here.

Marcus Bell

You are at 43 goals right now, 56 assists. The goal total is not the problem. Your assists are down from last year by a lot. Do you feel the team is not getting to the net as a group?

Eric Daze

Yeah, yeah. You know, last year I had 83 assists. Eighty-three. I was finding Flo, I was finding Z, the game was open because we were winning, you can push. This year the first period we are, we are good, the second period someone makes a mistake, we give up a goal, and then we are chasing and the game gets tight. Tight games, tight ice, fewer chances to make the pretty play. The goals I can still get because I can shoot, for sure. The assists, you need a team. You need a team.

Marcus Bell

One year left. If the phone does not ring, if you are in Chicago in October, what does that year look like?

Eric Daze

Then I try to get back to 150. I am kidding, a little. I think... I think that year I just play. I stop thinking about the trade, the contract, the book. I just play and if they want me here, here I am. If not, I go. But I want to feel like this, like right now, ninety-six on the morale, for sure. That is the thing. I am happy in the room. That is not nothing.

Marcus Bell

Eric Daze, thank you very much for the time.

Eric Daze

Thank you, Marcus. Good to be here.
