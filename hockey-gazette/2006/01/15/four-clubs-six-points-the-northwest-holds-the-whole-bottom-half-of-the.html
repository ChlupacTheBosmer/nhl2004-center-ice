Four Clubs, Six Points: The Northwest Holds the Whole Bottom Half of the West

Colorado, Vancouver, Minnesota and Calgary sit 5th through 8th in the West, six points apart, and no two of them built their points the same way.

Howard BlaineSenior writer, The Blaine Rankings · The Hockey GazetteSunday, 15 January 2006
ShareCopy link

Four clubs in the Northwest hold places 5 through 8 in the Western Conference, 6 points apart, with 37 or 38 games left. Colorado Avalanche leads on 55 points from 44 games. Vancouver Canucks has 53, Minnesota Wild 50 and Calgary Flames 49. Detroit is 9th on 46 and Edmonton 14th on 38. Colorado earned most of its points in regulation, Minnesota in overtime, Vancouver on the cheapest payroll in the group, and Calgary largely on the road.

Colorado gets its wins in regulation

Colorado Avalanche has won 8 straight games, the last of them 2-0 at Carolina on 2006-01-12. The club is 12-1-2 across its last 15, and its last ten have produced 31 goals for and 20 against. The Avalanche have 20 wins in regulation and 4 in overtime, the fewest overtime wins of the four.

Peter Forsberg95 has 26 goals and 29 assists for 55 points in 44 games, at 22.4 minutes a night, and the Book has him at 95. He is 32 and carries $9.90M with one year left. Paul Kariya89 has 14 goals and 37 assists for 51 points; Milan Hejduk88 has 23 and 23 for 46.

Colorado is 13-8 on the road and 11-12 at home, and the next two are at home: Detroit on 2006-01-16, then Dallas on 2006-01-20.

Minnesota scores, then waits

Minnesota Wild has 11 wins in regulation and 10 in overtime, the most overtime wins in the league, in 45 games. The Wild have scored 125 goals, more than any other club in the Western Conference, and allowed 123. They sit 7th in the West at 1.111 points per game.

Inside the division, Minnesota has 2 regulation wins in 15 games and 8 regulation losses, the worst record of the four. Richard Park79 has 28 goals and 23 assists for 51 points at 24.1 minutes a night. Tuomo Pihlman81, 21, has 17 and 29 for 46 at 23.6; he won the Calder last season. The Wild host Vancouver on 2006-01-16, a club they have not beaten in 3 meetings, then play at Anaheim on 2006-01-20.

Vancouver buys its points at $0.93M

Vancouver Canucks has 53 points on a $49.50M payroll, $0.93M a point. Only Pittsburgh ($0.85M) and Nashville ($0.91M) spend less per point in the league. Within the division, Colorado pays $1.21M a point, Minnesota $1.04M and Calgary $1.06M.

Markus Naslund91 leads the league with 65 points, 31 goals and 34 assists in 44 games at 22.6 minutes. Todd Bertuzzi94 has 34 goals and 27 assists for 61 points. Vancouver has 9 regulation wins in 14 division games, more than any other club in the Northwest.

This desk wrote on 2006-01-08 about a seven-game Vancouver run. That run has ended, and the Canucks arrive on a run of 2 with four straight on the road: Minnesota on 2006-01-16, Chicago on 2006-01-17, Detroit on 2006-01-19 and Nashville on 2006-01-21. They are 11-9 away.

Calgary's three home games

Calgary Flames is 15-8 on the road and 8-14 at home, the split this desk wrote about on 2005-12-29. Nashville comes to Calgary on 2006-01-16, Los Angeles on 2006-01-18 and Edmonton on 2006-01-20, which is three chances in one week to fix the record that has held the Flames down.

Jarome Iginla92 has 34 goals and 25 assists for 59 points at 22.2 minutes, and the Book has him at 92. Calgary has 7 division games left, more than Colorado's 4, Vancouver's 6 or Minnesota's 5, and the first of the three at home is against a Nashville club that is 8-14 on the road.

Dallas, which has allowed 95 goals in 46 games, is at Colorado on 2006-01-20. By then the four clubs will have played eleven games between them, and the margin at the top of this division will be somebody's arithmetic.
