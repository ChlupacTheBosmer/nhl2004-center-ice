Six One-Goal Wins in Seven Games: Atlanta Has the League's Cheapest Roster, 7 Straight, and 5 Points Between It and the Dance

The Thrashers dress the 1st overall pick from 2001 and the 2nd from 2000, spend $40.44M, and have won 7 in a row with six decided by a single goal.

Howard BlaineSenior writer, The Blaine Rankings · The Hockey GazetteFriday, 13 January 2006
ShareCopy link

Atlanta Thrashers has won 7 consecutive games. Six of them were decided by a single goal. The points pile up regardless: 14 in 7 nights, enough to pull the Thrashers to 13th in the Eastern Conference on 43 points, 5 behind Philadelphia Flyers for the 8th seed with 40 games left.

Atlanta carries the cheapest roster in this league at $40.44M, $52.51M lighter than New York Rangers, and it costs $0.94M per point earned, the 4th-best rate among all 30 clubs. The two men driving the top line were selected 1st overall in 2001 and 2nd overall in 2000: Ilya Kovalchuk89 carries 57 points (30 goals, 27 assists) in 42 games at age 22; Dany Heatley92 carries 54 points (21 goals, 33 assists) in 42 games at age 24.

The Eastern Conference playoff line sits at 48 points, held by Philadelphia Flyers, and the clubs within striking distance tell a story about what separates a team that wins close games from one that loses them. Pittsburgh Penguins at 54 points carries the highest overtime-win total in the conference at 8. Tampa Bay Lightning at 53 allows 2.44 goals a night, the best defensive number among these six. Boston Bruins and New York Islanders both score 2.93 a night and both allow 2.67 or 2.69, a razor-thin margin that has left them 4 and 3 points above the line respectively. Atlanta allows 2.79, better than the 8th club already, and scores 2.60, the worst among the six. The Thrashers are grinding out close ones.

The 7-game run began after a 3-4 loss to Boston Bruins on 2005-12-28. Since then: a 3-1 win at Pittsburgh Penguins on December 30, a 3-2 win at Ottawa Senators on January 2, a 4-3 win over Pittsburgh Penguins at home on January 3, a 3-2 win over Philadelphia Flyers at home on January 5, a 3-2 win over Carolina Hurricanes at home on January 7, a 3-2 win at Tampa Bay Lightning on January 9, and a 5-4 win at New York Islanders on January 11. The Philadelphia Flyers game on January 5 was the only meeting between these two clubs that Atlanta has won in regulation; the season series now stands 1-1-0 to Atlanta, 7 goals to 7.

Atlanta meets Philadelphia Flyers again tonight at the Wachovia Center. The game is the 43rd for Atlanta and the 43rd for Philadelphia, and the winner takes a full measure of the gap between them. A Philadelphia win restores the distance to 7 points with 39 games left. An Atlanta win, or an overtime point for either side, compresses everything.

What the Book says about the two engines

Kovalchuk carries an overall grade of 86 on the Bureau's Book, with a Development Index form of +5 and a potential of 85. The potential sits below his current grade, which means the Bureau views him as close to finished as a product. That is unusual for a 22-year-old and speaks to how much of his game the Book already measures. His 111-point pace would challenge for the Art Ross. Last season he finished 2nd in that race with 138 points.

Heatley carries an 85 overall, a form of 0 on the Index, and a potential of 85 as well. Same reading: the Bureau sees the man he is now as the man he will be. His 54 points at a 105 pace are a slight regression from 131 points in 2003-04, when he finished 4th in the Art Ross. At 24, the dip is a recalibration. The assist total of 33 is the same number he posted in 79 games last year, achieved in 42 this year, which tells you the playmaking is intact and the volume is what has shifted.

Neither man is on the injured list. The Thrashers have 5 other games against Philadelphia Flyers and Carolina Hurricanes before the end of February, two clubs separated from Atlanta by 1 point and 5 respectively.

The standings arithmetic

Atlanta sits 13th in the Eastern Conference on 43 points. The 9th through 13th clubs are separated by 3 points: Montreal Canadiens at 46, Buffalo Sabres and Ottawa Senators at 45, Carolina Hurricanes at 44, and Atlanta Thrashers at 43. The gap between 8th and 13th is 5 points across 39 or 40 remaining games for each club. In a 30-team league where the 8th seed last spring belonged to a club with a 15-loss first round, 5 points is nothing. It is also everything, because the club holding that 8th spot has 40 games left and plays Atlanta twice more before the end of February.

The desk logged no prediction on Atlanta this season.

Atlanta earns at least 1 point at Philadelphia Flyers tonight. The logic is the same as the last 7 wins: this team does not lose by more than a goal anymore.
