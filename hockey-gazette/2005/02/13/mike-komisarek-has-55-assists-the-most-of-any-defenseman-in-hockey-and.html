Mike Komisarek has 55 assists, the most of any defenseman in hockey, and the Canadiens are 15th in the East

A 23-year-old who played 24 games last season leads all blueliners in primary assists at $1.10M, up six on the Index, on the worst team in the conference

Howard BlaineSenior writer, The Blaine Rankings · The Hockey GazetteSunday, 13 February 2005
ShareCopy link

Mike Komisarek86 has 66 points in 58 games, including 55 assists, the highest assist total of any defenseman in the league. He is 23 years old, he earns $1.10M, he has 1 year left on his contract, and Montreal Canadiens has lost 33 games and given up 227 goals. The Canadiens sit 15th in the East at 46 points, and the 3.91 goals against per game they allow is the worst in hockey. None of that diminishes what Komisarek has done.

Last season he played 24 games and scored 3 points. The Book graded him 77 at the base. The Development Index has him up six positions this season, and the overall grade sits at 80 today. His potential grade is 90. The jump from 3 points to 66 is not a line anyone drew in October.

Komisarek is one of three defensemen level at 66 points. A Detroit defenseman graded 90 and a Washington defenseman graded 83 share the top line on the scoresheet. The Detroit club is 23-21-5 and 10 points back of 8th in the West. The Washington club has 71 points and a plus-31 goal differential. The Montreal club is 7-23 on the road. The scoresheet does not care about the construction underneath the production, which is precisely the problem.

What the Book says about the man, not the team

The attribute grades tell you the shape of the season. Komisarek carries a 90 on checking, 87 on speed, 86 on aggression, 86 on puck handling, 85 on shot power, and 83 on passing. His fight grade is 51 and his shot-block rating is 52. He is a skater who moves the puck through the neutral zone and finds the open man, which is what 55 assists looks like when you count the individual plays.

The 11 goals that ride alongside those assists tell you he is not a volume shooter who stumbled into a high-total season. The Canadiens produce 183 goals in 58 games, the fewest among every Eastern club. Every goal Komisarek puts on the board is one the team actually managed.

$1.10M and the February question

The contract ledger has him at $1.10M with 1 year remaining. Montreal Canadiens carries a $53.20M payroll against 46 points, which the Bureau's cost-per-point line prints as $1.16M for the roster as a whole. The club generates $62.87M in revenue, clears $17.71M in profit, and draws 15,537 people at $77 a ticket. The building is not empty and the owners are not losing money. What the building is watching is 15-33-4 hockey, and 7 wins in 30 road games.

The Norris Trophy went to Robert Svehla of Toronto Maple Leafs last spring, who played on a Cup club that allowed 51 goals in 20 playoff games. The voters reward the defenseman whose club also wins. Komisarek's 66 points put him level with two 90-grade and 83-grade defensemen on the scoresheet, but the trophy conversation at the end of April has always been about both halves of that equation, and the Canadiens are 3.91 goals against per game from having the second half.

The path to 29.7 minutes

Komisarek was drafted 7th overall in 2001 by Montreal, played two seasons at the University of Michigan where he earned First Team All-American honors in 2002, and skated in his first NHL game in 2002-03. He logged 11.8 minutes a game in 24 appearances last season. This season he logs 29.7. The jump of nearly 18 minutes a night on a club with 15 wins reflects a vacancy on the blue line.

The Canadiens' best player on the depth chart is Jose Theodore93 at 89, with Saku Koivu86 at 85 and Richard Zednik86 at 83. Andrei Markov80, the defenseman who plays beside him, is at 79 with 47 points in 48 games. No other player on the roster is graded higher than Komisarek's 80.

The trade deadline, what the calendar has as the trade deadline, is 34 days away. A 23-year-old defenseman on an expiring deal, graded 80 and rising, with 66 points on a club at 46: that is a piece a general manager moves when the standings say February is about salvage. It is also a piece a general manager keeps when the Index says +6 and the potential grade says 90 and the base has already climbed from 77 to 80.

Florida Panthers's Boris Van Ryswyk told The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk this week, at 45 points and facing the same arithmetic from the other end of the standings: "We're looking at what we've got and asking what it's worth to somebody else, and what comes back. That's the season." Montreal is at 46. The question is the same. The answer depends on whether anyone in the front office believes the 80 is the floor or the ceiling.
