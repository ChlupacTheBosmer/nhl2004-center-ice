Brodeur has 34 wins, a 2.55 GAA, and no Devil is in the league's top 30 in scoring

The Devils hold 2nd in the East on 196 goals scored, fewer than any club above 70 points in the conference, and the man in net carries the gap

Howard BlaineSenior writer, The Blaine Rankings · The Hockey GazetteMonday, 21 February 2005
ShareCopy link

Martin Brodeur95 has 34 wins and a 2.55 goals-against average in 48 starts, both the best marks at the position in the league, and the New Jersey Devils Devils hold 2nd in the Eastern Conference on 196 goals scored, fewer than any other club with more than 70 points in the conference.

The Devils allow 172 goals in 59 games, the fewest in the East behind Toronto Maple Leafs's 155. New Jersey has no man in the league's top 30 in scoring. The 2nd-place construction is the goaltender's, and Brodeur has filled the gap between what his forwards put on the board and what he keeps off his own all season.

He is 32. The Bureau's Book grades him 94, a base of 95, and the Development Index sits at 0: he is performing at the level the organization has carried him at since before the record opened. His strongest attributes are endurance at 97 and puck-play at 95. His weakest are toughness at 50 and composure at 51, and the Book does not pretend those are the marks of an iron man. His contract carries $6.70M with 4 years remaining.

The Vezina field, by the two numbers

The last Vezina went to Olaf Kolzig83 of Washington Capitals, graded 87 in the Book. Brodeur sat at 94 that season, too. The gap between the Bureau's ranking and the voters' choice is not new.

Jean-Sebastien Giguere95 of Mighty Ducks of Anaheim sits above Brodeur in the Book at 97, the highest grade on any goaltender in the league. Giguere has a 2.58 GAA and 28 wins in 53 starts. Marty Turco95 of Dallas Stars, graded 96, has 32 wins and a 2.77. Tomas Vokoun91 of Nashville Predators, at 90, has 31 wins and a 2.64 in 47 starts. Brodeur is 3rd in the Book's composite and 1st in the two numbers a ballot prints.

Brodeur's 2003-04 line, 39 wins in 64 starts and a 2.76 GAA, was the benchmark he entered this season against. He is ahead of last year's GAA on a lighter workload through the same number of calendar weeks.

Payroll, gate, and the profit margin

New Jersey Devils carries a $44.98M payroll, 18th of 30 clubs, and converts it at $0.54M per point. Only Vancouver Canucks at $0.45M and Edmonton Oilers at $0.47M have a better dollar conversion. The Devils drew 17,330 a night, the 2nd-highest gate in the league behind Toronto Maple Leafs's 17,681, and earned $77.84M in revenue, also behind Toronto alone. The profit of $37.72M trails only Minnesota Wild.

The roster behind Brodeur carries Patrik Elias88 and Jamie Langenbrunner88, both graded 88, and Jeff Friesen88 at 87. Scott Gomez84 at 84 is the most recognizable name in the forward group. None appears in the top 30. The Devils do not outscore anyone.

Ten divisional games left, the most in the Atlantic

NJ has played 10 of 20 divisional games, the fewest of any club in the Atlantic Division, and has 10 remaining, the most. The Devils went 5 regulation wins, 2 overtime wins, 2 losses and 1 overtime loss in the 10 they have played, and they are 3-0-1 against Philadelphia Flyers and 1-1-0 with an overtime win against Pittsburgh Penguins.

New Jersey Devils is on a 6-game winning streak and has outscored opponents 29-20 over its last 10 games. The next 4 dates inside 7 days take the Devils through Boston Bruins, Pittsburgh Penguins, New York Rangers and New York Islanders. Three of those four are divisional.

The standings at Christmas are the standings in April, and I see no reason to believe the 2nd place in the East is going anywhere. NJ sits on 83 points with 23 games remaining. Toronto, 1st at 96, has a 13-point lead and 22 dates left. The Devils have 5 points of daylight on Tampa Bay Lightning for 2nd, and the 10 divisional games ahead are the softest stretch on the schedule for a club in this position.
