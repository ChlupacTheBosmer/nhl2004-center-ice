Florida draws 15,051 for 9-18 hockey at home, and its general manager is already shopping the roster

The Panthers pay 12th-most in the league, draw 8th-best, and sit 14th in the East. Tobe Wong told The Tunnel this week that February is about what comes back.

Howard BlaineSenior writer, The Blaine Rankings · The Hockey GazetteWednesday, 9 February 2005
ShareCopy link

Florida Panthers pays $51.07M in payroll, draws 15,051 fans a night at $79 a ticket, and has produced 47 points through 54 games. The club is 8th in attendance in a 30-team league, 14th in the East, and 12th in payroll. The distance between those rankings is why the general manager is on the phone.

Boris Van Ryswyk is the Panthers' general manager. He told The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk on February 6 that the deadline in 38 days is the only conversation that matters: "It looks like forty-one days of phone calls. I'm not going to pretend this is a February where we're shopping for a playoff piece. Forty-five points, I mean, you look at the standings and you know."

The attendance number is the interesting one. Fifteen thousand and fifty-one is more than Minnesota Wild draws, more than St. Louis Blues, more than Phoenix Coyotes, more than half the league. Those people are paying $79 a head and watching a team that has won 9 of 27 home games. The Panthers' home record is 9-18, worst in the Southeast Division, and Van Ryswyk named it himself: "Fifteen thousand people are buying a ticket at seventy-nine dollars and watching a team lose at home nine and eighteen. That bothers me more than the profit."

The division tells the story

Florida's record inside the Southeast is 4-6-2. The 4 wins came against Atlanta Thrashers, the team below it in the standings. Against Washington Capitals, the division leader, the Panthers are 0-3-0 and have scored 5 goals in 3 games. Against Carolina Hurricanes, which sits 3rd in the East on the strength of 11 overtime wins, Florida is 1-1-2 and has lost both overtime games by one goal. Against Tampa Bay Lightning, the Panthers have played 1 game and lost it.

The Panthers beat the club beneath them and lose to every club above, which is not a pattern that fixes itself by March.

The cost side

At $51.07M for 47 points, Florida pays $1.09M per point, the 6th-worst rate in the league. Only Detroit Red Wings, Philadelphia Flyers, Montreal Canadiens, New York Rangers and St. Louis Blues are worse on that line. The Panthers' revenue of $55.15M covers the payroll with $13.35M left over, so the franchise is profitable. The ticket price of $79 and the attendance of 15,051 produce that revenue, while the on-ice product produces nothing.

Van Ryswyk put it plainly: "I'd rather be in the standings and losing money." He also said the roster is healthy, which removes the usual excuse. "The healthiest team in the league is last in the division, so the injuries aren't the story, I'll give you that."

With 38 days to the deadline, what Florida shops is not a franchise player. The club's top scorer is Richard Zednik83 at 84 points, 19th in the league. He is 29, graded 77, and on a $51.07M payroll that is the best man available. The next name on the scoring list is not in the top 30. That gap behind Zednik shows up in the standings.

What shopping at 47 points means

Van Ryswyk said it himself on The Tunnel: "You're not shopping a franchise guy at forty-five points." He was slightly ahead of the standings, which now show 47, but the point holds. At this stage of the calendar, a team in 14th place is selling draft capital, selling rental contracts, and hoping the return is a young player who develops into something the 2005-06 roster needs.

Florida drew 15,051 a night last season and is drawing 15,051 a night this season. The attendance has not moved. That is the franchise's asset right now, the only number in the business table that the on-ice product does not contaminate. "It says people in this market are patient. I don't know how patient. Fifteen thousand for a team at seventeen and twenty-seven, that's not nothing. But patience has a shelf life and I don't know where ours runs out."

The Panthers play New York Rangers on February 12 at home, then Boston Bruins on February 14, then Washington Capitals on February 15. Three clubs above them in the conference. The home crowd will be there. The wins probably will not.
