Nashville leads the West on a $46.77M payroll, no 80-point man, and a goaltender graded 92

The Predators sit 1st in the conference at 81 points with their top scorer at 77. That number would not make the top 30 in hockey.

Howard BlaineSenior writer, The Blaine Rankings · The Hockey GazetteSaturday, 19 February 2005
ShareCopy link

The Nashville Predators Predators are first in the Western Conference at 81 points, six games ahead of any pursuit they face, and not one of their players appears among the top 30 scorers in the league. Their leading point-getter is David Legwand at 77 points in 58 games. The 30th name on the Art Ross board belongs to Simon Gagne84 of Philadelphia at 81.

Nashville pays $46.77M, which is 15th in the league on payroll and 5th-lightest in the conference. At $0.58M per point it produces the second-cheapest hockey in hockey, behind only Vancouver Canucks at $0.45M. The club cleared a $20.70M profit on $61.38M in revenue through 58 games. Its 15,644 fans pay $80 a ticket and watch the best home team in the conference: 22-8, a figure no Western club can match.

The construction

The scoring depth on the Nashville roster runs through the middle of the league on individual numbers and the top on team ones. Legwand at 77 is the highest. The sheet carries no other Nashville player inside the top 30, and the Predators' 207 goals for sit 4th in the conference. No club outside the top three on points scored carries a shallower top end.

In net, Tomas Vokoun90 has played 47 games and gone 31-16 with a 2.64 GAA and a 0.871 save percentage. The Bureau grades him 92, which is 3rd among goaltenders with 25-plus games, behind only Jean-Sebastien Giguere97 at 97 and Marty Turco93 at 96.

The Predators are a 92-goaltender club with four lines that produce in the aggregate, and the aggregate is enough to win the conference.

The division record

Inside the Central Division, Nashville is 9 regulation wins, 2 overtime wins, 2 regulation losses, and 2 overtime losses in 15 games, with 60 goals for and 45 against. The Predators are 3-0 with one overtime win against Detroit Red Wings, 3-0 with one overtime loss against St. Louis Blues, 2-1 with one overtime loss against Columbus Blue Jackets, and 1-1 with one overtime loss against Chicago Blackhawks. Against the bottom three clubs in their own division, they have lost in regulation once.

Their last 15 games read: W, W, W, W, W, W, L, L, W, L, W, W, W, W, L. Nine regulation wins, six consecutive currently, with 43 goals for and 29 against across the stretch. The six straight includes three 2-1 wins and a 4-0, closed by the goaltender and the defensive structure.

The road ahead

The Week Ahead presents the test of whether depth holds on a short trip. Nashville plays at Ottawa Senators on February 22, at Toronto Maple Leafs on February 23, and at Columbus Blue Jackets on February 25. Three road games in four nights, the second against the 1st-place club in the entire league at 94 points, the third against a club one point behind 8th in this conference with two games in hand over St. Louis Blues.

If the thesis that Vokoun and four capable lines carry a conference lead survives a back-to-back-to-back into the East and then a game against a desperate Columbus club, it will hold through April. If it cracks, the gap back to 2nd is not large enough to absorb it: Dallas Stars at 78 points and Vancouver Canucks and Edmonton Oilers both at 77 sit within four points, and all three have players whose names appear on the scoring board.

The prestige number

Nashville's prestige points sit at 680, the highest number in the league, ahead of Toronto at 563 and New Jersey at 638. The Predators have never won the Cup, have reached the conference final once (last spring, where they won 4 and lost 4 before the run ended), and carry a prestige score that reflects how the league's writers and voters regard the construction. The desk agrees: leading a 15-club conference without a top-30 scorer is a fact that the numbers behind it explain better than any Art Ross omission would suggest.
