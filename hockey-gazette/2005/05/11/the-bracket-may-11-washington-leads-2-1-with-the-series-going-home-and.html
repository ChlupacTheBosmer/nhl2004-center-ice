The Bracket, May 11: Washington leads 2-1 with the series going home, and Dallas holds a 3-0 lead on the road

Both conference finals turn tomorrow. The East shifts to Washington. The West shifts to Anaheim.

Howard BlaineSenior writer, The Blaine Rankings · The Hockey GazetteWednesday, 11 May 2005
ShareCopy link

Two series remain. Both play tomorrow. The East final stands at Washington 2, Toronto 1, with Game 4 at the MCI Center. The West final stands at Dallas 3, Anaheim 0, with Game 4 at the Arrowhead Pond.

The East Final: Washington 2, Toronto 1

Toronto came back on Tuesday night at Washington, 3-2 in overtime, to take the first game of this series from the Capitals. Roberto Luongo95 stopped 35 of 37 shots in the winner; the goal belonged to the road team in the sense that Washington scored first and Toronto scored last.

The series has gone the other way in every other respect. Washington won Game 1 at Toronto, 3-2 in overtime on May 6, then came back to the Air Canada Centre and shut out the defending champion 4-0 on May 8. Toronto holds 1 Stanley Cup, won last spring on this same roster; the Capitals hold none.

Olaf Kolzig89 carries 8 wins and 1 loss in this postseason, 1.48 goals against, a .937 save percentage, and 1 shutout. Jaromir Jagr91 leads all scorers with 18 points in 11 games. Pascal Dupuis80 has 14. The Capitals are 10-1 in the postseason after two sweeps and two wins in this round; Toronto has made one series last seven games and is now one loss away from being out.

Toronto's scoring this spring belongs to Gary Roberts85, who has 13 points in 15 games, and Mats Sundin92, 12 in 15. Marian Gaborik99 has 10 in 8 games, missing time that has not been recorded on the injury list.

The regular season record between these two clubs reads Toronto 4-2-1, but three of those seven games went to overtime or regulation with a one-goal margin. The postseason record between them says something else: 2-0 Washington, 1-0 Toronto.

Game 4 is tomorrow at Washington, where the Capitals went 29-12 this season.

The West Final: Dallas 3, Anaheim 0

Dallas scored five on Tuesday at Anaheim, its third win in three games, and has outscored the Ducks 12-4 across the series. Marty Turco97 has 11 wins in 13 postseason starts, a 1.49 goals-against average, and a .913 save percentage. Mike Modano88 leads the club with 18 points in 13 games; Brenden Morrow86 has 16.

Anaheim's postseason has been carried by Sergei Fedorov86, 35 years old, 16 points in 15 games. He is the only Duck in double digits. Jean-Sebastien Giguere93, whose 97 overall grade in the Bureau's Book is the highest of any goaltender in the league, has a .872 save percentage in this postseason, 7 wins and 6 losses. Against regular season marks of 0.877 and 2.44 goals against, these are a long way down. Dallas has scored on him at a rate that has made him look ordinary.

The regular season record between these clubs reads Dallas 6-1-1, with 29 goals for and 19 against. Five of those eight games were decided by one goal. None of the three postseason games has been close: 4-1, 3-2 in overtime, 5-1.

Game 4 is tomorrow at Anaheim.

Out

The 14 clubs eliminated: in the East, Boston (lost Round 1 to Tampa Bay), New York Islanders (Round 1 to Toronto), New Jersey (Round 1 to Pittsburgh), Carolina (Round 1 to Washington), Tampa Bay (Round 2 to Washington), Pittsburgh (Round 2 to Toronto). In the West, Nashville (Round 1 to Anaheim), Colorado (Round 1 to Phoenix), Detroit (Round 1 to Dallas), Vancouver (Round 1 to Edmonton), Edmonton (Round 2 to Anaheim), Phoenix (Round 2 to Dallas).

The Presidents' Trophy went to Dallas in the regular season with 109 points, and the Avalanche had 112. Both the first and second seeds in the West are gone; only the second remains. In the East, the first and fourth seeds play for the conference.

What tomorrow could do

Dallas leads 3-0. If Dallas wins Game 4, the series ends and Dallas plays in the Stanley Cup Final for the first time since the 2003-04 postseason, when it went 8-7 in 17 games before elimination. Anaheim has won two series this spring to reach this stage: over Nashville 4-2, over Edmonton 4-2.

Washington leads 2-1. The series is not decided. Toronto has the most points in the league (133), the best record (59-13-5), and one Cup. Washington has the best postseason goaltending of any remaining club and 18 points from a 33-year-old man who scored 145 in the regular season.
