The Bracket, May 31: Toronto takes a second straight Cup, and Phoenix's upset of the West's best team still has no headline

Every series from the spring's bracket, with the Cup decided and a first-round result from April that should have been a bigger story than it was

Howard BlaineSenior writer, The Blaine Rankings · The Hockey GazetteTuesday, 31 May 2005
ShareCopy link

Toronto Maple Leafs defeated Dallas Stars 4-2 in Game 6 on Monday to win the Stanley Cup 4-2, a second consecutive title and the only two Cups in Toronto's history.

Mats Sundin92 finished the postseason with 11 goals and 18 points in 25 games. Gary Roberts87, last spring's Conn Smythe winner, had 19 points on 7 goals and 12 assists. Roberto Luongo97 went 16-9 with a 1.81 goals-against average, a .943 save percentage and 3 shutouts across the run.

The Cup Final went: DAL 0-2 TOR, DAL 2-1 TOR, TOR 2-3 DAL (OT), TOR 3-1 DAL, DAL 0-2 TOR, TOR 4-2 DAL. Toronto outscored Dallas 14-8 over six games. Dallas entered the series holding the Presidents' Trophy at 109 points and a $67.99M payroll, the fourth-highest in the league, and had gone 2-5-0 against Toronto in the regular season while outscored 11-20.

Mike Modano84 led the Stars with 24 points in 21 playoff games. Brenden Morrow82 added 19. Marty Turco93 was 14-7 with a 1.65 GAA and a .921 save percentage.

Phoenix beat the 1 seed in the West and stopped there

The Western playoff field's 8th seed, Phoenix Coyotes, finished 40-31-9 at 93 points and met Colorado Avalanche, the top seed at 112 points and 310 goals for, in the first round. Phoenix won 4-3. The regular-season series produced nothing to forecast it: 11 meetings split 5-5-0 with one overtime game for each side, Colorado scoring 35 goals and allowing 30. Seven of those 11 games were decided by one goal.

Colorado carried Peter Forsberg95 (129 points), Milan Hejduk88 (124) and Teemu Selanne86 (104) in its top three. Phoenix had no player in the league's top 30 in scoring. Sean Burke86 went 29-34 with a 2.77 GAA on the season. The Coyotes then took Dallas to six games in the conference semifinal before going out, matching the second-round finish they posted last spring.

Phoenix spent $47.81M for 93 points, $0.51M per point, with the league's lowest average attendance at 12,112. The club went 19-22 on the road, the only team to reach a conference final this spring with a losing road record.

Lower seeds won 4 of 8 first-round series across both conferences. The Phoenix-Colorado result carried the largest point gap of the four: 19 points between the 1 and the 8.

The three cheapest teams in the league won one playoff series between them

Vancouver Canucks at $0.36M per point, Edmonton Oilers at $0.38M and New Jersey Devils at $0.40M were the three most cost-effective clubs in the 2004-05 regular season. Edmonton beat Vancouver Canucks in the first round; New Jersey Devils and Vancouver lost theirs, leaving a single series win among the three.

The Devils finished second in the East at 114 points on a $45.15M payroll and met the 7 seed Pittsburgh Penguins, 29 points behind, in Round 1. Pittsburgh won 4-2. Eight of 11 regular-season meetings between these clubs went to one goal, with New Jersey holding a 3-5-1 edge counting overtime wins. Five of six playoff games were decided by one goal. Martin Brodeur95 went 46-20 with a 2.42 GAA for the season and could not close out the series.

Pittsburgh Penguins carried the run to Toronto and took the Maple Leafs to seven games in Round 2 before falling.

Washington went 8-0, then led the champion 3-1

Washington Capitals swept Carolina Hurricanes 4-0 and Tampa Bay Lightning 4-0 through two rounds, outscoring both 27-11 across eight games. In the conference final they led Toronto Maple Leafs 2-0 and 3-1 before Toronto won three straight, including a 2-0 shutout in Game 7. Jaromir Jagr91 had 145 points in 82 regular-season games and 30 points in 13 playoff appearances.

General manager Shawn Holliday told The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk on May 17: "The 104 points, I think that tells you the group competed. We weren't good enough in the round after that, and that's on the roster I built. I own that part."
