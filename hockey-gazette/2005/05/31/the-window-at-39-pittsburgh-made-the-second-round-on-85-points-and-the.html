The Window at 39: Pittsburgh made the second round on 85 points, and the contract says how long that takes

The 7-seed Penguins knocked off a 114-point Devils team and pushed the eventual champion to 7 games, all on a roster whose center costs $9.90M for 4 more seasons.

Howard BlaineSenior writer, The Blaine Rankings · The Hockey GazetteTuesday, 31 May 2005
ShareCopy link

Pittsburgh Penguins finished 7th in the East on 85 points, 24 wins, 11 overtime losses, and won a playoff series. The Penguins beat the 2-seed New Jersey Devils, who finished with 114 points, in 6 games, then took Toronto Maple Leafs, the 1 seed and the eventual champion, to 7. That is the deepest run Pittsburgh has produced since the record opened, and every move the front office makes from here, the draft in 8 days and everything after it, belongs to a franchise that built its season around a man born on 1965-10-05.

Mario Lemieux85 played 73 games, scored 58 goals, added 59 assists, 117 points on 21.0 minutes a night. The pace over 82 reads 131. He is 39. The Bureau's Book grades him 88, which the Development Index puts 2 below his base of 92, and the potential line sits at 61. His contract on the record reads $9.90M for 4 more seasons. Pittsburgh's total payroll is $48.15M.

No Penguin outside Lemieux finished among the league's top 30 in scoring. The highest-rated man on the roster in the Book is Johan Hedberg88 at 91, a goaltender who started 63 games and carried 33 losses. The best forward is Martin Straka83 at 86. Behind them sit Aleksey Morozov78 at 79, Eric Meloche75 at 78, Mikael Renberg75 at 78. None of them puts up the kind of line that changes a series on its own. They are capable players who score 50 or 60 points and cannot sustain that for 82 games without the man at center.

The business column puts Pittsburgh 9th in the league in cost per point at $0.57M, behind Edmonton Oilers at $0.42M and Vancouver Canucks at $0.45M. Revenue reads $77.44M, profit $25.87M, attendance 13,990 a night at $79 a ticket. The gap between $48.15M in payroll and $77.44M in revenue is the room to improve, and the room is not large enough to add a true second option on top of $9.90M for a man who turns 40 on 2005-10-05.

The free-agent pool is empty by every account. Shawn Holliday of Washington told The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk this week that "there's nobody unsigned." The path to a better roster runs through the draft, 8 days away, or through trade. The trade option in Pittsburgh means shipping Straka or Morozov or Meloche for a package that brings a young body and a pick and almost certainly lowers the floor of a lineup that already lacks one.

The pipeline is one man, and he is 35th

The Bureau's under-23 rankings place 1 Penguin in the top 40: Tomas Surovy74, 23 years old, 30 games, 2 points, overall 77, 3 on the Index, 35th on the board. Pittsburgh does not carry a young player whose trajectory points toward a top-six role in 2 years, let alone a 100-point season by the time Lemieux's contract runs out.

Four more years at the same number

This desk has printed the view that the standings at Christmas are the standings in April often enough that a reader might expect one now. Pittsburgh made the second round because a 39-year-old center played 73 games at 117 points and carried a 7th seed through 13 playoff games. The same fact, that the team cannot win without him, is what the front office walks into on 2005-06-08.

A draft pick will not fix a roster whose best forward outside Lemieux rates 86. A trade of Straka brings back pieces that do not score 100 points. Running it back trusts that a Development Index that dropped 2 will not drop 6, that a potential of 61 will not prove right, and that 4 more years of $9.90M will produce what 4 years of $9.90M has always produced at this stage of a career.

Lemieux's playoff line this spring: 13 games, 3 goals, 8 assists, 11 points, 21.2 minutes a night. The series against the 1 seed went 7 games, and Toronto won the last one by 1 goal. The margin between a second-round exit and a trip to the conference final was one goal in one game. A roster that clears that margin needs someone beside Lemieux who scores 80 points, and that someone is not on the roster, not in the pool, and not in the top 40 of the under-23 list.
