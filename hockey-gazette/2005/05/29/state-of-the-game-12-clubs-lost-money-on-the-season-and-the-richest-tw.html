State of the Game: 12 clubs lost money on the season, and the richest two of them missed the playoffs

The payroll-to-point spread across 30 clubs runs from $0.36M to $0.93M per point, and the league's best customer service story belongs to a team nobody talks about

Howard BlaineSenior writer, The Blaine Rankings · The Hockey GazetteSunday, 29 May 2005
ShareCopy link

The 2004-05 regular season is finished for 28 of 30 clubs. Two are still playing for the Stanley Cup. The rest have 127 days until the season begins again, and their general managers have had a month to look at what the season cost them.

Vancouver Canucks spent $35.16M and won 99 points, the best ratio in the league at $0.36M per point. Edmonton Oilers spent $36.81M for 98 points at $0.38M. New Jersey Devils spent $45.15M for 114 points at $0.40M. Three clubs spent under $47M and produced 98 points or more. The cheapest roster in the league carried the fourth seed in the West and went to the playoffs for the third straight spring.

At the other end, New York Rangers spent $78.14M for 84 points, the worst rate in the league at $0.93M per point, and missed the playoffs by 1 point. Detroit Red Wings spent $80.89M, the highest payroll in the league, for 96 points at $0.84M and lost 4 games to Dallas Stars in the first round. Two of the three biggest cheques in the league bought nothing in April.

The profit line

Toronto Maple Leafs took in $125.89M in revenue against $60.04M in payroll, a profit of $53.43M. No club has ever turned a wider spread in this league's short record. The ticket is $107, the highest in the circuit, and the attendance is 17,713 per game, also the highest.

New Jersey Devils turned $45.96M on a payroll of $45.15M, a profit line nearly equal to their spending. Mighty Ducks of Anaheim did $45.21M against $41.01M in payroll. Both are clubs in smaller markets running tight operations that produce deep playoff runs: New Jersey Devils as the 2 seed, Mighty Ducks of Anaheim as the 6 seed that won eight postseason games before Dallas ended it.

Philadelphia Flyers spent $66.73M and cleared $2.14M. That is the smallest profit on the record, and it belongs to the club with the sixth-highest payroll. The Flyers missed the playoffs by 2 points. They spent more than Toronto Maple Leafs and won 51 fewer points.

Attendance and the gate

The attendance range is from 12,112 at Phoenix Coyotes to 17,713 at Toronto Maple Leafs. Phoenix Coyotes charged $80 per ticket, mid-table, and drew the smallest crowd in the league despite 93 points and a second-round appearance. New York Islanders drew 12,943 at $79, dead last in the East by attendance, and finished 84 points with a first-round exit.

The clubs that drew the largest crowds: Toronto Maple Leafs (17,713), New Jersey Devils (17,261), Colorado Avalanche (16,577), Carolina Hurricanes (16,311), Washington Capitals (16,203), Tampa Bay Lightning (16,191), Dallas Stars (16,067). All seven made the playoffs. The building fills when the team wins.

The room for spending

Olaf Kolzig89's club at Washington Capitals sits at $55.92M with $28.91M in profit. Shawn Holliday, the Washington general manager, said on The Tunnel this week that the building can carry more: "When I look at the payroll, $55.92M, and I look at the profit line, I know I've got room. I know the building can carry more." He named no target, but the arithmetic is visible on the page. Washington scored 287 goals, the seventh-highest total in the league, and lost in the conference final. The gap between 104 points and the Cup is a roster-construction problem, and Holliday knows it.

Colorado Avalanche spent $71.56M, the third-highest payroll, and cleared $20.90M on $100.00M in revenue. They scored 310 goals in the regular season, the most in the league, and lost to Phoenix Coyotes in seven in the first round. The money bought a Presidents' Trophy race they lost by 3 points and an early exit.

Detroit Red Wings at $80.89M and New York Rangers at $78.14M both carry "n/a" in the profit column. They are spending more than the league can see them earning back. The two most expensive clubs in the circuit were a first-round sweep and a one-point miss. Money does not move a club past the first round, and the clubs that prove it cheapest are the ones nobody writes about in the spring.

Where the margins are worst

Six clubs turned profits under $15M: Philadelphia Flyers ($2.14M), St. Louis Blues ($11.18M), New York Islanders ($12.46M), Los Angeles Kings ($16.34M), Columbus Blue Jackets ($14.42M), Calgary Flames ($14.43M). Five of them missed the playoffs. The exception, St. Louis Blues, finished 9th in the West by 7 points.

The draft is 10 days away. Whatever these clubs pick will be the first decision made with a full season of this arithmetic in front of them.
