The Bracket, May 29: Toronto leads the Cup 3-2, and Game 6 is at home tomorrow

Two games in four days have put the Maple Leafs back in front, and a shutout in Dallas on Saturday was the one that changed the feel of this series

Howard BlaineSenior writer, The Blaine Rankings · The Hockey GazetteSunday, 29 May 2005
ShareCopy link

Toronto leads the Stanley Cup Final 3 games to 2. Game 6 is at the Air Canada Centre on 2005-05-30, and the schedule lists one more game, at Dallas on 2005-06-01.

The last four days produced two results that swung this series back to the home side. Game 4 on 2005-05-26 went to Toronto 3-1 in front of their own crowd, the first time this series had a winner in regulation with multiple goals either side. Game 5 on 2005-05-28 went to Toronto again, 2-0 in Dallas, a road shutout that took the lead back from a club that had held it after three games.

Roberto Luongo97 made 30 saves in Game 5 and carried his playoff line to 15-9, 1.81 GAA, .944, 3 shutouts. That third blank came on the road against a Dallas club that scored 279 goals in the regular season, the second-highest total in the West. Luongo's counterpart Marty Turco93 sits at 14-6 with a 1.54 GAA and .924, a better number in two fewer starts. Neither man has been beaten badly, and the close scores reflect that.

The scoring

Toronto's depth has spread the load across four lines. Mats Sundin92 leads the club with 18 points (11+7) in 24 games, Gary Roberts87 has 16 (5+11), and Alexander Mogilny89 has 15 (7+8). The 19-year-old Petr Chlup87 carries 10 points in 24 games at 16.9 minutes a night, which is the most ice time any teenager has received in this postseason.

Dallas has its own answer. Mike Modano84 is the leading scorer of this entire postseason with 23 points (10+13) in 20 games. Brenden Morrow82 has 19 (5+14) and Sergei Zubov80 has 15 (5+10). But 23 points from Modano and 19 from Morrow have not been enough against a Toronto defence that has allowed 44 goals in 24 games, the fewest of any club still standing.

Gaborik's ankle

Marian Gaborik99 is out with an ankle and his return date is 2005-06-02, one day after the last game the schedule currently lists. Whether Toronto needs him depends on what happens tomorrow and possibly Monday, but the roster has played on without their highest-rated player all postseason, and the numbers have not collapsed. The club scored 54 goals in these 24 games without him.

What each series became

Every other series is over. In the East, Washington Capitals went 8-0 through two rounds before Toronto ended it in seven, the only team to take the Maple Leafs past six games in this postseason. Pittsburgh Penguins beat New Jersey Devils in six, then pushed Toronto to seven before losing Game 7 at home. Tampa Bay Lightning beat Boston Bruins in six before Washington swept them in four.

In the West, Mighty Ducks of Anaheim beat Nashville Predators in six and Edmonton Oilers in six before Dallas closed them out in five. Phoenix Coyotes ended Colorado Avalanche's 112-point season in seven games in the first round, then lost to Dallas in six. Detroit Red Wings was swept by Dallas in four.

The regular season head-to-head

Toronto Maple Leafs went 4-1-1 against Dallas Stars in the regular season, outscoring them 16-9 on the season series. Two of those six meetings were one-goal games. The only loss in regulation was 1-3 on the road. The series the two clubs split before the spring looks almost nothing like the five-game chess match they have built in the Final, where four of five games have been decided by one or two goals.

The clubs eliminated across both conferences: Boston Bruins, New York Islanders, Carolina Hurricanes, Pittsburgh Penguins, New Jersey Devils, Tampa Bay Lightning, Washington Capitals, Colorado Avalanche, Phoenix Coyotes, Detroit Red Wings, Vancouver Canucks, Edmonton Oilers, Nashville Predators, Mighty Ducks of Anaheim. Fourteen clubs out, two left, one game scheduled inside 24 hours.

Toronto has won the Cup once. Dallas has not won it at all. The home crowd tomorrow night is the only advantage in the building.
