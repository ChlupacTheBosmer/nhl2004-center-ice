The Bracket, May 21: Every series is over but one, and the 1 seed leads the Cup Final on the road

Toronto won Game 1, 2-0 at Dallas. The rest of the field is home.

Howard BlaineSenior writer, The Blaine Rankings · The Hockey GazetteSaturday, 21 May 2005
ShareCopy link

The Cup Final is live and nothing else is. Toronto Maple Leafs leads Dallas Stars 1-0 with Game 2 on Sunday at the American Airlines Center, and across both conferences every other series has its winner and its loser.

Game 1 on Friday was a 2-0 shutout at Dallas. Roberto Luongo97 stopped everything in front of him, and Toronto came into the Final with a 2-0-0 regular-season record against Dallas, 8-3 on goals. The 1 seed from the East opened the Cup on the road and took the first one without letting Dallas score.

The bracket below carries every series from the first round through the Final, with the score, the last game, and what each pairing became.

East: three rounds of survival

Toronto Maple Leafs won 4-1 over the 8-seed New York Islanders, 4-3 over the 7-seed Pittsburgh Penguins, and 4-3 over the 4-seed Washington Capitals. Two of three series went to Game 7. The 1 seed with 133 points and the best record in the league spent 20 games getting here, and at no point led a series by more than two games.

Washington Capitals beat Carolina Hurricanes 4-0 and then swept Tampa Bay Lightning 4-0 before losing to Toronto in seven, including a 2-0 loss in the clincher. Olaf Kolzig91's club won 12 straight from 2005-04-25 through 2005-05-12 before the last four games of the East Final went 1-2-1. Shawn Holliday told The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk after the Washington series ended: "The 104 points, I think that tells you the group competed. We weren't good enough in the round after that, and that's on the roster I built."

Pittsburgh Penguins took the 2-seed New Jersey Devils in six games, losing the last one in overtime, and then took Toronto to seven in the second round before falling 3-2 at home on 2005-05-04. Tampa Bay Lightning beat Boston Bruins in six, then went home 0-4 against Washington.

West: a smoother road, until the Final

Dallas Stars beat Detroit Red Wings 4-0, Phoenix Coyotes 4-2, and Mighty Ducks of Anaheim 4-1. The Presidents' Trophy winner spent 16 games getting to the Final, with 44 goals for and 26 against. Marty Turco95 carries a 12-4 record and a 1.51 GAA through the postseason.

Mighty Ducks of Anaheim beat the 3-seed Nashville Predators in six and the 5-seed Edmonton Oilers in six before Dallas closed the West Final 4-1. Jean-Sebastien Giguere97's club won 5 of 7 games against Dallas in the regular season, including overtime results, and managed 3 wins in the Final itself.

Phoenix Coyotes took the 1-seed Colorado Avalanche in seven in the first round, a result that ended Colorado's 112-point season, and then lost to Dallas in six. Edmonton Oilers beat Vancouver Canucks in six before losing to Anaheim.

The schedule puts Game 2 and Game 3 in Dallas, with Games 4 and 5 moving to Toronto. The regular-season head-to-head was two games, both won by Toronto, neither decided by more than 2 goals. Dallas has lost 3 in 16 postseason games. Toronto has lost 7 in 20.

Both clubs won 39 games in regulation during the regular season. Toronto brings 133 points to Dallas's 109, a payroll of $60.04M against $67.99M, and the record of the two games they played against each other. Toronto is one game into a series it is leading, on the road, having just shut out a club that scored 279 goals in 82 games. Dallas has not lost at home in this postseason except for Game 1.
