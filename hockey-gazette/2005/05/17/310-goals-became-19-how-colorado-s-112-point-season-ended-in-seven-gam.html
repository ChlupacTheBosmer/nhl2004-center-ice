310 goals became 19: how Colorado's 112-point season ended in seven games

The Cup finalists from last spring had the West's best offense and the league's best-rated player. They went home in the first round to the 8th seed.

Howard BlaineSenior writer, The Blaine Rankings · The Hockey GazetteTuesday, 17 May 2005
ShareCopy link

Colorado Avalanche scored 310 goals this season, more than any other club in the West. Peter Forsberg89 graded 95 in the League Scouting Bureau's Book, the highest mark of any player in the league. Milan Hejduk82 had 124 points in 82 games. Teemu Selanne80 had 104. The club finished first in the Northwest, first in the West, at 112 points.

They lost in the first round to the eighth seed, Phoenix Coyotes, in seven games. The gap between them was 19 points. In seven playoff games Colorado scored 19 goals and allowed 19.

The 19-point deficit between seeds 1 and 8 in the West was not the widest upset of Round 1: New Jersey lost to Pittsburgh from a 29-point position. But the context at Colorado is different. This club went 13-8 in 24 games last spring, winning the Clarence S. Campbell Bowl and logging the most playoff games in the league, before falling to Toronto in the Cup Final. They went from the last two standing to the first round gone in a single summer, against a club that won 40 games and 93 points, 11 fewer wins than Colorado's 43.

Colorado Avalanche's offense was not shut down in the traditional sense. They scored in every game, putting up 5 in Game 4 and 3 in each of Games 2, 3, 6, and 7. The problem was that Phoenix scored 2 or 3 in every game except one, and in the seven games that decided the series the sum came out even, 19 to 19. Phoenix simply won more of the close ones. Five of seven games were decided by one goal. Colorado won two of those five. Phoenix won three.

The goaltending split

Jamie Storr77 was Colorado's starter all season: 42 wins, 25 losses, a 2.77 GAA, an .863 save percentage, and an overall grade of 84. Sean Burke88 in Phoenix's net had 29 wins, 34 losses, the same 2.77 GAA, an .863 save percentage, and an overall grade of 88. The grades were close. The results were not.

Storr conceded 19 goals in the series across 7 games. That is nearly identical to his regular-season mark of 2.77. The Colorado defense held, but the offense could not outrun a club that matched them goal for goal; over seven games against a team that scored in every game, the margin came to one.

Phoenix Coyotes went into the series as the lower seed, with a payroll of $47.81M against Colorado's $71.56M. They cost per point $0.51M against Colorado's $0.64M. The business sheet does not determine who wins a seven-game series, but it tells you the margin was never supposed to be thin. Colorado had $36M more in payroll, two players in the league's top 12 in scoring, and the highest-rated player in the Book. Phoenix had Sean Burke at 88 and 40 wins.

The score sheet

The series was decided by the road team winning more often than the home team. Phoenix won Games 1 and 5 in Colorado, and Game 7 at Colorado. They lost at home in Games 4 and 6. The 4-3 result came from a 3-2 road record balanced by a 1-2 home record.

Colorado's playoff journey, at 2 wins and 4 losses in 7 games, is the shortest run of any club that made the postseason last spring with a deeper result. They played 24 games in the 2003-04 playoffs. This spring, seven.

The 112 points are on the record. The 310 goals are on the record. Forsberg's 129 points at 21.4 minutes a night, Hejduk's 124 at 21.8 minutes, Selanne's 104 at 18.2 minutes. None of it converts into a second-round appearance.

The offseason question is whether a club that spent $71.56M and reached the Final last spring has the appetite for another push, or whether the gap between 43 wins and a first-round exit produces a sell. The payroll sheet has them at $71.56M, third highest in the league, and the prestige points at 498, behind only Toronto, New Jersey, Minnesota, and Edmonton. Nobody at 498 prestige points is rebuilding on a $71M budget, though nobody expected seven games either.
