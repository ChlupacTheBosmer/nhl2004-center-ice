The Opening Ledger: New York's $92.95M leads the league, and its 2-0 start runs on a 32, a 33 and a 34

The Rangers missed the playoffs last spring on the biggest payroll in hockey. Two games in, the top of their scoring column hasn't moved.

Howard BlaineSenior writer, The Blaine Rankings · The Hockey GazetteWednesday, 12 October 2005
ShareCopy link

The New York Rangers carry the league's largest payroll at $92.95M. They carried it last season too, and it bought them nothing: the club finished outside the postseason, recorded a playoff run of 0, and sat at 526 prestige points in a league where the Stanley Cup champion Toronto has 1092.

Two games into the 2005-06 season, New York leads the Atlantic Division with 4 points on two wins. The top of its scoring column belongs to Alexei Kovalev86, age 32, with 3 points in 2 games; Pavel Bure84, age 34, with 2 goals; and Petr Nedved84, age 33, with 2 goals. No Ranger under 30 appears anywhere in the top 30 on the scoring chart.

The contracts walk

The roster carries its own clock. From the salary data the desk pulled, seven players on one-year deals cost the Rangers a large share of that $92.95M: Kovalev at $8.57M, Brian Leetch at $8.20M, Nedved at $4.60M, Eric Lindros at $4.50M, Tom Poti at $4.20M, goaltender Mike Dunham at $3.10M, and Jussi Nurminen at $1.11M. Each of those contracts expires at the end of this season.

Bure is the exception: $9.70M with 3 years remaining, the longest single commitment on the books. Bobby Holik follows at $9.00M with 2 years. Anson Carter holds 2 years at $6.84M. Everything else on the top of the roster is shorter.

Dunham is 87 overall, the highest-rated player on the squad, on one year at $3.10M. Behind him sits Dan Blackburn83, 22 years old, 83 overall, on a contract with 0 years remaining. If Dunham leaves, the Rangers have a young backup with an expiring deal. If he stays, they pay $3.10M for a netminder already past 30.

What the spending bought last time

The 2003-04 record shows what $92.95M of roster did when the season ended. New York is absent from the playoff table entirely: no games played, no wins recorded. The club's attendance of 13,831 per game and ticket price of $82 kept the revenue line at $31.23M and the profit at $21.41M, numbers the league's accountants are satisfied with regardless of what the standings say.

Prestige points, which the Bureau tracks as a measure of a club's standing in the league's history and culture, put New York at 526. That is below the league median. Edmonton has 1024 on a $42.61M payroll. Atlanta, the league's cheapest club at $39.32M, sits at 547. The Rangers spend more than any of them and hold less institutional capital than most.

The East, at two games

The Atlantic Division table after one week is noise. Four clubs have 2 points, one has 4, and the gap between first and ninth is 5 points. But the Rangers are the club with the most money and the least recent success trying to close that gap first. Their schedule over the next seven days includes games at Pittsburgh Penguins on the 12th, home against Toronto Maple Leafs on the 15th, at Buffalo Sabres on the 17th, and home against Nashville Predators on the 19th.

The Toronto game on the 15th is the one that matters for the narrative if not the standings. The Maple Leafs have the Presidents' Trophy, the Stanley Cup, and the second-largest payroll at $89.94M. They also have Marian Gaborik99 at 99 overall, the best-rated player in the league, and Rick Nash85 at 85, and Rostislav Klesla87 at 87 on the blue line. Toronto's under-23 list in the Bureau's development table reads like a depth chart for the next decade. New York's youngest regular is Blackburn in the crease.

What the desk logged

This column has a 23-21 record on settled predictions for the 2005-06 cycle, and one of those calls concerned Pittsburgh, not New York. The desk has no open prediction on the Rangers. That changes here.

The question the sheet poses is not whether New York can win games in October. It is whether a club with seven one-year contracts, three regular scorers over 32, and a 0-and-0 playoff record from last spring can be the same club in April. The trade deadline the calendar has marked falls on 2006-03-19, 158 days from now. A team 2-0 and out of contention at that point has a different set of options than a team in the playoff picture.

Two wins set the question without answering it.
