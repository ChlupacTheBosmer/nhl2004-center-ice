Washington has 17 road wins, the most in the league, and plays .440 hockey at home

Among every club with 60 points this season, the Capitals are the only one that cannot win in front of its own crowd

Howard BlaineSenior writer, The Blaine Rankings · The Hockey GazetteMonday, 31 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Olaf Kolzig93 has 25 wins and 22 losses. That is the most contradictory line a goaltender can put on a sheet in January, and it belongs to the club that wins on the road and loses at home.

Washington Capitals has 62 points, 5th in the East, and a goal differential of 24 that sits 4th in the conference. The construction of that record is what makes it unusual: 17 regulation wins on the road, tied with Toronto Maple Leafs for the league lead, and 11 at home, which is fewer wins in its own building than any other team above the 60-point line.

Every club in the table above has 60 points or more. Twelve of the 13 have more wins than losses at home. Washington's 11-14 is the only losing record among them, against a best of 23-4 in Toronto and a next-worst of 14-12 in Colorado. The Capitals have built a playoff-caliber standing out of away hockey, and the league's points system does not care where the wins come from.

The explanations on the sheet are thin. The attendance is 15,974 per night, 8th in the league, and the ticket price is $78, which is in the middle of the pack. The payroll is $57.88M, 7th in the league. Revenue of $58.56M against that payroll leaves a profit of $14.97M. The building is full, the money works, and the team loses more than half its games inside it.

The road construction

The away record tells you something specific about how this team operates. Seventeen road wins in 27 games is a .630 winning percentage. Jaromir Jagr90 has 97 points in 52 games at a 153 pace, which is 3rd in the league, and the Capitals' 196 goals for is 5th in the conference. When Washington plays open hockey away from home, it scores enough to win.

At home, the same team has scored and allowed at rates that produce an 11-14 record. The 24-goal differential is real but compressed: it comes almost entirely from the road, where the games are looser and Jagr has room to operate. In a building where teams park the bus and play in front of their goaltender, the Capitals' 45 goals become harder to generate.

The standings give a point for an overtime loss and a point for a regulation win, and Washington has harvested both on the road at a high rate. The 62 points are as legitimate as the +140 differential Toronto Maple Leafs has produced through 52 games. The question for February is whether a team that wins on the road can close out a playoff spot when the schedule tilts toward home ice.

The week ahead

The Capitals play at Tampa Bay Lightning on February 4, a game where their road form should travel well, and at TB they sit 1-2-0 in the season series, trailing in goals 12-11. Then two at home: New Jersey Devils on February 5 and New York Islanders on February 7. New Jersey is 4th in the conference at 66 points with Martin Brodeur93 in net, and the Islanders are 8th at 55, fighting to stay within reach.

If Washington wins on the road at Tampa and then drops both home games, it will have proven the pattern again. If it wins both at home, the pattern breaks, and a 66-point standing comes into view with 28 games still to play.

Jagr at 32 is 97 points into a season that has him at a 153 pace. That is the best offensive production of any Washington player since the record began, and no other Capital appears in the top 30 in scoring. Jagr is carrying an entire conference playoff bid on the road because the home building will not help him.
