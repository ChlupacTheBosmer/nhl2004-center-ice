Mario Lemieux is 39 and second in the league in points per game, and Pittsburgh has 33 points

The Book has him at 86 on a base of 92. His potential is 59. He is scoring better per shift than he did last season, and the room around him will not get better.

Howard BlaineSenior writer, The Blaine Rankings · The Hockey GazetteTuesday, 4 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Mario Lemieux85 has 66 points in 32 games, a 2.06 per-game average that sits behind only Jaromir Jagr90's 170-point pace among all players with 25 games or more. He is 39 years old. He is on a contract worth $9.90M with 5 years left, and Pittsburgh Penguins has 33 points in 38 games, last in the Atlantic Division, 5 points behind New York Islanders and 19 behind New York Rangers.

The Bureau's Development Index has him down 1 on the season. His base grade is 92; the current overall is 86. The potential number is 59, which means the Bureau does not believe there is more to extract. What there is, he is producing now, and perhaps more of it than he managed last season, when he played 82 games and finished with 150 points at 1.83 per game. This year the number is 2.06.

The player is getting better per shift while the team is getting worse around him. The 19.9 minutes a night he plays this year sit below the 21.8 he logged last season, and yet his per-game output is higher by a full fifth of a point. The legs are shorter, the pace is the same, the passing grade at 97 is the highest on the roster by any player at any position.

Lemieux's $9.90M is 22.8 percent of Pittsburgh Penguins's $43.35M payroll. He accounts for 22.7 percent of the team's 141 goals. The gap between those two numbers is almost nothing: the club's most expensive asset is producing goals at exactly the rate his salary demands and not one percentage point more. The rest of the roster produces the other 77 percent of the goals for 77 percent of the salary. That is why the club has 33 points.

The next man up is Martin Straka83, at 2.00 points per game in 26 games played. After Straka it falls to Ramzi Abid80 at 1.29 in 38. The third-best scorer on this team is a 26-year-old left wing on a minimum-salary contract.

The body keeps talking

The injury record lists 4 separate absences between December 8 and January 4. None lasted more than 3 days. The League Scouting Bureau does not record the cause, but the frequency tells you what you need: at 39, with a 50 in fighting and a 52 in shooting from the pocket, the body is withdrawing from contact and asking for rest in small installments. The 19.9 minutes a night reflect a coach managing the load, and even that is more than he played last season.

What the record does not show is the last game. Lemieux last carried the league's form marker between January 1 and January 4, which is not an injury. He returned. He has been in the lineup since.

What he told them in 1984

When the Penguins held the first overall pick in the 1984 draft and Lemieux refused to shake general manager Eddie Johnston's hand, refused to put on the sweater, refused to smile for the camera, he said that Pittsburgh didn't want him bad enough. Over 3,000 people watched the broadcast in the Civic Arena that night, a building which seated roughly 16,000 and typically drew fewer than 7,000 on a game night.

Twenty-one years later the Civic Arena is gone and the Penguins draw 14,097 a night at $79 a ticket. The attendance record is not the problem. The problem is that a club with that market size and a 39-year-old at center has lost 19 regulation games in 38 and allowed 171 goals. The building fills. The team does not win.

The Hart question nobody is asking

At a 169-point pace, Lemieux sits 1 point of pace behind Jagr's 170 and 1 ahead of Bill Guerin's 168. His team has 33 points. Guerin's has 55. Jagr's has 47. The Hart went to Ryan Smyth82 last season on a Edmonton Oilers club that finished with 97 points. The voters reward teams that are in the race. Lemieux is 39, playing 19.9 minutes a night, carrying 22.7 percent of a club that loses more than it wins, and producing the second-best per-game numbers in the league. The award belongs to someone with better company.

What belongs to him is the fact itself: 66 points in 32 games on a man with a 50 in fighting and a 97 in passing, at an age when most of his contemporaries are retired or on third lines. He was drafted first overall in 1984. The contract that night was $600,000 for two years plus a $150,000 bonus. The contract tonight is $9.90M per year with 5 left. The Civic Arena drew 7,000. The current arena draws 14,097. Everything around the man has changed except the production.

The standings say Pittsburgh Penguins will miss the playoffs. The Development Index says Lemieux will not get better. None of that changes the 2.06: a 39-year-old playing the second-best hockey in the league on a team that cannot win, with the rest of the league knowing that the only thing between Pittsburgh Penguins and 4 goals a night is a man with a $9.90M contract and no help.

Correction. An earlier edition of this piece read the league's roster designation as an injury. It is not one: the marker carried here is a form or fitness icon, and the men named were available. Only a designation that names a body part is an injury. The passage has been corrected.
