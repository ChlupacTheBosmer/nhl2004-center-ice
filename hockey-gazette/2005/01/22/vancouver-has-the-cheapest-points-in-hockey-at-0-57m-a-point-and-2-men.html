Vancouver has the cheapest points in hockey at $0.57M a point, and 2 men have scored 82 of the team's 187 goals

The Canucks sit 3rd in the West on the second-cheapest payroll in the league, but the goal distribution tells you what the standings cannot: this is a two-man operation.

Howard BlaineSenior writer, The Blaine Rankings · The Hockey GazetteSaturday, 22 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Vancouver Canucks has 61 points in 47 games, the third-best record in the Western Conference, on a payroll of $34.94M. That is $0.57M per point, the cheapest rate in the league by a clear margin. Minnesota Wild below them in the payroll column spends $29.97M and gets 43 points, a rate of $0.70M. Vancouver is the most efficient hockey operation in the league, and also the most fragile, because 2 players account for more than 40% of every goal the team scores.

Markus Naslund89 has 49 goals and 92 points in 47 games, good for a pace of 161 and second place in the Art Ross race. Todd Bertuzzi93 has 33 goals and 84 points, a pace of 147 and ninth place in the same race. Between them they have put 82 of the club's 187 goals on the scoresheet. The third-highest scorer on the roster is Trevor Linden with 53 points in 47 games, a gap of 31 points below Bertuzzi. The fourth is Brandon Reid at 47.

In the Western Conference, where Edmonton Oilers has 64 points and Colorado Avalanche has 59 chasing the same division, Vancouver holds 3rd place on the strength of two men who are 31 and 29. No other player on the roster appears in the Bureau's top 30 scorers. No Vancouver player appears anywhere on the Bureau's top-40 list of under-23s, a distinction shared only by a handful of clubs in this league.

The goaltender is graded 82 and the team is 61 points

Dan Cloutier85 has played 36 games, won 20, carried a 3.32 goals-against average and a .849 save percentage, and sits in the Book at 82. That is the lowest overall grade for any goaltender who has started at least 30 games and played for a club with more than 55 points this season. The Predators have a 90-goaltender on the 16th payroll. The New Jersey Devils have a 93 on the 22nd payroll. Vancouver has an 82 behind a 2nd-cheapest payroll, and it is holding because Naslund and Bertuzzi outscore the opposition before the goaltending deficit catches up.

The season series against division rivals tells the rest. Vancouver is 3-0-1 against Minnesota, 3-1-0 against Calgary Flames, and 2-0-1 against the division-leading Oilers. Against Colorado Avalanche, the 5th-place club in the conference with Peter Forsberg95 and Milan Hejduk89, Vancouver is 1-3-0. The Canucks beat everyone they are supposed to beat and lose to the one team in the Northwest that has depth beyond its top two. Colorado has 3 players in the top 30. Vancouver has 2.

The financial model works until it does not

Vancouver Canucks generated $55.95M in revenue and kept $28.37M in profit, the 7th-best profit margin in the league. Attendance sits at 14,816 per game at $78 a ticket. The organization is making money while winning, and the per-point rate of $0.57M is what a front office points to when it says it is building smart.

Building implies a structure that outlasts the men who put it up. Naslund is 31 and carrying a 161-point pace, which is career-best production on the back of a 133-point season last year. Bertuzzi is 29 and carrying a pace of 147, up from 101 points in 63 games last season. Both are in their best year, possibly their two best years, happening simultaneously on the same roster. Neither will produce at this rate at 35 and 33. The goaltender is 29 and graded 82, which is not a number that improves into a Vezina. And behind the top two, the next man is Linden at 53 points, a journeyman center on a one-year contract who was picked 14th overall in 1988 and will not get younger.

The Bureau's Development Index carries no Vancouver player in its top 40 under-23 list. The Junior Bureau has not supplied a name at this club that the desk would put on a Calder ballot. The one after that is not on any list the Bureau publishes.

What the standings say at Christmas is not always what they say in April

I have written elsewhere that the standings at Christmas are the standings in April, and I still believe it for most clubs. Vancouver is the exception. The Canucks are 3rd in the West with a 11-point cushion over the 8th-place Columbus Blue Jackets club and 35 games left, and the odds they miss the postseason are low.

The question for April is whether a team that scores 82 of its goals from two players can keep those two healthy for 14 more games in the regular season and then whatever the playoff format requires on top. A 3.32-goaltender with a grade of 82 is a 3.32-goaltender in a first-round series against a team with depth. Colorado already leads the season series 3-1. If the bracket puts those two together, Vancouver has no answer for the third and fourth lines Colorado can field.

The cheapest points in hockey are also the points most likely to evaporate in April, because they belong to 2 men who cannot be everywhere for 82 games and then 16 more.
