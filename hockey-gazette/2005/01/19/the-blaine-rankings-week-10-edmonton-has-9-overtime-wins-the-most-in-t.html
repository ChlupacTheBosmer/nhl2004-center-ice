The Blaine Rankings, Week 10: Edmonton has 9 overtime wins, the most in the league, and a minus-4 goal differential

The Oilers are 61 points on $0.60M per point, the second-cheapest rate in hockey, and the man carrying them earns $1.00M on a line the Bureau graded 9 below his base.

Howard BlaineSenior writer, The Blaine Rankings · The Hockey GazetteWednesday, 19 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Edmonton sits 4th in the West with 61 points, the same total as Nashville Predators and Vancouver Canucks, and a goal differential of minus 4. The other two 61-point clubs have differentials of plus 24 and plus 23. The Oilers are 171 for, 175 against, and they are winning games they have not deserved to win at a rate no other club in this league matches.

The mechanism is overtime. Edmonton has 9 overtime wins, the most in the league. The next-highest total belongs to Carolina at 8, then Columbus at 7. In 46 games the Oilers have gone to overtime 12 times and won 9 of them, a .750 overtime record in a league where no other club has converted at better than .571. It is the main reason a team with a negative goal differential sits 15 points clear of the 8th seed.

The standings at Christmas diverge from the standings in April. A team that wins its overtime games at a .750 clip is running hot, and that kind of run does not hold. If Edmonton loses even 4 of those 9 overtime wins, it drops to 57, where Colorado Avalanche, Washington Capitals, New York Rangers and Carolina Hurricanes all sit. They are still in the playoff picture, just not comfortable.

The payroll tells the rest. Edmonton spends $36.81M for those 61 points, $0.60M per point, the second-cheapest rate in the league behind Vancouver Canucks at $0.57M. The Oilers carry the 4th-lowest payroll in a 30-club league and sit 4th in the West. You can build a case that this is the most efficient roster in hockey. You can also build a case that it is the thinnest.

Comrie is the point

Mike Comrie82 has 70 points in 46 games, a pace of 124.8, on a contract of $1.00M with 1 year left. He is 24. The Book has him at 78 overall, against a base grade of 87, with 0 on the Development Index. That is 9 below his base. A player whose production holds while his grade falls is a player whose underlying game has thinned even as the counter still runs.

Last season Comrie scored 133 points in 75 games, after holding out for more than 30 games in a contract dispute that kept him out of the Oilers' training camp and well into the schedule. This season's 124.8 is a decline, and the Book says the decline is structural, not a slump.

He is also, at 70 points on $1.00M, the cheapest production in the league. No other player in the top 30 scorers earns less than $1.10M. The roster around him is what a $36.81M payroll builds. Tommy Salo85 is the best player on the club at 82 overall, a goaltender with a 3.35 GAA who has won 23 games. Ryan Smyth84 is 79 overall and 10 below his own base, the desk wrote two weeks ago, and he is second on the team with 66 points. The third-best player is Mike York80 at 76. There is no one graded above 82 on the entire roster.

What holds and what breaks

The 9 overtime wins are the number the desk is watching. The Bureau has no grade for them and no coach can install them; they are 12 games that went Edmonton's way 9 times, and in the 36 remaining the probability the Oilers repeat that clip is low. The desk would not be surprised to see them win fewer than half their remaining overtime games, and each loss costs a full point the standings currently credit as two.

What holds is Comrie's contract. At $1.00M with 1 year left, he is entering the market at 25 on a pace that, even regressed, makes him one of the more attractive centers available. The desk logged no prediction on this player before today. It logs one now: Edmonton will not re-sign Comrie at a number that matches his production, and the Oilers will trade him at what the calendar has as the trade deadline or lose him in July.

The Blaine Rankings put Edmonton 6th in the West for Week 10, not 4th. The standings say 4th. The desk's position since December has been that the standings in April are not the standings in January, and here is the first week where the two disagree. The desk picks the April number. The overtime wins will cool. The payroll will not grow. The grade cliff below Comrie is as steep as the one in Atlanta.
