Geoff Sanderson is 5th in the league, graded 8 below his base, and the best player on his team is a 22-year-old goaltender

The Columbus captain has 84 points in 43 games on a roster where nobody else is graded above 78. The Blue Jackets are even in goal differential.

Howard BlaineSenior writer, The Blaine Rankings · The Hockey GazetteFriday, 14 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Geoff Sanderson83 sits 5th in the league with 84 points in 43 games. His pace, 160, would put him at 160 by April if he does not slow. The Book grades him at 79, which is 8 below his base of 87. His potential is 59. He is 32 years old.

The team around him is the problem. The best skater in the Columbus room by the Bureau's overall grade is Todd Marchant82 at 78. David Vyborny78 is 74. The two goaltenders, Pascal Leclaire86 and Marc Denis86, are both 82. The Blue Jackets' top three players by the Book are two goaltenders and a 32-year-old left winger from Hay River, Northwest Territories, who was drafted 36th overall in 1990 and has played for six clubs since.

They are 15-19-2, with 46 points and an even goal differential on 142 for and 142 against. They spend $1.06M per point, which puts them 13th in the league by that measure, ahead of clubs like Detroit and Philadelphia and behind everyone in a playoff race.

The arithmetic is grim. Marchant has 55 points. Vyborny has 54. The third line, the fourth line, the defense: none of them are graded above 74. Sanderson is not carrying Columbus on his back, but he is the only man on the roster the Bureau thinks can score 50 goals, and he is doing it on a grade that says he is a below-average player relative to himself.

The Development Index has him 8 below his base, which means the Bureau thinks the form has slipped from the tools. His speed grade is 87, his accuracy 86, his puck possession 86. His fighting grade is 50, his injury grade 50, his speed off the break 52. Those three are not new, but at 32, with a potential of 59 and 2 years left on a $6.34M contract, the trajectory is clear. The book says he will not get better, that the 79 is the direction.

He scored 115 points in 64 games last season for Vancouver. He is producing more now, at an age when the slide is supposed to have started. When Marcus Bell asked him about it on The Tunnel on January 5, Sanderson said he was pleased but stopped short of surprised: "I had a good year last season, 115 points in 64, that was a good year. But I didn't feel like I was in the right system. Not that I was bad. I was, you know, a piece of the puzzle. Not the center of it."

The suitcase

Sanderson was drafted by Hartford, played through the relocation to Carolina, was traded to Vancouver mid-season in 1998, moved to Buffalo weeks later, was claimed by Columbus in the 2000 Expansion Draft, returned to Vancouver at the 2004 deadline, and came back to Columbus. Six clubs in a little over a decade. That is the career that gives the 160 pace its weight. He told Bell the suitcase metaphor before he could stop himself: "You get moved enough times and you stop being a guy who belongs somewhere. You're just a guy with a suitcase. But I'll tell you, coming back here, to Columbus, that was the first time I picked a place. Not the other way around."

He chose the captaincy, or it chose him in a way that felt deliberate. "They asked me to be a captain. Before I'd even signed, before the ink. Somebody said, we want you here to hold this together. I've never had that. Thirty years old, twelve seasons in, and nobody had ever stood in front of me and said, you're the one."

The quote is not about his scoring. It is about why a man with a potential of 59 and a morale of 87 is putting up 160-pace numbers on a team that has no business making the postseason. The system asks him to be the center of it, and he is answering.

Leclaire, two weeks on

The December 29 Blaine Rankings said Columbus was "38 points on a grade of 50," which was a way of saying the Blue Jackets were surviving on goaltending that the Bureau did not think would hold. Pascal Leclaire had 10 wins in 15 starts and a consistency grade of 50, and the argument was that regression was coming.

It has not come. Leclaire has 14 wins in 23 starts, a 2.96 goals-against, a .862 save percentage. The Bureau's Development Index now has him 4 above his base, up from the negative reading the desk reported in late December. His overall is 82, his potential is 93. The regression the Book projected has not arrived, which is the second thing about this roster that the grades have gotten wrong.

What it costs and what it gets

Columbus pays $48.98M in total payroll. Sanderson's $6.34M is the largest single line on a sheet that has no other forward graded above 78. The Blue Jackets score 3.23 goals per game and allow 3.23. They are 46 points at 43 games.

And yet a points percentage of .535 puts them 3rd in the Central, behind Nashville Predators at 59 and Detroit Red Wings at 50. The fourth seed is Chicago Blackhawks at 34. Columbus is 12 points clear of the bottom and 13 points back of the top. They are in the middle of their own division, which is the same position they occupied at the start of December and will likely occupy when the standings close.

Sanderson will finish with points. The question for Columbus is what they do with a 32-year-old captain who has 2 years left on his contract and a Book that says he is past his base. The Blue Jackets can run it back with him next season and hope Marchant and Vyborny move up a few grades. They can try to build around Leclaire, who is 22 and graded 93 in potential. Or they can accept that the 160 pace is an outlier produced by a man playing on his own terms in a building that let him choose to be there, and that it does not survive without him.

The suitcase theory says he will not be here in the off-season. The captaincy says he wants to be. The Development Index says the 79 is closer to the truth than the 87.

My prediction, logged: Sanderson finishes above 130 points. The pace says 160. He has the remainder of the season to prove the 8-point slide is real.
