Nashville sits 2nd in the West on the 16th payroll, and the best player on the roster is a goaltender

The Predators have 61 points on $46.77M and no scorer among the league's top 30. Tomas Vokoun is doing most of the lifting.

Howard BlaineSenior writer, The Blaine Rankings · The Hockey GazetteTuesday, 18 January 2005
ShareCopy link

The Western Conference table on the morning of January 18 reads: Dallas 62, Nashville 61. The Predators have played 45 games, the Stars 46. On raw points per game, Nashville is the best club in the West, and the league has paid almost no attention.

The payroll column explains part of the silence. Nashville Predators spends $46.77M, 16th of 30 clubs. Sixty-one points on that money is $0.77M per point, tied with Anaheim for the 5th-cheapest season in the league. Only Vancouver ($0.59M), Edmonton ($0.60M), Minnesota ($0.75M) and Tampa Bay ($0.73M) extract more from less. Nashville does it without the league's cheapest roster, without a top-30 scorer, and without anyone in the building who is a household name outside Tennessee.

The goaltender at the center of it

Tomas Vokoun90 carries a 92 overall in the Book, the highest grade on the roster the Bureau publishes. In 37 starts he has 22 wins, a 2.87 goals-against average and a 0.862 save percentage. Those numbers are not the best in the league, but they sit in the top tier, and they come on a club that gave up 146 goals in 45 games. The top eight in the West spans Dallas at 151 goals against in 46 games to Columbus at 145 in 45. Nashville's offense does not win them games in the way Colorado Avalanche does with 193 goals for, or Toronto Maple Leafs does with 257. The Predators win them by not losing the puck and by making Vokoun's 2.87 look like something smaller on the night.

The Development Index has Vokoun's form at 0, dead level, no movement in either direction. He is a 32-year-old in his 10th season playing exactly as much hockey as the Book says he should. The grade says he was good before this season and he is good still.

No scorer, no problem

The 30th scorer in the league is Marian Gaborik98 of Toronto Maple Leafs with 68 points. No Nashville player appears anywhere near that mark. The club's highest-scoring player visible on any sheet the Bureau publishes is Martin Erat79, a 23-year-old left winger graded 80 with 36 points in 42 games, pacing 70 over 82. That is a second-line scorer in a good year. The 22-year-old Scott Hartnell78 has 15 points in 45 games, which is a checking-line number.

A team 2nd in the West whose leading scorer paces 70 points is built on goaltending, structure, and divisional futility on the part of its opponents.

The Central Division is soft, and Nashville knows it

In 12 divisional games, Nashville is 8 wins, 1 overtime win, 1 loss, 2 overtime losses. That is 19 of a possible 24 points from divisional play. The records read: 3-0-0 against Detroit Red Wings, 3-0-0 (+1 OT) against St. Louis Blues, 1-1-1 against Chicago Blackhawks, 1-0-1 against Columbus Blue Jackets. The Predators have beaten the two clubs below them in the Central more than they have blinked.

Detroit, on the league's highest payroll at $80.89M, is 0-3-0 against Nashville this season. What this says is about divisional arithmetic: a $46.77M club with a 92-goaltender plays a $80.89M club that is 1-3-0 (+1 OT) against Chicago Blackhawks and 0-3-0 against Nashville in 12 games. The divisional run favors clubs bad enough to lose to the division's bottom feeder and good enough to beat it four times.

What the next week says

Nashville opens its week in Edmonton on January 18 against a club with the same points total and a 4-game win streak. Then Vancouver Canucks visits on January 21, the New York Rangers on January 23, and Tampa Bay Lightning on January 25. Four games against the 3rd, 4th, tied-4th in the East, and 2nd in the East. The divisional run that has carried Nashville to 61 points does not help here; none of those four opponents are in the Central.

The season series against Edmonton reads 1-0-0 to Nashville, 5-3, and the next meeting is tonight at Rexall Place. Edmonton is 15 and 7 at home. Nashville is 14 and 9 on the road. The desk has seen enough 1-0 season-series leads evaporate on a single road loss in January to not trust the head-to-head record beyond its sample size. But the grade differential favors Nashville: Vokoun at 92 against Tommy Salo85 at 88, and a goals-against margin of 0.48 between them.

The pace right now projects to 111 points over 82. The last ten games have run at 11 points per ten, which projects to 102 if sustained. One hundred is a floor the desk is willing to put its name against, and it will be settled on April 6.
