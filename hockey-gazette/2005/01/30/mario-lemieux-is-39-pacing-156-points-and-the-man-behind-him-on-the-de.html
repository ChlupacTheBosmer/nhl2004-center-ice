Mario Lemieux is 39, pacing 156 points, and the man behind him on the depth chart is a goaltender

Pittsburgh leads the Atlantic Division in division play on a 39-year-old earning $9.90M on a $44.95M payroll, and the 11 games that built that lead tell you everything about what happens when he stops.

Howard BlaineSenior writer, The Blaine Rankings · The Hockey GazetteSunday, 30 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Mario Lemieux85 has 80 points in 42 games. The pace is 156 per 82, which is the same rate Bill Guerin85 is carrying at Dallas and behind only Markus Naslund89 at 158 in the entire league. He is 39 years old. His contract runs five more years at $9.90M.

The Pittsburgh Penguins Penguins sit first in the Atlantic Division on the strength of that number. In 11 division games they have 7 regulation wins, 2 overtime wins, 2 losses and no overtime losses. No other Atlantic club has more than 4 division wins. Pittsburgh has built its standing against the four clubs it sees most, and the rest of the league has been a different season.

The roster behind the number

Lemieux is graded 89 in the Book, down from a base of 92 on the Development Index. The next-highest grade on the Pittsburgh Penguins roster is Johan Hedberg82 at 88, who is a goaltender. The next-highest skater is Martin Straka82 at 86. After him comes Aleksey Morozov75 at 80 and Eric Meloche74 at 78.

There is a 3-point gap from Lemieux to Straka at center, and a 9-point gap to Morozov at left wing. The Penguins have $44.95M on the books, the 19th payroll in the league, and $9.90M of it belongs to one 39-year-old center. The per-point rate is $0.88M. That number looks good until you subtract Lemieux's contribution and ask what the remaining $35.05M has bought.

Straka has 64 points in 51 games this year at a pace of 103. Meloche, the 2004 Calder winner, is graded 78 today and plays limited minutes. The roster has one man producing at an elite level and everyone else producing at a level that puts them 10th in the Eastern Conference.

Division first, conference tenth

Division games and non-division games have produced two different teams. In 11 Atlantic Division games, Pittsburgh Penguins has gone 7 and 2 with no overtime losses. Outside the division, in 40 games, the Penguins have 9 regulation wins, 5 overtime wins and 21 losses. The division record earns the standing. The non-division record earns the 10th place.

Pittsburgh's 18 division points in 11 games is more than New Jersey has earned in its 7 Atlantic meetings, more than New York has in 9. The Penguins beat the division. They lose everyone else.

The home and away numbers make it stranger. Pittsburgh Penguins is 11 and 13 at home, 12 and 15 on the road. The Penguins are one of the few clubs in the league whose road record is better, though "better" is generous at 12 and 15. The last 10 tell you the recent direction: 7 wins, 2 losses, 1 overtime loss, with 28 goals for against 21.

What 156 means at 39

Lemieux scored 150 points in 82 games last season. This year, across 42 games before a rib injury that kept him out from sometime between January 19 and January 22, he has matched that production at a faster rate. The pace of 156 is not a decline from the man who had 150. It is a 6-point improvement per 82-game equivalent.

His strongest attributes are grading at 99 for deking and passing, 97 for heroics, 96 for puck control. His fight grade is 50. He takes almost no penalties this season: 8 minutes in 42 games, where he took 26 in 82 last year. The man is not playing differently. He is playing the same game on a different schedule, and the body is holding so far.

The contract, though, runs five more years. He is producing now, at 156 points a pace. If he plays the full term, the question at 44 is whether the Penguins have built anything around that production that survives when the pace drops.

The gap to the eighth spot

Pittsburgh Penguins sits 10th in the Eastern Conference. New York Islanders holds 8th at 55 points in 50 games. Pittsburgh has 51 in 51. The gap is 4 points, and Pittsburgh has played 1 game more than New York. The Islanders hold a game in hand. Over the 31 games remaining for Pittsburgh against 32 for New York, the Penguins need a 0.16-point-per-game swing just to level.

The 9th club is Buffalo Sabres at 51 points in 49 games, tied with Pittsburgh on points but ahead on wins. The Atlantic Division has 4 clubs in the top 11 of the conference: New Jersey at 4th, New York at 6th, New York Islanders at 8th, Pittsburgh at 10th, and Philadelphia at 11th. Five clubs in the top 11. The division is deep enough that 1st place in Atlantic play can sit 10th in the conference.

Lemieux cannot play 82 games. He has not played 82 since 2003-04, and that was the last full one. Forty-two games in 51 team games is a pace that already says the schedule is protecting him. If the 156 pace holds across a full 82, the arithmetic says 156 points. If it drops to his last-season 150 pace across a full year, it says 150. Both are more than any other Penguin has scored in any season on the record.

The Pittsburgh Penguins problem is that Pittsburgh looks like a playoff team when Lemieux is in the lineup, and when he is not, the 9-point gap to Morozov and the 3-point gap to Straka make it look like a rebuilding roster wearing a 39-year-old's sweater.
