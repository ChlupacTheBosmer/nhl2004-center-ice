Sergei Fedorov is 35, 70.7 percent of his points are goals, and the next man on the Anaheim roster is graded 79

Fedorov has 53 goals in 49 games and 22 assists, the fewest of any player among the top 30 scorers. He is not creating offense. He is finishing it.

Howard BlaineSenior writer, The Blaine Rankings · The Hockey GazetteWednesday, 26 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Sergei Fedorov90 has scored 53 goals in 49 games for Mighty Ducks of Anaheim. That is 1.08 goals a night, the highest rate in the league, ahead of Markus Naslund89 at 1.02 and Jarome Iginla94 at 1.00. His 75 points place him 23rd in the league. His 22 assists are the fewest of any player among the top 30 in scoring.

Fedorov is 35 years old, drafted 74th overall in the 1989 draft by a club whose identity the record does not carry. He won the Pearson Award last spring as the most outstanding player, voted by the players. He is having the most goal-heavy year of his career, and the numbers say a good deal about why Anaheim sits 7th in the West.

The goal rate

Fedorov's 53 goals come from 75 points. That is 70.7 percent of his production arriving on the scoresheet as a goal, the highest share among every player in the league's top 30. The next closest among the group on the sheet is Zigmund Palffy87 of Los Angeles Kings at 56.1 percent, and Palffy is 32 with a career built on the same one-dimensional finish. Iginla at 58.9 percent rounds the top three.

The comparison worth making is with the men who play a more complete game. Naslund has 93 points on 50 goals and 43 assists: a 53.8 percent goal rate, a complete scorer. Jaromir Jagr84 has 95 on 44 and 51: 46.3 percent. Fedorov makes fewer plays than anyone in the top 30 and still scores more goals than any of them.

What does a 70.7 percent goal rate mean on a team that scores 152 goals in 49 games? It means Fedorov has put 34.9 percent of Anaheim's goals into the net himself.

The roster cliff

The second-highest scorer on the Anaheim roster is Erik Cole79 with 36 points in 27 games, a pace of 109.3 over 82 games that would be respectable in any other room. The third is Vaclav Prospal80 with 35 in 25. The fourth is Andy McDonald with 30 in 49. Fedorov's 75 is more than double Cole's output, and Cole has played 22 fewer games.

The Bureau's Book grades Fedorov at 90. The next-best forward on the roster is Cole at 79. That is an 11-grade gap between the team's first center and its top left wing. Anaheim's version is narrower in name but not in effect: one man at 90 producing more than the next three forwards combined in points-per-game terms.

The defense contributes 3 goals from Ruslan Salei and 9 from Niclas Havelid77. The bottom six forwards do not crack 30. The attack runs through one man, and that man is 35 with a goal rate of 1.08 a game.

The pace and what it means

Fedorov's pace is 126 points over 82 games. On a scoring basis, that is 23rd in the league. But his goal pace is 88.7 per 82 games. If he maintains this rate through the remaining 33 games, he finishes with 88 goals. The Maurice Richard Trophy last spring went to Iginla at 92 in 73 games. Fedorov is on a pace that approaches those numbers at 35.

The assists are what make this different from a volume scorer. At 22 assists in 49 games, Fedorov is on a 37-assist pace. He has stopped distributing and started finishing, and the goal rate follows accordingly. His time on ice is 23.4 minutes a game, the highest for any player in the top 30 in scoring.

What it costs and what it earns

Anaheim pays $41.01M in total payroll, the 27th-highest in the league. At $0.71M per point, it is the 5th-cheapest club in hockey. The profit sits at $27.32M on revenue of $59.24M, the 5th-highest in the league. Attendance is 14,500 per game at $79 a ticket. This is a franchise operating efficiently on a small budget, getting 70 percent of its offense from one 35-year-old center, and sitting 7th in the West with 10 overtime losses the club cannot close out because the depth behind its engine does not extend past the second line.

The 10 overtime losses do not trace to goaltending. Jean-Sebastien Giguere97 carries a 97 overall on the Book, the highest grade of any goaltender in the league, and a 2.64 goals-against average on 44 starts. The problem is that when Fedorov scores and the other 48 names on the roster do not match him, the game reaches the third period tied, and a tie in a building where the third-highest scorer has 30 points in 49 games is a coin flip the club loses more often than not.
