Vezina Deep-Dive: Giguere is graded 97, the highest number on any goaltender, and Anaheim has 10 overtime losses

The Book rates him above Brodeur, above Luongo, above every man between the pipes in hockey. He has 22 wins and not one shutout.

Howard BlaineSenior writer, The Blaine Rankings · The Hockey GazetteWednesday, 26 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Jean-Sebastien Giguere97 carries the highest overall grade in the league among goaltenders, and among all players except Marian Gaborik98 of Toronto Maple Leafs. The Bureau's Book has him at 97 today, three above his base of 93, on a Development Index that has climbed since December. No goaltender has a higher number. Martin Brodeur91 is at 92. Roberto Luongo94 is at 94. Ed Belfour93, who won the Jennings last spring, sits at 93.

He also has 0 shutouts in 44 starts.

The grade and the wins do not line up

The gap between what the Book says Giguere is and what the standings say Mighty Ducks of Anaheim has done is the story of the season in Anaheim. The Ducks are 19 and 15 and 10, sitting 7th in the West at 58 points. Those 10 overtime losses are the most in the league, one clear of Carolina at 9. Anaheim has played 15 games past regulation and lost 10 of them. A goaltender graded 97 whose team cannot close a game in 60 minutes is an argument the standings make and the Book cannot.

His numbers on the year: 22 wins, 21 losses, a 2.64 GAA, a .872 save percentage. Brodeur has 26 wins on a .874, a GAA of 2.73, and a club payroll $3.97M higher. Nikolai Khabibulin92 of Tampa Bay Lightning has 27 wins on a .867, graded 86. The Book says Giguere is a better goaltender than Khabibulin by 11 points. The wins column says Khabibulin has five more.

The attribute grades explain what the Book sees. Giguere carries 99 in five-hole, agility, speed, reactive quickness, and short-scope vision. His shot label reads 97. His weakest attributes are toughness at 50, fighting at 51, and flopping at 51. The Bureau is grading him as an athlete between the pipes, a goaltender whose movement and reflexes are the best in the league. The standings reward something else.

What 10 overtime losses cost

Anaheim's 2.64 GAA is the third-best in the league behind Belfour's 2.35 and David Aebischer86 at 2.51. The save percentage at .872 sits behind Brodeur's .874 and Rick DiPietro84's .873. On a per-shot basis, Giguere is one of the best goaltenders in hockey. When the puck is on him, he stops it at an elite rate. What happens after regulation ends is a different story.

Anaheim scores 152 goals in 49 games, 3.10 a night. They allow 151, or 3.08 a night. That one-goal differential across 49 games is what makes the overtime losses so expensive. When a team scores 3 and allows 3 in regulation, the game goes to overtime, and the loser gets 1 point. Anaheim has been the loser 10 times. At 2 points a regulation win, those 10 points would put the Ducks at 68 and atop the Western Conference.

The club's home record is 15 and 9. The road record is 9 and 16. The overtime losses come in both buildings, but the away record tells you the Ducks do not carry enough roster depth to win a 3-3 game in someone else's building at 10 p.m. Pacific.

The roster behind the 97

Giguere's teammates do not reach him. The best forward the roster offers is Sergei Fedorov90 at 90, and the next two are Erik Cole79 at 79 and Vaclav Prospal80 at 78. The gap from Giguere at 97 down to the third-best skater on the roster at 78 is 19 grades. Only a handful of clubs in the league carry that kind of drop from their top player to their fourth option.

Anaheim pays $41.01M, the 5th-cheapest rate in the league at $0.71M per point. The revenue is $59.24M, the profit is $27.32M. This is a franchise that spends well. The money buys one 90 player and a goaltender the Book loves, and the rest of the roster does not close games.

The contract

Giguere's deal is $0.80M. It has 0 years left. He is a free agent this summer.

He signed a four-year extension on September 10, 2003, after winning the Conn Smythe as the most valuable player on the losing side of the 2003 final. That contract expires in June. A goaltender whose base grade has climbed from 93 and whose Index has pushed him to 97, on a club that sits 7th in the West and cannot close a regulation game, will not lack suitors in July. Whether Anaheim can re-sign a 97-graded goaltender on a $41.01M payroll that already carries Fedorov at 90 is the question that decides whether this is a building project or a window that slams.

The Vezina last spring went to Olaf Kolzig87, graded 92, who went 24 and 20 with a 3.22 GAA and 2 shutouts. Giguere's 2.64 and .872 with a 97 grade is, on the sheet, a better case than the one Kolzig made twelve months ago. The wins are 22 against Kolzig's 24. The shutouts are zero against two. The overtime losses are 10, which is a number that has never won a Vezina for anyone, anywhere.

The Bureau's Book is the only document in this league that grades a goaltender without the wins column. It says Giguere is the best there is. The standings say his team is 7th in the West. That gap is the conversation this January.
