Detroit pays $80.89M, the most in the league, and it is 0 and 6 against the two clubs above it in the Central

The Red Wings have the highest payroll, the largest home-away swing in the league, and a 40-year-old with a 20-point lead on his own second-leading scorer.

Howard BlaineSenior writer, The Blaine Rankings · The Hockey GazetteSaturday, 15 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Detroit Red Wings has the highest payroll in the league at $80.89M. Through 44 games, it produces 50 points at a cost of $1.62M per point, second only to Philadelphia's $1.65M. Three clubs with payrolls below $47M have more points than Detroit: Nashville Predators at 59 on $46.77M, Edmonton Oilers at 59 on $36.81M, Vancouver Canucks at 57 on $34.94M. Vancouver spends less than half of what Detroit spends and sits 7 points below it.

In 11 Central division games, Detroit is 2 wins, 1 overtime win, 7 losses and 1 overtime loss. Against Nashville Predators it is 0-3-0. Against Chicago Blackhawks it is 0-3-0. Six games against the division's top two teams, six losses, no overtime losses, no points. The Predators and the Blackhawks score a combined 38 goals on the Red Wings in those meetings while Detroit manages 33. All 3 games against Chicago were decided by one goal. Detroit scored 5 and allowed 8 across those three.

The home and road splits are the widest in the 30-club league. Detroit is 17-6 at Joe Louis Arena and 6-15 on the road. No other club is farther from .500 on the road than Detroit's 6-15. Philadelphia Flyers is 13-8 at home and 6-16 away, the only other club with a single-digit road win total.

The man carrying the offense is Brett Hull, who is 40. He has 72 points in 44 games, a pace of 134 over 82, and 20 points on the next Red Wing in the scoring column, whose name does not appear in the league's top 30. Dominik Hasek is 20-17 with a 3.46 GAA and carries a grade of 91, the 6th-highest goaltender in the Book. They are 39 and 40 on the sheet of record. No other Detroit player appears in the top 30 scorers or the goaltending table.

Detroit sits 6th in the Western Conference, 4 points above the 8th-place Columbus Blue Jackets with both clubs at 44 games played and 38 remaining. But the Red Wings have 8 divisional games left, and the schedule brings Chicago Blackhawks tonight and Colorado Avalanche tomorrow, two clubs they have not beaten in six games this season against the top two divisional opponents. The season's closest series table lists the Detroit-Chicago games as one-goal margins in all three meetings, a total goal differential of minus 3. If that pattern repeats in the two remaining games, the points go to Chicago.

Detroit has scored 162 and allowed 163 across 44 games, a goal differential of minus 1. That is the arithmetic of a team hovering near .500, which it is: 18-17-4. The 50 points come from home wins and overtime luck, 5 overtime wins in the win column, and they have lost 4 in overtime and shootouts. The Jennings Trophy that Ed Belfour took last spring, the 97-point season that got them into the postseason as the 10th seed, the first-round exit in 7 games: that was last year. This year's record is 50 points on the most expensive roster in the league.

I believe the standings at Christmas are the standings in April. I have said so when they were not. Detroit is in a position where the structural facts, the divisional record, the road record, the age of the two men at the top of the roster, point in one direction. Thirty-eight games is a long time. But the two teams that beat them six times in six meetings have not lost a game to Detroit this season, and those six meetings were against the division's 1st and 4th-place clubs, not its bottom feeders.
