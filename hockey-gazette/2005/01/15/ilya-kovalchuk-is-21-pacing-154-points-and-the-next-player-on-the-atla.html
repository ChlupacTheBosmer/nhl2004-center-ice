Ilya Kovalchuk is 21, pacing 154 points, and the next player on the Atlanta roster is graded 82

The Thrashers have two players the Book rates above 90, both under 24, and 36 points in 43 games. The grade cliff below the top two is the whole explanation.

Howard BlaineSenior writer, The Blaine Rankings · The Hockey GazetteSaturday, 15 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Ilya Kovalchuk86 has 81 points in 43 games. The pace is 154. He is 21 years old, he is graded 92 in the Book, and he plays for a team with 36 points that has been outscored by 19 goals.

The Development Index has him at +5 over a base grade of 89, which means the Bureau thinks the form is real and the ceiling has not moved. His potential reads 86, and his contract carries one year left at $1.10M. Set against Atlanta Thrashers's standing, the contrast is this: a club paying its first overall pick one million dollars to score at a Hart pace while the fourth-best player on the roster is graded 81 and the starting goaltender has a 4.12 goals-against average.

Kovalchuk at 92 and Dany Heatley84 at 90 are the top two grades in the dressing room. The next name on the list is Sami Kapanen76 at 82, then Patrik Stefan75 at 81. Three points separate Heatley from Kapanen; eleven separate Kovalchuk from the third-best player he shares a locker room with. In a league where the Book's 90-plus threshold marks a player as a genuine game-breaker, Atlanta has two of them, both under 24, and nobody else within nine points of the line.

Atlanta Thrashers's $36.79M payroll is the second-lowest in the league, ahead of only Minnesota Wild. The cost per point is $1.02M, which is not cheap, but it is not the reason the team has 36 points. The reason is that Atlanta scores 3.74 goals per game and allows 4.19. The 3.74 is serviceable; the 4.19 is what costs them games.

Byron Dafoe77 has played 40 games, won 15, and carries a 4.12 goals-against average on a 0.825 save percentage. He is graded 82. In the goaltending table, only Martin Biron84 of Buffalo Sabres has a worse save percentage, and only Jose Theodore85 of Montreal Canadiens has a worse goals-against average among men with 35 or more starts. The Bureau's Book puts him 10 points below Roberto Luongo94, who plays for a team with 4 more points on the board and 12 fewer wins.

What makes the Kovalchuk situation different from, say, the Geoff Sanderson79 situation in Columbus is age and the shape of the gap. Sanderson is 32 with a base grade of 83 and a roster where nobody else clears 78. Kovalchuk is 21 with a base of 89, a linemate at 90, and the Bureau's Index moving him in the right direction. The Blue Jackets will not get better because Sanderson will not get better. The Thrashers might, because the two players carrying the offense are 21 and 23 years old and the contract year on Kovalchuk expires in the same window where Heatley's development either confirms or fails.

The potential line is the cautionary half. At 86, the Bureau rates the likelihood that Kovalchuk develops further as high but not exceptional. His current overall of 92 already exceeds the potential reading, which is how the Index works: the 92 is base plus form, not a projection. The base is 89. The potential is 86. If the Index regresses to zero, the Book puts him at 89, which is a very good player and not the player pacing 154 points tonight. The question is whether 89 at 22, in a building that cannot win 30 games, is enough to matter.

Atlanta Thrashers scored 272 goals last season and allowed 334. The 19-goal deficit in 43 games is the distance between the two 90-grade players and the rest of the roster made visible on a scoresheet. Kovalchuk can score 70 goals in a season and the team will still be outscored by a wide margin if the men around him are graded 81, 80 and 79 and the goaltender is 82 with a 4.12. The first overall pick of 2001 is the second player in this league, behind only Mario Lemieux88 in points per game among players over 35, and ahead of everyone else his age. The team has 13 wins.
