The Blaine Rankings, Week 6: the Southeast is a race of two, and the cheapest point in the league is second in it

Tampa Bay is 42 points, 6th in the league, 1 behind Carolina, and the cost-per-point is $1.09M. The center is 24, on a $2.60M contract, and the room is not loud.

Howard BlaineSenior writer, The Blaine Rankings · The Hockey GazetteFriday, 17 December 2004
ShareCopy link

The Tampa Bay Lightning Lightning are 42 points in 31 games, 1 behind Carolina, on a 4-game win streak, and their cost-per-point is $1.09M, the best in a 30-club league. The figure is the kind that separates a team that finishes 6th from one that finishes 20th, and it is the reason the Southeast is the division to watch this month.

Carolina leads at 43, Tampa Bay is at 42, and Washington is 36. The gap between first and third is 7 points in 31 games. Florida and Atlanta are 15 and 20 points back, and the math does not support a 30-point comeback in 51 games. The gap at the top is 1 point. Washington is 6 back, which is 3 wins, and in 7 weeks that gap will not close on its own.

The Lightning's efficiency is the thing that makes the 1 point meaningful. $45.79M in payroll, 42 points, $1.09M per point. Toronto is at $1.34M, Colorado at $1.43M, Dallas at $1.49M. Tampa Bay is 3 places behind the league leaders on points and 27 places ahead on cost. The profit column says $23.18M. The attendance is 15,926. The ticket is $79.

The center is 24, and the numbers say the captaincy was about timing

Vincent Lecavalier84 is the reason the Lightning are at 42, and the reason is not dramatic. 55 points in 31 games, 22 goals, 33 assists, 145 pace, 22.4 minutes a night. The Bureau's Book has him at 90 overall, up 1 on the Development Index. The HERO grade is 98, the SPEE is 97, the SHPW is 96. The FIGH is 51. He is 24 years old, on a $2.60M contract with 2 years left, and he was the 1998 first overall.

Lecavalier was stripped of his captaincy before the 2001-02 season because the Lightning decided he was too young. He was 21. Now he is 24, and the numbers say the decision was about timing, not ability. 138 points in 82 games last season. 55 in 31 this year. The 22.4 minutes are the most he has played in a season, and the 33 assists in 31 games is a rate that does not require the puck to be in his hands for the point to happen. He is not carrying the team in the way that Mike Modano90 carries Dallas or the way that Peter Forsberg95 carries Colorado.

Nikolai Khabibulin86 is 17-9-1 with a 3.16 GAA and a 0.860 save percentage in 27 games. He is not the best goalie in the league. Martin Brodeur93 is at 2.69, Jamie Storr83 at 2.69, David Aebischer86 at 2.80. But Khabibulin is good enough, and the 11-7 road record says the team is good enough in front of him. The 4-game win streak is the visible sign. The 11 road wins are the more telling number, the most in the division and the 4th most in the league behind New Jersey's 10 in fewer games, Colorado's 14, and Nashville's 10.

The next two games decide the shape of Christmas

The Lightning play Carolina on the road on Saturday and host Nashville on Wednesday. Two games, 4 points, and the 1-point gap either widens or closes or holds. The standings at Christmas will be the standings in April, and if the 42 holds, if the 11-7 on the road holds, if Lecavalier keeps 22.4 minutes and Khabibulin keeps 3.16, the Lightning are a team that finishes 6th in the league and makes the playoffs and no one writes a book about them.
