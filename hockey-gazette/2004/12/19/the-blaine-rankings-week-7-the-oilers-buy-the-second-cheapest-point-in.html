The Blaine Rankings, Week 7: the Oilers buy the second-cheapest point in the league, and the goaltending says they are borrowing from next spring

Edmonton is 10th at 41 points on a $36.81M payroll, Mike Comrie leads the league in assists at 24, and Tommy Salo's 3.68 GAA is why the 124 goals scored feel like 134 goals against

Howard BlaineSenior writer, The Blaine Rankings · The Hockey GazetteSunday, 19 December 2004
ShareCopy link

Edmonton sits 10th in the league at 41 points on a $36.81M payroll, which puts the cost per point at $0.90M, second only to Vancouver's $0.83M. The Oilers have scored 124 goals and conceded 134 in 32 games. Every other team in a playoff position in this 30-club league, and the 16 teams made the field last spring, has a goals-for number above or equal to its goals-against. Edmonton is the only one that does not.

The Christmas standings are the April standings. I have said it six times in six weeks and I will say it in Week 8. The teams at the top belong there. Toronto at 52, Colorado at 50, New York at 46, Dallas at 45: none of them are there by accident. But Edmonton makes me test the line, because the arithmetic of the roster and the arithmetic of the goaltending point in opposite directions.

Mike Comrie84 has 44 assists, the most of any player in the league. He has 56 points on 12 goals and 44 helpers, a pace of 144 over 82, 21.9 minutes a night, an 82 overall in the Bureau's Book, 24 years old, and a fourth-round selection in 1999 at pick 91. He is the best passer in this league right now, and the team that has him is spending $36.81M on its entire roster. Nobody outside this building has written a word about it, because nobody outside this building has looked.

The assists are real. The 12 goals are real. Both together, on a team conceding 3.44 goals a game, are the engine of a club that should not be a playoff team by the arithmetic of its own netminding.

Tommy Salo87 is 15-8 with a 3.68 GAA and a 0.842 save rate across 25 starts. He has not kept an opponent off the board. In a league where Martin Brodeur91 is at 2.69 and Ed Belfour92 is at 2.57 in limited work, 3.68 is the number that stops a team from climbing.

Ryan Smyth85 won the Hart Trophy last season on 163 points. He is at 53 now, on a 136 pace. At 28, an 83 overall, 22.7 minutes a night, he is the safest floor in the league: you can build a season around a man who scored 74 goals last year and is currently on a pace to score 69. But a 136 pace from Smyth and a 144 pace from Comrie cannot fully paper over 134 goals conceded in 32 games. They can paper over enough of them to be 10-7 on the road, which is the fourth-best road mark in the league, and enough of them to be 12-11-1 overall and alive in the race at Christmas.

The comparison that makes the point

Vancouver Canucks is the clean version of the same trade-off the Oilers are running. Vancouver has 42 points on a $34.94M payroll, $0.83M per point, 132 goals scored, and 115 conceded. Their goaltending is no better than Salo's: Dan Cloutier88 sits at 3.34 and a 0.852 save rate. The difference is in front of him. Vancouver Canucks concedes 115 in 32 games, Edmonton Oilers concedes 134 in 32 games, and what separates one team building something from the other gambling on two scorers outrunning their own defence is that gap.

Edmonton has one fewer point than Vancouver, pays $36.81M to Vancouver's $34.94M, and carries a goals-against of 134 against a goals-for of 124. That gap may not sink the team by February, but it marks the line between holding position and sliding into the 13th-through-16th cluster where New York Islanders, Ottawa Senators, Columbus Blue Jackets, and Mighty Ducks of Anaheim currently fight it out with three teams separated by one point.

Where the line sits

The 16th position, which was the cutoff last spring when 16 clubs made the field, belongs to Mighty Ducks of Anaheim at 32 points. Above them: New York Islanders at 34, Ottawa Senators at 33, Columbus Blue Jackets at 33. Above those three: Detroit Red Wings and Washington Capitals at 38. Edmonton at 41 is the first team with real distance between itself and that scramble, and the only reason is that Comrie and Smyth outproduce every other duo in that middle pack by a wide enough margin to turn three-goal deficits into two-goal wins.

Detroit pays $2.13M per point to be two goals behind its own offence. Washington pays $1.52M per point to be one of the better-balanced teams in the East. Edmonton pays 90 cents on the point to be in a worse differential than both of them. The goaltender is not going to get better at 31, and a 3.68 is a 3.68 regardless of who scores.

The call

Edmonton Oilers holds a playoff position as of February 15. That is the prediction, logged and revisitable. The two scorers make enough plays that this team wins 35-something games at home and on the road against other bad teams, and that is what a 41-point pace through 32 games means: the club wins roughly 60 per cent of its remaining games, which is comfortably a 16th-place pace in a league where the bottom 14 clubs are winning 37 per cent or less of theirs.

The Oilers have a real offence and a goaltender who cannot hold the ice. The payroll is cheap enough that the gap between those two facts survives 82 games. The 44 assists will carry them. The 134 goals against will not stop them. But if you are asking where this room is in April, I say: in, and no higher than the second round they drew last spring. The cheap point buys you a place. It does not buy you a window.
