Pascal Leclaire has won 10 of 14 in 15 starts, and Columbus is 38 points on a grade of 50

The 22-year-old Blue Jacket has the fifth-best goals-against average in the league on a one-year deal, and the Book thinks he will regress

Howard BlaineSenior writer, The Blaine Rankings · The Hockey GazetteWednesday, 29 December 2004
ShareCopy link

Pascal Leclaire86 has started 15 games for Columbus Blue Jackets and won 10 of 14 decisions. His goals-against average is 2.94, which is the fifth-lowest among the 30 goaltenders in the league who have played at least five games. His save percentage is 0.867, which is fifth in the league. He is 22 years old. He plays on a one-year contract that pays him $1.10M.

Columbus is 11-14-2 in 34 games with 38 points, third in the Central Division, 10 points behind the division-leading Nashville Predators. The Blue Jackets have 18 wins on the season. Leclaire has 10 of them. In the 19 games the 22-year-old from Repentigny, Quebec, did not start, the club won 8 and lost 12. That is the season in one line: a 22-year-old on a one-year deal has carried a 38-point team to the edge of a playoff spot, and the organization has no reason to believe the same goaltender who put up a 2.94 in December will put up a 2.94 in February.

The grades say what the record confirms, and what it conceals

The Bureau's Book has Leclaire at 84 today, which is a base grade of 83 and four points on the Development Index. The Index is the form term. It has him climbing, and his season line looks like a man climbing: 10 wins in 15 starts after 11 in 24 a season ago. His potential sits at 94. The gap between 84 and 94 is the gap between a good young goaltender and a very good one, and the gap between 50 and everything else in his grade card is the gap between a good young goaltender and a reliable one.

His strongest attributes are agility at 91 and glove at 91, with lateral movement at 88 and speed at 87. That is the toolkit of a goaltender who makes the difficult stop. His weakest attribute is consistency at 50. Style is also 50. Fighting is 51. He does not make the difficult stop reliably. He makes it when he is there, and he is not always there.

Ed Belfour92, 39 years old and the most inconsistent goaltender in the league by his own account, told The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk this week that he has been inconsistent his entire career. The difference, Belfour said, is that he no longer loses the game on the nights he is not great. Belfour has won 12 of 13 starts with a 2.35 goals-against average. He is not inconsistent in any way that hurts his team. Leclaire is inconsistent, and the 50 in the Book is a current grade, the Bureau's judgment of the last month, which means the 2.94 is a snapshot of a man at his best.

What the contract means

Leclaire is on $1.10M with one year remaining. That is a contract a club gives a kid it thinks is still figuring out whether it is a 94 or an 80. Columbus drafted him eighth overall in 2001, the first goaltender taken, and the club has now seen him play 39 NHL games across two seasons. He has won 21 of 39 decisions, a .538 winning percentage. Ten of those wins came in the 15 games he has started this season.

The Blue Jackets' payroll is $48.98M, their 1.29M per point is 16th in the league, and their attendance is 13,693 per game. They are not a club that can outspend its way to a goaltender if the one they drafted eighth overall turns out to be the man with the 50 in consistency. They can, however, sign him to a long-term deal in the summer and hope the 94 arrives before the 50 gets worse.

Geoff Sanderson83 [876] at 32 has 61 points on a 147 pace, the team's leading scorer and a man who has done this before. The room is not loud. The club's previous playoff run lasted two rounds. The infrastructure around Leclaire is competent and unspectacular: Todd Marchant82 [616] at center, Marc Denis86 [221] as the backup with a base grade of 82, Dimitri Kalinin78 [461] and David Vyborny78 [1049] at 76. Nobody on this roster is going to fix a 50 in consistency.

The January question

The Blaine thesis is that the standings at Christmas are the standings in April. That thesis has a failure mode, and it is a goaltender on a one-year deal with a 50 in consistency. If Leclaire regresses to his career norms, his goals-against average rises, and Columbus is not a 38-point team. It is a team with 26 or 28 points, sitting sixth in a division that is a race between Nashville Predators and Detroit Red Wings, and the 19 games without him this season are a preview of what his absence looks like.

The Vezina race, for context, does not start until 20 wins. Jean-Sebastien Giguere93 has a 97 in the Book and 13 wins, and he is not in it. Martin Brodeur93 has 20 wins and a 2.68, and he is the frontrunner. Leclaire has 10 wins in 15 starts, a pace that projects to 45 or 46 by mid-April. If he holds it, the Vezina conversation opens in March. If the 50 shows up, the conversation never starts, and Columbus plays for a lottery pick.

The base grade is 83, the Development Index +4. The potential is 94. The consistency is 50. The contract is $1.10M and expires after this season. Every number above is a question January answers.
