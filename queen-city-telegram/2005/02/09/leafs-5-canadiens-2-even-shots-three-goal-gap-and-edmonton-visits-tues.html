Leafs 5, Canadiens 2: Even shots, three-goal gap, and Edmonton visits Tuesday

Toronto won 5-2 on a night the shot clock came down to a one-shot margin. The finishing did the work.

Sam OkaforLeafs beat reporter · Queen City TelegramWednesday, 9 February 2005
ShareCopy link

The shot clock said 32 to 31. The scoreboard said 5 to 2. That is the whole game in one line.

Marian Gaborik98 and the Leafs did not need a 20-shot cushion to beat the Canadiens. Montreal came to the Air Canada Centre on Saturday night at 15-32-4, in the middle of a third straight loss, and made it competitive on the shot count. Thirty-one attempts is not a blowout on paper. Toronto converted five and Montreal two.

The desk called this game two editions ago and collected the prediction. The shot balance is the thing to look at here. After 58 shots against in overtime at Tampa on February 6, the worry was that Toronto needed a 30-shot cushion to control games. Against Montreal, the cushion was one. The Leafs scored five anyway.

Toronto sits at 39-9-4, 88 points, 24-4 at home. The division crown is a formality: Boston is 30 points back with 5 division games left on the schedule. The Leafs' 62-34 goal differential in 13 division games is the number that describes the season better than the record alone.

Edmonton at 72 points, the first real visitor in two weeks

Edmonton Oilers comes to the Air Canada Centre on Tuesday. Edmonton is 22-15-6, 72 points, second in the Northwest, and lost 4-2 at home to Calgary Flames on Saturday. The only meeting this season ended 4-0 in Toronto's favor.

In Toronto's last 10 games, no opponent with more than 70 points has visited the Air Canada Centre. The highest-point home opponent in that span was Colorado Avalanche at 67 on January 25, and the Leafs won that one 3-0. Edmonton at 72 raises the ceiling.

This is a different challenge than Montreal. Edmonton Oilers has Ryan Smyth82, last season's Hart Trophy winner, and a structure built on shot discipline. They will generate roughly even shot totals the way Montreal did Saturday. The difference is Edmonton scores back at a rate that keeps them in a division race with Vancouver Canucks and Colorado Avalanche rather than a race for the draft lottery.

Roberto Luongo94 starts. He has 39 decisions this season and carries the workload with 61.1 minutes of rest on average between starts. Ed Belfour93 is the backup at 39 years old and 21 games played.

Toronto has won 5 of 7 at home since the turn of the year. Edmonton's road record is 14-12. Tuesday is the first genuine chance since January to see what this team does against a top-half opponent with something to play for.
