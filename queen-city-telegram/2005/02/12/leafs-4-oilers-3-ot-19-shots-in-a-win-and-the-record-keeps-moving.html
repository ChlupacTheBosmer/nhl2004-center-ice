Leafs 4, Oilers 3 (OT): 19 shots in a win, and the record keeps moving

Toronto takes the second overtime point in three tries this month, but the shot clock told a different story than the scoreboard

Sam OkaforLeafs beat reporter · Queen City TelegramSaturday, 12 February 2005
ShareCopy link

The Toronto Maple Leafs beat Edmonton Oilers 4-3 in overtime on Thursday, collecting their 39th win and a 90th point in 56 games. They were outshot 19-41.

That is the fewest shots Toronto has put on a goaltender in a game they won, and the gap of 22 was the widest the Oilers produced against anyone this season. Edmonton carried 41 shots against a Toronto defence that has surrendered 147 in 56 games, a league-best total by 14 over second-place Colorado Avalanche.

Marian Gaborik99 now carries 76 points in 56 games, good for the league lead. His Development Index rating sits at +6, tied for the highest riser in the league this edition. Rostislav Klesla87 moved up 5, Rick Nash85 up 5, and Brad Richards86 up 4, giving Toronto four names on the Bureau's risers list.

Tonight: first look at Chicago

The Leafs travel to face Chicago Blackhawks tonight, the first meeting of the season between the clubs. Chicago sits 18-27-8, 50 points, last in the Central. They are on a one-game losing streak after falling 3-2 to Vancouver Canucks on February 10.

The Chicago Blackhawks lineup carries a new face: Theoren Fleury82 returned to the dressing room after missing the previous slate with a hand, and the coaching staff shuffled four defensive pairings between the February 8 loss at Edmonton Oilers and the February 10 game at Vancouver. Toronto has not faced this version of the roster.

Three Chicago players are out with injuries: Steve Poapst67 (hand, back March 20), Shawn Thornton64 (hand, back March 15), and Igor Korolev69 (wrist, back March 2).

What comes next

After tonight, the Leafs host Ottawa Senators on Saturday and Carolina Hurricanes on Tuesday. The road trip continues February 20 at Washington Capitals and February 22 at Montreal Canadiens.

Toronto's home record stands at 25-4. The road sits at 18-9. The four-game road stretch through late February will decide whether this team can close on the top seed or settle for second.
