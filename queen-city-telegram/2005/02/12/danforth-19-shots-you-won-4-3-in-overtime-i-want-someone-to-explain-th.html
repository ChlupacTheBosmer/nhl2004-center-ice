DANFORTH: 19 shots. You won 4-3 in overtime. I want someone to explain the plan in April.

The record is Green. The shot clock is not. The four risers on the Index are progress, and the possession problem is still the one thing that loses series.

Frankie DanforthColumnist · Queen City TelegramSaturday, 12 February 2005
ShareCopy link

The Panic Meter sits Yellow.

I know what the record says. 39 and 9 and 4, ninety points on 56 games, thirty clear of Boston Bruins, a 25-4 home record, a league-best 147 goals against. I know all of it. I counted it every morning with my coffee the same way you did. The meter should be Green and by the arithmetic of a season standings it is.

And then you look at Thursday night. Nineteen shots on goal. Forty-one against. A 22-shot gap, the worst Toronto has played all season, and they needed overtime to get out of it. A coin flip in extra time with the goaltender standing on his head while the other team fires from every angle.

You win that game and you celebrate. You win it in April and the other goaltender is better, or your goaltender has one bad bounce, and you go home in six with 19 shots across two periods. That is the arithmetic of a seven-game series against a team that can carry play.

The Edmonton Oilers on Thursday are 22-16-7, missing Ryan Smyth84 to an abdomen injury, Raffi Torres73 carrying the forward group. They are not the NJD. And they put 41 shots on Toronto's net.

I want someone to tell me what changes in March. I want to know who carries the play when the fourth line is matching up against a team's first, because that is what happens in April. You play against someone's top three lines. Not their fourth.

The risers and the problem they do not solve

Four Toronto names on the Bureau's risers list. Gaborik at +6. Klesla at +5. Nash at +5. Richards at +4. I read it and I am happy. Those are four young players getting better, and the front office did not trade them for a draft pick.

Richards86 up 4 is the one I watch. I have been writing about him since December. 54 points on the third line, 17.2 minutes a night, a one-year contract. He is good, but he is not the answer to 19 shots. The answer is a second-line centre who takes draws, wins them, makes the other team's defenceman turn his skates the wrong way so the next three shifts happen in the other end. Richards plays the job the way a winger plays a centre, which is to say he is fast and creative and scores, and the possession does not follow.

Petr Chlup85 is 18 years old and carries a 95 potential. I like him. I also know that 14.7 minutes a night on the fourth line is not a centre who fixes a 22-shot gap.

Tonight

First meeting with Chicago Blackhawks tonight. They are 18-27-8 and they have three defencemen out. This is the kind of game that produces 40 shots for your side and nobody cares because you win 6-2. Then Ottawa Senators visits Saturday and Carolina Hurricanes Tuesday and you find out whether the shot totals were a bad night or a season.

I want a draw-winning centre. I want to stop writing this paragraph. I will be writing it again in March if the plan is the same plan it has been since October.

Yellow.
