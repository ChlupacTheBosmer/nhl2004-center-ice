Leafs 1, Senators 4: 10 shots at home, and Carolina visits Tuesday with a 6-2 lead in the head-to-head

Toronto managed 10 shots on goal in a 1-4 home loss to a 20-28-7 Ottawa club. The standings barely moved. The shot clock did not.

Sam OkaforLeafs beat reporter · Queen City TelegramWednesday, 16 February 2005
ShareCopy link

Toronto dressed 13 forwards and 7 defencemen Saturday night at the Air Canada Centre and put 10 pucks on net. Ottawa Senators answered with 34. The scoreboard read 4-1 for Ottawa at the end, and the shot column told the rest.

The loss dropped Toronto to 40-10-4, still 92 points in first place in the Northeast, still 29 ahead of second-place Boston Bruins. The 10 shots follow 19 in Tuesday's overtime win over Edmonton Oilers.

The Toronto Maple Leafs goals-for sits at 296 through 58 games. One goal in 60 minutes is not what that average looks like.

The possession thread keeps pulling

The shot-gap problem flagged throughout January and February produced its sharpest data point Saturday. Ottawa outshot Toronto 34 to 10 in a game the Senators entered at 20-28-7. A 57-32 goal differential in the division does not explain 10 shots on a home sheet against a losing club.

Marian Gaborik99 leads the league's Development Index risers at +6, sitting at 77 points in 58 games. Brad Richards87 sits at +4 with 55 points. Neither found a shooting lane Saturday.

Carolina comes calling Tuesday

Carolina Hurricanes visits the Air Canada Centre on 2005-02-18. The Hurricanes lead the season series 1-0-0, having beaten Toronto 6-2 on the road earlier this season. Carolina sits at 20-15-12, 74 points, with a league-high 12 overtime losses. They keep games close and make them ugly.

Toronto has 6 games left in the Northeast, none of them against Carolina. Tuesday is an intra-conference date against a Southeast club that handed Toronto one of its 10 losses.

Back from injury

Stanislav Chistov87 returns from a knee injury on 2005-02-17, the day before Carolina arrives. He had 5 goals and 16 points in 44 games before the injury. His return gives the coaching staff a body for the third line that has been running short since he went out.

The Leafs' next five: CAR at home (Tuesday), WSH on the road (Thursday), MTL on the road (Saturday), NSH at home (Sunday), NYI at home (Tuesday, March 1). Four of those five are against teams with winning records or near them.

The record is still 40-10-4. The 19 and the 10 are what to watch.
