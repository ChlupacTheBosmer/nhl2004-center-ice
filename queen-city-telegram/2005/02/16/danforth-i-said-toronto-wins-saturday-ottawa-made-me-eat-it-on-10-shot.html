DANFORTH: I said Toronto wins Saturday. Ottawa made me eat it on 10 shots, and the only club in my division that already beat us comes to town Tuesday

The Prediction Desk logged a miss. The Panic Meter goes to Yellow. Here is why the shot gap stopped being an abstraction.

Frankie DanforthColumnist · Queen City TelegramWednesday, 16 February 2005
ShareCopy link

The piece

We said Toronto beats Ottawa on 2005-02-15. Toronto lost 1-4. The Prediction Desk logged the miss. I am logging it here, in ink, for anyone who wants to throw it back at me in April.

10 shots. Ten. On a home sheet. Against a club that came in at 20-28-7 and left with two points and 34 shots on my goaltender. I have been writing about the shot gap since January, and every time the record said hush, and the record said hush every time, and now the record still says 40-10-4 and 29 points clear and hush, but the shot column just said something else out loud.

What 10 shots means in a 296-goal season

The Toronto Maple Leafs goals-for through 58 games is 296. That is over 5 a night, most nights. Saturday they scored 1. The gap between those numbers is the gap between winning regular-season hockey and surviving it. You can win with 19 shots on a night where the power play converts. You cannot win with 10.

I do not want anyone telling me the record is 40-10-4 and I should be grateful. I am grateful. I am also terrified, and the terror has a number attached to it, and the number is 34-10.

The Carolina problem

Carolina Hurricanes comes to the Air Canada Centre Tuesday. They already beat us 6-2. That is the only division loss the Boston Bruins, Buffalo Sabres, Montreal Canadiens, and Ottawa Senators collection does not include, because Carolina is not in my division. But they beat us, and they play the style that makes a 10-shot night look plausible: low-event hockey, lots of blocked shots, 12 overtime losses that mean they keep games at arm's length until someone blinks.

We blinked Saturday. Ten shots is blinking.

What the Index does not tell you

Four Toronto players are on the Bureau's risers list: Marian Gaborik98 at +6, Rostislav Klesla84 at +5, Rick Nash83 at +5, Brad Richards87 at +4. That is progress on individual form. A Development Index grade does not generate a secondary scoring chance when the first two lines are pinned to their own blue line.

The Meter goes Yellow today. Not Orange: the standings are still dominant, the lead is 29 points, nobody in this division is catching them before April. But Yellow, because the thing I have been writing about since January produced its worst single night, and the next man through the door already has a 6-2 score in his pocket from the first meeting.

I want to see 25 shots Tuesday. If the number is not there, I want to know the plan in April again, and this time I want a better answer than the one I got in February.
