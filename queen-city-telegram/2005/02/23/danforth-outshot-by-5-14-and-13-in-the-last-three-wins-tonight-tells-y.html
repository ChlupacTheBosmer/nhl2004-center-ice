DANFORTH: Outshot by 5, 14, and 13 in the last three wins. Tonight tells you if that holds.

Nashville scores 210 goals in 59 games. Toronto allows 156 in 61. One of those numbers has to blink.

Frankie DanforthColumnist · Queen City TelegramWednesday, 23 February 2005
ShareCopy link

The last three wins read like this: at Montreal Canadiens on Tuesday, 22 shots for, 27 against, won 3-1. At Washington Capitals on Sunday, won 5-1. At home to Carolina Hurricanes on Friday, won 3-1. Toronto gave up more shots than they took in each one and the goaltending held and the record moved.

I do not doubt it holds tonight. Maxime Ouellet82 has done the job, Robert Svehla78 and Bryan McCabe83 and Rostislav Klesla83 have blocked enough to bury a team, and Marian Gaborik97 at 80 points and Gary Roberts87 at 65 will find two or three goals from nothing. That is what good teams do. They win when they are bad.

But tonight Nashville Predators comes in averaging 210 goals in 59 games. They are on 7 straight wins. They have not lost since before the calendar turned to February. The Preds have 9-2-2 inside the Central, and they have scored 5 goals in four straight meetings against St. Louis Blues this season. They will outshoot you tonight.

The Bureau's Development Index has four Leafs on the risers this check: Gaborik at +6, Klesla at +5, Rick Nash82 at +5, Brad Richards86 at +4. That tells you the talent is peaking in February. I am glad. Gaborik is reading 6 above his base, Richards has held at +4 for a third straight check, and none of it tells me who takes the draw behind Mats Sundin90 on April 6 when a series is 3-3.

If you are outshot and you win tonight, I will not write that it matters. If you are outshot and you lose to the best team you have faced since December, that is the night the pattern broke and everybody calls it a fluke by March.

24 days until what the calendar has as the trade deadline. 43 wins already banked. The Panic Meter sits at Green because you are winning. I hate that it sits at Green. I would rather it sat at Yellow because the record looked comfortable and the second centre was settled. Neither of those things is true.
