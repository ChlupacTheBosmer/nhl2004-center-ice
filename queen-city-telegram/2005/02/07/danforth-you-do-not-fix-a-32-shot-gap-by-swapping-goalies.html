DANFORTH: You do not fix a 32-shot gap by swapping goalies

Montreal is last in the division with 44 points and a coach who says his road modifier is minus six. We should win tomorrow. The question is whether we can do it for 60 minutes

Frankie DanforthColumnist · Queen City TelegramMonday, 7 February 2005
ShareCopy link

58 against. 26 for. That is what Tampa Bay Lightning did to us in Tampa on Tuesday, with 7 of their regulars on injured reserve.

The crease moved overnight. Roberto Luongo97 is G1, Ed Belfour is G2. I wrote on January 30 that Belfour's 16 and 3 with 5 shutouts was the crease I wanted, and on January 31 that he was back at G1. That held for one road trip. I am done writing the goalie column. The 32-shot gap is the problem, and a different goaltender does not fix it.

Marian Gaborik99 has 74 points. He is up 6 on the Development Index. He is the best player on this roster by a wide margin, and a team whose best player is a winger carrying the offense is a team whose structure bends under pressure. When Gaborik gets smothered, which happens in every playoff series, the rest of the roster does not bend the game back.

Brad Richards89 has 54 points and skates on the third line. The second-line centre is Petr Chlup87, who is 18 and has 30 points in 43 games. He is not a problem in February. He is a problem in April when a coach matches his checking line against the second line in a series and that line is a teenager and two wingers who do not play 20 minutes. Nobody in that front office has answered the centre question since September.

Montreal Canadiens is in the building tomorrow. 44 points. 7 and 20 on the road. Their coach told The Tunnel's Marcus Bell on January 25 that the travel budget is zero and the road modifier is minus six on every attribute. They have Richard Zednik82 and Chad Kilger77 out.

We are 23 and 4 at home against a team that has lost 20 on the road. We win this game. I expect we win this game.

What I want is 60 minutes of holding the puck. Not 40 of dominance followed by a stretch where 58 shots come back at us and the crease order changes by morning.

The code before the homestand

PANIC METER: Code Yellow. The shot gap at Tampa is the kind of number that shows up in April if the structure does not tighten. But 38 wins, 9 losses, 4 overtime losses, 86 points, 30 clear of Boston Bruins, and the calendar hands us Montreal Canadiens and Edmonton Oilers at home before we leave town. Two games to prove this team can control a game without Gaborik scoring 3.
