Two goals or one: the only pattern that matters in this Final

Every Toronto win has come by 2 goals. Every Dallas win has come by 1. Neither margin has held for more than 2 games at a time

Sam OkaforLeafs beat reporter · Queen City TelegramSunday, 29 May 2005
ShareCopy link

The Stanley Cup Final has produced 5 games and 2 margins. Toronto has won 3 times, each by exactly 2 goals: 2-0, 3-1, 2-0. Dallas Stars has won twice, each by exactly 1: 2-1, 3-2 in overtime. Game 6 lands tomorrow at the Air Canada Centre with the series at 3-2 Toronto.

Toronto wins when it scores first and holds a 2-goal cushion. Dallas wins when it stays inside 1 and pushes into overtime. Five games in 9 days, five times those two approaches played out the same way, every result decided by one or two goals and not a single one running away.

The defensive floor

Toronto allowed 190 goals in 82 regular-season games, the fewest in the league. The next-best club gave up 226. In 24 playoff games, the Leafs have held opponents to roughly the same structure, and Roberto Luongo97 has posted a 1.81 GAA on a .944 save rate. He has 3 shutouts. Two came in this Final, both on the road, both by the same score.

Marty Turco93 is 14-6 with a 1.54 GAA and a .924 through 20 starts for Dallas Stars. Both goaltenders have faced the same number of shots and allowed the same number of goals over these 5 games. The goaltending ledger is even. What separates the teams is the forward depth, and Toronto has more of it.

Who the wins belong to

Mats Sundin92 has 18 points in 24 spring games. Gary Roberts87 has 16 on a $2.70M one-year contract. Alexander Mogilny89 has 15 on a 2-year deal at $5.50M. The Petr Chlup87 line produced 10 points from a 19-year-old with 16.9 minutes a night. That is four lines of production, and Marian Gaborik99 has not played since late April.

For Dallas Stars, Modano84 carries 23 points and Morrow82 has 19. The two of them have combined for 42 points in 40 games between them, and they are the reason this series is 3-2 instead of 4-1.

The 2-goal margin means Toronto wins clean. The 1-goal margin means Dallas wins on a single play. Tomorrow, on home ice, the Leafs get the margin they need most.
