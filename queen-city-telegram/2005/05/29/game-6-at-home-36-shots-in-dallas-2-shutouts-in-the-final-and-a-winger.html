Game 6 at home: 36 shots in Dallas, 2 shutouts in the Final, and a winger whose return lands on June 2

Toronto plays for the Cup tomorrow night at the Air Canada Centre without its 99-point right wing

Sam OkaforLeafs beat reporter · Queen City TelegramSunday, 29 May 2005
ShareCopy link

Toronto leads the Final 3 games to 2 and plays for the Cup tomorrow night at the Air Canada Centre. Marian Gaborik99 cannot dress. His ankle, designated by the league between April 26 and April 28, keeps him out until June 2. That falls after Game 6 and after any Game 7, which would sit on June 1 in Dallas Stars.

Gaborik carries a 98 overall, the highest number on any Toronto sweater. His 55 goals are 11 more than the next man on the roster. His salary reads $1.10M on a one-year deal, and none of it helps the top six tonight.

The lineup that played Saturday in Dallas is the one that lines up tomorrow. Roberto Luongo97 stopped everything he faced in a 2-0 shutout, and the Leafs outshot Dallas Stars 36 to 15. That margin was the widest any club has posted in this Final. Toronto has carried the shot clock in the last 3 games of this series and won all three.

What the spring has said

Luongo is 15-9 with a 1.81 GAA and a .944 save rate across 24 playoff starts. Three shutouts. Two of the three came in this Final: Game 1 in Dallas Stars, Game 5 on Saturday, both 2-0. His counterpart Marty Turco95 sits at 14-6, a 1.54 GAA, and a .924 through 20 starts. Both goaltenders have allowed 6 goals in 5 games in this Final.

Mats Sundin92 leads all skaters this spring with 18 points in 24 games. Gary Roberts85 has 16. Alexander Mogilny89 has 15. Petr Chlup87, 19 years old, has 10. The four of them account for 59 points, and none of them needs Gaborik to clear a lane.

For Dallas Stars, Mike Modano88 has 23 points in 20 games, the highest total in the league this spring. Brenden Morrow86 has 19. The Dallas top two have outproduced the Toronto top two over the full spring, and it has not turned the series.

The regular-season echo

Toronto went 4-1-1 against Dallas Stars this season, outscoring them 16 to 9. The Leafs gave up 190 goals in 82 regular-season games, the fewest in the league by 34. That defense has held in the postseason. The Final is 3-2 because Toronto Maple Leafs has scored 9 goals on 6 against, and Dallas Stars has never led this series after Game 2.

The building that watched Toronto close out Washington Capitals in Game 7 is full tomorrow night, and its club enters on a 3-game win streak after trailing 1-2. If the Leafs close it out, Gaborik lifts a Cup in street clothes on June 2.
