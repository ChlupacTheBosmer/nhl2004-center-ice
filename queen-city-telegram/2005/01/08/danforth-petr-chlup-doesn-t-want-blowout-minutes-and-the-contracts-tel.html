DANFORTH: Petr Chlup doesn't want blowout minutes, and the contracts tell me why that matters

The 18-year-old's answer in the Tunnel says more about this roster's future than 69 points ever could

Frankie DanforthColumnist · Queen City TelegramSaturday, 8 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Petr Chlup87 told The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk that he scored top shelf on New Jersey in the 7-0 game, and then added a sentence I have read back three times: "I want those minutes when it's one-nil. That's what I'm working toward. I don't want to be a blowout guy."

He is 18 years old. He has 22 points in 30 games. He skated 13.3 minutes a night before his return. He carries $2.34M with two years left. Most 18-year-olds would take a goal on a 7-0 night and call it a season. Chlup wants the same clean look against a 1-0 opponent. That is the answer of a player who knows what a centre does when the game is tight.

The window has a price, and it comes due on schedule

Here is the top of the Toronto Maple Leafs roster by age. Mats Sundin92, 33, $8.40M, five years left. Alexander Mogilny89, 35, $5.50M, two years. Gary Roberts87, 38, $2.70M, one year. Ed Belfour, 39, $6.50M, one year. Four men over 33. The two oldest are in the last year of their deals. Mogilny has two. Only Sundin has room to grow old in this sweater.

Mogilny told The Tunnel's Marcus Bell that the standings mean nothing in February. He is right. He is also the first to say it out loud: "I want the puck more than I show. That is the truth." He is 35 with $5.50M. When those two years are gone, the 35-year-old is gone. The 22-year-old with 61 points stays.

Now the kids. Marian Gaborik99, 22, 61 points, $1.10M. Brad Richards89, 24, 46 points, $1.00M. Rick Nash85, 20, 30 points, $1.20M. Rostislav Klesla87, 22, 21 points, $0.90M. Steve Eminger82, 21, 18 points, $1.20M. Carlo Colaiacovo76, 21, 11 assists, $1.10M. Chlup, 18, 22 points, $2.34M.

The veterans are expensive and expiring. The kids are cheap and climbing on the Bureau's Development Index. I have been logging the second-centre problem since 2004-12, the first thing I ever filed on this desk. Brad Richards has 46 points on a million-dollar cap hit with one year left, and the Index has him at plus 4. I was wrong about wanting a different man. Richards is the second centre. He has been the second centre all season and I just did not trust the sheet.

The Panic Meter stays at Green. The first time I said Green this month it was because the wins kept coming and I did not trust the schedule. Now I say Green because I finally looked at the contracts and saw the shape of the roster. Four old men are walking out of this window and seven young men are walking in. The road trip is four of five, starting Monday at Pittsburgh. I will hate every bus ride and every morning skate in a strange building, and I expect us to win most of it anyway.
