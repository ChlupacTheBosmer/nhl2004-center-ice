DANFORTH: Tellqvist falls three spots on the Index, and the crease question gets one inch louder

Three goaltenders on expiring contracts, one of them 39, and a backup whose form is sliding as the most important month arrives.

Frankie DanforthColumnist · Queen City TelegramTuesday, 18 January 2005
ShareCopy link

The piece

Mikael Tellqvist75 is 3 spots below his base on the Bureau's Development Index this edition. The overall reads 74, the base 77. Ten games played, a 25-year-old backup on a one-year contract at $0.80M, and a faller's grade in the middle of the season's back half.

The Panic Meter is Code Green. Toronto is 34-8-1 with 75 points, 26 ahead of the second-place club in the Northeast. Nothing about tonight, or Thursday, or the next three weeks, requires anyone to lose sleep.

I am losing patience with the crease question, and this Index drop is why.

Ed Belfour93 is 39. His contract pays $6.50M for one year and expires after this season. David Aebischer86 has 19 starts, an overall of 85, and a $0.50M cap hit that also expires. Tellqvist's $0.80M one-year deal expires. Three goaltenders, three expiring deals, 46 games played, and the only answer anyone in this building has offered is tonight's starter.

GM Ed Taylor told The Tunnel's Marcus Bell on January 15 that the club has never suggested Belfour would not be the number one. "If you look at the numbers and the track record, our number one is definitely Ed," Taylor said. He called Aebischer the stronger second and said the club is "glad" the Swiss netminder joined the squad.

That answers tonight and February. It does not answer what happens in July when three contracts hit the open market and the body carrying the workload is 40 next season.

Belfour told The Tunnel after the 7-1 home win over Calgary Flames on January 14 that the 15 wins were not built on thinking about April in a January game. "They're there because the guys in front of me are doing the work," Belfour said. A general manager cannot afford to stop at that answer when July is four months out.

Workload through 46 games: Belfour 19 starts, Aebischer 19, Tellqvist 10. The coaching staff has rotated without a clear answer through the season's first half. Tellqvist is moving in the wrong direction on the Index. Belfour's age tells me something the coaching staff has chosen not to print.

The trade deadline is 60 days away. A club with a 26-point lead in its division does not need to sell. It needs to decide who it trusts in the crease when the leads are 1-0 in April instead of 6-1 in January.

The record makes the Panic Meter Green. The crease question is a spring problem, and the Leafs are winning enough in January to push it there. But every edition that tells me the backup is slipping, and every night Belfour carries 57 minutes of hockey at 39, the question gets one inch louder. I will keep writing it until someone in this building writes an answer.
