Northeast Ledger: the one series that will not separate Toronto from Montreal

The Leafs lead the division by 26 points. Two meetings with the Canadiens have produced a tie and 8 goals apiece.

Sam OkaforLeafs beat reporter · Queen City TelegramTuesday, 18 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Toronto visits Montreal Canadiens tonight with 75 points in 46 games. Montreal has 38. The 37-point gap between the best and worst record in the Northeast has produced two meetings this season, one win apiece, 8 goals for each club.

Toronto's division slate reads 7 wins, 3 losses, 50 goals for and 26 against through 10 games. The record against Buffalo Sabres is 2-0-0. Against Ottawa Senators, 1-0-1 including an overtime win. Against Boston Bruins, the only club that has split with Toronto, the mark is 2-2-0. Montreal is the one opponent sitting even.

The Canadiens are missing Yanic Perreault78, out with a back through February 3, Chad Kilger79, out with an elbow through March 22, and Joe Juneau74, out with a concussion through January 26. Three middle-six absences in a club that already ranks last in the Northeast in goals scored, 155 in 46. Montreal's home record is 11-11, its road mark 6-18.

Stanislav Chistov81 carries a return date of January 19. He has 12 points in 32 games on the third line. The date lands between tonight and Thursday's home meeting against Philadelphia Flyers, giving the coaching staff a single practice before the Flyers arrive at the Air Canada Centre.

The Leafs arrive on a two-game winning streak: 6 goals at Washington Capitals on January 17, 7 at home against Calgary Flames on January 14. The road stretch that opened January 9 included losses at Boston and New York before the Washington result. Marian Gaborik97 has 68 points in 46 games, a +6 lift on the Bureau's Development Index. Brad Richards86 has 51 points at plus 4.

Toronto's 257 goals in 46 games sit well above Buffalo's 163 in 44, the division's second-highest total. Montreal's 155 in 46 is the lowest.
