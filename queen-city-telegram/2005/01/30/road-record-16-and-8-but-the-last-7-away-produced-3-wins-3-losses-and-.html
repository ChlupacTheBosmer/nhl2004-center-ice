Road record: 16 and 8, but the last 7 away produced 3 wins, 3 losses, and a 6-2 collapse

Toronto's home record is 23-4. The road is where this team has to prove the thing that matters, and the last stretch has not gone well.

Sam OkaforLeafs beat reporter · Queen City TelegramSunday, 30 January 2005
ShareCopy link

The Toronto Maple Leafs Maple Leafs are 23-4 at the Air Canada Centre. They are 16-8 everywhere else. That gap between a winning percentage above .850 at home and below .670 on the road is the one that should worry a team that expects to need the road again in April.

The last seven road games tell a story that runs against the 81-point record. Three wins, three losses, one overtime loss: at Pittsburgh Penguins (2-1 W), at Boston Bruins (2-3 L), at New York Rangers (3-6 L), at Washington Capitals (6-1 W), at Montreal Canadiens (4-5 OTL), at Buffalo Sabres (3-1 W), at Carolina Hurricanes (2-6 L). The only road win since January 17 came at Buffalo on January 24.

What changes, what does not

Nothing in the standings changes because of Carolina. Toronto Maple Leafs leads Boston Bruins by 25 points with 8 division games left. The Northeast is decided. The only thing still open inside the division is seeding, and even that looks secure.

What does change is the confidence available for a playoff series. Tonight's game at Atlanta Thrashers sits against a team that is 9-15 at home but riding a 5-game win streak. The next week brings Florida Panthers on February 5 and Tampa Bay Lightning on February 6, both on the road. The Tampa Bay Lightning Lightning are 15-12 at home and sit at 67 points, tied with Carolina Hurricanes for first in the Southeast.

The pattern: at home, Toronto scores and shuts people out. On the road, the offense drops. The last three away games produced 3 goals, 1 goal, and 2 goals. That is 6 goals in 3 games after a 6-goal performance at Washington that was the outlier.

The goaltending split

Ed Belfour92 has 19 starts, 16 wins, 3 losses, and 5 shutouts this season. Roberto Luongo93 has 37 starts, 16 wins, 20 losses, and 1 shutout. The gap between the two on the scoreboard is the widest Toronto has seen between a starting pair in years.

Maxime Ouellet81 has dressed for 8 games and Mikael Tellqvist74 for 11. Neither has been needed on a nightly basis since the Luongo trade. The workload is split almost entirely between the two starters: Luongo carries the volume, Belfour carries the results.

The question for the road trip is whether the team can carry two goaltenders through February and into the playoffs. Belfour is 39. Luongo is 25. Belfour has played 57.8 minutes per game against Luongo's 61.1. Neither number is comfortable, and neither man gets a full night off.

Mats Sundin89 has 60 points in 51 games. Marian Gaborik96 has 72. The offense is there. The road is where it has to show up, and it has not shown up for 3 of the last 4 tries.
