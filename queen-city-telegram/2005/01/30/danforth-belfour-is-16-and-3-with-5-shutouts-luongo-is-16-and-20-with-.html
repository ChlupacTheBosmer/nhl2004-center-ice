DANFORTH: Belfour is 16 and 3 with 5 shutouts. Luongo is 16 and 20 with 1. Carolina just made the math louder.

The crease moved six days ago. It has not settled, and the latest data point is a 6-goal game.

Frankie DanforthColumnist · Queen City TelegramSunday, 30 January 2005
ShareCopy link

I want to put this in front of everyone one more time because the numbers are right there and they are not going away just because we traded for a 92-overall goaltender in the middle of a 25-point division lead.

Ed Belfour: 19 starts, 16 wins, 3 losses, 5 shutouts, 1099 minutes. Win percentage of .842.

Roberto Luongo97: 37 starts, 16 wins, 20 losses, 1 shutout, 2262 minutes. Win percentage of .444.

Both men have 16 wins. One got there in 19 starts. The other needed 37. The shutout column reads 5 to 1.

And then Carolina happened. 6 goals. 30 shots faced, 24 stops. That was Luongo. The blowout came three games after a 3-0 win over Colorado Avalanche where Belfour was not in the building, and two games after a 3-1 win at Buffalo Sabres where Belfour was not in the building either.

I am not saying Luongo lost this game by himself. You do not lose 6-2 on one goaltender. But when the crease question is live, every goal against the new number one is a data point in a column nobody at Toronto Maple Leafs wants to read, and Carolina just added six.

What Taylor said and what he did

Six days ago the crease moved. Luongo became G1, Belfour slid to G2. The trade was done, the press release went up, and Ed Taylor said what Taylor always says: the best man plays. He told The Tunnel on January 22 that Luongo would start, that the priority was Marian and Brad, that the goaltending was settled. I gave it Green that day. I was wrong to give it Green that day.

What I see now is a team that just got scored on six times with its newly acquired starting goaltender, a 39-year-old backup who has 5 shutouts in 19 games this season, and a schedule that opens tonight at Atlanta Thrashers against a team riding a 5-game streak with a man named Ilya Kovalchuk91 who has 90 points.

Taylor has a choice tonight. He can put Luongo in and trust the process, or he can give Belfour the game and trust what he has watched for 19 starts. I want to see the lineup card.

The Panic Meter

The division is won. 81 points, 25 ahead of Boston Bruins. No one is panicking about the standings. The worry is about April. You cannot win a playoff round on a goaltender with a .444 win percentage and one shutout in 37 games, and you cannot win it on a 39-year-old carrying the whole load because the younger man is not delivering.

Carolina did not change the standings. It changed the urgency of the question. I am moving to Code Orange.

The trade deadline is 48 days away on what the calendar has as the trade deadline. I want a centre behind Mats Sundin92. I have wanted a centre behind Sundin since September. The four kids on the Development Index risers are Gaborik, Klesla, Nash, and Richards. Three of them score goals from the wing or the blue line. One of them is a center. That one is Richards, and he is 24 with 51 points and a $1.00M salary and 1 year on his contract, which means the window to lock him up closes in July and the window to trade for the piece I want closes on March 19.

Nobody is answering me. Carolina just gave me another reason to keep asking.
