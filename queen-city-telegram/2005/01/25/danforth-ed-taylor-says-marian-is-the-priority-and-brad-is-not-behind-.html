DANFORTH: Ed Taylor says Marian is the priority and Brad is not behind. I want to see the lineup that matches.

A 70-point winger and a 51-point centre both make about a million. The front office says it has $20M to spend. The problem was never money.

Frankie DanforthColumnist · Queen City TelegramTuesday, 25 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Ed Taylor told The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk after the January 21 loss that Marian Gaborik98 is "definitely our priority" and that the club counts on extending Brad Richards87. He also said the organization is working with roughly $20 million of room below the cap. The extension conversation, he said, is for the summer.

I am glad. I have wanted the front office to say that out loud since Richards signed at $1.00M with one year left and went out and scored 24 goals and 27 assists. Gaborik at $1.10M with 70 points in 49 games is the easiest extension in league history. Nobody disputes those contracts. Nobody disputes the money.

The problem is usage. I have been writing about the second-centre gap since 2004-12. Richards plays L3C_, the third-line centre slot. An 18-year-old with 26 points in 38 games, Petr Chlup86, plays L2C_ ahead of him. The 51-point man is the third-line centre in a lineup where the second-line centre is a teenager earning $2.34M.

You can extend a man and still play him on the third line. The money buys the contract. The lineup buys the minutes.

The arithmetic nobody disputes

Gaborik: 70 points, 19.1 minutes a night, $1.10M, one year on the deal.

Richards: 51 points, 17.5 minutes a night, $1.00M, one year on the deal.

Both are the Toronto Maple Leafs's best pure producers behind Sundin, who sits at 56 points and 18.4 minutes on $8.40M. Ed Taylor called Gaborik and Richards two first lines in the same breath, and the numbers back that up. The Development Index has Gaborik at plus 6 and Richards at plus 4, both well above their base.

The Bureau's Book grades both at 85 or higher overall. Gaborik sits at 96 with the Index. Richards sits at 85. These are top-six numbers from men on minimum-adjacent contracts.

What I want to see in the summer

The extension is fine. I will write the same column in July that I write now: sign them both, and sign them before the market moves. The $20M room makes this trivial. The Leafs will find the money.

What I want, what I have wanted since December, is the lineup that puts Richards at second-line centre. Sundin needs a partner behind him. Chlup is 18 and climbing, and Ed Taylor himself told The Tunnel in the January 15 interview that he considers Chlup a top-six forward. I believe that. I also believe a second-centre problem is not solved by putting your best second-line scorer on the third line and calling it development.

The Panic Meter sits at Green. The team leads the division by 27 points. The front office is talking extensions. The best player in the building is a priority, not a question mark. I am not worried.

I am annoyed. That is different. And it will stay until a lineup card says L2C_ next to Brad Richards.
