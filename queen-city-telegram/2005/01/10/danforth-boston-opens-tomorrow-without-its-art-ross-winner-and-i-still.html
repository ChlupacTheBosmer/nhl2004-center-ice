DANFORTH: Boston opens tomorrow without its Art Ross winner, and I still do not know who starts in net

Glen Murray's arm keeps him out one more night. The Northeast crown is not the question tonight. The crease is.

Frankie DanforthColumnist · Queen City TelegramMonday, 10 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Glen Murray83 is out until January 12. His arm says so. The Bureau puts his return date at tomorrow, which means the Boston Bruins face Toronto tonight without the man who scored the most points in this league last season, the Art Ross winner, the Lady Byng winner, 85 overall, the reason this franchise was supposed to be climbing out of the basement and not sitting at 39 points.

That is a gift. Boston is 15-19-3, 39 points, 7-13 at home. They are one of the four worst home teams in the league. Strip away Murray and the lineup gets thinner by a margin that matters. Their fourth line already carries a plus-minus problem that I cannot fix with words, and their power play has been a tax on every neutral-zone faceoff.

But a team that is desperate and missing its best forward plays like a cornered dog, and the Mellon Arena lesson is still four hours old. Thirteen shots. We won. I did not like it. I liked it even less at 2 a.m. on the bus.

Tomorrow is the crease, and it is not a question I can answer from where I sit. Belfour is 39, $6.50M, 16 games, 92 overall. Tellqvist returns tomorrow with 9 games played, minus 3 on the Development Index, 74 overall, and a $0.80M salary that means nothing when you are asking him to backstop a 26-point lead in the division. Aebischer has 19 starts and sits at 85 with form carrying him up. Three goalies, 44 appearances, 42 games. Someone sat the bench more often than the sheet shows.

I have wanted this answer since August. I want the starter to be Belfour because he was the Jennings Trophy winner last season and because the playoff arithmetic at 39 means every start matters and you do not gamble with that currency in a race this far along. I also want Tellqvist to be ready because the day after Toronto is eliminated from this race, whether that comes in April or in June, is the day we need him to already be the answer. The two timelines collide in January.

Mats Sundin91 said on The Tunnel after the New Jersey game that he does not look at the table. I do not believe him, because nobody who plays 17.4 minutes a night for 42 games does not look at the table, and because 71 points with 32 wins is a number that changes how a room breathes in January. The table does not start the game tomorrow. The crease does.

Brad Richards87 told The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk after the Boston game this week: "You know, Ed's Ed. Nobody in that room thinks any less of Ed. But the young guy's earned it." That is as close as the dressing room will get to a vote, and it was about the shutout, not the plan. Still, the kid on the wing knows what the coach does not have to say.

The panic meter

Code Green. 71 points. 26-point lead. 4-game win streak. 110 goals against in 42 games. The Panic Meter is about the variable you cannot control in April: a 39-year-old man's legs in Game 7. Not Murray's arm.

Green, because Green is honest. I despise the calm.
