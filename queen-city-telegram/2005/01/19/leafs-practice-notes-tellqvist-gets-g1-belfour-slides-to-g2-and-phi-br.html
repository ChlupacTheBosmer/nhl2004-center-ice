Leafs Practice Notes: Tellqvist gets G1, Belfour slides to G2, and PHI brings two bodies back

The depth chart flipped on the same day the Leafs lost a one-goal OT game for the third straight meeting with Montreal

Sam OkaforLeafs beat reporter · Queen City TelegramWednesday, 19 January 2005
ShareCopy link

The piece

The goaltending depth chart changed on the same day the lineup card sat Brad Richards87. Mikael Tellqvist75 moved from G2 to G1. Ed Belfour93 moved from G1 to G2. Both remain dressed.

The move comes one edition after the Bureau's Development Index placed Tellqvist at minus 3, a form slide from his base of 77 to his current overall of 74. He has 11 appearances this season. Belfour, 39, carries a 92 overall and 19 appearances. His contract expires after this season. He earns $6.50M.

David Aebischer86, 26, is undressed on the chart with 19 appearances, an 85 overall, and a $0.50M expiring deal. Three goaltenders on the roster, all three on expiring deals, and the starter slot just moved between them in a single day.

Belfour told The Tunnel after the 7-1 win over Calgary on January 14: "the team's 33 and 8. I still believe the team doesn't lose on my average nights. I just wanted the blank tonight. Five shutouts, I want six." Nine days later, he is G2 on the chart. The team is 34 and 8. The crease question Ed Taylor called "misplaced" on The Tunnel this week has become something the GM did not predict.

The MTL game: Toronto lost 5-4 in overtime at Montreal on January 18, outshot 30 to 28. It was the third consecutive one-goal game in this season series. The rivalry table puts the series at 2 wins to 1 in Montreal's favour (1 regulation win, 1 overtime win, 1 regulation loss to Toronto). Combined goals across three meetings: 13 for Montreal, 12 for Toronto. Two meetings remain; the next is February 8 at the Air Canada Centre.

Toronto's road record drops to 15 and 7. The home record remains 22 and 3. The overall standing: 34 wins, 8 losses, 2 overtime losses, 76 points in 47 games. Twenty-six points ahead of second-place Buffalo in the Northeast.

PHI arrives with reinforcements

Philadelphia visits on January 21. The Flyers are 15-21-4, 42 points, on a one-game losing streak. Two bodies return into their lineup around game day: Keith Primeau84 (arm, expected January 21) and Justin Williams75 (hip, expected January 20). Neither played in the three previous meetings, all Toronto wins by a combined 23-6.

Toronto's season record against Philadelphia is clean: 3 wins, 0 losses, 23 goals for, 6 against. The Flyers have lost to the Islanders three times, to the Rangers once, and their lone divisional win against Pittsburgh came in overtime. A 6 and 16 home record. They cannot win at home.

The rest of the week ahead: Buffalo visits the Air Canada Centre on January 24, Colorado follows on January 25. Toronto has already beaten Buffalo twice in two meetings this season without a loss. Colorado is 24-15-3, 57 points, and visiting as the final game of the week.

What the sheet does not say is whether the goalie change is permanent or a one-night adjustment. Tellqvist's form is negative. Belfour's is not. The chart moved, and the coaches decide tomorrow morning.
