Leafs 7, Devils 0: Belfour's clean sheet comes with two names on the injured list

Gaborik and Koltsov both return January 7, the same day Boston visits the Air Canada Centre

Sam OkaforLeafs beat reporter · Queen City TelegramWednesday, 5 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Toronto buried New Jersey 7-0 on Tuesday night and found two of its fastest wingers on the injured list by the time the room cleared.

Marian Gaborik97 carries an unspecified injury with a return date of January 7. Konstantin Koltsov72 has the same body-part designation, the same onset window between January 4 and January 5, the same return date. The Leafs don't play until that Saturday, when Boston Bruins visits, so both will be game-morning decisions.

Gaborik leads the club with 35 goals and 24 assists for 59 points in 40 games. He is 22, makes $1.10M, and is in a contract year. He sits at plus 6 on the Bureau's Development Index, the largest form gain on the board. Koltsov has 4 goals and 12 assists in 24 appearances on 9.6 minutes a night. The two-day window and the shared return date put both men's availability behind the same call.

The shutout came against 19 New Jersey shots on 16 for Toronto. Ed Belfour93 finished his 16th start of the season. He shares the crease with David Aebischer86, who has 19 starts.

Alexander Mogilny87 returned from a one-night absence and sits at 15 goals and 36 assists for 51 points in 36 games. Mats Sundin88 told The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk after Monday's 4-1 road win at New Jersey: "Tonight we... pass first. We arrive second. That is the difference, for sure."

Boston arrives with its own injured list

Boston Bruins enters Saturday with 37 points, a 14-18-3 record, and two players on the shelf: Glen Murray79, out with an arm injury returning January 12, and Sergei Samsonov81, listed unspecified and returning January 6. Samsonov should clear in time. Boston is 11-8 on the road, a stronger mark than its 6-13 home record, so the Bruins carry their better profile into Toronto.

The Leafs hold 67 points in 40 games against Buffalo's 43 in 38, Ottawa's 40 in 38, and Montreal's 32 in 39. Toronto's 232 goals for against 109 against stands alone in the Northeast.

If Gaborik plays Saturday, the Northeast race is over and the rest of the season is about seeding. If he doesn't, Toronto still has Mogilny, Sundin, Brad Richards86, and Gary Roberts87, a top six deep enough to beat this Boston team without its fastest winger. But two wingers on the shelf in the same two-day window is the pattern the medical staff watches before the lineup goes up.
