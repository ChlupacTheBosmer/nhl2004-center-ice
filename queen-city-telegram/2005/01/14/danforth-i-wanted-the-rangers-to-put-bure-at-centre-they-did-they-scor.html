DANFORTH: I wanted the Rangers to put Bure at centre. They did. They scored six.

The second-centre problem I have been writing about since December is now somebody else's solution, and it beat us on the road.

Frankie DanforthColumnist · Queen City TelegramFriday, 14 January 2005
ShareCopy link

On the 12th I wrote that Pavel Bure87 was on the wing and the New York Rangers had handed me the matchup I wanted. A winger who could not take the draw, a second centre who could not close. I thought the problem belonged to them.

They moved Bure to centre before Thursday's game. The slot change is on record: second right wing to second centre, with a power-play forward spot attached. The Rangers beat us 6-3 at Madison Square Garden. We had 29 shots. They had 25. They scored six times. We scored three.

That is what happens when a team solves the second-centre problem and you keep telling yourself you already solved yours.

Marian Gaborik97 told The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk after the 3-2 loss in Boston Bruins: "We have the puck, we have the chances, and the three they take, three go in. So what do you do with the 32?" Two nights later the number was 29 and the result was worse. Toronto keeps losing on the road while the shot clock says the game is not lost.

Tonight Toronto Maple Leafs put seven past Calgary Flames and allowed one. Three of the last ten at the Air Canada Centre all went the same way: 7-0 over New Jersey Devils, 5-0 over Boston Bruins, 7-1 over Calgary Flames. Twenty-one and three at home. Fourteen and six on the road.

Brad Richards86 has 47 points in 44 games, plus 4 on the Bureau's Development Index, and a $1.00M cap hit. I have been telling you since December that he is the answer. The Rangers just moved a winger to centre and scored six on the road, and I am still writing the same column I wrote in December. Maybe Richards is the answer. Maybe I have been right for twelve weeks and it does not matter because somebody else found the same answer first.

Washington Capitals on Tuesday, Montreal Canadiens on Wednesday. Both on the road. Washington has 55 points and five straight wins. Montreal has 36 points and is last in the Northeast, but Montreal just beat Buffalo Sabres in overtime. Neither one is a tune-up.

The Northeast crown is not in question. 71 points, 24 ahead of Buffalo Sabres. The second centre is still in question. I have game tape I did not ask for and a column I do not want to rewrite.

The road is where this gets decided

I keep saying the formula works in the building. It does. It works the way a furnace works in July. The question is what happens in the other 41 games, and the New York Rangers just gave me a sample size of one that I cannot pretend is not there.
