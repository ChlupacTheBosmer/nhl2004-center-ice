DANFORTH: Bure is on the wing, Lindros is at centre, and the Rangers finally handed me the matchup I wanted

The Rangers reshuffled everything except their record. Tomorrow we find out if 32 shots was a fluke or a formula that broke.

Frankie DanforthColumnist · Queen City TelegramWednesday, 12 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Pavel Bure87 is playing left wing on the Rangers' second line tomorrow night. Anson Carter86 skates on the top power play unit. Eric Lindros87 has moved to centre on the fourth line, which means New York's top two centres are now Bobby Holik81 and whoever is replacing him at the second slot, because Lindros will not be the answer to anything except a knee replacement.

This is the matchup I have wanted. A winger with Bure's hands, a power play unit with Carter's reach, and a fourth-line centre who will not survive a shift against Mats Sundin88. The Rangers are 20-16-4, 54 points, third in the Atlantic. They lost 2-3 to Montreal on the 9th and they are on a two-game losing streak. They are not good. They are interesting, and that is worse.

The 32-shot game

I watched Boston 3, Toronto 2 from my kitchen and saw a team that cannot finish on the road without getting desperate. Thirty-two shots, two goals. The Toronto Maple Leafs away record is 14-5, which is fine, which is second in the division, which is not what I want to talk about.

What I want to talk about is that Gary Roberts85 is available, that he told The Tunnel's Marcus Bell he likes doing it the hard way, and that doing it the hard way against New York Rangers with Bure on your wing's side of the ice means your defencemen cannot breathe. Jiri Fischer82 and Bryan McCabe81 have been carrying a 23.1 and 22.0 minute load because the team is up three and four and nobody needs them to pinch. Against a second line with Bure, that changes.

The Panic Meter and the arithmetic

71 points. 25 ahead of Buffalo Sabres. I have been writing about the second-centre problem since 2004-12-28 and here is Brad Richards84 with 46 points on a million-dollar cap hit and a +4 on the Development Index, which means the answer I wanted is the one I already had and did not trust. The Panic Meter stays Green. It stays Green because 25 points is not a lead that two road losses erase, and because tomorrow's opponent has 54 points and a 16-9 home record, and we are the road team.

The thing I cannot stop thinking about is Tellqvist. Minus 3 on the Index, base 77, now 74. Belfour sits at 92. Ten starts to six. The crease question is not loud because the wins are covering it. The wins are not covering it anymore.

Tomorrow: Bure on the wing, the man skating on a second line against Rostislav Klesla81 and Steve Eminger74. I wanted this matchup for as long as I have had a column and I am terrified of it.
