DANFORTH: four players on the Bureau's risers, and one of them is in a contract year

Gaborik, Klesla, Nash, and Richards are all climbing on the Development Index, and the second-centre answer I have wanted for twelve years is sitting at plus 4 with a million-dollar cap hit

Frankie DanforthColumnist · Queen City TelegramTuesday, 28 December 2004
ShareCopy link

The Bureau's Development Index lists four Toronto players among its top 20 risers. Marian Gaborik98 at +6. Rostislav Klesla84 at +5. Rick Nash83 at +5. Brad Richards87 at +4. I have been writing about the second-centre problem since December of 2004, which makes me sound like a man with a broken record player. The record just changed.

Brad Richards has 41 points in 36 games at $1.00M cap hit. He is 24. He is in the final year of his deal. The Bureau's base grade is 82 and the Index has him six above where he was last June. He has become the second-best centre on the roster, fourth-best player by overall grade at 83, nine points behind Gaborik at 94.

I wanted a centre who could play behind Mats Sundin91 without needing a line change every four minutes. I wanted a centre who could kill a penalty and win a draw. I wanted one who would not cost $5M. I got one who costs $1M and is up 4 on the form chart. I am going to stop complaining about it. This time.

Rostislav Klesla is the other one nobody flagged early. 20 points, 36 games played, 14.6 minutes a night, +5 on the Index, a base of 78 and a potential of 97. The 12/17 piece on him was early. He has not dropped. Rick Nash at +5 with 30 points in 34 games and 13.0 minutes is a 20-year-old on a first contract who the Bureau has graded at 79 with a 95 ceiling.

Petr Chlup86 is not on the risers because he is 18 and his overall is already 80 with a 95 potential, so the Index has nowhere to climb to. He has 20 points in 25 games at 12.2 minutes a night. Vaclav Spacek77 has 11 in 19. Jason Spezza72 has 14 in 29 but is not dressed tonight. The middle of this roster is crowded and cheap, and all of it is rising on a published form metric that I used to think was a myth.

The Panic Meter

Five straight wins, 60 points, a 23-point lead in the division, four risers on one team, and a $1.00M centre playing at an 83 overall. The only thing that gives me pause is the margins: three of the last five by one goal. That is what winning looks like in late December when the legs are heavy and the schedule is a meat grinder.

Code Green.

Alexander Mogilny90, after the 9-2 on St. Louis this month, told The Tunnel's Marcus Bell: "You make it sound like I was out three weeks. I miss a day here, a day there. My body, it is thirty-five years, of course it talks to you in December. But I am here. That is the answer." He has 45 points in 32 games. I used to worry about him. I do not worry about him.
