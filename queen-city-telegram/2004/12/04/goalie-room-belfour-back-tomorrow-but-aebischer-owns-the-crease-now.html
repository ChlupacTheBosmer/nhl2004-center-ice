Goalie Room: Belfour back tomorrow, but Aebischer owns the crease now

The 39-year-old returns from a 2004-12-05 injury with a $6.50M cap hit and a 92 overall. The 26-year-old behind him has 13 starts, a 10-game winning streak, and zero reasons to give his job back.

Sam OkaforLeafs beat reporter · Queen City TelegramSaturday, 4 December 2004
ShareCopy link

Ed Belfour93 is expected back tomorrow, 2004-12-05, after being rested sometime between 2004-12-01 and 2004-12-04. His return date makes him available for Tuesday's game at New York Islanders on 2004-12-06.

The question that follows him into the locker room: who comes out?

David Aebischer86 has carried the G1__ slot throughout the winning stretch, starting 13 games to Belfour's 10 through the first 25 contests. Mikael Tellqvist75 has made 4 appearances from the third-string role. In that window, Toronto Maple Leafs has won 10 straight, including a 13-0 blanking of Buffalo Sabres on 2004-11-30 and a 6-0 shutout of Tampa Bay Lightning on 2004-12-03.

Aebischer carries an 85 overall on the Book. Belfour, the team's highest-graded player at 92, is 39 years old with a $6.50M cap hit and one year remaining. The numbers say Aebischer deserves to start. The name says Belfour will. The coaching staff says nothing yet.

Morale tells a story. Aebischer sits at 80 — not unhappy, but aware he is the better option by recent results. Belfour's 95 suggests a man who believes his job is his regardless. Neither number screams conflict. Neither number whispers acceptance either.

The immediate test comes Thursday, 2004-12-07, when New Jersey Devils visits for the second meeting of the week. The Devils carry 37 points in 22 games and ride a 4-game win streak. More critically, they have made a goalie change: Martin Brodeur91 was removed from the G1__ slot during this stretch and is not dressed. Ari Ahonen84, 23 years old, carries the G1__ assignment now with an 86 overall and a +4 jump on the Development Index. He has started 3 games. Brodeur has not been placed on injured reserve. He is simply sitting.

The Leafs' next five: at New York Islanders on 2004-12-06, vs New Jersey Devils on 2004-12-07, vs Pittsburgh Penguins on 2004-12-10, at Philadelphia Flyers on 2004-12-12, vs New York Rangers on 2004-12-14. Four of those five opponents sit in the top four of the Atlantic Division.

Two goalies who both want the start is not a problem the Leafs have had to solve recently. A 10-game win streak might solve it the only way that matters: by making the answer obvious.

Correction. An earlier edition of this piece read the league's roster designation as an injury. It is not one: the marker carried here is a form or fitness icon, and the men named were available. Only a designation that names a body part is an injury. The passage has been corrected.
