THE PANIC METER: code Green for the first time I can remember, and I hate how it feels

Ten straight, Gaborik is the best player in the league, McCabe is putting up numbers no defenseman should. So why can't I stop looking at Brad Richards?

Frankie DanforthColumnist · Queen City TelegramSaturday, 4 December 2004
ShareCopy link

I have been a Leafs fan for longer than is strictly healthy. I have watched teams lose to Ottawa Senators in April and teams lose to Buffalo Sabres in October and teams lose in ways that seemed personally directed. I have never once set the Panic Meter to Green. Not once. Not when Mats was 25 and flying. Not when the Cup was theoretical.

So here we are, 40 points in 25 games, ten straight wins, 147 goals for. The Meter is Green. I am writing the word and my hand is shaking.

Let me give you the parts that make sense. Marian Gaborik96 has the Book's highest grade in this league — 96 overall, up six on the Index from a base of 91. Twenty goals, fourteen assists, thirty-four points in twenty-five games. He is twenty-two years old on a million-dollar contract and he is the most terrifying winger I have watched in a Leafs sweater, and I watched Mats Sundin89 in his prime.

And Bryan McCabe82 has thirty-five points. Thirty-five. From the blue line. Eight goals, twenty-seven assists, twenty-three and a half minutes a night. I keep reading the sheet expecting a typo.

Mats Sundin is still Sundin — thirty-five points, sixteen goals, sixteen minutes a shift. Gary Roberts86 is thirty-eight years old with thirty-two points. Alexander Mogilny86 leads the team at thirty-nine points and is thirty-five. The old men are carrying this thing on their backs and the young man from the West is the tip of the spear, and it works.

The shutout over Tampa Bay Lightning on Friday — six-nothing — came the night after thirteen-zero against Buffalo Sabres. Nineteen goals in two games. Nineteen. And Ed Belfour92, who turns into a pumpkin every time I look at his contract, has been rested. Ten starts at thirty-nine years old. Aebischer has thirteen. The workload is manageable for now, but "for now" is the two words that get Leafs fans killed in April.

And here is the part that will not let me enjoy this. Brad Richards85. Twenty-seven points. Eighty-five overall. One year left at a million. He is not bad. He is not good enough. He is the fourth-line-center-on-paper playing second-line minutes because there is nobody else. I have been saying this since the season opened and I will keep saying it until the trade deadline or until someone hands me a true centre to play behind Mats, whichever comes first.

The Meter is Green. The schedule says New York Islanders Tuesday and New Jersey Devils Wednesday. New Jersey is thirteen and two and three — thirty-seven points in twenty-two games. They are the first thing on this calendar that looks like a test.

I'll enjoy Monday. Just Monday.
