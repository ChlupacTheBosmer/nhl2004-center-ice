DANFORTH: the coach moved Gaborik to the second line and I am calling it a solution

Mogilny is back, the top power play has Roberts and Sundin and Gaborik together, and the second-centre question changes shape even if I do not like the answer

Frankie DanforthColumnist · Queen City TelegramMonday, 20 December 2004
ShareCopy link

You have asked me for 33 games where the second centre is. I have told you he does not exist. I still believe that. But Alexander Mogilny90 is back in the lineup, Marian Gaborik98 is on the second line, and the coach has found something that is not a second centre and is not the nothing I was afraid of.

The new look: Gary Roberts88, Mats Sundin91, Mogilny on the first line. Then Maxim Afinogenov86, Brad Richards87, Gaborik on the second. Roberts has 40 points. Sundin 44. Mogilny 42. Gaborik 47. Richards 35. Those are the forwards who carry this season. The top power play is Roberts, Sundin, Gaborik, Bryan McCabe84 and Tomas Kaberle84. The second unit has Mogilny at left wing, Richards at centre, and Nikita Alexeev78 on the right.

Mogilny played 29 of the team's 33 games and sat the previous edition entirely. He came back and went straight to the top line at right wing and to the first penalty-kill centre spot. Richards dropped to the second unit on the PK. The coach is asking a 35-year-old with a $5.50M contract to kill penalties and score 42 points, and asking a 24-year-old whose deal expires after this season to create with Gaborik and Afinogenov instead of carrying a line alone.

That is not a second centre. That is a coach who watched the ATL game, a 3-7 loss on December 16, and decided he would not lose another one watching his best player waste shifts behind the other team's top pairing.

Gaborik told The Tunnel's Marcus Bell after the Florida game that he likes the shorter shifts. "The coach, he say, you go hard, you come off, you breathe, you go again," he said. He averages 19.3 minutes a night. On the second line, with a winger like Afinogenov beside him and a centre who wants the puck, he gets into space against the other team's second pairing. Against St. Louis Blues tomorrow, that means a defenseman who has 17 points in 31 games has to cover a 22-year-old with 28 goals.

I still want a draw-winning centre who kills penalties in the third period of a playoff game. I have said this since October and I will say it in April. But 197 goals for and 96 against, and a 7-1 at Tampa on Monday night, make a coach right before he has proved anything to me.

The Panic Meter is green. I am writing that with the same mouth that said Yellow on Friday. St. Louis Blues is 8-17-2 with 26 points. Win that. Win Atlanta Thrashers on Friday. Then we go West and find out if this team can do it on the fourth night of a trip.

I still want a second centre. But I will take this.
