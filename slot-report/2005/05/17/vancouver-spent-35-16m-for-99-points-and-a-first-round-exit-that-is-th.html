Vancouver Spent $35.16M for 99 Points and a First-Round Exit. That Is the Ceiling.

The cheapest roster in hockey produced the league's best salary-per-point ratio and the 6th-highest profit. It also produced 2 players in the Book's top 60 and a sweep against [[team:EDM]]'s top seed.

Doug ReimerAnalytics editor, ex-video coach · The Slot ReportTuesday, 17 May 2005
ShareCopy link

Vancouver Canucks finished the season with the lowest payroll in the league at $35.16M, the best cost-per-point at $0.36, and the 6th-highest profit at $40.84M. Markus Naslund89 scored 138 points (second in the league). Todd Bertuzzi91 scored 128 (seventh). The Canucks made the playoffs as the West's 4 seed and went out in Round 1. Whether this model has a floor it can't get past is the question the numbers should answer.

Three of the five most efficient clubs in the league were eliminated in Round 1. The fourth (Edmonton) won one series. Only Anaheim, at 4th-best efficiency, made the Conference Finals. The most efficient club in hockey, Vancouver, won zero.

Two stars, no depth

The Book's top 60 lists exactly 2 Vancouver players: Bertuzzi at 93 overall (7th) and Naslund at 89 (25th). Compare the other Conference Finalists: Toronto has 5 players in the top 60, including Marian Gaborik99 at 98. Dallas has none in the printed top 60 but won 109 points on balanced scoring. Washington has none either but went 39-26-8 on a 104-point season built around Jaromir Jagr91 at 84 overall, a grade well outside the top tier.

The problem is the men below the two stars. VAN logged 60 injury spells and 456 days lost, 2nd-most in the West behind Anaheim's 64 spells and 527 days. When one of Bertuzzi or Naslund went down, there was no third option.

Against Edmonton in the regular season, VAN went 6-3-2 and outscored them 111-96. In the playoffs, the Oilers won 4-2. Two of the six games went to overtime. Vancouver's 23.2% OT share is mid-pack; Edmonton's 36.7% is the second-highest in the league. The team built on overtime wins beat the team built on two stars.

What it costs to be cheap

VAN's revenue is $83.38M against a $35.16M payroll. The payroll eats 42% of revenue. That's the lowest ratio in the league and it produces $40.84M in profit, which is 6th. Toronto Maple Leafs spends 50% of its $119.16M revenue on payroll and still makes $46.70M in profit because the revenue base is enormous.

You can have the money or the points. Vancouver chose the money. The league's scoring concentration problem punishes that choice when the two stars are on the same line. Naslund played 20.1 minutes a night. Bertuzzi played 21.1. The next-highest Vancouver forward in ice time carries no grade that would make an opponent adjust a game plan. Vancouver Canucks finished 9th in club morale at 93.6, so they're not unhappy. They're just thin.
