6-for-11: Dollar-per-Point Called the Right Side of 11 Playoff Series Through 12 Rounds. In Round 2 It Went 1-for-4.

The metric's edge is real in Round 1 and gone by the second week. Tonight's Conference Finalist tests both outcomes.

Doug ReimerAnalytics editor, ex-video coach · The Slot ReportTuesday, 17 May 2005
ShareCopy link

The dollar-per-point metric measures payroll efficiency: total salary divided by season points. A lower number means the club got more production per dollar spent. Over 11 decided playoff series this spring (12 pairings, 1 tie on per-point), the cheaper club advanced 6 times. That's a coin flip.

The method: I printed the business table's per-point column before Round 1 and called each series on the cheaper side. The per-point values themselves told me where confidence was high and where the coin was close.

Round 1 went 5-for-7. The two biggest gaps called it correctly: Detroit at $0.84M per point lost to Dallas at $0.62M. Toronto at $0.45M beat New York at $0.62M. The metric works when the financial spread is wide.

The two misses came on minimal gaps. Washington barely edged Carolina on per-point ($0.54M vs $0.56M) and won. Vancouver barely edged Edmonton ($0.36M vs $0.38M) and lost. A two-cent spread is noise.

Round 2: the metric collapsed

1-for-4. The one call that held was the one with a clear moderate spread ($0.45M vs $0.53M). Three misses across spreads from small to moderate. In Round 1 those same spread classes produced 4 correct and 2 wrong. In Round 2 they produced 1 correct and 3 wrong.

What changed? Round 2 has only strong teams. Every club that survived Round 1 proved it could win close games regardless of its balance sheet. The financial signal is strongest when there's a true bad team to beat. Against equal competition, payroll efficiency stops mattering.

The Conference Final: one likely miss, one coin

SeriesCheaperGapCurrent StateMetric Confidence
Toronto Maple Leafs vs Washington CapitalsTOR ($0.45M vs $0.54M)ModerateTOR leads 3-3Low
Mighty Ducks of Anaheim vs Dallas StarsANA ($0.42M vs $0.62M)LargeDAL leads 4-1Failed

The Western metric has the largest spread in the bracket calling for Anaheim. Dallas leads 4-1 with a shot to close tonight. If the metric can't survive the biggest gap in the conference finals, it doesn't mean much.

The Eastern call is a coin. The moderate spread between $0.45M and $0.54M is the same class that went 2-for-5 through Rounds 1 and 2. Toronto is home for Game 7 tomorrow with the metric on its side.

What the metric actually measures

Across the full 30-club payroll table, Vancouver Canucks at $0.36M per point and New York Rangers at $0.93M per point sit at the extremes. In a regular season, that spread produces real wins: Vancouver got 99 points on $35.16M, the Rangers got 84 on $78.14M. But a playoff series isn't 82 games. It's 4 to 7 games against one opponent, where roster depth, goaltending, and hot streaks swamp efficiency.

The metric is useful as a screen in Round 1, when you have true mismatches. Once both sides have proved themselves, it tells you nothing.
