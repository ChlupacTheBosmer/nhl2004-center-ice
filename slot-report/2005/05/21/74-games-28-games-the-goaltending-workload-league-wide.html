74 Games, 28 Games: The Goaltending Workload League-Wide

Twenty-one clubs rode one starter past 80%. Two couldn't get their best man past 40.

Doug ReimerAnalytics editor, ex-video coach · The Slot ReportSaturday, 21 May 2005
ShareCopy link

The piece

Olaf Kolzig92 played 74 of Washington's 82 games, a 90.2% share that no other goaltender in the league matched. At the other end, Robert Esche76 managed 28 starts for Philadelphia Flyers, a 34.1% share that made him the least-used primary goaltender in hockey. The two ends of the workload table show you how this season's goaltending split.

The method: take the top goaltender at each club by games played, divide by the club's 82 games, and rank. That is the single number the league's workload table reduces to for every team.

The pattern the table does not support

The conventional read is that riding one goaltender wins games. Jose Theodore carried 89.0% of Montreal Canadiens's games and finished with 66 points, dead last in the East. Roman Turek held 86.6% for Calgary Flames, which finished with 70 points and dead last in the West. Patrick Lalime played 86.6% for Ottawa Senators, 13th in the East with 77 points.

Three of the eight clubs with the highest single-goalie share missed the playoffs. The riders don't win more often than the committees, and they usually don't have a choice.

What separates the playoff clubs from the non-playoff clubs is whether that 85% or 90% belongs to a goaltender the Book rates above 85. Kolzig is 87. Theodore is 91. Turek is not in the Book's top 60. The pattern is "ride one man who is good," not "ride one man."

The two clubs that had no one to ride

Philadelphia Flyers's goaltending season broke in slow motion. The league's workload table lists only Robert Esche (28 GP) and Neil Little72 (8 GP) at Philadelphia Flyers. The injury ledger carries 5 spells against Esche's name and 2 against Little's. Philadelphia Flyers lost 48 total injury spells and 324 days across the roster this season.

Florida Panthers ran into the same problem with two men in rotation. Felix Potvin played 37 games at a 45.1% share. David Aebischer88 played 33 at 40.2%. Each carried 4 separate spells. Florida Panthers lost 52 spells and 216 days. Neither man held the net long enough to build rhythm, and the club's 76 points put them 8 behind the playoff line.

Tommy Salo is the cautionary tale at the opposite end of the workload curve. Salo played 71 of Edmonton Oilers's 82 games (86.6%), carried a 74 morale reading (the lowest on his club), and sat on a 4-year deal worth $3.50M. Edmonton Oilers survived to 98 points and a second-round series with Anaheim. The starter did not enjoy it.

The three starters hitting the market

Marty Turco ($0.60M, overall 94, 85.4% share), Jose Theodore ($5.50M, overall 91, 89.0% share), and Roman Cechmanek ($3.30M, overall 91, 80.5% share) all carry contracts with one year remaining as of this edition. Turco's spring line reads 12 wins, 4 losses, a 1.51 GAA and a .913 save percentage across Dallas Stars's 16 playoff games. Theodore's club finished last in the East. Cechmanek's club finished 11th in the West.

Turco at $0.60M with a 94 overall is the cheapest high-end goaltending in the league and Dallas Stars's profit line of $22.87M gives them every reason to re-sign him. Theodore at $5.50M for a 66-point club is the one whose club faces a decision: keep a 91-rated starter on a big deal or move him while he holds value. Cechmanek at 34 years old and $3.30M sits between those two poles.

The draft is 18 days away. Whatever Montreal Canadiens does with Theodore between now and July, the decision starts here: 73 games, 89.0% of a season, 66 points.
