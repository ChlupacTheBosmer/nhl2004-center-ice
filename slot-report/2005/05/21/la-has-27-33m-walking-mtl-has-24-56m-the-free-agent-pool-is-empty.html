LA Has $27.33M Walking. MTL Has $24.56M. The Free-Agent Pool Is Empty.

Two clubs carry over half their payroll on expiring deals. Neither has to re-sign anyone this summer. Both should.

Doug ReimerAnalytics editor, ex-video coach · The Slot ReportSaturday, 21 May 2005
ShareCopy link

Los Angeles Kings carries 14 contracts with one year remaining, worth $27.33M. Montreal Canadiens carries 15, worth $24.56M. Those are the two largest expiring payrolls in the league by total dollars, and both clubs cross the threshold where more than half their books come due at once.

The free-agent pool, as things stand, has nobody in it. Washington's GM told The Tunnel this week that there is no one unsigned. Every player in this league is attached to a contract. The summer will be about which clubs decide to let their own men walk.

What LA can afford to lose

Los Angeles Kings finished 79 points, 11th in the West. Their revenue of $73.32M produced a profit of $16.34M. Their payroll of $52.43M is mid-pack. If they let every expiring man walk, they drop to $25.10M in committed salary.

The three expiring players whose names the Book puts in the league's top 60 are Roman Cechmanek84 (overall 91, $3.30M, 66 GP), Jason Allison80 (overall 87, $6.70M, 77 GP) and Adam Deadmarsh81 (overall 87, $2.80M, 31 GP). Allison is the one who costs real money. His 91 points over 77 games made him the production center of a team that never made October. At $6.70M he is the second-highest-paid expiring player in hockey, behind only Chris Pronger86 ($9.50M, St. Louis Blues).

Deadmarsh played 31 games. He carried spells for the groin, the knee, and unnamed issues across the season. At $2.80M, re-signing him is a bet on health, not production.

What LA keeps if they reset: Mike Cammalleri81 (form +6 on the Index, 61 games, 60 points) and Jared Aulin71 (form +6, 72 games, 15 points). The cap room to rebuild through the draft and then sign from a market that is empty is what makes LA's situation different from every other club on this table.

What MTL is deciding

Montreal Canadiens finished 66 points. Last in the East. Their 15 expiring contracts total $24.56M against a payroll of $45.26M, and their revenue ($80.19M) and profit ($21.59M) tell you the money is there. They can re-sign everyone. They can re-sign no one.

The single decision that defines their summer is Jose Theodore87. Overall 91, $5.50M, 73 games played, 89.0% of the club's season in his hands. He is the highest-rated player on a club that won 21 games. His contract-year class row shows morale at 94, which means he is not unhappy. Whether a 66-point club wants to carry a 91-rated goaltender on a $5.50M deal into another rebuild year is not a money question. It is a roster-architecture question.

The other 14 expiring contracts at Montreal Canadiens average $1.36M each (from the $24.56M total less Theodore's $5.50M, divided by 14). Those are depth deals: third-pairing defensemen, middle-six wingers. They are replaceable in June or in July through trades.

Mike Komisarek81 is the exception. Form +6 on the Index, overall 82, 82 games played. If his contract is among the expiring ones, he represents the kind of young asset that changes a rebuild timeline. The Development Index already has him rated above his base.

The empty pool problem

Washington's GM addressed it directly when asked about payroll flexibility: "I look at what's available in the pool right now, and the free-agent pool is... empty. There's nobody unsigned. So if I want a difference-maker, I'm buying him off another club's roster," he told The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk.

The clubs with the most money expiring, LA and Montreal Canadiens first, are the clubs that create the market by choosing not to re-sign. Until they decide, every other GM in this league is buying off a roster, not signing from a pool.

The draft is 18 days away. The decisions on these 29 combined contracts won't wait for July. They'll wait for June 8.
