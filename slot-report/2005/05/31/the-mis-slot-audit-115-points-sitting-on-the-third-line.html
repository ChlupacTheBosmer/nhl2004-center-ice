The Mis-Slot Audit: 115 Points Sitting on the Third Line

Five players rated 76 or above are buried behind men rated 10 or more below them at the same position.

Doug ReimerAnalytics editor, ex-video coach · The Slot ReportTuesday, 31 May 2005
ShareCopy link

Sergei Samsonov82 scored 115 points in 67 games for Boston Bruins at a pace of 141 per 82. He centered the third line. The men he played behind: Mike Knuble74 at 76 overall on the first line, Ivan Huml72 at 74 on the second. The grade gap between the league's 12th-leading scorer and the player directly above him is 10 points, the single largest mis-slot on the roster.

The method: third-liners rated above first-liners

The Bureau's line-chart data lets me flag every instance where a man slotted on L3 or L4 carries a higher overall than a teammate at the same position on L1 or L2. The gap is the difference in overall grades. A gap of 10 or more is not a coaching preference; it is a man wasted.

The table below lists every case in the league with a gap of 8 or more, ranked by size. Production figures appear where the sheet carries them.

The Samsonov entry is the one that ends a season. He played 21.3 minutes a night, centered the third line, and still scored at a pace that would have won the Art Ross in most other conferences. Joe Thornton86, the 1st overall pick, played the 1st line center slot at 88 overall. Samsonov is two grade points below Thornton and scored 4 more points in 15 fewer games.

BOS lost 400 days to injury this season, the 8th-highest total in the league. Samsonov missed 7 separate spells, including a groin injury from 2005-01-19 to 2005-01-30 and an elbow injury from 2005-04-04 to 2005-04-13. When he was in the lineup, the coach gave him 15 fewer minutes than his production demanded.

Buffalo buried four men behind a 66

Buffalo Sabres has the densest concentration of mis-slots in the league. The player at the bottom of every one of them is Eric Boulton67, a 66-overall enforcer who carried top-pair minutes on the left side. Every Buffalo mis-slot in the table above is a man rated above Boulton who plays on the same side of the depth chart.

Miroslav Satan at 80 is the worst case: 14 grade points above the man whose slot he should hold. He played left wing on L3 and fourth-line power play. Jean-Pierre Dumont at 77 sits on L3 right wing, 8 above Tony Virta69 at 69. Andrew Brunette at 74 centers L4 and plays first-line penalty kill, which means the coach trusted him on the penalty kill but not at even strength on a top line. Ulf Dahlen75 at 75 centers L3. Four players rated 74 to 80, all behind a man rated 66.

BUF missed the playoffs by 0 points to ATL (both at 84, BUF behind on wins). The grade surplus sitting on the third and fourth lines is larger than any single line BUF needed to win a playoff berth.

Carolina stacked two 80s on one line

Carolina Hurricanes put Rod Brind&#x27;Amour, an 80-overall center who won the Selke Trophy last season, on the third line behind Steve Rucchin76 at 76. The gap is only 4 grade points, which makes it look reasonable until you check who Brind'Amour was playing beside: Mike Peluso63, rated 62.

The same club put Pavel Brendl, a 77-rated right winger, on L4. The gap between Brendl and Peluso on the depth chart is 15 points, the largest single grade gap in the league. Radim Vrbata at 76 is on L3 with a 14-point gap to the same 62. Jeff Heerema70 at 72 is on L4, also above Peluso.

CAR finished 5th in the East at 100 points and were swept by Washington Capitals in Round 1. The Brind'Amour line scored at a rate the third-line slot caps. You cannot build a Cup run on a third line that should be a first line.

What the gaps cost

The five players rated 76 or above buried behind men rated below 67: Samsonov (86), Satan (80), Brendl (77), Arvedson (76), Vrbata (76). That is a combined 395 overall points of top-six talent slotted as depth. The clubs carrying them: BOS (97 pts, playoff), BUF (84 pts, out), CAR (100 pts, swept), ATL (84 pts, playoff).

BOS and ATL both made the playoffs with these men on the bottom half of their roster. BUF and CAR did not. The difference between 84 and 100 points is roughly the production a 77- or 80-rated player generates when he gets 18 minutes instead of 11.

Arvedson played 11.6 minutes a night across 82 games at 76 overall. His morale reads 74, tied for the 4th-lowest in the league. The contract carries $2.50M and 2 more years. He turns 34 in September.
