Dollar-per-Point: The League's 5 Best-Value Clubs Are a Perfect 5-for-5 for the Playoffs

The bottom 5 in cost per point include 3 that will miss. Payroll doesn't buy wins. Efficiency does.

Doug ReimerAnalytics editor, ex-video coach · The Slot ReportMonday, 7 March 2005
ShareCopy link

The 5 clubs that spend the fewest dollars per point produced are 5-for-5 for playoff positions. The 5 that spend the most per point produced include 3 that will not be in the postseason.

I sorted all 30 clubs by payroll divided by points earned through 65-69 games. The league average sits at $0.72M per point. Full board below, with conference standings attached:

The top 5 cost per point, from Vancouver Canucks at $0.39M through Tampa Bay Lightning at $0.53M, all hold playoff positions. The bottom 5, from Philadelphia Flyers at $1.10M through Calgary Flames at $0.88M, include Philadelphia Flyers, Calgary Flames and St. Louis Blues sitting outside the eight. Only Detroit Red Wings and New York Rangers are alive, and both hold the final spot in their conference by 8 points or less.

Cheap rosters outperform their payroll

Vancouver Canucks carries the lowest payroll in hockey at $35.16M. Their $0.39M per point is nearly half the league average. Edmonton Oilers sits second-lowest at $36.81M and produces at $0.43M per point. These two clubs spend less than half of what Detroit Red Wings or New York Rangers spend and earn the same or more.

The 5 best-value clubs share another trait: 4 of the 5 sit below the league's median payroll. Vancouver Canucks is 30th of 30, Edmonton Oilers is 28th, New Jersey Devils is 19th, Mighty Ducks of Anaheim is 25th. Only Tampa Bay Lightning sits at the median line ($47.75M, 15th of 30).

On the other end, the 3 most expensive clubs in hockey, Detroit Red Wings at $80.89M, New York Rangers at $77.49M and Colorado Avalanche at $71.56M, produce very different efficiency. Colorado Avalanche gets $0.82M per point, roughly league-average, because their 87 points amortize the payroll. Detroit Red Wings at $1.08M and New York Rangers at $1.02M get roughly 15 points less than Colorado Avalanche on nearly identical spending.

What the gap says about the deadline

With 12 days to what the calendar has as the trade deadline, the efficiency ranking suggests which clubs should be buyers and which should be sellers. Philadelphia Flyers pays $1.10M per point and carries the league's 4th-highest payroll. Their max of 97 points sits 29 above the 8th-place New York Islanders at 68, but Philadelphia Flyers would need 34 points from 17 games at a 0.97 pace to reach even 80. They're selling. Calgary Flames and St. Louis Blues face the same arithmetic: too expensive for the product, too far behind to catch the pace.

Detroit Red Wings and New York Rangers present the interesting case. Both are expensive, both are inefficient, both are holding on to playoff spots by 6 to 8 points. Detroit Red Wings has 16 games left; Phoenix Coyotes has 17 and trails by 6. New York Rangers has 13 games left; New York Islanders has 16 and trails by 8. Both clubs are paying more per point than any other playoff-position team except each other, and they cannot afford to lose their current pace because the cushion is thin.

Across the full 30-club board, points pace tracks the per-point column almost linearly through the top 15. Below that line the noise grows because clubs at 52 to 65 points are spread across a $30M payroll range, but every club producing 80+ points pays at or below $0.82M per point.
