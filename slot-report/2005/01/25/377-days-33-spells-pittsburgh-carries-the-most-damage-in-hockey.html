377 Days, 33 Spells: Pittsburgh Carries the Most Damage in Hockey

The Penguins have lost more time to injury than any club in the league and sit 4 points from 8th. The 39-year-old center absorbing it all is producing at a 158-point pace.

Doug ReimerAnalytics editor, ex-video coach · The Slot ReportTuesday, 25 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Sort all 30 clubs by total days lost to the league's injury list. Pittsburgh Penguins is first at 377. Tampa Bay Lightning is second at 336. The gap between 1st and 2nd is 41 days, the same margin separating 2nd from 5th.

Pittsburgh has logged 33 designated spells across 20 unique men. Nine of those men appear on the list twice or more, carrying 19 of the 33 designations between them. The repeat pattern is the same one the league-wide list shows: short windows, often same-day returns. The 377 days come from the spells that carry a named body part, and those body parts are the expensive ones.

Surovy missed a stretch with a back. The league's back injuries average 31 days. Morozov carried a knee, league average 23. Holzinger had a concussion, average 26. Salminen had a hip, average 27. Pushor and Lupaschuk each landed on the list with a shoulder, average 21. Straka had a wrist, average 28. That is 6 injury types above the league-wide spell average of 19 days, concentrated on one roster.

The nine who went on twice

Nine men carry 2 or more entries on the injury ledger. Three the desk can track by name:

The other six are defenders and forwards whose repeat windows, based on the dates the ledger carries, cost a day or nothing at all. The 377 figure belongs to everyone else.

The man absorbing it

Mario Lemieux is 39. He has played 41 of Pittsburgh Penguins's 49 games, missing 8. His 5 list appearances carry dates that, taken at face value, cost him fewer than 4 days. The absences come from somewhere the list does not capture, or from short knocks that add up without registering as a spell.

He has 79 points in 41 games: 37 goals, 42 assists, at 19.8 minutes a night. His pace of 158 over 82 sits one point behind Jaromir Jagr90's 159. At 39, on a Book grade of 89, against a payroll of $9.90M with 5 years left, he is producing like the second-best forward in hockey while his team loses more days than anyone.

The Book's grades tell you what he still has: 99 deking, 99 passing, 96 puckhandling, 97 heroics. The Index has him at 89 against a base of 92, three points below his ceiling. For a man who logged 21.8 minutes a night last season and now carries 19.8, that three-point gap is the cost of 39 years and 5 trips through the trainer's room in 49 games.

The ice-time consequence

With 20 men on the list and many of those carrying named, lengthy injuries, the minutes land on fewer shoulders. PIT's top skater has logged 1,498 minutes across 49 games. The second carries 1,403 across 49. Those two men have not missed a game. The men who have missed games, the backs and the knees and the hips, are the ones the club cannot replace at 30 minutes a night.

Pittsburgh Penguins's payroll is $44.95M. Their point total is 47. The cost per point sits at $0.96M, middle of the league. The revenue is $47.21M, barely above payroll. They are spending like a team that expects to stay up, and the injury ledger says they should have spent it on depth.

The standings arithmetic

Pittsburgh Penguins is 15-23-5-4, 47 points, 11th in the East. New York Islanders holds 8th at 51 points with 35 games left; Pittsburgh Penguins has 33. The gap is 4 points with 33 games for the Penguins and 35 for the Islanders to close or extend it.

The injury list does not predict standings. Tampa Bay Lightning lost 336 days and sits 2nd in the East. Nashville Predators lost 223 and leads the West. But Pittsburgh Penguins is the only club that has lost 377 days, and the only club whose best player is 39 years old and playing 158-point hockey while his team absorbs 20 spells from 33 injuries.

Whether that holds for 33 more games is the question the room cannot answer from the Book.
