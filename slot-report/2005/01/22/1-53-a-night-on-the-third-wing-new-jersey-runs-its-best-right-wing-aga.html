1.53 a Night on the Third Wing: New Jersey Runs Its Best Right Wing Against the Wrong Defensemen

The Bureau's slot system, the points column and the ice-time ledger disagree about Jamie Langenbrunner. The disagreement costs the Devils on the scoresheet.

Doug ReimerAnalytics editor, ex-video coach · The Slot ReportSaturday, 22 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Jamie Langenbrunner88 has 49 points in 32 games for New Jersey Devils. That is 1.53 points per game, which ties Scott Gomez84 for the team lead. The Book grades him 88. The slot system says he is the Devils' third right wing.

The men above him: Turner Stevenson74, rated 74, plays the top right wing slot and has 14 points in 33 games. Grant Marshall79, rated 79, plays the second right wing slot and the first power-play unit and has 21 points in 45 games. Both fall below 0.48 ppg. Langenbrunner, buried on L3RW, produces at three times their rate.

Langenbrunner missed games in early December with an ankle; the league designated the body part and set his return at December 30. He played 32 of the Devils' 45 games. The absence explains the games missed. It does not explain the slot. His ice time, 19.8 minutes a night, exceeds both Marshall (18.7) and Stevenson (17.7). The coach trusts him for minutes. He simply lines him up on the wrong unit.

The deployment and the discount

Langenbrunner's slots read L3RW P2RW S4__. Third line even strength. Second power-play unit. Fourth shorthanded. He costs $2.10M on a contract with one year left. At $0.043M per point, he is the most efficient dollar-per-point man on the roster. New Jersey Devils as a club runs $0.75M per point on a $44.98M payroll, the 3rd-best rate in the league. Langenbrunner at $0.043 per point is what makes those team-level numbers possible.

The Devils are efficient. They also leave points on the table by hiding their most efficient scorer on a line that faces third-pair defenders and gets secondary power-play looks.

Marshall gets the first power-play unit and the 2nd line. Stevenson gets the 1st line. Neither approaches Langenbrunner's production rate. The coach has given his best right winger the fewest quality opportunities.

What the slot actually says

The Bureau's slot system is a deployment record, not a merit ranking. It tells you who lines up where on a given night. When a man rated 88 plays L3RW, that is not a system error. It is a coach's choice.

New Jersey is 22-6-13-4, 60 points, 4th in the East. They have a plan. The plan puts Langenbrunner on the 3rd line, presumably against softer matchups, and trusts his hands to produce against weaker competition. He has produced: 23 goals in 32 games, a rate of 0.72 goals per game.

On the 2nd power-play unit he gets fewer looks with the top four shooters. On the 3rd line, he inherits the minutes when the game is out of reach or when the coach is protecting a lead. His 19.8 minutes are real but situational. He is not driving play at even strength against top competition.

The same flag at two other clubs

Phoenix Coyotes carries two mis-slots the sheet flags: Mike Sillinger81, 88 in the Book, plays L3RW (right wing, not his natural center) above Jan Hrdina71 (78) on the 1st line. Ladislav Nagy79, 86, plays the 3rd line center slot. Sillinger has 29 points in 27 games (1.07 ppg). Nagy has 32 in 38 (0.84 ppg). Neither gets the deployment his grade suggests.

At New Jersey Devils, the gap is wider. Langenbrunner out-produces the man above him on the depth chart by a factor of three. At Phoenix, Sillinger's advantage over Hrdina is 1.07 versus 0.40. Both are real mis-slots. New Jersey's is the most profitable one left unfixed.

What happens in July

Langenbrunner turns 30 in July. His contract expires with 49 points on the board and 1 year of team control gone. Any club with a top-six wing opening will look at 88 overall and 1.53 a night. The $2.10M that makes him a bargain in Newark will not survive contact with a free-agent market that has no reason to give him the 3rd-line slot.

New Jersey Devils earns $34.95M in profit this season. That number depends partly on a 29-year-old Cup winner who produces at 1.53 ppg for $2.10M and accepts the role he is given. His morale is 94. He does not complain. The mis-slot costs the team points, not goodwill.
