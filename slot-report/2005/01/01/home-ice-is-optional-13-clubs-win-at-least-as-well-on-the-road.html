Home Ice Is Optional: 13 Clubs Win at Least as Well on the Road

BOS wins 61.1% of its road games and 31.6% at home. COL wins 82.4% away. The 3 most expensive clubs in hockey split between the best road team and the worst.

Doug ReimerAnalytics editor, ex-video coach · The Slot ReportSaturday, 1 January 2005
ShareCopy link

The method: home wins divided by home games, minus away wins divided by away games. A negative number means the club wins a higher share of its road games than its home games. Thirteen clubs in the 30-team league sit at -3.1 or lower. Boston's gap, at -29.5, is the largest in either direction.

Boston Bruins went 6-13 at home and 11-7 on the road through 37 games. That means 11 of their 17 wins came away from FleetCenter. Colorado Avalanche went 11-9 at home and 14-3 on the road through 37 games. Fourteen of their 17 wins came on the highway. Their road win percentage, 82.4%, is the single highest figure in the league by any measure, home or away.

The league average home advantage sits near zero. Thirteen of 30 clubs are negative. Only 8 clubs have a gap wider than +20 points (DET at +45.6, DAL at +29.1, CAR at +28.8, PHI at +27.8, MTL at +25.1, ANA at +23.8, OTT at +22.2, NYR at +18.2). The remaining 19 clubs win within 15 percentage points of each other on the road and at home, and 13 of those actually prefer the road.

The Money and the Road

The three highest payrolls in hockey: DET at $80.89M, NYR at $77.49M, COL at $71.56M. DET wins 29.4% of its road games, the worst in the league. NYR wins 50.0%. COL wins 82.4%, the best.

The Marty Turco96 Test

Marty Turco told The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk after Dallas Stars's 3-2 win over Detroit Red Wings on December 29 that the difference between his club's 13-3 home record and its 12-11 road mark was the schedule. "You come in sharper when you're here. You're sleeping in your own bed, you're eating your own food, you're not on a bus at two in the morning." Then he named the gap: "That's the bus, man."

Turco's number is real. Dallas runs 81.3% at home and 52.2% on the road, a 29.1 point drop. But 13 clubs in this league do not run that number. Boston won 11 on the road with the same bus and the same late arrival. The real gap is what happens when you trail by one on someone else's ice with 10 minutes left and the building against you.

The WSH Counterexample

Washington Capitals at $57.58M (7th in payroll) went 7-9 at home and 13-9 on the road. Their away win percentage of 59.1% ranks 3rd in the league behind COL and NJ. 13 of their 20 wins came away from MCI Center. Jaromir Jagr89 has 80 points in 38 games on a 173-point pace. Peter Bondra86 has 61 points in 33 games. That's enough individual quality that the building stops mattering.

Compare that to Detroit Red Wings, $80.89M in payroll, $1.84M per point, 5 wins in 17 road games. The Red Wings have the highest payroll in the league and the lowest road win percentage. Nicklas Lidstrom88 is a Norris-caliber defender. Chris Pronger86 is on the same roster. They win 75% at home and 29.4% away. The talent is there. The translation to hostile ice is not.

What the Spread Buys

If home advantage is not a constant, then home-ice advantage in a playoff series is worth less than the league assumes. A 2-2-1 format with the better road team (COL, BOS, WSH) gives that club a structural edge that the standings alone do not capture. COL is 52 points in 37 games, the same as NYR in 40 games. The table treats those as similar. On the road, COL is 14-3 and NYR is 9-9. A series against COL starts with a built-in road advantage that the raw standings hide. The standings do not measure where the points came from, and 13 clubs this season proved that matters.
