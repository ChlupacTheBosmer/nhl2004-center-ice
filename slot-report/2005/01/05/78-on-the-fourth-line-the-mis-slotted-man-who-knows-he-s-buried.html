78 on the Fourth Line: The Mis-Slotted Man Who Knows He's Buried

Ladislav Nagy, 78 in the Book, plays the 3rd wing at Phoenix, and the Morale Scale gives him more than his coach

Doug ReimerAnalytics editor, ex-video coach · The Slot ReportWednesday, 5 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Ladislav Nagy78 is graded 78 in the Bureau's Book. The lineup audit puts him on L3C_ at Phoenix Coyotes, sitting below Nikolai Antropov73 (73) and Brian Savage71 (71), who carry the 1st and 2nd wing slots. The Book says Nagy is the better player. The lineup says otherwise.

The mis-slot table flags 11 cases league-wide. Nagy's is the one that matters most today because of what it says about how a coach allocates a resource the Bureau values at 78.

Method. I took the Bureau's lineup-audit table, joined it to current ice time (minutes per game, games played), the Book's overall grade, the Morale Scale reading, and the salary column. For each flagged inversion, I checked whether the higher-rated man's morale reflects his role.

The Langenbrunner case is extreme: an 85 in the Book, 45 points in 25 games, playing the 3rd wing behind a 76 and a 71. But he's been injured (ankle, 25 GP) and his minutes are artificially depressed. The Brylin case is cleaner: 38 games, 9.3 mpg, 77 overall, playing 4th-line center below a 74 who carries 2nd-line, PP, and PK duties at New Jersey Devils. Brylin's morale reads 74, tied for the 12th-lowest in the league.

The one that can't be an injury

Travis Green at Calgary Flames is the most complete case. 22 games, 10.9 mpg, $2.97M, 3 years remaining, 72 on the Morale Scale (the lowest reading of any player in the league tied with Roman Turek80, Chris Schmidt63, and Jaroslav Spacek70). His overall is 73. He plays H5__/L3C_/S4__, which means he's a 5th man on the PK, a 3rd-line center, and a 4th-line shifter. Above him in the lineup: Blair Betts67, 65, on the 1st wing.

That's a grade inversion of 73 over 65. Green is better by the Book. He gets scraps. He reads 72 on the Scale, the same mark as a backup goaltender earning $4.40M who hasn't been good since October. Green's salary is $2.97M, locked for 3 more years. The arithmetic of that situation (expensive, locked, buried, miserable) is the league's worst contract outcome available in January.

Nagy at Phoenix: the case for moving him

Nagy sits in the mis-slot table because the Book says 78 and the lineup says 3rd line. Antropov (73) plays 1st wing. Savage (71) plays 2nd. The audit says Nagy belongs above both.

Phoenix's payroll is $47.81M for 34 points, a $1.41M per-point cost. The club's morale average is 88.5, tied with Montreal Canadiens for the 6th-lowest reading in the league. Only Daymond Langkow73 at 74 breaks the team's 80-floor among the 15 lowest in the league. The room is not unhappy in aggregate. Nagy simply isn't getting the shifts his grade commands.

A team that expensive, producing 34 points, has no business burying a 78 on the 3rd line while fielding a 73 on the 1st wing. That's the allocation error: the coach trusts a 73 over a 78 in a top-six role.

What the Book's grades don't tell you

The mis-slot table is built on overall grade. It says: if a man at 78 plays below a man at 73 at the same position, the lineup is wrong. It doesn't say: the lineup is wrong because of a style preference, a chemistry issue, or a system that demands something the 78 doesn't provide.

The data supports the inversion, not the motive.

The last mis-slot worth printing: Phil Housley at Detroit Red Wings, 76 in the Book, 41 years old, listed as 41RD/L3LD/P1RD. Above him: Duvie Westcott59, 65, on the 1st right D. The mis-slot is real. The body may not be.

The league's lineup audit says 11 men are buried below players the Book rates lower. The Morale Scale says the most miserable of them (Green, 72) is locked in at $2.97M for 3 more years. Phoenix, where the highest-rated buried man sits, spends $1.41M per point and holds 34 points in the standings.
