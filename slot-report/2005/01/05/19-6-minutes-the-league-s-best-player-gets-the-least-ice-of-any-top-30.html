19.6 Minutes: The League's Best Player Gets the Least Ice of Any Top-30 Scorer

Gaborik's grade is 97. His ice time is the lowest in the top 30. His per-60 rate went up as his shifts got shorter.

Doug ReimerAnalytics editor, ex-video coach · The Slot ReportWednesday, 5 January 2005
ShareCopy link

The piece

The Bureau's Book gives Marian Gaborik96 a 97 overall. That is the highest mark on any skater in hockey, tied with Jean-Sebastien Giguere93 among goaltenders. He plays 19.6 minutes a night. No other player in the league's top 30 in scoring plays fewer.

The player the Book grades highest on skaters gets the least ice among the players who actually produce, and all 29 of his peers on that leaderboard play more.

I ran the numbers before I wrote a word. Method: take every player on the scoring leaderboard (top 30 by points), compare each one's overall grade to their minutes per game, and ask who plays more than the 97 while carrying a lower grade. Every single one of them.

Gaborik has 59 points in 40 games. His pace sits at 121 points over 82, which is the lowest pace on the top-30 table. A low pace can mean a player who cannot finish, but here the minutes explain it. At 4.52 points per 60 minutes (59 points in 783 minutes this season), he is more efficient per shift than he was last season at 4.38, when he played 21.9 minutes a night.

The reduction in ice time has not cost him per-minute production. It has made him slightly better in that measure. Last season he played 21.9 minutes and produced 4.38 per 60. This season he plays 19.6 minutes and produces 4.52 per 60. The Bureau's own form measure puts that swing at 0.14 points per 60 in the shorter window.

The Development Index has him at +6, tied for the largest form jump in the league. His base grade is 91. The current mark of 97 is the product of the Index pushing his overall six above that base. Best player in hockey and fastest-rising player in hockey, and his coach hands him 19.6 minutes.

The efficiency argument against shortening his night

The coaching logic for limiting a winger's minutes is usually fatigue management. The assumption is that a 22-year-old at full speed for 22 minutes drops off in the second half. The pts/60 comparison says that did not happen here. His per-60 output went up in a shorter window. The Bureau's Book grades his skating (speed 99, acceleration 99, agility 99) in a way that should not degrade over two or three extra minutes of ice. He weighs 183 lb. His weakest attributes are fighting (50) and injury resistance (52), neither of which limits how long a shift can run.

Alexander Mogilny86, who plays alongside him at Toronto Maple Leafs, described the style change to The Tunnel's Marcus Bell after Toronto's 7-0 win over New Jersey on January 4: "Before, I carry. I drive through, I make the play with the puck on my stick, 23 minutes I own the shift. This year I play less and I play different. I open the ice, I make the first pass, and I go."

Mogilny is describing his own shift, but the same logic applies to Gaborik. The room has adapted to shorter possessions, quick first passes, open-ice finishes. That system rewards the player with 99 speed and 99 acceleration most, and it is the system that produces 4.52 per 60 in 19.6 minutes. At 4.52 per 60 in 22 minutes, the total goes up and the rate holds.

The contract clock

Gaborik is signed for 1 year at $1.10M. He turns 23 in February. At 59 points in 40 games, his cost is among the lowest in hockey. The Toronto Maple Leafs business line puts the club's dollar-per-point at $1.00M, the cheapest in the league. A 97-grade player at $1.10M is an anomaly in any room.

Next summer he hits the open market. A 22-year-old who finished last season with 131 points, who is currently rated 97 with +6 on the Index, and who plays 19.6 minutes, will not come cheap. His morale sits at 93, which is healthy but below the 100 that Cory Stillman82 and Martin St. Louis85 carry. A man at 93 morale playing 19.6 minutes when his Book grade says 97 is a man who might take that number somewhere else.

The injury record does not explain the ice time. Gaborik has 3 injury spells this season. All 3 are one-day designations. He has not missed a game to the IR list. The 19.6 minutes is a coaching decision, not a medical one.

The Book grades him first in hockey. The ice sheet disagrees. And the per-60 numbers say that 4.52 per 60 over more minutes simply raises the total.
