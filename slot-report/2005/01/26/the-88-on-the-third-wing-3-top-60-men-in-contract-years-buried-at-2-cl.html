The 88 on the Third Wing: 3 Top-60 Men in Contract Years, Buried, at 2 Clubs Nobody Covers

The Bureau flagged 14 mis-slots this season. I filtered them against the contract-year class. Three survive the cut, and 2 sit at the same club.

Doug ReimerAnalytics editor, ex-video coach · The Slot ReportWednesday, 26 January 2005
ShareCopy link

The Bureau's mis-slot list this season names 14 players rated above a top-six teammate at their position. I cross-referenced all 14 against the contract-year class: players with one year left, ranked by the Book's grade. Three appear on both lists. Two of the three sit at Phoenix Coyotes. One of them is the 19th name in the Book's top 60, carrying an 88 overall, playing right wing on the third line, earning $1.80M with 183 days until free agency.

The overlay turns a 14-name list into a timing problem. Three players have deadlines. Mike Sillinger82's clock, Jamie Langenbrunner87's clock, and Brad Richards83's clock all stop on July 28. None of the line cards have moved.

Phoenix Coyotes Puts a Top-20 Player on the Wing

Mike Sillinger is a center. The Book lists him at 88, 19th in its top 60, behind Tommy Salo82 (88) and ahead of Sean Burke82 (88) and Dan Cloutier82 (88). The Phoenix Coyotes line card lists him at L3RW. Jan Hrdina (78) plays L1C. Robert Reichel72 (78) plays L2C. The man with the highest grade at center in that dressing room plays the wing on the third line.

Sillinger has 30 points in 29 games. A hand spell accounts for the missing games, so the GP total reflects injury. The slot still reflects the coach. Phoenix Coyotes sits 10th in the West at 46 points on a $47.81M payroll, costing $1.04M a point. Vancouver Canucks costs $0.55M a point on $34.94M and has 63 points. Attendance at the PHX building reads 12,002, the lowest in the league. The club clears $13.39M in profit, which means the building generates money while the roster generates nothing close to what the payroll should buy.

Sillinger's morale is 94 on the Bureau's Morale Scale. A man at 94 does not demand a trade. He finishes the year, turns 34 in September, and evaluates offers in July. Phoenix Coyotes has 52 days until what the calendar has as the trade deadline. If the slot hasn't changed by then, the only way to extract value from a top-20 center in a contract year is to trade him. If they don't trade him, July decides the destination, and Phoenix Coyotes gets nothing back.

The Same Club Buries a Second Man the Same Way

Ladislav Nagy80, 86 overall, plays H5__ and L3C_. The first-line left wing is Nikolai Antropov75 (81). Nagy sits above him in the Book and below him on the lineup card. He's not in a contract year, so the July deadline doesn't bind the same way. The structural read does. Phoenix Coyotes carries 3 of the Bureau's 14 identified mis-slots, tied with Ottawa Senators for the most in the league.

The pattern is a staff that fills slots by need and keeps players where the season started. Sillinger plays RW because the third line needs a RW. Nagy plays center on the third line because the third line needs a center. The Book grades what a man is. The lineup card fills what a line needs. Those are two different operations, and only one of them produces wins.

New Jersey Devils Clears $33.85M and Buries an 87 Anyway

New Jersey Devils earns $67.38M in revenue, spends $44.98M on payroll, and clears $33.85M in profit, behind only Minnesota Wild's $36.37M. Attendance reads 17,495, second only to Toronto Maple Leafs's 17,598. Prestige stands at 622, behind Nashville Predators's 664. The club sits 4th in the East at 62 points on $0.73M a point. They have played 47 games, the fewest of any club in the conference.

Jamie Langenbrunner carries a Book grade of 87. The Bureau's top 60 places him above Mario Lemieux89 (85), Joe Thornton91 (85), and Ryan Smyth79 (85). The line card places him at L3RW and P2RW. Turner Stevenson (73) plays L1RW. Grant Marshall78 (78) plays L2RW and P1RW. Langenbrunner's grade sits above the man at his position on the first line and above the man on the second.

He has 50 points in 34 games. An ankle spell cut the season short. His salary is $2.10M, one year left, morale 94. The games in hand are real assets the rest of the East doesn't have. The mis-slot is not costing New Jersey Devils wins today; the room is producing 62 points and $33.85M in profit. It is costing them Langenbrunner in July. A club that clears that kind of margin can absorb losing a $2.10M player in the office. It cannot absorb losing him for nothing.

The Toronto Maple Leafs Case: Same Shape, Smaller Price

Brad Richards at 86 sits at L3C_ behind Petr Chlup at 83 on L2C_. The gap is small. Richards has 51 points in 48 games, earns $1.00M, and his morale reads 96. Toronto Maple Leafs general manager Ed Taylor told The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk on January 22 that the club counts on extending both Richards and Marian Gaborik94: "Oh Marianne is definitely our priority but we of course count on extending Brad." The Toronto Maple Leafs case is smaller because the gap is smaller and the salary is lower. The structural read is identical: the Bureau ranks the third-line center above the second-line center, and the lineup card hasn't moved.

The Common Thread Is Timing

Morale is high at all 3: 94, 94, 96. No player is complaining. All 3 clubs are producing. Toronto Maple Leafs has 81 points. New Jersey Devils profits $33.85M. Phoenix Coyotes profits $13.39M despite the lowest gate in the league. The mis-slot is invisible because the room is winning or printing money.

The contract year turns an invisible problem into a dated one. What the calendar has as the trade deadline is 52 days away. Free agency closes in 183. The coaches have until March to change the lineup card. If they don't, the front office gets one conversation in March and silence until July, when the player picks his next team.
