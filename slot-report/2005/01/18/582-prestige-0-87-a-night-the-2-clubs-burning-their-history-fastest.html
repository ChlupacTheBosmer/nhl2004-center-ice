582 Prestige, 0.87 a Night: The 2 Clubs Burning Their History Fastest

Minnesota and Chicago carry top-5 institutional standing and produce the fewest points per night of any club with a real brand behind it.

Doug ReimerAnalytics editor, ex-video coach · The Slot ReportTuesday, 18 January 2005
ShareCopy link

The piece

Minnesota Wild carries 582 prestige points, 3rd highest in the league. It earns 0.87 points a night, 6th lowest. Chicago Blackhawks carries 496, tied 5th with Washington Capitals. It earns 0.80, tied for lowest.

The league's prestige column is its measure of institutional standing. History, market, fan investment, Cups, attendance over years: the sum lands in that column. The question I want answered with 36 to 38 games still to play is whether the clubs with the deepest histories are turning them into wins. The method is simple: rank every club by prestige, then by points per night, and look for the ones where the two rankings disagree most.

The bottom 6 by points per night

The 6 clubs producing the fewest points per night are St. Louis Blues (0.80), Chicago (0.80), Calgary Flames (0.83), Montreal Canadiens (0.83), Atlanta Thrashers (0.86), and Minnesota (0.87). Four of those six carry thin histories. STL has 306 prestige, the lowest in hockey. MTL has 313, the 2nd lowest. ATL has 346, 5th lowest. CGY has 366, 7th lowest. Those four are where the standings say a thin brand should be.

Minnesota and Chicago are not. They are the only 2 clubs in the bottom 6 of production who carry a top-5 brand. The Wild have 582 prestige, ahead of Toronto Maple Leafs (517), Washington Capitals (496), and every club except Nashville Predators (660) and New Jersey Devils (618). The Blackhawks have 496, the same number Washington carries while producing 1.24 points a night.

Washington and Chicago carry identical prestige. Washington has 57 points on 45 games at 1.24 a night. Chicago has 36 on 45 games at 0.80 a night. The gap is 21 points with the same brand behind both. Washington runs a $57.58M payroll. Chicago runs $39.27M. The cheaper club is producing less on identical standing.

Minnesota's office and ice

The Wild have the league's most profitable operation. A $29.97M payroll against $60.61M in revenue produces $36.30M in profit per game. The payroll-to-point cost is $0.75M, the 2nd best in hockey behind Edmonton Oilers at $0.60M. Every dollar Minnesota spends returns a point at a rate only Edmonton matches.

But the team wins 0.87 of a point per night. The Columbus Blue Jackets club holding the 8th spot in the West has 46 points on 45 games at 1.02 a night. Minnesota sits 6 behind with 36 games left. At its current pace it adds 31 and finishes at 71, which clears CBJ's current mark. But CBJ has 37 games left at its own pace and would finish near 84. Minnesota needs to roughly double its nightly output to pass Columbus, and it has not produced above 1.00 in any 10-game window the sheet records.

The top of the list tells a different story

Nashville Predators has the highest prestige in hockey at 660 and produces 1.36 points a night, 2nd best. New Jersey Devils has 618 and produces 1.33. Edmonton Oilers has 592 and produces 1.33. The 3 clubs with the most prestige behind MIN are all above 1.30 a night. Minnesota alone among the top 5 in prestige sits below 1.00.

Toronto Maple Leafs offers the sharpest counterpoint. Toronto carries 517 prestige, the 4th lowest in the top 8, and produces 1.63 points a night, the highest in hockey. It has 75 points on 46 games, 14 ahead of 2nd-place Tampa Bay Lightning. The Maple Leafs' brand is thinner than Minnesota's by 65 prestige points and its point total is 35 higher.

What prestige is and is not

Prestige is a record of what a club has built. Cups won, decades of tickets sold, revenue that holds even when the product fails. Minnesota takes in $60.61M a game at 40 points. That revenue is the prestige at work: the building sells itself.

What prestige is not is a forecast. The 5 clubs with the most prestige points (NSH 660, NJ 618, EDM 592, MIN 582, TOR 517) occupy positions 2, 4, 3, 12, and 1 in their respective conferences. Four of those five are playoff-contending clubs by the arithmetic. One is 6 points below the 8th seed in the West. The column tells you what a club was. The standings tell you what it is.

Ottawa Senators sits in the middle at 436 prestige and 0.93 points a night. General manager Michael Hui told The Tunnel's Marcus Bell this week that the club doesn't have a player to build the next five years around. The prestige column says Ottawa was once a destination. The standings say the room is producing 7 points below the 8th seed in the East.

Carolina Hurricanes is the cleanest counterexample. Carolina carries 310 prestige, 4th lowest in hockey. It has 55 points, 6th in the East, at 1.22 per night. The Hurricanes spend $55.96M, more than Minnesota and Chicago combined on payroll, and their cost per point is $1.02M. They are out-producing clubs with twice their history on a per-point spending rate that sits in the middle of the league.

The waste

I have argued for six weeks that ice time is the most mis-allocated resource in hockey. The prestige column is its institutional version. A club that sits on 582 prestige points and plays 0.87-point hockey is wasting a resource the same way a coach wastes a shift by burying a top scorer. Minnesota has the 3rd best brand and the 6th worst nightly output. Chicago has the 5th best brand and the worst nightly output.

Washington and Dallas show the other side. The Washington Capitals club converts 496 prestige into 57 points. The Dallas Stars club converts 405, 17th in hockey, into 62 points and 1st place in the West. The New York Rangers club carries 335 prestige and produces 57 points at 1.19 a night while spending $77.49M. Expensive per point at $1.36M, but the history is thin and the output exceeds it anyway.

The season ends on 2005-04-06. The question is whether Minnesota and Chicago close the gap between brand and bench, or whether 0.87 and 0.80 are what 582 and 496 actually buy in 2004-05.
