51 Percent, 1st in the East: Toronto Runs Hockey's Least-Dedicated Goaltender and Leads the Conference by 24

Every starter share in the league, ranked. The committee at the top and the committee at the bottom tell different stories.

Doug ReimerAnalytics editor, ex-video coach · The Slot ReportSunday, 15 January 2006
ShareCopy link

Roberto Luongo95 has started 23 of Toronto Maple Leafs's 45 games. A 51% share. Maxime Ouellet86 has 12 (27%), Mikael Tellqvist73 10 (22%). No other club gives its primary goaltender fewer than 55% of the schedule. Toronto leads the Eastern Conference by 24 points over the 8th-place club.

The reigning champions run the least-dedicated starter in the league.

The 5 clubs at 86% or above include 3 of the East's top 5 and 1 of the West's bottom 3. The club at 93% sits 4th in the East. At the other end, the 3 clubs below 70% include the league leader and two teams outside the playoff arithmetic.

What the 85% clubs have

Pittsburgh Penguins's Hedberg is the outlier at 93%. I wrote about him on January 12: his consistency grade of 50 is the floor of the Book's range, and the club carries him 95% of the schedule anyway. His contract reads 3 more years.

New Jersey Devils runs Brodeur at 86%. The 36-year-old carries a $6.70M cap hit with 3 years left and a 93 overall. Tampa Bay Lightning has Khabibulin at 86%, 38 starts, 2 years at $3.80M remaining. New York Islanders runs DiPietro at 86% on a deal that has 1 year left at $1.10M; his form has him 13 above base on the Development Index, the 8th-highest riser in the league.

Los Angeles Kings is the counter-example. Cechmanek at 86% and 40 points. The Kings sit 12th in the West, 9 points back of 8th with 1 game in hand. A high share on a 92-overall goaltender has not produced wins at the same rate it has elsewhere.

The committee tier

Below 70%, the table reads: Detroit Red Wings at 64%, Chicago Blackhawks at 55%, Toronto Maple Leafs at 51%. The first two are 9th and 13th in the West. The third is 1st in the East.

Detroit Red Wings splits between Manny Legace (28 GP, 64%) and Curtis Joseph82 (14 GP, 32%). Both carry expiring deals. Joseph's $8.00M salary is the 3rd-highest on the club's books. The club has 46 points, 3 back of the 8th-place line.

Chicago Blackhawks splits between Ari Ahonen (24 GP, 55%) and Jocelyn Thibault85 (20 GP, 45%). Both are 85 overall. Neither has a contract year remaining. The club has 39 points, 10 back of 8th, and the worst goal differential in the West at minus 25.

Toronto Maple Leafs's split is the one that doesn't fit. Luongo at 51%, Ouellet at 27%, Tellqvist at 22%. Three goaltenders above 75 overall (Luongo 95, Ouellet 86, Tellqvist not graded in the top 60 but carries 22% of the schedule). The club's defense carries Rostislav Klesla93 at 93 overall and Barret Jackman88 at 88. The offense has Gaborik, Sundin, Chlup, Klesla. The goaltending is the least-dedicated in the league, and the team is the best in the conference.

Luongo walks in July

Luongo carries $1.60M and 1 contract year. At 95 overall, he sits 3rd in the Book among goaltenders, behind only Turco (97) and Bacashihua (97). His 23 starts produce no meaningful sample for a workload assessment, but the Development Index has him at base. He is 26. The Toronto Maple Leafs payroll is $89.94M, 2nd in the league.

Toronto Maple Leafs's profit is $28.28M. The club has 2 Cups and 1,160 prestige points. If the front office trades Luongo at what the calendar has as the trade deadline (38 days away), they would be moving the league's most valuable expiring goaltender while leading the East by 24 points. If they keep him, he hits unrestricted free agency in July at 26, with 1 year of team control remaining between now and then.

Ouellet also carries 1 year at $0.90M. Tellqvist is not in the contract-year class, which means his deal runs longer. The club has locked in cheap goaltending depth behind a starter who may not be there next October.

The method

Share is calculated as the primary goaltender's games played divided by the club's total games played, from the Bureau's workload table. I sorted all 30 clubs by that number and lined them up against their conference points. The correlation between high starter share and playoff position is real for 4 of the 5 clubs at 86%+, but Los Angeles Kings breaks it and Toronto Maple Leafs inverts it. A single season of 44-odd games per club cannot separate cause from effect. What it can do is show you who trusts one man and who doesn't, and where each group sits when the standings print.
