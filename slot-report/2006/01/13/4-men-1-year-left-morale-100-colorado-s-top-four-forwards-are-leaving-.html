4 Men, 1 Year Left, Morale 100: Colorado's Top Four Forwards Are Leaving and the Room Is Fine With It

The Avalanche carry the league's happiest roster. The four players who produce its offense all carry expiring deals and perfect morale. The club profits while it watches.

Doug ReimerAnalytics editor, ex-video coach · The Slot ReportFriday, 13 January 2006
ShareCopy link

The piece

Colorado Avalanche reads 98.9 on the Bureau's Morale Scale, the highest club average in hockey. The league range runs from 86.1 at Carolina Hurricanes to that 98.9. No other room sits above 97.8.

The four players who carry the Avalanche's offense all read morale 100. All four carry one year remaining. All four walk in July unless Colorado re-signs or trades by what the calendar has as the trade deadline on 2006-02-22, 40 days away.

Here they are, from the Bureau's contract-year class table and scoring leaders.

Kariya leads the league in assists at 37. Forsberg sits 5th in pace at 97. The four carry the top four scoring lines on the roster by points, GP, and minutes; none is buried or mis-slotted.

The business case for patience

Colorado's payroll reads $66.38M. Cost per point reads $1.16M, 5th-best in hockey. The club sits 4th in the West at 57 points, 9 points ahead of 8th-place Minnesota Wild, with 38 games remaining. Revenue reads $64.79M. Profit reads $21.28M.

A club that clears $21.28M while spending $66.38M can afford to rebuild. The question is whether it should, while sitting 9 points above the 8th seed and carrying the league's most profitable room.

The injury ledger shows 30 spells and 100 days lost. That is tied for the fewest days lost by any club with more than 25 spells. The room is healthy, happy, expensive, and leaving.

What the trade deadline actually solves

The four contracts total the second-highest expiring figure in the league, behind only New York Rangers. The difference: New York is 16th in the East and spending $92.95M for 64 points at $1.45M a point. Colorado is 4th in the West and spending $66.38M for 57 points at $1.16M a point.

This changes the deadline calculus. A club that profits and wins doesn't need to sell. It needs to decide whether the 4th seed is a floor or a ceiling. If Forsberg at 95 and Kariya at 89 believe the roster can win in June, they stay. If they don't, Colorado gets draft picks instead of nothing.

Hejduk is the cleanest case. At 29, rated 88, costing $2.50M, producing 45 points: no contender passes on that at the deadline. The market pays in first-round picks plus a prospect. The alternative is July, where Colorado gets a compensatory pick at best and a phone call at worst.

Selanne is the hardest. At 35, on $6.94M, with 18.5 minutes a night: the trade value is thin. No GM trades a first-rounder for a 35-year-old winger on that salary. Colorado likely gets a late pick for him, if anything.

Forsberg at $9.90M and Kariya at $10.11M carry the most value and the most risk. Both are 95 and 89 respectively. Both lead the league in pace among forwards. Neither will sign a short-term extension in Denver while the club is still 9 points from a division title.

The mis-allocated resource

I have argued for three months that ice time is the most mis-allocated resource in hockey. Colorado is the counterexample. Forsberg logs 22.6 minutes a night at 95 overall. Kariya logs 21.2 at 89. Selanne logs 18.5 at 86. None is buried. None sits on a line that undervalues his grade. The coaching staff has used its most expensive assets at full weight, and the assets responded with 52, 50, and 45 points.

When the assets leave, the ice time redistributes. The question is to whom. The Bureau's Book shows no COL forward rated above 86 who carries a contract beyond this season. The remaining top-six minutes go to players graded in the 70s and 80s. That is a step down of 6 to 10 points on the Book's scale at every forward position.

The morale paradox

Carolina has 14 men with one year left, the most in hockey, and the lowest club morale at 86.1. San Jose has 14 expiring contracts and morale 87.9. Both rooms look like they know what's coming.

Colorado's room reads 98.9. Every player the contract-year class lists for COL carries morale 100. The injury ledger shows 30 spells across 30 players: nobody is sitting in a treatment room feeling sorry for himself or on the bench feeling expendable.

The men are content, the money is leaving, and the front office has to decide whether contentment is a reason to hold or a reason to collect.

The last time a room this happy saw its top four forwards walk in the same summer, it rebuilt around the next man up. That process takes three years. Colorado is 9 points above the 8th seed and can see the window closing. The next date that matters is 2006-02-22.
