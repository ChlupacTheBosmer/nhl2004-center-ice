Faceoff Kings: The Best in the Circle, by the Book

Mike Sillinger grades 96 on the draw, seven clear of the field. The league's best draw man is its cheapest at the position.

Doug ReimerAnalytics editor, ex-video coach · The Slot ReportMonday, 13 December 2004
ShareCopy link

Mike Sillinger82 of Phoenix Coyotes grades 96 in the circle, the top draw rating in the league by the Book, seven points clear of the next man. The Bureau grades FACE for every player who takes a draw, and its current reading puts one man alone at the top of the position.

Method first. Below is the Book's top ten in the circle as it reads today, with each man's current overall. Games and points, where the season to date carries them, sit in the prose after the table.

Sillinger, 33, carries that 96 in the final year of a $1.80M deal. He has 27 points in 26 games and morale 94, and he holds the widest margin at the position in the league. The next man, Jason Allison of Los Angeles Kings, grades 89 and makes $6.70M, also on the walk year of his contract. The Book's numbering and the payroll numbering point in opposite directions: the top draw grade sits on an expiring bargain, the second grade on an expiring $6.70M contract.

The clubs sit in the same order. Los Angeles Kings holds 23 points. Phoenix Coyotes holds 28. The league's best two men in the circle play for clubs that, on current points, do not look like April clubs. The draw grade is the one specialty that doesn't buy a place in the table. It buys a place at the dot and nothing else.

Allison has 43 points in 28 games and grades 87 overall, so he isn't overpaid on production by the Book's measure. He is overpaid relative to the man ahead of him at the one skill that grades. $6.70M for the second draw man, $1.80M for the first, and the cheaper man's club holds more points on the table.

The next two grades sit in the same room. Eric Lindros and Petr Nedved of New York Rangers grade 88 and 86. Lindros has 54 points in 32 games, Nedved 55 in 32, and New York's center depth is the league's deepest by the grade without buying the top of the draw.

The return that matters in the circle this month is Colorado's. Joe Sakic grades 90 overall and 82 on the draw, and he has been out with a neck injury. He told The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk on the sit-down: "It's a neck thing, you know, one of those. I'm skating this week, getting the strength back. The 17th's the date we're aiming at." When he is back, Colorado adds a 90 overall and an 82 in the circle to a club sitting on 47 points.

Behind Sakic the grade falls away fast. Mike Modano of Dallas Stars grades 83, Jason Arnott of the same club 81, Sergei Fedorov of Mighty Ducks of Anaheim 80. The tenth-best draw man in the league grades 80, one point above Fedorov. The Book's two 90-overall centers, Modano and Sakic, grade 83 and 82 on the draw; the top five men in the circle all stand at 86 or better.

Among the Book's top 60 overall, the lowest draw grades belong to players who do not take draws: Ilya Kovalchuk92 of Atlanta Thrashers at 54, Todd Bertuzzi93 of Vancouver Canucks at 75, Markus Naslund89 of the same club at 76. A 96 at the dot belongs to a 33-year-old on an expiring $1.80M deal; a 54 belongs to a 21-year-old with 48 points who will never be asked to take one. The Book knows the difference.

The position's money runs backwards. The best in the circle is a bargain; the second best is a $6.70M contract; the field behind them grades between 80 and 89, and none of it threatens the top. Sillinger's 96, on an expiring deal, will be the most interesting contract conversation of the summer for a reason that has nothing to do with his 27 points.
