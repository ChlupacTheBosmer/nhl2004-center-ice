218 Days Lost: The Most Injured Team in Hockey Is Being Carried by a 39-Year-Old

Pittsburgh has lost 18 spells and 218 days to the injury ledger, the most in the league. Mario Lemieux, who owns two of those spells, is on a 173-point pace.

Doug ReimerAnalytics editor, ex-video coach · The Slot ReportWednesday, 22 December 2004
ShareCopy link

Pittsburgh Penguins has lost 218 days to the injury ledger this season. No other club is above 200. The Penguins sit last in the standings at 29 points and carry the league's oldest leading scorer: Mario Lemieux86, 39, 55 points in 26 games, a 173-point pace.

The Bureau records every designated spell by body part and every return date. I summed the days per club, counted the spells, and crossed the result against the scoring table to find who is actually producing under the load.

The days pile up

Pittsburgh Penguins's 218 lost days come across 18 separate spells. The next clubs are Tampa Bay Lightning at 195 days and 11 spells, and Nashville Predators at 146 days and 18 spells. Pittsburgh carries the most spells in the league tied with Nashville, but Pittsburgh's days are 72 higher, so each Pittsburgh absence runs longer on average.

Who is still on the ice

Lemieux's 2 spells put him in the injury column, yet he has played 26 of Pittsburgh's 32 games. His 55 points rank 19th in the league. The 173-point pace is 4th among all skaters with 25 games played or more, behind only Bill Guerin87 (178), Mike Modano88 (175), and Martin Straka85 (164), his own teammate.

Straka missed time with a wrist and returned to produce at a 164 pace. The top two scorers are both over 30. The rest of the Pittsburgh roster: Geoff Sanderson81 is gone to Columbus Blue Jackets, and the next Penguins scorer with a meaningful sample is hard to find on the top-30 list.

The goaltending shuffle

The injury load hits the crease too. Johan Hedberg85 has 24 starts across 32 team games (75%). Sebastien Caron79 has 12 (38%). Together they account for 36 starts across 32 games, which means Pittsburgh has used both netminders in the same week more often than not. Hedberg's workload sits in the lower tier of No. 1s: Jean-Sebastien Giguere93 in Mighty Ducks of Anaheim carries 91%, Olaf Kolzig93 in Washington Capitals carries 94%. Pittsburgh's committee exists because men keep missing time.

Jean-Sebastien Aubin74 dressed for 1 game and spent 47 minutes in net. His morale sits at 76, below the club average of 88.4. The depth chart is thin because the injuries keep promoting men who then need their own spells.

The contract problem underneath

Pittsburgh spends $43.35M and gets 29 points: $1.49M a point, which puts them 20th in the league on cost efficiency. The payroll is mid-pack. The output is not. The money allocated to men on the injury list does not appear on the scoresheet.

Aleksey Morozov78 has 25 games, 10.7 minutes a night, and a morale grade of 76. He is on the injury list with a knee spell already logged. Keith Tkachuk90 is on St. Louis Blues. The forward group that Pittsburgh graded well enough to spend against is fragmented by bodies that will not stay healthy.

The age problem on top of the injury problem

Lemieux turns 40 before the All-Star break would land. His 19.4 minutes a night is the 3rd-lowest among the league's top 20 scorers. The shorter shifts are deliberate: the coach limits exposure on a body that has absorbed 2 designated spells in 32 games. At 173 pace, he is still the most productive Penguin by a margin. The next man is Straka at 164 pace, 7 years younger, also carrying a wrist spell.

The Bureau's Development Index does not list either man among the risers or fallers, which means their form is holding at base. A 39-year-old who shows no decline through the most injury-shortened season in the league is what those numbers indicate. Pittsburgh has 11 more days of games before the calendar flips to January. At the current rate of 18 spells in 32 games, the projection is 46 spells by season's end. 26 games from Lemieux turns into 26 more only if the next spell is not the one that stays.
