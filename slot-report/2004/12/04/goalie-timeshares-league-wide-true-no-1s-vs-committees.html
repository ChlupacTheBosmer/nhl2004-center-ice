Goalie Timeshares League-Wide: True No. 1s vs. Committees

Twenty-eight clubs have a starter. Only two do not — and one of them is spending $67 million to split work between a 92 and an 85.

Doug ReimerAnalytics editor, ex-video coach · The Slot ReportSaturday, 4 December 2004
ShareCopy link

The method is simple. I ranked every club by its lead goaltender's share of games played through the December 4 snapshot, then classified each into one of three tiers: Workhorse (85%+ of starts), Clear Starter (70–84%), or Committee (below 70%). The finding: the league has consolidated. Twenty-six clubs carry a defined starter; four carry a workhorse who is carrying too much. Only two clubs — Toronto Maple Leafs and Columbus Blue Jackets — split their starts so evenly that no single goaltender owns more than 60% of the net.

The Washington Capitals situation is the league's most extreme. Olaf Kolzig92 has started all 25 of Washington's games — 100% share, no relief from Maxime Ouellet81 beyond four spot appearances. At 92 overall, Kolzig is the highest-rated goaltender in the league to be used as a full-time starter, and Washington Capitals's 27 points and second-best morale average (96.2) suggest it is working. But the Development Index has him right at base. The workload has not moved him. It will.

The Toronto Maple Leafs committee is the one that bothers me. Toronto carries three goaltenders with meaningful minutes: David Aebischer85 (52%), Ed Belfour92 (40%), and Mikael Tellqvist74 (16%). No other club in the league uses three goaltenders for more than 10% of starts. The payroll is $66.97M — fifth in the league — and the per-point cost is $1.67M, which is third-best efficiency on the sheet. That is a roster built to win now. Belfour is a 92-overall goaltender on a $6.50M expiring contract who is watching nearly three of every five games from the bench. Tellqvist is at -3 on the Development Index, reading 74 against a 77 base.

The mis-slot table already flags Rostislav Klesla82 as buried at Toronto's third defensive pair. But the goaltending committee is the larger mis-allocation of ice time on the same roster. A 92-overall goaltender should be the default starter or should not be on the roster at $6.50M. A -3 form goaltender should not be splitting starts with him. The ice time is the most mis-allocated resource in this league, and nobody allocates it more wastefully than a coaching staff that cannot pick between Belfour and Aebischer.

Columbus is the only other true committee. Marc Denis83 at 58% and Pascal Leclaire84 at 33% split the net. Leclaire is +4 on the Index (84 against an 83 base) and on a one-year deal at $1.10M. If the Index holds, Columbus is running a committee that is actually a succession plan in disguise. Toronto's is not a succession plan. It is indecision at the fifth-highest price point in the league.
