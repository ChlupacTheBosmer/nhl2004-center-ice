4 of 15: Goaltenders Own the Morale Basement, and Three of Them Are Starters

The Bureau's Morale Scale has 4 goaltenders in the league's 15 lowest marks, despite the position filling roughly 1 in 6 active roster spots. Roman Turek ($4.40M, 3 years) and Tommy Salo ($3.50M, 4 years) are locked in and miserable.

Doug ReimerAnalytics editor, ex-video coach · The Slot ReportTuesday, 28 December 2004
ShareCopy link

Roman Turek86 sits at 72 on the Bureau's Morale Scale, tied for the lowest mark in the league. He earns $4.40M with 3 years left on his contract and has started 30 of Calgary Flames's 36 games. Tommy Salo81 is 74, $3.50M, 4 years remaining, 28 starts for Edmonton Oilers. Garth Snow77 is 74, $1.40M, 16 games on a committee in New York Islanders. Jean-Sebastien Aubin71 is 76, a backup who played once.

Four goaltenders in the league's 15 lowest morale marks. A goaltender fills roughly 1 of every 6 active roster spots league-wide. The position is 2.7 times over-represented in the morale basement.

The method

I took the Bureau's published Morale Scale, ranked all players, and pulled the bottom 15. I sorted them by position and cross-referenced salary, contract years, and games played. Then I asked a simple question: how many of those 15 are players a club depends on every third night?

Turek and Salo are starting goaltenders. Snow splits work with Rick DiPietro80 (25 GP, 74% share). Aubin is a third-stringer. Three of four carry meaningful workload, and two of them are locked into long contracts at salaries the clubs cannot move.

What the money buys when the starter is at 72

Calgary Flames sits at 28 points, last in the league alongside St. Louis Blues. Turek has 30 starts at a 54.1 minutes-per-game average, which means the Flames lean on him roughly 27 of 36 nights. Calgary's club morale average is 87.0, the lowest in the league. The starting goaltender is the single least-happy player on the roster.

Edmonton Oilers sits at 43 points, 8th in the league, on the cheapest payroll at $36.81M. Salo has started 28 of 35 at 57.4 minutes a night, the heaviest goaltending workload in hockey relative to team games. Edmonton's club morale average is 88.0, 3rd-lowest. The room's two lowest morale marks (Salo at 74, Brad Isbister73 at 76) are both players the club depends on.

Neither man has spoken on The Tunnel in the last two weeks. I looked. The record carries Ryan Smyth77, Hasek, Mogilny, Giguere, Sundin, Turco, Gaborik, Murray, Vokoun, Klesla, Legwand, and Thornton. The two most expensive unhappy goaltenders in the league said nothing.

Jean-Sebastien Giguere93, who sits at the other end of the position's experience (97 overall, 86% of Mighty Ducks of Anaheim's games, no visible distress on the Scale), told The Tunnel's Marcus Bell after a December loss that he knows the numbers: "I see them every morning. And, well, it's, it's everything. Sometimes I'm slow across, sometimes I'm early. And when you're early, the puck is gone."

That sounds like a man in his prime whose spot is safe. Turek at 36 with three years of a $4.40M deal and a team that cannot win is somewhere else entirely. The contract locks the price, the standing locks the result, and neither moves in the next 12 months.

Why the position over-indexes

The Morale Scale measures satisfaction with role, usage, and environment. A skater who is unhappy can be scratched, traded mid-season, or buried to the fourth line. A starting goaltender is alone. He plays every third night whether the defense in front of him works or not. He carries the result on his name with no teammate to share the box score.

3 of the league's 4 lowest-paid starting goaltenders (by salary among 25+ start men) are in the bottom 15. The one exception is Marty Turco96 at $0.60M and 92 morale, who has a multi-year deal and a 47-point team.

The clubs that succeed in goal this season share a pattern: the starter is young, cheap, or both. Giguere (30, 97 overall, ANA at 37 points). Turco (29, $0.60M, DAL at 47). Vokoun90 (28, $0.90M, NSH at 48). The clubs with unhappy starters are Calgary (28 points), Edmonton (43 on a thin roster), and New York (34 points on a committee).

The Bureau's Development Index does not carry Turek or Salo as fallers, which means the form is holding. They are stuck on high-term deals at clubs that cannot move them and cannot improve the room around them. Turek's deal has 3 years. Salo's has 4.

At $0.97M per point, Nashville has solved the problem these two clubs cannot: buy cheap, spread the work, and the room stays content. At $4.40M with three years of guarantee, Calgary has not solved anything.
