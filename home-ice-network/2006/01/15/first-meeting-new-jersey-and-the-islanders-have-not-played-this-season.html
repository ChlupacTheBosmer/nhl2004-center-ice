First Meeting: New Jersey and the Islanders Have Not Played This Season, and Tonight That Changes

Atlantic rivals with a combined 114 points and an 8-point gap between them meet for the first time. New Jersey is 16-4 on the road; New York is 8-10.

Ray CallumSenior correspondent · Home Ice NetworkSunday, 15 January 2006
ShareCopy link

New Jersey Devils hosts New York Islanders tonight at home for the first time this season. Division rivals, 42 and 43 games on the board, zero meetings between them. No other Atlantic pairing enters the back half of January without having faced each other at least once.

The Devils are 24-10-3 with 61 points, third in the Eastern Conference. Their 90 goals against is the fewest in the conference, built on Martin Brodeur93 starting 36 of 42 games, 86 percent of the schedule. Their goal differential is plus-30.

The Islanders are 22-14-5 with 53 points, sixth in the East. They have scored 126 goals and allowed 115. The split that matters tonight is the road number: New York is 8-10 away from home. New Jersey Devils has been better on the road than in its own building: 16-4 away, 13-9 at home.

Both teams arrive on a one-game losing streak. The Devils dropped a 2-1 decision to Florida Panthers at home on 2006-01-13, outshot 22 to 20. The Islanders lost 3-1 at Washington Capitals the same night.

Rick DiPietro90 returns to the crease tonight. The depth chart moved him back to G1 after he was off the dress list; Garth Snow80 started the loss in Washington. DiPietro carries 37 starts this season, 86 percent of New York's schedule.

New Jersey holds a 13-point cushion over 8th-place Philadelphia Flyers with 40 games left. The Islanders sit 5 ahead of the same line with 39 to play. Neither is in any danger tonight, but the gap between these two has run the Atlantic standings since October.

The series begins 0-0. If the standings are still tight in April, these games will decide who finishes ahead of whom.
