Goalie Watch: Hedberg at 38 of 40 and Pittsburgh Has No One Behind Him

The Penguins carry the league's heaviest goaltending workload. Their backup has started 3 games since October.

Ray CallumSenior correspondent · Home Ice NetworkSunday, 8 January 2006
ShareCopy link

Johan Hedberg86 has started 38 of Pittsburgh Penguins's 40 games this season, a 95 percent share. No goaltender in this league has a higher percentage.

The Penguins' backup, Sebastien Caron76, has 3 starts and an 8 percent share. The next closest starting goaltenders are Roman Cechmanek88 of Los Angeles Kings and Nikolai Khabibulin92 of Tampa Bay Lightning at 88 percent, and Rick DiPietro86 of New York Islanders at 87.

At 15-11-6 and 52 points, Pittsburgh is 5th in the East, 7 points ahead of 8th-place Philadelphia Flyers with 42 games left. A goaltending collapse with no depth behind it is the one scenario that erodes a lead like that fast enough to hurt in April.

The injury column does not help. Pittsburgh has absorbed 33 spells and 206 days lost this season, the 8th-heaviest burden in the league. Rico Fata70 (abdomen) returns 2006-01-12. Ross Lupaschuk70 (hip) is out until 2006-01-31. Neither replacement gives the coach a reason to give Hedberg a night off.

The Penguins beat the Islanders 4-3 in overtime on January 4 and sit a point ahead of Tampa Bay for 5th. The schedule opens with Toronto at home on January 9, then New York Rangers on January 11, Boston on January 13. Three straight against East opponents.

Thirty years watching goaltenders, and a 95 percent workhorse share never sits right with me. It's a warning label.
