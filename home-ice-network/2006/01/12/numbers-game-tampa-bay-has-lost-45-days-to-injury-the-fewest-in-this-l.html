Numbers Game: Tampa Bay Has Lost 45 Days to Injury, the Fewest in This League

Khabibulin has started 88 percent of Tampa Bay's schedule, and the only name on the Lightning's medical list returns tomorrow

Ray CallumSenior correspondent · Home Ice NetworkThursday, 12 January 2006
ShareCopy link

Tampa Bay Lightning has logged 45 days on the injured list this season, fewer than any other club. New York Island has lost 352 days, Vancouver 347, Columbus 280.

The Lightning have 17 injury spells across the whole campaign. Their list reads one name right now: Jeff Halpern77, an abdomen designation, returning on 2006-01-13. By tomorrow the medical room is empty.

Nikolai Khabibulin92 has started 37 of Tampa Bay's 42 games, or 88 percent of the schedule. Only one goaltender in the league carries a heavier share: Johan Hedberg86 at Pittsburgh, who has played 40 of 42, or 95 percent.

Tampa Bay sits 5th in the Eastern Conference with 53 points in 42 games. They have won 6 of their last 10, including 4 straight in a stretch that lifted them past Boston and Pittsburgh. The 8th-place Philadelphia Flyers hold 46 points in 41 games.

What the health has bought, in standings terms

Every club between 1st and 7th in the East that has lost fewer days than Tampa Bay either has not had a losing streak this season or plays behind a goaltender with a lower workload share. The New Jersey Devils have lost 56 days, 11 more than Tampa Bay, and play behind Martin Brodeur95, who carries 85 percent of their schedule. The Toronto Maple Leafs have lost 72 days, more than half again what Tampa Bay has lost, and rotate three goaltenders.

The clubs below Tampa Bay in the East that are within 7 points of a playoff position all have far worse medical ledgers. Pittsburgh, 4th at 54 points, has lost 206 days. Boston, 6th at 52 points, has lost 236. Buffalo, 9th at 45 points, has lost 190.

The workload question

Khabibulin is at 88 percent in mid-January. He has no loss record on the sheet, but 37 starts in 42 games means his crease time is not getting lighter. Tampa Bay's next four games are all on the road: at Ottawa Thursday, at Pittsburgh on 2006-01-17, at Philadelphia on 2006-01-18. Halpern comes back into the lineup before that trip begins.

I have spent 30 years saying goaltending decides April. Tampa Bay's goaltending is not the question. Whether the men in front of Khabibulin are all standing when April arrives, that is the question. Right now the answer is yes, and it is the only answer like it in this league.
