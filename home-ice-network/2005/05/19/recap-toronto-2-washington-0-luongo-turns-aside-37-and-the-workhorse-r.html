RECAP: Toronto 2, Washington 0 — Luongo turns aside 37, and the workhorse runs dry

The Maple Leafs win Game 7 to advance; Olaf Kolzig's 74-game season ends without a single goal to show for 37 shots

Ray CallumSenior correspondent · Home Ice NetworkThursday, 19 May 2005
ShareCopy link

Roberto Luongo94 stopped all 37 Washington shots in Game 7 on Wednesday, and the Maple Leafs advance to face Dallas, which closed out Anaheim five games ago.

The final margin was 2-0. The shot line read 37 for Washington, 25 for Toronto. The Capitals had volume and nothing to show for it.

The split that paid

Olaf Kolzig87 played 74 of 82 regular-season games, 90 percent of Washington Capitals's schedule. Roberto Luongo played 45. Ed Belfour93 took the remaining 37. Toronto split its crease all season, and on the night that decided the series, the legs were fresh.

Luongo finished the postseason 12-7 with a 1.98 goals-against average, a .942 save percentage, and 1 shutout. Kolzig went 9-4 with a 1.71, a .926, and 1 shutout across 15 starts. The numbers were close, but the workload was not.

Jaromir Jagr84 led the postseason with 21 points in 15 games. Pascal Dupuis74 and Robert Lang75 finished with 15 each. Sergei Gonchar78 had 14. None appeared on the scoresheet in Game 7.

For Toronto Maple Leafs, Mats Sundin91 led all scorers with 15 points in 19 games, four more than Gary Roberts88 and five more than Alexander Mogilny90. Toronto's run has lasted 19 games this spring.

Two overtimes, three one-goal games

Three of the seven meetings were decided by one goal. Two went to overtime. Two produced shutouts. Wednesday's belonged to both goaltenders: Luongo finished with a clean sheet, Kolzig did not.

Washington finished the regular season with 104 points, fourth in the East. General Manager Shawn Holliday, speaking to The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk after Game 6, owned the gap before the series ended: "The 104 points, I think that tells you the group competed. We weren't good enough in the round after that, and that's on the roster I built. I own that part."

Peter Bondra81 (knee) is out until 2005-05-29. He returns to a team that is done playing.

Toronto holds one Cup. Dallas won last season's Presidents' Trophy, swept Detroit in the first round, and closed out Anaheim in five. The Maple Leafs now face the team that finished with 109 points in the regular season.
