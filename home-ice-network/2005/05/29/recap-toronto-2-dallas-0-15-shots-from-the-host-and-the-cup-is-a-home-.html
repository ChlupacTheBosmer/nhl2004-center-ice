RECAP: Toronto 2, Dallas 0 — 15 shots from the host, and the Cup is a home win away

Luongo's third shutout of the spring ends a 2-0 series that keeps repeating itself

Ray CallumSenior correspondent · Home Ice NetworkSunday, 29 May 2005
ShareCopy link

Toronto Maple Leafs won 2-0 on the road at Dallas Stars on Saturday night, taking a 3-2 lead in the Final. The Maple Leafs need one more victory, on home ice Monday, to repeat as champions.

The scoreline is the same as Game 1: 2-0, Toronto, in Dallas. The shot differential was not. In Game 1, Toronto managed 31 on Marty Turco94; on Saturday they fired 36 at him while the Stars answered with just 15 shots on Roberto Luongo94. Luongo's third shutout in 24 playoff games came against the lowest shot total Dallas has managed in any game this spring.

Home ice hasn't mattered in this Final. Toronto is 2-1 on the road and 1-1 at home across five games. The home club is 2-3 overall. For a team that went 33-8 at home in the regular season, losing Game 3 in overtime on its own sheet and then splitting the next one at the same rink is the anomaly the coaching staff has been trying to fix for six days.

Mike Modano88 has 23 points in 20 playoff games, including 10 goals, the highest point total of any player still playing. Brenden Morrow84 sits at 19 in 20 games. Dallas needed its top line to carry the offense against a Toronto defense that has allowed 2 goals or fewer in 4 of 5 Final games. They scored once in Game 2 and Game 4 and nothing in Games 1, 3, and 5.

Turco entered Game 5 with a 1.54 GAA and a .924 save percentage across 20 playoff starts. He now has 6 losses and 14 wins on those same numbers. Dallas shot 15 times on Saturday, the fewest by either club in this series. In Game 4, the Stars managed 31 shots and scored once. The gap between volume and production has been the story of their spring.

Game 6 is Monday, May 30, at Toronto. The Maple Leafs have not lost more than once in a single building since Game 3 of this Final. If they win, the Conn Smythe goes to the man who has faced the fewest shots per game in 24 starts.
