Bottom Four: 21 wins, 5 goaltenders, and the mess behind the crease

The four clubs with the longest break also had the deepest, most unstable goaltending committees of the season

Luc PelletierMontreal correspondent · Home Ice NetworkSunday, 29 May 2005
ShareCopy link

Montreal finished 21-46-6 for 66 points, last in the league. Calgary went 26-43-8 for 70. San Jose posted 76 on a 25-38-12 record. Florida matched those 76 at 25-36-16. Four clubs, four months of baseball to watch while everyone else is still playing hockey, and all four share a problem at the one position that keeps a team from scoring five goals against every night.

Montreal Canadiens dressed 4 goaltenders: Jose Theodore93 (66 games, 80%), Dwayne Roloson85 (21, 26%), Eric Fichaud70 (14, 17%), and J.F. Damphousse72 (5, 6%). Theodore was on the injured list 6 separate times this season. None carry a named body part. The league did not record one for any of the six.

Calgary Flames used 3: Roman Turek85 (67 games, 82%), Jamie McLennan82 (24, 29%), and Dany Sabourin78 (3, 4%). Turek appeared on the injured list 6 times, all unnamed. Calgary had only 43 injury spells across the whole roster and 164 days lost, the fewest of any club in the league. They were also healthy enough to win 26 games, which is apparently the ceiling for this group.

San Jose Sharks used 5: Evgeni Nabokov91 (69 games, 84%), Jeff Hackett84 (59, 72%), Vesa Toskala78 (14, 17%), Mathieu Garon80 (2), and Miikka Kiprusoff78 (2). Hackett was on the injured list 6 times. Five goaltenders in 82 games, and the Sharks finished last in the Pacific with a 211 goals-for total, the lowest in the conference.

Florida Panthers used 4: Felix Potvin86 (37 games, 45%) and David Aebischer87 (33, 40%) split the primary workload, with Jani Hurme85 (2) and Mathieu Landry73 (1) providing relief. Potvin had 4 injury spells. Aebischer had 4 as well. Both were hurt enough times that neither could claim the crease outright, and the Panthers lost 11 straight at one point.

The draft is 10 days away. These four clubs have the longest offseason, the most picks, and the deepest questions at the position that decides April. Whether they use those picks on a goaltender or on someone who can score 25 goals and take pressure off whoever is standing in the crease is the 10-day question.
