Goalie Watch: Kolzig played 74 and won't get a rest in Game 7

The Capitals' workhorse carries a 1.69 postseason goals-against into Toronto on Wednesday, but the body count is real

Ray CallumSenior correspondent · Home Ice NetworkTuesday, 17 May 2005
ShareCopy link

Olaf Kolzig82 played 74 of Washington Capitals's 82 games this regular season. That is 90 percent of the schedule, the highest workload among goaltenders in the league. He is in Game 7 on Wednesday at Toronto, 14 days removed from the last of his 9 postseason wins, and no backup with more than 7 starts this spring exists.

Josh Harding77 dressed for 7 games in the regular season and 2 in the playoffs, allowing 1.00 goals per game on a .952 save percentage. Two starts. Neither came in this series.

The case for and against a change

Kolzig's numbers this spring are sound: 9 wins, 3 losses, a 1.69 goals-against average, a .926 save percentage, 1 shutout. No starting goaltender remaining in the field has a lower goals-against. Roberto Luongo95 is at 2.09 in 18 starts. Marty Turco97 finished at 1.48.

Against the goaltender's own season: 74 games plus 12 more means 86 appearances before Game 7. Washington Capitals lost 28 games all year and 3 in the postseason. He was in the building for 87 percent of every puck that crossed the blue line in this organization this year.

The Toronto Maple Leafs side has the rest advantage. Luongo split the regular season with Ed Belfour (45 games, 55 percent). He took the net for all 18 playoff games and never gave it back. Toronto's coaching staff had the luxury of choosing one man once the postseason began. Washington did not have that option because the season demanded all 74 from its starter.

What the numbers suggest about Wednesday

Toronto Maple Leafs scored 381 goals in the regular season, the most in the league. Against Washington Capitals specifically, they put up 32 in 10 games. Luongo, with 18 postseason starts under his belt and a .939 save percentage, will be the fresher goaltender by a margin of 40 games of rest across the season.

Kolzig has not been pulled. His 3 losses this spring have been by scores of 1, 2, and 1. The margins are razor-thin across the entire series. If Washington can get ahead early at the Air Canada Centre, the question of a starter answers itself before the second intermission. If they fall behind, nobody on that bench has a Game 7 start.

Game 7 is Wednesday. The winner plays Dallas Stars.
