Goalie Watch: Olaf Kolzig has started 49 of 52, the league's highest workload by 4 percentage points

The Vezina winner has not missed enough ice to matter yet, but 30 games sit ahead and his backup has played 3

Ray CallumSenior correspondent · Home Ice NetworkMonday, 31 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Olaf Kolzig89 has started 49 of 52 Washington Capitals games this season. His 94% share is the highest in the league. The next three goalies, Jean-Sebastien Giguere97 at Anaheim, Byron Dafoe85 at Atlanta and Dominik Hasek at Detroit, all sit at 90%. Kolzig has played 5 more games than the next man on the list.

Josh Harding84, his backup, has 3 appearances and a 6% share.

Washington Capitals is 26-18-6 through 52 games, sitting 5th in the East with 62 points. The 8th spot belongs to New York (Islanders) at 55, so Washington has a 7-point cushion and 30 games left. The cushion is real but the season isn't done.

Kolzig won the Vezina last season. He has appeared on the injury ledger exactly twice, neither a designated body part, both short windows that closed before the end of December. His most recent absence lasted 4 days. Nothing on the current list puts him out.

Washington Capitals plays at Tampa Bay on February 4 and hosts the Islanders on February 6. If Kolzig starts those, he moves to 51 games. He is on pace for 60 or more, which would be the highest total since the league returned to an 82-game schedule. No goaltender in this league has topped 56 starts yet this season.

What makes the number harder to read is the schedule split. Washington is 17-10 on the road, 11-14 at home. Kolzig carries the same load either way, but the road record means the team is winning in buildings where travel and rest matter more. Thirty-one teams play back-to-backs in February. Washington's calendar, as it stands, gives them 2 back-to-back pairs before the February 18 break. If Kolzig takes those, the workload is 53 in 54 games.

A club that has leaned on one man through January and February carries that into the stretch run. Washington's staff knows it. The next three weeks show whether they plan to do anything.
