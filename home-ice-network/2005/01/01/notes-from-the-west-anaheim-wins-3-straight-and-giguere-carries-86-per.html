Notes from the West: Anaheim wins 3 straight, and Giguere carries 86 percent of the load

The Ducks' lineup shuffle from last week turned into two road wins, and the power-play unit held 15 goals against in 3 games

Ray CallumSenior correspondent · Home Ice NetworkSaturday, 1 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Mighty Ducks of Anaheim extended their win streak to 3 games Thursday with a 4-2 road win at Calgary Flames, following a 4-3 overtime victory at Vancouver Canucks on Tuesday. The Ducks are now 15-13-7, 41 points, third in the Pacific.

The lineup changes from last week, when the Ducks sat Niclas Havelid77 and Tuomas Lydman74, moved Todd Reirden72 to the press box, and inserted Vitali Vishnevski74 and Nikos Tselios65, appear to have stuck. Havelid landed on injured reserve with a foot injury, return targeted for Jan 23. Reirden also landed on IR.

Jean-Sebastien Giguere97 has started 32 of 37 games, an 86 percent share. After a win earlier in December he told The Tunnel's Marcus Bell: "The guys in front, they work hard, but if I'm not tracking the puck through traffic, that's on me." The Ducks have allowed 33 goals in their last 10, or 3.3 per game, and the 3-game win streak has produced 9 goals against.

The road record is still the problem. Anaheim is 7-13 away from home. Two of the three wins on this streak came on the road, which helps the percentage but does not fix the split. The 41 points in 37 games put them 10 points behind Dallas Stars and 3 behind San Jose Sharks for the final playoff spot in the Pacific.

Sergei Fedorov90 carries the offense. The Pearson Trophy winner from last season is driving a team that scores only 121 goals in 37 games, the 5th-fewest in the league. When Anaheim wins, they tend to outscore opponents by one goal and rely on Giguere to close it out.

The Ducks host Philadelphia on Jan 3, then travel to Dallas on Jan 5. Dallas Stars leads the Pacific at 51 points in 39 games, and Anaheim needs road points to get within reach.
