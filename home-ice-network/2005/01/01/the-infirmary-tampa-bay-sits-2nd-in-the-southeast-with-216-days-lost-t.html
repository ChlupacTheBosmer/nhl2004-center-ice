The Infirmary: Tampa Bay sits 2nd in the Southeast with 216 days lost to injury

Only Pittsburgh has lost more days. The Lightning are 19-11-3 and riding a 3-game win streak anyway

Ray CallumSenior correspondent · Home Ice NetworkSaturday, 1 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Tampa Bay Lightning have filed 15 injury spells this season and lost 216 player-days to the shelf. That is the second-highest total in the league, behind Pittsburgh's 295. The Lightning sit second in the Southeast Division at 19-11-3, 49 points in 37 games, and they enter Saturday on a 3-game winning streak.

You lose Vinny Prospal to a hand, Martin St. Louis to something, and Brad Richards misses a stretch, and nobody outside the building notices because Khabibulin keeps stealing the ones that should go the other way. Nikolai Khabibulin92 has started 33 of 37 games, a 89 percent share, and the 2nd-place record is his doing as much as anyone's.

The current list is short. Jassen Cullimore75, a wrist, returns Jan 28. Randy Robitaille74, also a wrist, is out until Feb 25. Tampa has cleaned out the trainer's room faster than most clubs refill it.

The Lightning beat New York Rangers 6-3 on the road Thursday, outshooting the Rangers 33 to 31. That was a win at a 19-win team with Pavel Bure84 and Eric Lindros85 still in the lineup. The road record reads 12-8, the best in the Southeast.

Khabibulin told The Tunnel this week about staying sharp when the rotation thins: "You prep the same, you trust the work."

The 19 wins are the most in the Southeast. Carolina Hurricanes leads at 50 points but has played 37 games with 14 wins. The Lightning have 19 wins in 37. If they stay healthy through the next three weeks, the division lead is within striking distance.

The 216 days lost is a number that tells you how thin the margin was. Every day that Robitaille stays out is another night without depth down the middle. Every day Cullimore stays out is another night without a second-pairing defenseman who can eat 20 minutes. Tampa has survived that. Whether they keep surviving depends on whether the next spell comes from a wrist or something worse.
