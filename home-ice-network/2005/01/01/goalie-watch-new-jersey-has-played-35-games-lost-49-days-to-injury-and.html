Goalie Watch: New Jersey has played 35 games, lost 49 days to injury, and is better on the road than at home

The Devils lead the fewest-games-played column with the fewest days lost, and Brodeur carries 89 percent of the workload

Ray CallumSenior correspondent · Home Ice NetworkSaturday, 1 January 2005
ShareCopy link

New Jersey Devils have played 35 games, the fewest in the league. No other club is below 36. They sit 3rd in the Atlantic with 49 points, three behind New York Rangers, with five games still in hand. Martin Brodeur91 has started 31 of those 35, carrying 89 percent of the workload.

The number nobody is printing: New Jersey has lost 49 days to injury this season. That is the fewest of any club in the league, and the Devils have logged 17 injury spells to get there. The average absence is under 3 games. Pittsburgh has lost 295 days from 25 spells. Tampa Bay has lost 216 from 15. Carolina has lost 142 from 15. New Jersey's medical staff patches men up in time for the next shift.

The result shows up in the standings. The Devils are 12-5 on the road, better than their 11-7 home record. The team plays more consistently away from the building, where the routine is tight and the travel keeps the group locked in. Their 109 goals against in 35 games is the second-lowest total in the league, behind only Toronto Maple Leafs's 108 in 38.

Ari Ahonen84 has started 7 games, giving Brodeur a night off here and there. The backup is not the issue. The issue is that Brodeur, 32 this season, has taken 89 percent of the starts and the Devils have 5 games fewer on the schedule than anyone else in the Atlantic. If those 5 games come in a compressed window, the workload creeps up.

They visit Florida Panthers tonight and Toronto Maple Leafs on January 4, then Montreal Canadiens on January 7 and New York Rangers on January 8. Four games in eight days, three on the road. Brodeur has 4 games in hand on every starter in the Atlantic except Mike Dunham89, who has 32 starts in 40 for the Rangers.

The 12-5 road record means the schedule does not scare them. When injuries pile up in January and short-handed teams start sliding, the Devils should be fine. They are 49 points in 35 games. At that pace, over the full season, they are a 56-point club. The question for the division is whether the 5 extra games land before February or after.
