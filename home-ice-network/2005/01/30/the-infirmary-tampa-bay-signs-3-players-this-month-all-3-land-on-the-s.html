The Infirmary: Tampa Bay signs 3 players this month, all 3 land on the shelf

The Lightning carry the league's worst medical chart and still sit 2nd in the East

Ray CallumSenior correspondent · Home Ice NetworkSunday, 30 January 2005
ShareCopy link

The piece

Tampa Bay sits 2nd in the Eastern Conference with 67 points. It also carries the league's worst medical chart: 30 injury spells, 497 days lost, and seven men on the shelf heading into tonight's date with Carolina Hurricanes.

Three of those seven signed in the past 8 days and never suited up for a game.

Cory Stillman86 signed January 23 for $429,600. Wrist, return March 5. Francis Bouillon74 signed the same day for $538,490. Leg, return April 25. The season ends April 6, so Bouillon won't wear a Tampa sweater this year. Tim Taylor69 signed January 26 for $626,050. Hip, return March 13.

All three contracts, $429,600, $538,490 and $626,050, sit on the injured list.

The other four: Dan Boyle82 (head, return February 8), Bill Lindsay74 (concussion, return March 11), Nolan Pratt67 (head, return February 23), and Randy Robitaille75 (wrist, return February 25).

Tampa's 30 injury spells this season are 7 more than any other club has recorded. Its 497 days lost are the most in the league.

Nikolai Khabibulin91 has played 45 of Tampa's 51 games, an 88% share. He appears twice on the injury ledger, both entries unnamed by the league, both brief. He has held the crease while the roster around him thinned to seven men.

The Lightning are on a 3-game losing streak. They host Carolina Hurricanes tonight, a club tied with them on points. Carolina has won 5 straight and sits in 3rd in the conference on the wins tiebreaker, 18 regulation victories to Tampa's 28. Tampa Bay is 17-7 at home; Carolina is 10-14 on the road. The two clubs have met 3 times this season and every game has gone to overtime. Tampa Bay has won 2 of those, Carolina 1.

If Tampa Bay wins tonight, it clears Carolina in the standings by 2 points and puts daylight between itself and 4th-place New Jersey Devils. If it loses, the Southeast division tightens at the top with a quarter of the season left to play.
