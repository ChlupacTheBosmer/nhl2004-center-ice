Morning Skate Notes: Columbus moves 18 men and rebuilds a fourth line overnight

Three came in, three went, and a complete line got turned inside out between Sunday and Tuesday's win at Calgary

Ray CallumSenior correspondent · Home Ice NetworkFriday, 14 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Columbus turned over 18 of its lineup slots between the previous edition and Tuesday's 2-1 win at Calgary Flames, the largest single reshuffle in the league over these 2 days.

Three men who had not been dressed on Sunday found their names in the lineup Tuesday: Espen Knutsen72 at second-line center, Krzysztof Oliwa58 at left wing on the fourth line, and Jaroslav Spacek70 on the third defensive pair. Three men came out: Kent McDonell62, Rob Ray58, and Richard Smehlik68.

Adam Mair63 shifted from right wing to center. Sean Pronger62 moved the other way, from center to wing. Oliwa filled the open left-side slot.

The middle of the order moved too

Todd Marchant78 lost 2 pairings, dropping from 6 slots to 4. David Vyborny74 moved from the extra-forward role to the penalty kill. Tyler Wright72 slid from the top extra man to the bottom one. On the back end, Henrik Tallinder70 added a pair, moving from the third defense to 3 pairings. Dimitri Kalinin74 lost 1 slot from his total.

Geoff Sanderson, the team captain, told The Tunnel's Marcus Bell this week that he has tried to hold things steady through the churn. "I'll just play and if they want to watch, they can watch," Sanderson said. "When you're twenty-three, twenty-four, and the captain is in front of the net late in the third, and he's getting hit, and he's not going down? That's a conversation without words."

Columbus Blue Jackets sits 15-19-2, 46 points, third in the Central Division. The crease stays split: Pascal Leclaire82 has started 23 of 43 (53%), Marc Denis82 19 (44%). No other club outside Toronto Maple Leafs runs a closer tandem.

The 2-1 win at Calgary ended a 2-game losing streak. Columbus returns home Saturday to host Mighty Ducks of Anaheim, a club on a 4-game winning streak of its own.
