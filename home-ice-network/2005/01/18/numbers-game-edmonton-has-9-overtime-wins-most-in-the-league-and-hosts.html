Numbers Game: Edmonton has 9 overtime wins, most in the league, and hosts Nashville tonight

The Oilers are 9-3 in extras, riding a four-game streak, and tied with Nashville for 2nd in the West

Ray CallumSenior correspondent · Home Ice NetworkTuesday, 18 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Edmonton Oilers has won 9 overtime games this season, the most of any club in the league. The Oilers have gone to 12 games past regulation and converted 9 of them into wins, a record that has carried them to 61 points and a tie with Nashville Predators for 2nd in the Western Conference. Dallas Stars holds 1st with 62.

The next-closest in overtime wins are the Columbus Blue Jackets at 7, and the New Jersey Devils and Pittsburgh Penguins at 6. Edmonton reached 46 games played as of Tuesday.

Tommy Salo85 has started 39 of those games, an 85 percent share of the workload. The Oilers beat Los Angeles Kings 3-1 on Tuesday night to extend their winning streak to four games. They outscored opponents 34-24 over their last ten, which includes two overtime losses in that span.

Tonight, Nashville Predators visits. The Predators have 25 wins, 3 overtime wins of their own, and the same 61-point total as Edmonton. The Oilers won the only prior meeting 5-3, a game decided in regulation.

The extra-time difference

Nine wins in twelve overtime attempts separates Edmonton from every other club. Mighty Ducks of Anaheim has played 13 overtime games, one more than the Oilers, and won only 4. Carolina Hurricanes has gone to 16 overtimes, the most in the league, and converted 7. Edmonton also wins a higher share once it gets there.

Edmonton's home record sits at 15-7, and the road record is 14-10. Both include overtime wins. The 9-3 overtime mark includes wins at home and on the road, in regulation and in extra time.

Calgary comes to Edmonton on Thursday, a club the Oilers lost to once earlier this season. Phoenix Coyotes arrives Monday. Nashville tonight puts a half-game tiebreaker on the line: two clubs at 61 points, one meeting already decided, and the loser falls behind.
