Numbers Game: Edmonton leads the West on 67 points, and has been outscored by 3 all season

No other top-five club in either conference carries a negative goal differential. The last 10 games show the correction arriving.

Ray CallumSenior correspondent · Home Ice NetworkWednesday, 26 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Edmonton Oilers sits first in the Western Conference with 67 points through 50 games. The Oilers have scored 180 and given up 183. That minus-3 is the worst differential among the top five clubs in either conference. Dallas Stars is second at 66 points and plus-32. Nashville Predators is third at 65 and plus-25. The gap between where the standings place Edmonton and where the goals-for and goals-against place it is the largest in the league.

The last 10 games show a different team. Since mid-December the Oilers have outscored opponents 25-17, an 8-goal swing in a stretch that includes 7 regulation wins and 3 overtime losses. In the first 40 games they were outscored by 11. Whatever went wrong in November and December has been addressed, or at least papered over.

Ethan Moreau72 was signed on January 22 for $616,660, two days before a 2-0 home shutout of Phoenix Coyotes. The knee landed on the injury report between January 25 and January 26, return targeted for February 2. Moreau watches the Minnesota Wild date on January 29 from the press box and very likely the trip to Vancouver Canucks the following night.

The home record is the reason for the standing: 17-8 at home, 14-11 everywhere else. Thirty-one wins on the season, 22 in regulation and 9 in the extra period. Those 9 overtimes get their own piece of the story; the differential gets this one. They add up to a club that keeps ending games with a chance to win, and lately has taken it.

Tommy Salo85 has started 43 of 50, carrying 86% of the workload. That is why a minus-3 team sits in first. If the recent form holds, the differential closes on its own. If it does not, the standings flatten by March and first in the West starts to look like fifth.
