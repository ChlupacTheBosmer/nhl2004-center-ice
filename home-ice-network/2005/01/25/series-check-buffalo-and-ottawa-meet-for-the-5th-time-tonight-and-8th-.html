SERIES CHECK: Buffalo and Ottawa meet for the 5th time tonight, and 8th place in the East is a tie on points

The Sabres sit 9th on the wins tiebreaker with New York. Both clubs play tonight, and only one outcome moves Buffalo up

Ray CallumSenior correspondent · Home Ice NetworkTuesday, 25 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Buffalo visits Ottawa tonight, the fifth meeting of a season series the Sabres lead 3 games to 1. The standings say both clubs are the same team tonight: 51 points for Buffalo Sabres and 51 for New York Islanders, but New York holds 8th in the Eastern Conference on the wins tiebreaker (22 to Buffalo's 18). A Buffalo win and a New York loss or overtime loss tonight puts the Sabres in the 8th spot outright.

Tonight's math

Buffalo Sabres dropped a 3-1 decision to Toronto on January 24, outshot 30 to 21. The Sabres sit 18-19-5 through 47 games with 5 overtime wins and 5 overtime losses, a .500 mark in extras that has kept them exactly where they are: tied on points with the 8th seed, one tiebreaker below it.

New York Islanders plays at Columbus Blue Jackets tonight. If New York loses in regulation, the Islanders stay at 51 and Buffalo moves ahead with a win. If New York takes an overtime point, the Sabres need two more points than the Islanders over any span where both play.

The series

Buffalo has beaten Ottawa twice in regulation and once in overtime this season, outscoring the Senators 18 to 15 across four meetings. Ottawa is 16-24-5, 45 points, 12th in the East, and carries no players on the injured list tonight. The Senators fell 4-3 in overtime to Atlanta Thrashers on January 23 after beating Florida Panthers 3-2 the night before.

Martin Biron88 has started 41 of Buffalo's 47 games. Ryan Miller83 has 9 and Mika Noronen82 7. The Sabres are without Taylor Pyatt78, out through February 23 with a foot injury.

The Senators' season series against Buffalo has gone to overtime once.

What else is on tonight

Twelve games fill the sheet on January 25, including first meetings between Colorado Avalanche and Toronto Maple Leafs, and between New Jersey Devils and Los Angeles Kings. But the Sabres-Senators game is the one where the table itself is at stake.
