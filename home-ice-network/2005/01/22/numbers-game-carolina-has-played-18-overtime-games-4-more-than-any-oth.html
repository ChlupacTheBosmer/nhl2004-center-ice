Numbers Game: Carolina has played 18 overtime games, 4 more than any other club, and its record there is 9 wins and 9 losses

No team goes to the extra period more than the Hurricanes. The question for April is who's in their net when the coin lands.

Ray CallumSenior correspondent · Home Ice NetworkSaturday, 22 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Carolina Hurricanes has won 9 games in overtime and lost 9 this season. Eighteen of their 48 games have gone past 60 minutes. No other club in the league has played more than 14. Mighty Ducks of Anaheim sits second at 14. Edmonton Oilers is third at 13.

The record is exactly even. Carolina wins the third intermission one week and surrenders it the next. Its goal differential after 48 games is +2: 182 scored, 180 allowed. They're 6th in the Eastern Conference with 59 points, 10 above the line where New York Islanders and Buffalo Sabres sit at 49.

But the path to it has been through overtime. Kevin Weekes88 has started 39 of Carolina's 48 games, 81 percent of the load. His backup, Arturs Irbe79, has 9. In a season where Carolina keeps finding itself in extra periods, Weekes is in net for those 65-minute nights more often than any other starter.

Carolina's overtime record splits cleanly by opponent type. Against the division, they are 1 and 3 in extras across 11 games. They are 0 and 2 against Tampa Bay Lightning in two meetings, both decided in overtime. Against Atlanta Thrashers, they are 1 and 1 with an overtime decision. Carolina drags opponents to the third intermission against everyone, and roughly half the time they lose it.

Olaf Kolzig89 of Washington Capitals told The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk after his own team's loss earlier this month what those extra periods cost a starter: "We win 2-1 and I'm in there for the whole 60 and the guys in front of me, they're clearing, they're blocking, and the one, the one puck gets through." Kolzig knows. He has started 45 of Washington's 48, the heaviest workload in the conference.

The arithmetic heading into the second half: 34 games remain for Carolina. Their overtime rate is 18 of 48, and no reason to think it slows. If it holds, roughly 12 more games will go to the extra period, and the coin will land in each direction about 6 times. On this pace Carolina makes the playoffs. The question for April is who's in net when the games that decide a series go past 60 minutes, and for now that's Weekes, 33, at 81 percent of the starts.

Carolina visits Washington Capitals tonight. They split their two meetings, 1 win and 1 loss. The next game against Tampa Bay Lightning comes January 30, and it will be the third meeting of what has been, so far, a two-game overtime series.
