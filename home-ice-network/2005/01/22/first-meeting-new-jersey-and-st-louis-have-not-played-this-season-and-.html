First Meeting: New Jersey and St. Louis have not played this season, and the table says this much

A 23-point gap separates the two clubs tonight. Neither goalie has seen a light workload.

Ray CallumSenior correspondent · Home Ice NetworkSaturday, 22 January 2005
ShareCopy link

New Jersey Devils visits St. Louis Blues tonight for the first meeting of the season. The two clubs have not played a game against each other since October. Tonight is game 1.

The table does not make this close. New Jersey sits 4th in the Eastern Conference with 60 points, a 22-13-4 record, and a +14 goal differential over 45 games. St. Louis is 15th in the Western Conference at 37 points, 11-26-3, and 49 goals worse than its opponents. That is a 23-point gap between two clubs who have never met this season.

The splits point the same direction. New Jersey is 13 and 8 on the road, one of the better travel records in the league. St. Louis is 8 and 15 at home, the worst home record in the Central Division tonight. St. Louis Blues is on a 1-game losing streak. New Jersey Devils won its last outing.

Brodeur has started 38 of New Jersey's 45 games, 84 percent of the load. Brent Johnson85 has started 36 of St. Louis' 46, 78 percent. Both men are carrying their clubs. Brodeur's team wins tight games; his +14 differential on a 45-game sample means they are grinding through one-goal games, not blowing anyone out.

Brodeur told The Tunnel's Marcus Bell after New Jersey's 3-2 win at Montreal Canadiens on January 7: "I win them tight. The problem is I don't win them clean. And in April, the clean ones, they matter." He was talking about the difference between 2-1 wins and 5-1 wins, and his club has not had one of the latter in weeks.

St. Louis Blues has nobody on the injured list. That is unusual for a club at 37 points. The Blues are outscored, not hurt. Their 142 goals for and 191 against over 46 games is the shape of the season.

There is no head-to-head data and no rivalry here. One game tonight, and the schedule does not return them to each other until later in the season. For Brodeur and Johnson, both carrying heavy loads on January nights, neither has film on the other from this year.

New Jersey has won 13 on the road. St. Louis has lost 15 at home.
