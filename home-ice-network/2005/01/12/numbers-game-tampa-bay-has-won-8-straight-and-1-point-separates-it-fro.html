Numbers Game: Tampa Bay has won 8 straight, and 1 point separates it from Dallas

The Lightning's streak is the longest in the league, and it has survived 22 injury spells and 219 lost days

Ray CallumSenior correspondent · Home Ice NetworkWednesday, 12 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Tampa Bay beat Atlanta 3-2 on January 9 for its eighth straight win, the longest streak in the league. The Tampa Bay Lightning Lightning sit at 59 points. Dallas Stars Dallas has 60. The gap between the top two clubs in this league is 1 point, and Tampa Bay has done it while losing more days to the injured list than all but two other clubs.

The streak has 43 goals for and 33 against over the last 10 games. The schedule splits tell the story: 14-6 at home, 14-8 on the road. Fourteen road wins is the sort of number that holds up when a playoff spot is decided by tiebreakers in April.

The crease shifted this week. Nikolai Khabibulin92, who has started 37 of the club's 42 games at an 88 percent share, moved from the healthy scratch list back to G1, sliding John Grahame84 into the backup role. Grahame had 9 starts this season. Khabibulin carried his own spell on the injured list during the season's opening weeks and is back between the pipes.

The Lightning have collected 22 injury spells this season and lost 219 days to the injured list, third-most in the league behind Pittsburgh (329) and the New York Islanders (244). Jassen Cullimore75 (wrist, out until January 28) and Randy Robitaille74 (wrist, out until February 25) remain on the shelf. Patrik Svedberg73 (concussion) is scheduled back on January 13.

What the schedule does next

Tampa Bay travels to Ottawa on January 14 to face a club on a 6-game losing streak, then to Pittsburgh on January 17. Dallas plays at home against St. Louis Blues St. Louis and visits Detroit over the same stretch. Two clubs 1 point apart at the top, both on the road Tuesday.

Tampa Bay scored 165 goals and allowed 141 across 42 games. Dallas scored 183 and allowed 150 across 45. The Lightning have been leaner up front and tighter in their own end.
