Three signings, three designations: every February 9 free agent lands on the injured list

Rem Murray, Mike Ricci and Marcel Hossa were all signed, all hurt, all out before suiting up

Luc PelletierMontreal correspondent · Home Ice NetworkSunday, 13 February 2005
ShareCopy link

Three players signed free-agent contracts on February 9. All three carry league-designated injuries with onset windows of February 12 or 13. None of them has suited up for the club that signed him.

Nashville Predators signed Rem Murray73 for $21,990. An arm injury came back with a February 19 return date. Ten days of lost time on a 74-overall left wing who was meant to add depth to a team already missing a winger.

Montreal Canadiens signed Mike Ricci79 for $77,110. A groin designation puts him out until March 20. That is 39 days of lost time on a 79-overall center for a club that sits at 15 wins and 33 losses, last in the Eastern Conference with 46 points.

San Jose Sharks signed Marcel Hossa76 for $241,300. An elbow injury with a return of March 8. Twenty-seven days on a 76-overall left wing whose new club is in the middle of a five-game losing streak.

The arithmetic nobody wanted

Each of these three contracts is a line item on the salary book: $21,990 for Murray, $77,110 for Ricci, $241,300 for Hossa. For Montreal, the Ricci signing was supposed to address the center position behind Patrik Stefan79, who is himself out with a foot injury until March 14. Now the Habs have neither player available.

For San Jose, the Hossa signing added a left wing to a group already thin after the injuries designated over the past week. The Sharks scored 16 goals in their last 10 games and allowed 26. Hossa's $241,300 buys nothing for the next four weeks.

The Nashville Predators case is the mildest: $21,990 and 10 days. Murray is back on February 19 and Nashville leads the Central Division at 75 points. The club can absorb a depth winger's absence. The other two cannot.

Injuries happen everywhere this season. Thirty-four clubs have designated 700 or more spells. What set these three apart is that all three clubs spent money on February 9, and all three woke up on February 12 or 13 to find the purchase was a number in a column that subtracts from the roster.

Montreal is the club where this stings the most. The Canadiens are 7-23 on the road, they are 46 points, and every roster move that fails lands on a club with 58 games played and 24 left. Thirty-nine days of Ricci is roughly seven and a half games of a 79-overall center the Canadiens cannot afford to lose to a groin designation before the first skate.
