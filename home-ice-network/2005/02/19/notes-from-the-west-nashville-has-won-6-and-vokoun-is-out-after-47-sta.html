Notes from the West: Nashville has won 6, and Vokoun is out after 47 starts

The Predators lead the West at 81 points, and their workhorse goaltender watched the 4-0 shutout of Boston from the bench

Ray CallumSenior correspondent · Home Ice NetworkSaturday, 19 February 2005
ShareCopy link

The longest win streak in the league belongs to Nashville Predators. Six in a row, 30 goals for, 18 against, and a first-place standing in the West at 81 points with 24 games left. Nobody has been writing about it.

Nashville Predators leads Dallas Stars at 78, Vancouver Canucks at 77 and Edmonton Oilers at 77. The Predators went 34-15-5 through 58 games. They are the only club in the West above 80 points, and they allowed the fewest goals in the conference at 170.

Tomas Vokoun90, who started 47 of 58 games (81% share), was scratched from the lineup during the current stretch. He had not been benched in a winning position all season. Jan Lasak75 moved from backup to No. 1, and Brian Finley73 was added as the third man.

Lasak has 13 career starts this season, a 22% share before the promotion. The Predators scored 4 and allowed 0 in their Feb 17 game against Boston with the new pairing on the bench. Whether that shutout belongs to Lasak or was a Vokoun start that landed inside the change window, the Predators have not restored the old order.

The schedule gives Nashville Predators four games in six nights starting Sunday: Calgary Flames at home on Feb 20, Ottawa Senators on the road Feb 22, Toronto Maple Leafs on the road Feb 23, Columbus Blue Jackets at home Feb 25. Two of those opponents are below .500 (Calgary Flames at 19-33-6, Ottawa Senators at 20-28-7). Toronto Maple Leafs is the reigning Cup champion and the best team in the league at 94 points. Columbus Blue Jackets is 1 point back of the 8th playoff seed in the West at 58.

The Predators are 2-1-1 against Columbus Blue Jackets this season, and that Feb 25 game is the fifth and final meeting in the division. The division record shows Nashville Predators at 9-2-2-2 across 15 games (9 regulation wins, 2 OT wins, 2 regulation losses, 2 OT losses).

Does Dave Tippett's team ride the hot hand with Lasak, or does a two-game slide trigger the old pattern of putting the workhorse back in?
