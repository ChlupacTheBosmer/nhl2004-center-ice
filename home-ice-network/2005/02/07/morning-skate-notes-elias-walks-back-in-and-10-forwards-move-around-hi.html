Morning Skate Notes: Elias walks back in and 10 forwards move around him

The Devils reshuffled their entire forward group for the Washington game after their top center returned from a concussion designation

Ray CallumSenior correspondent · Home Ice NetworkMonday, 7 February 2005
ShareCopy link

Patrik Elias86 skated in the New Jersey Devils lineup on February 5 after carrying a concussion designation this season, and the Devils restructured 10 forward slots around him en route to a 3-2 overtime win at Washington Capitals.

Elias took a second-line center spot, a slot on the second power-play unit and a shorthanded role on the second penalty-kill unit. That displaced Steve Guolla74, who had been running the second line at center and holding a power-play slot before the injury. Guolla dropped to third-line center with no special-teams assignment at all.

Jamie Langenbrunner86 made the biggest jump on the roster. He moved from third-line right wing to the top line, inherited the first power-play unit's right side and took over faceoff duties on the opening draw. The vacancy opened when Turner Stevenson72 sat the game and landed on the injured list with a knee designation, return targeted for February 22.

Brian Gionta73 climbed from the fourth line up to the third at right wing. Sergei Brylin78 switched from the fourth-line center slot to the fourth-line wing and lost his first-line faceoff assignment to Langenbrunner. Craig Darby72 slid from third-line center down to the fourth line. Grant Marshall77 dropped from the first power-play unit to the second. John Madden79 picked up an added center slot on the defensive fourth line.

On the back end, New Jersey Devils sat Bryan Muir67 and dressed Sean Brown67. In net, Ari Ahonen89 took the backup job over Corey Schwab74.

The table says Newark has room to breathe

The Devils sit 25-15-4, 70 points, 2nd in the East. They carry a 17-10 home record and host Atlanta Thrashers tonight. New Jersey won the only meeting, 4-3.

NJ is 15 points ahead of 8th-place New York Islanders with 30 games left, so none of this restructuring is about survival. It is about walk-in conditioning. Elias has been out long enough that the coaching staff moved every line around him before the Washington game rather than ease him in slowly. The result suggests the gamble landed.
