Notes from the West: Vancouver has won 5 straight and sits tied with Nashville for the conference lead

The Canucks' heater arrived in late January, and Buffalo and Colorado are about to test it

Ray CallumSenior correspondent · Home Ice NetworkSunday, 6 February 2005
ShareCopy link

Vancouver Canucks is 28-15-5 through 53 games, 71 points, first place in the Western Conference by virtue of a 5-game winning streak that started with a 4-3 overtime win over Edmonton Oilers on January 30 and continued with a 4-2 road victory at Pittsburgh Penguins on February 4.

Nashville Predators has the same 71 points and a better goal differential, +28 to +25, but the Canucks' 29 games remaining match Nashville's 29 exactly. Neither club is ahead in any column that would separate them on the current record.

Vancouver's offense has been the league's second-best in the conference all season, 203 goals for behind Colorado Avalanche's 210. The streak has run at 3.20 goals a night against 1.60 allowed, well above the season rates of 3.83 for and 3.36 against.

The return of Henrik Sedin74 to the lineup this week put a center on the second line and the third line. Bryan Allen77 returned to the blueline. Both men went from undressed to dressed between the January 31 edition and this one. Ed Jovanovski87, the 81-overall defenseman, remains out with a knee designation. The league has him returning February 19.

Buffalo arrives February 7, a team that has won 2 straight on the road and is fighting for 8th in the East. Chicago Blackhawks visits February 10 with 44 points, tied with St. Louis Blues for fewest in the West. Colorado Avalanche comes February 13 with the conference's best goal differential at +45 and a 3-1-0 season record against Vancouver.

Vancouver lost 3 to the Avalanche this season, including 2 on the road. The Avalanche sit 5th at 65 points and trail the Canucks by 6. The February 13 meeting in Vancouver would be a 9-point game in all but name.
