Goalie Watch: Two creases, one question — who starts when the numbers don't lie?

Toronto's 10-game heater has been built on suffocating defence, while New Jersey just sat down its all-time wins leader. Both netminders face a Saturday reckoning.

Ray CallumSenior correspondent · Home Ice NetworkSaturday, 4 December 2004
ShareCopy link

Thirty years I've watched clubs go cold because a coach wouldn't change his goalie. Twice that many, I've watched a starter lose his job for no reason a human being could see on the sheet. Saturday, when New Jersey Devils visits Toronto Maple Leafs, both benches face a version of that question.

Start with Toronto. Ten straight. Seventy-five goals for, eighteen against. That's a defensive performance that changes how a series is played. David Aebischer85 has carried the bulk of the load — 13 games to Ed Belfour90's 10 through 25, a 52 per cent share that tells you the bench already trusts the younger man when it matters. Belfour landed on the shelf sometime between December 1 and December 4, targeted to return December 5. He'll be available for Saturday.

Should he start? That's the wrong question, maybe. The 18 goals allowed in ten games is a collective achievement — the skaters in front of both goalies have turned Toronto into a club that actually converts its chances. The 147 goals for, league-leading, say that's not just clogging. But a hot backup with 52 per cent of the work and a 92-overall starter on the shelf is a coach's dilemma with a short fuse.

Across the league, Martin Brodeur94 has been reduced to a G2 designation in New Jersey Devils's latest lineup card — first time the record shows him dressed-and-not-playing this season. Ari Ahonen86 slid into the top crease. Brodeur still has 20 games played, 91 per cent of Newark's workload, so this is one decision, not a season-long demotion. But it lands in a week when New Jersey is 9-1 on the road, riding a four-game win streak, and missing Patrik Elias88 to an injury that keeps him out until December 12.

New Jersey has allowed 63 goals in 22 games. The Devils are 13-2-3, and if Ahonen can keep the crease quiet without their best skater, that's a problem for Lou Lamoriello in April.
