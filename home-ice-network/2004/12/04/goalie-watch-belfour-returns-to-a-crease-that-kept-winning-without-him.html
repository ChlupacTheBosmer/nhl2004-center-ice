Goalie Watch: Belfour returns to a crease that kept winning without him

Toronto's 10-game streak produced 18 goals against. Two netminders share the credit. Only one can start Saturday.

Ray CallumSenior correspondent · Home Ice NetworkSaturday, 4 December 2004
ShareCopy link

The Toronto Maple Leafs hockey club has won 10 straight, and the story of that run isn't a single goaltender. It is two of them trading shifts in a rotation that has produced 75 goals for and 18 against over that span.

David Aebischer86, rated 85 overall, has taken 13 starts and gone 9-3-0. Ed Belfour93, rated 92, won 9 of his 10 decisions with 3 shutouts before being rested sometime between 2004-12-01 and 2004-12-04. His return is set for 2004-12-05.

The question isn't who earned the crease. It's who the coach trusts with a divisional gauntlet arriving this week.

Toronto sits atop the Northeast at 18-5-0 and 40 points. The club has scored 147 goals and allowed just 76, a goal differential no other team in the league approaches. The 18 goals surrendered in the last 10 games belong in a highlight reel, not a standings sheet.

Aebischer carried the bulk of that defensive stretch. His 9 wins in 13 starts don't carry the pedigree of Belfour's 9 in 10, but they came while the winning habit was still forming. Belfour, meanwhile, hasn't faced a shot since the injury window opened. His 3 shutouts all came before the break.

The immediate test arrives 2004-12-06 against New York Islanders, which is 12-10-1 and riding a 2-game win streak. The bigger test follows 2004-12-07, when Toronto hosts the New Jersey Devils Devils, who are 13-2-3 with 37 points and have allowed just 63 goals in 22 games.

Aebischer will want the job. Belfour will expect it back. Toronto's coaching staff can ease Belfour in against the Islanders, save Aebischer for the Devils, or ride the hot hand through both nights.

The 10-game streak is the longest in the league. Whether it becomes 11 or 12 may depend on which name drops into the lineup card on Saturday morning. After 3 games of watching from the press box, Belfour knows exactly what he's walking back into: a crease that didn't miss him, and a schedule that won't forgive a rust problem.
