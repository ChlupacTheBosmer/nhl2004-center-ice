The Infirmary: Pittsburgh's 208 lost days lead the league, and the list keeps growing

Straka and Morozov go down days after the Penguins signed them; Lemieux's club has 5 out and a 9-2 beating fresh in the books.

Ray CallumSenior correspondent · Home Ice NetworkSaturday, 11 December 2004
ShareCopy link

Pittsburgh Penguins lost 9-2 in Toronto on 2004-12-10, and the scoreboard was the second-worst thing about the Penguins' week. The first is the trainer's ledger: 15 injury spells this season, 208 man-games lost, both the worst in the league.

Martin Straka86 is the newest name, out with a wrist until 2005-01-27 after going down sometime between December 8 and December 11. Aleksey Morozov80 is out with a knee until 2005-01-04. Both were signed on 2004-12-05, Straka for $388,040. Morozov's is his second spell of the season already.

Five out, and the wrong five

The Penguins have 5 men unavailable, tied with the Rangers and Carolina for the league high. The payroll quality of Pittsburgh's five is what separates the list: an 85-overall left wing in Straka, last season's Calder winner Eric Meloche78 (foot, due back 2004-12-13), and defenseman Ross Lupaschuk71, now on his second spell with a shoulder designated until 2005-01-25.

The young goaltending has held up. Johan Hedberg88 has 22 starts in 27 games, an 81% share, behind a roster that has given up 126 goals in 27 games. The last ten read 54 against. A team bleeding goals and missing its reinforcements has the week ahead to stop it: at St. Louis on 2004-12-12, at Los Angeles on 2004-12-14, at Anaheim on 2004-12-15.

New Jersey, by contrast, has run 10 spells for just 43 lost days. Toronto has 13 spells and 11 days lost. The difference between a limp and a season is the calendar, and Pittsburgh's calendar is brutal.
