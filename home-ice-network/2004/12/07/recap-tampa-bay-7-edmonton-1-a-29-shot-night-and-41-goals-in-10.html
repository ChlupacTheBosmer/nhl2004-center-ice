RECAP: Tampa Bay 7, Edmonton 1 -- a 29-shot night and 41 goals in 10

The Lightning's 7-1 over the Oilers keeps a 7-3 run alive and puts them 3 points behind Carolina.

Ray CallumSenior correspondent · Home Ice NetworkTuesday, 7 December 2004
ShareCopy link

Nikolai Khabibulin92 watched from the crease as Tampa Bay Lightning put 7 past Edmonton Oilers on 2004-12-05, on 29 shots to 22, the most lopsided result either team has recorded this season.

The Lightning are now 7-3 in their last 10, with 41 goals for and 31 against across that stretch. Their season line reads 12-9-1, 33 points, second in the Southeast Division behind Carolina Hurricanes (36 in 27). At 99 goals for and 89 against, Tampa Bay Lightning carries a +10 differential that sits ahead of what the point total alone suggests.

Khabibulin has started 22 of 26 games, a 85% share of the workload. John Grahame84 has made 6 appearances at 23%. Scott Clemmensen77 has 2 games played. The two-goalie rotation has held without drama all season.

Tonight Tampa Bay Lightning visits Boston Bruins (9-12-3, 23 points, home record 3-10). The Bruins have scored 90 and allowed 98. Andrew Raycroft81 has started 18 of 25 games at 72%. Boston's four injuries include Glen Murray83 (85 overall, out until 2005-01-12), Andy Hilbert77 (77, out until 2004-12-31), Jonathan Girard73 (75, out until 2004-12-08), and Ivan Huml74 (74, out until 2004-12-12). The Bruins won their last game 4-3 in overtime against Atlanta on 2004-12-05, so the record understates the fight.

Tampa Bay Lightning has gaps of its own: Arron Asham75 (75, out until 2004-12-28), Jassen Cullimore75 (75, out until 2005-01-28), and Randy Robitaille74 (74, out until 2005-02-25). Cullimore and Robitaille are the long-term cases, and their absence has kept the blue line and the fourth-center spot thin all season.

On the road Tampa Bay Lightning is 8-6, nearly matching their 8-4 home mark. Carolina Hurricanes leads the division with 36 points but has gone 11-5 at home and 4-7 on the road; Tampa Bay Lightning travels well enough to close the gap on someone's bad night.

The next 2 games come against teams under .500: Boston (12/07) and Chicago Blackhawks (12/08, a team at 6-12-5 on a 4-game skid). After that, Tampa Bay Lightning visits Minnesota Wild (12/10) and Montreal Canadiens (12/12).
