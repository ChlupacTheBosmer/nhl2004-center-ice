Notes from the East: Carolina's division lead is built on a 30-game schedule and seven overtime games

The Hurricanes beat Edmonton 6-0 to reach 41 points and first in the Southeast on the league's busiest schedule

Ray CallumSenior correspondent · Home Ice NetworkMonday, 13 December 2004
ShareCopy link

Carolina Hurricanes beat Edmonton Oilers 6-0 on Saturday, outshooting the Oilers 26-19, and moved to 41 points, first in the Southeast. Edmonton has lost four straight.

Carolina Hurricanes have played 30 games, tied with Dallas Stars, Mighty Ducks of Anaheim and Phoenix Coyotes for the most in the league. 7 of them have gone to overtime, the most of any club. That gives the club 12 regulation wins, 6 regulation losses, 7 games past sixty minutes, and 41 points, a lead in a division where Tampa Bay Lightning sit at 38. Only Toronto Maple Leafs at 48, Colorado Avalanche at 47 and New York Rangers at 44 sit on more points.

Tampa Bay have 14 regulation wins, more than Carolina have in all, and still trail in the standings. The extra-time points are the difference between leading the division and sitting third, where Washington sit at 34.

The roster churns to support the pace. Petr Sykora79, Radim Vrbata76, Sandis Ozolinsh83 and Steve Rucchin77 all moved into new slots this week. Jeff Heerema72 came up from the minors. Corey Hirsch71 took the backup job in goal as Arturs Irbe75 left the crease, and Kevin Weekes86 has started 25 of the 30 games, 83%.

The cost shows on the injury list. Rod Brind&#x27;Amour80 is out with a back problem until 2005-01-22, Sean Hill76 with a hip until 2005-01-16, and Josef Vasicek75 with a leg due back 2004-12-15.

Seven extra-time games are seven points a 12-win club would not otherwise hold, and they carry the league's busiest schedule.
