The Most Expensive Hockey in the League: San Jose Spent Its Way to Level Five Equipment and a 3-17 Home Record

$63.99M buys the best equipment room in the league and a practice rink at Level Zero. The profit is $5.55M, the lowest of thirty clubs.

Ruth HalloranBusiness of hockey · The BuildingSunday, 15 January 2006

San Jose pays $1.68M per point. That is the worst number in the league. Los Angeles is next at $1.48M, Atlanta at $1.46M, and Pittsburgh at the other end at $0.85M. The club that earns the worst figure has the best equipment room in the league and the worst practice rink, tied at Level 0 with four other clubs.

Jules Tong's payroll is $63.99M. Thirty-eight points came back from it. The revenue is $49.42M, the second-lowest in the league behind Phoenix at $48.37M. The profit, $5.55M, is the smallest of any club's season.

The gap between the equipment room and the training room is where the money went. Equipment sits at Level 5, the only Level 5 in the league, carrying a +1 modifier to agility, acceleration, balance and shot accuracy. Practice sits at Level 0, carrying a -6 to every attribute at home. The Sharks have gone 3-17 inside their own building.

"I walk into a practice facility that is the worst in this league and I look at the man's face and I know what he's thinking," Tong told The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk on January 5. "He's thinking, 'How am I going to get better here?'"

Tong named the tradeoff himself. "Equipment got to five, that took time and that took money I pulled from somewhere else. But somewhere else was practice. That's the honest answer."

The on-ice facility reading for San Jose is -0.79, the least negative of any club outside Toronto's 0.00. That number is a blend. It averages the +1 from equipment against the -4 from the workout room and the -2 from travel and the -6 from practice at home. The equipment room is so good it pulls the average up. But the home reading is the practice modifier, and the practice modifier is -6 to every attribute. Thirty games at home, a -6 to everything, and 3-17 on the board.

The club's 12-13 record on the road carries a -2 travel penalty to every attribute. The 3-17 at home carries a -6 to every attribute. The difference is four attribute points a night, and it shows up in the home-ice split. San Jose is the only club in the league with a worse-than-.250 home percentage and a better-than-.500 road percentage. That is not a roster problem. That is a building problem.

The cheapest way to lose at home

Four rooms can move a rating in this build. Equipment and practice are two of them. A club that maxes one and leaves the other at zero is choosing which end of the game it can afford to train in. San Jose chose the front end. Agility and shot accuracy get their +1. Strength, checking, endurance, and every attribute at home get nothing.

The Sharks play 19 more home games before what the calendar has as the trade deadline, and 18 after it. The -6 does not change unless the room goes up. The room has not appeared in any upgrade record the league has held since December 2004. Tong has spoken about it. The money is $63.99M deep and coming back as $5.55M in profit.

San Jose is not a small-market club pretending to be big. It is a $63.99M payroll paying the league's highest per-point cost to lose at home because the building costs its men six attributes a night on everything that is not agility or shooting.
