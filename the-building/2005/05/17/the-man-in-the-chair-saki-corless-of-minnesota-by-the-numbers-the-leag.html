The Man in the Chair: Saki Corless of Minnesota, by the numbers the league keeps

Third in prestige, sixth in profit, joint last in the building. The league's own tables tell the story without help.

Ruth HalloranBusiness of hockey · The BuildingTuesday, 17 May 2005

Saki Corless sits 3rd in the League Scouting Bureau's prestige table at 659. His club runs a live sum of -18, tied for worst in the league with Mighty Ducks of Anaheim, Columbus Blue Jackets, Florida Panthers and St. Louis Blues. His club made $39.52M in profit this season. He made zero upgrades.

That is the whole paragraph. The rest fills in the columns beside it.

The money

Minnesota Wild finished the season with revenue of $79.24M against a payroll of $37.91M. The profit line reads $39.52M, which is the 6th-highest figure in this league. The payroll is the 4th-lowest. The club made more in profit than it spent on players.

The per-point cost lands at $0.48M, the 5th-best mark among the 30 clubs. Vancouver Canucks ($0.36M), Edmonton Oilers ($0.38M), New Jersey Devils ($0.40M) and Mighty Ducks of Anaheim ($0.42M) bought points more cheaply. Nobody else did it for less than Corless, and he did it on a building that the tuning table reads as a net drain on every player wearing the sweater.

Four of the five clubs with higher profit built something during the season. Corless did not. The Bureau's upgrade record has held since December 4 and lists 20 entries across the league. Minnesota does not appear in it.

The building

Equipment sits at zero. The tuning file gives that level a modifier of -6 on agility, acceleration, balance and shot accuracy. Travel sits at zero: -6 on every attribute on the road. Workout is at 1, worth -4 on strength, checking and endurance. Practice is at 2, worth -2 on every attribute at home.

Those four tracks add to a live sum of -18. The on-ice figure, which is the facilities term averaged over the Wild's own players, reads -3.53. That number is what the building costs them on paper, night after night, in the league's own arithmetic.

The locker room carries a curve in the file that nothing in this pipeline reads. The scouting room sits at 3 and feeds only a trade valuation that nobody outside the club can see. Neither shows up in the -18.

The result

22-38-9, 79 points. The Wild finished 4th in the Northwest, 13 points behind Colorado Avalanche at the top and 4 ahead of last-place Calgary Flames. Their goal differential was 245 for, 281 against. They won 17 of 41 at home, 18 of 41 on the road. No split suggests the building or the bus is the deciding factor in any individual result. The aggregate does.

Attendance averaged 13,610 against a ticket price of $80. That is the 8th-lowest gate in the league. The 12,112 in Phoenix Coyotes's building and the 12,906 in San Jose Sharks's and the 12,943 in New York Islanders's are all lower. The revenue of $79.24M on that attendance is carried by the gate being small but not tiny.

The standing

Corless entered the season 3rd in prestige and left it there. The table reads "level" in the since column. Behind him sit Richards at 722 (Nashville Predators, 101 points, 2 rounds) and Rampton at 704 (New Jersey Devils, 114 points, 3 rounds). Ahead of no one.

The injury ledger shows 44 spells and 130 days lost. That is the 2nd-fewest days lost in the league, behind only New Jersey Devils at 122. The men stayed healthy. The morale average of 92.8 ties Carolina Hurricanes for 19th. The building does not appear to be making anyone unhappy, or at least the file does not say so.

The draft is 22 days away. Corless holds the 3rd-highest prestige standing in this league and a profit line that would fund rooms anywhere. The record shows what he did with it last winter: nothing that shows up in the file.
