The Gate: Buffalo sells the cheapest seat in the league, and 16,080 people buy it

The only $76 ticket in this league fills one of the top five buildings in the league. It does not fill the building with wins.

Ruth HalloranBusiness of hockey · The BuildingTuesday, 17 May 2005

Buffalo Sabres charges $76 for a seat. No other club in this league charges less. The next cheapest are Calgary Flames, Boston Bruins, Montreal Canadiens and Chicago Blackhawks at $77. Toronto charges $101.

16,080 people showed up per game at that price. That is 5th in this league, behind only Toronto at 17,713, New Jersey at 17,261, Colorado at 16,577 and Carolina at 16,311. All four of those clubs charge between $80 and $101. Buffalo draws 96.8% of Carolina's crowd at a price $4 lower.

The building fills. The season did not.

What a $76 seat does to the revenue line

Buffalo's season revenue sits at $75.36M. That is below the league's halfway mark. Washington Capitals drew 16,203 people at $78 and took in $93.29M. Tampa Bay Lightning drew 16,191 at $79 and cleared $97.57M. The crowd gap is under 100 seats. The revenue gap is $18M and $22M.

The club could sell at $78 or $80 and likely hold most of the crowd, given that Buffalo Sabres draws more than Carolina Hurricanes at $80 and nearly matches Washington Capitals at $78. That revenue is on the table and is not being taken.

What the payroll says

Buffalo spent $44.16M on salaries. That is 25th of 30. Per point, the number is $0.53M. The club made $24.30M in profit, which means the $76 seat is covering a roster that did not get there.

The 84 points put Buffalo in a four-way tie with New York Islanders, New York Rangers and Atlanta Thrashers. New Jersey took the eighth eastern seed and the tiebreaker that goes with it. Buffalo watched from home. Their spring ended on 2005-04-06 with the regular season.

The building as a going concern

Buffalo is profitable. $24.30M of profit on a $44.16M payroll is a club that does not need the gate to go up just to survive. The practice rink is at level 1. The workout room is at level 1. Travel is at level 1. Equipment is at level 1. The live sum of the four tracks that move a rating is -16, 19th of 30. The club has not filed a single upgrade since the record opened in December.

The discount is voluntary. The profit comes from keeping payroll low and ticket low and letting the crowd fill the building anyway. 16,080 at $76 is a building that fills on a low ask.

The question for Buffalo is whether the $76 seat and the $44.16M payroll can turn 84 points into 100. The practice rink at level 1 costs -4 on every attribute at home. The workout room at level 1 costs -4 on strength, checking and endurance. Those are the two rooms that read on the ice, and they are both sitting one level above the floor. There is $24.30M in profit and the money has not moved toward a single upgrade.

16,080 at $76 means the fans are willing. $44.16M and the live sum of -16 say the front office is waiting. The tiebreaker went to New Jersey.
