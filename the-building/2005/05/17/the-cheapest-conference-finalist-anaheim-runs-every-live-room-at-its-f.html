The Cheapest Conference Finalist: Anaheim runs every live room at its floor and makes $45.21M

A $41M payroll, a -18 building, 64 injury spells, and one win from the Stanley Cup Final

Ruth HalloranBusiness of hockey · The BuildingTuesday, 17 May 2005

Mighty Ducks of Anaheim is one win from the Stanley Cup Final. They spend less on players than 24 clubs in this league. They run the joint-worst building by the league's own tuning table. They have more injuries than anybody.

All four facts are in the same save. The club at the bottom of the building table is still alive on 2005-05-17, trailing Dallas Stars 4-1 in the Conference Final, one game from a series they have already drawn level once.

The money

The payroll is $41.01M. That is the 6th-lowest in a 30-club league. It buys $0.42M per point, which is the 4th-best rate in the league. The revenue is $93.63M, 5th-highest, drawn from 14,567 seats a night at $79. The profit is $45.21M, which trails only Toronto Maple Leafs ($46.70M) and New Jersey Devils ($45.96M).

Anaheim carries the smallest payroll of the three clubs still standing in the Conference Finals. The other two have better buildings, bigger gates, and in Dallas Stars's case a profit that is less than half what Anaheim's is.

The building

Anaheim's four live tracks sit at equipment level 0 (-6), workout level 1 (-4), practice level 1 (-4), travel level 1 (-4). The live sum is -18, which is tied for the worst in the league with Columbus Blue Jackets, Florida Panthers, Minnesota Wild and St. Louis Blues. Only Los Angeles Kings is worse at -19.

That means every Anaheim player loses 6 points of agility, acceleration, balance and shot accuracy from the equipment room. They lose 4 points of strength, checking and endurance from the workout room. They lose 4 points of every attribute at home from the practice rink and 4 points on the road from the travel budget.

The other six tracks in the league's tuning table do nothing in this build. Coaching, medical, marketing and legal are commented out of the shipped data. The locker room and the scouting room carry numbers that nothing in this pipeline reads.

The injuries

Anaheim filed 64 injury spells this season, the most of any club. They lost 527 days to those injuries, the 4th-highest total. The medical room, which the tuning file says moves injuries and recovery, is switched off entirely. There is no building explanation for the injury count, and there is no building explanation for its absence. The number is the number.

Sergei Fedorov90 played through 3 separate spells. Jean-Sebastien Giguere97 missed time 4 times. Vaclav Prospal80 went down twice with a foot. The dressing room is 17 deep this spring, and none of those absences came from a room the league can point at.

What the building is

The four live tracks move attributes: they make a player's numbers better or worse on a given night. They do not move attendance, profit, the medical file or morale. Morale at Anaheim averages 91.4, the 13th-best in the league.

The gate does the money work. The payroll does the roster work. The building is a modifier on attributes between 0 and -6 per track, applied to men who play through what their bodies give them regardless of whether the equipment room has a number beside it.

Anaheim's profit came from $93.63M in revenue against $41.01M in salaries, minus the other costs the save does not itemize. The profit came from the gate. The Conference Final came from the roster. The building, by the league's own tuning file, cost them 18 points of attributes every night and made no difference to any of those two outcomes.

The series goes to Game 6 on 2005-05-16 at Anaheim, trailing 4-1. The travel room, which is what they feel on the road, is at level 1 and costs them 4 points of every attribute in Dallas Stars's building. They are down 4-1 anyway.
