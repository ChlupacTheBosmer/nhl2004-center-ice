The Invisible Six: 6 of 19 upgrades went into rooms that show no output

Washington raised its scouting room this week. It is the latest of a third of the league's building work to land where nobody outside a club can see the number.

Ruth HalloranBusiness of hockey · The BuildingWednesday, 11 May 2005

Washington Capitals raised its scouting room between May 9 and May 11, the latest building project the record can show. It is the 6th upgrade since December to go into a room whose effect cannot be read in any pipeline this desk has access to.

The scouting room carries a curve in the tuning file, from minus-1 at level 1 to zero at level 5. The file says it feeds trade evaluation. That is the mechanism. The number it produces is visible to no one outside the club, and this desk cannot say whether raising it from level 0 to level 1 changed anything the standings or a payroll figure reflects.

The locker room has the same problem: the file gives it a curve, the pipeline reads nothing, and the morale the curve supposedly moves does not appear in any formula this desk can verify. Six upgrades went into those two rooms. Thirteen went into the four rooms that move a rating.

What the record can show

The tuning table has four tracks that move a rating (equipment, workout, practice, travel) and two that carry a curve no pipeline reads (locker room, scouting room). Between December 4 and today, the league filed 19 upgrades. Here they are.

Five clubs made their only upgrade this season into an invisible room. Vancouver Canucks raised its scouting room in December and finished 99 points in the Northwest. Florida Panthers raised its scouting room in December and finished 76 points, last in the Southeast. Chicago Blackhawks raised its locker room in January and finished 78. Ottawa Senators raised its locker room in April and finished 77. St. Louis Blues raised its scouting room in April and finished 86.

Toronto Maple Leafs made three upgrades, all into rooms that move a rating: travel, then workout twice. They finished with 133 points. Washington Capitals made two: equipment in January (which moves a rating), scouting in May (which feeds a number nobody outside the club can see). They are leading Toronto 2-1 in the Eastern Conference Final.

The four rooms that move a rating received 13 upgrades. The two that do not received 6. The clubs spending on the visible rooms include the league's best team and the club currently beating them in a playoff series.

The balance

Boris Van Ryswyk of Florida Panthers told The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk on May 4: "The rooms... the rooms help you in three years, four years. So if I have to sequence it, I've been putting the player money first." His club raised its scouting room in December. That was their only upgrade. They carry a $51.07M payroll against $72.88M in revenue, and booked $13.64M in profit.

The cost of any upgrade is not on the record. What the record can say is that WSH has $25.51M in profit, raised a room that produces a number nobody outside the club can see, and is one win from the Stanley Cup Final.
