The Road Team: 3 clubs won more away than at home, and all 3 practise at zero

A practice rink at level zero costs 6 points per attribute at home. Two of these clubs run the only travel budgets that are not a penalty on the highway.

Ruth HalloranBusiness of hockey · The BuildingWednesday, 25 May 2005

Three clubs won more on the road than at home in 2004-05. All three practise at level zero. Two of them run the best travel budgets in the league.

At home a player feels his club's practice rink. On the road he feels his club's travel budget. Equipment and workout follow a man into every building, but these two tracks apply differently depending on whose rink he is standing in. A practice rink at level zero carries a -6 modifier on every attribute. A travel budget at level 5 carries +1. That 7-point spread is what a club's own building costs a man versus what a bus gives him in someone else's city, and it shows up in the standings in the same three places every time.

Boston Bruins is the only club in the league with a positive travel modifier. Jimmy Benton runs a level 5 travel budget that gives +1 to every attribute on the road and a level zero practice rink that costs -6 on every attribute at home. The Bruins went 19-22 in front of their own crowd and 25-16 in everyone else's building.

Colorado Avalanche went 29-12 on the road, tied with Washington Capitals for the best away record in the league. Kerry Morassutti's practice rink is at zero. His travel budget is at level 4, which carries a +0 modifier, the same effect as if the room did not exist. The Avalanche posted a 58.5% home clip against a 70.7% road clip. Morassutti spent $71.56M on payroll to build a club that performs 12.2 percentage points better on the highway than in its own arena. At $0.64M per point, that is still the 4th-best efficiency in the league, but the buildings are holding the number higher than the payroll alone would suggest.

Washington Capitals carries the widest gap in the league: 24.4 percentage points between a 46.3% home clip and a 70.7% road clip. Shawn Holliday's practice rink is at level zero. His travel budget at level 3 carries a -1. The swing between the two tracks is 5 points, the smallest of the three road clubs, but the record gap is the widest because the rest of the roster and schedule amplify what the rooms do.

Holliday addressed the practice rink to The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk after his club's second-round exit. "The practice rink at zero is... it's not where I want it. I won't stand here and tell you that's fine, it isn't."

The clubs the swing helps run the other direction

Detroit Red Wings sits at level 5 on practice (+1) and level zero on travel (-6), a 7-point swing toward the home side. Tobe Wong's club went 27-14 at home and 18-23 on the road, a 22-point gap that mirrors Boston's almost exactly but points the opposite way. Detroit's practice rink is the only one in the league above par, and its travel budget is at the floor. The building gives the Red Wings every advantage at home and takes it all away on the highway.

Vancouver Canucks runs practice at level 2 (-2) and travel at level zero (-6), a 4-point home-side swing. Emmanuel Yau's club went 28-13 at home, a 68.3% clip that trails only Toronto's 80.5%, and 18-23 on the road. The 24.4-point gap matches Washington's size but faces the other direction.

The three road clubs share a fix that is one upgrade wide. Moving a practice rink from level zero to level one removes 2 of the 6-point penalty. The league record shows 20 upgrades filed between 2004-12-04 and today. None went to Boston's, Colorado's, or Washington's practice rink. Washington Capitals upgraded its equipment room in January and its scouting room in May. The practice room did not move.

The draft is 14 days away. The 2005-06 season begins in 131 days. A club that practises at level zero and travels at or above par will play in October the way it played in April, and the building will still be the cheapest win on the sheet.
