The Upgrade Report: Tampa Bay raised four rooms, and three of them read nothing

The biggest facility window on record split the league's money between rooms that move a rating and rooms that only carry a number

Ruth HalloranBusiness of hockey · The BuildingSunday, 29 May 2005

In the four days the league's facility record moved this month, Tampa Bay Lightning bought four room-levels and put three of them into rooms that produce nothing the rating model reads.

Tampa pushed scouting from 2 to 4 and the locker room from 3 to 4. The tuning file carries a curve for both of those rooms, but nothing in this pipeline reads either number, so a Tampa fan cannot be shown a result from them. The fourth level went into equipment, 2 to 3, which does move a rating. All three rooms are still below par, which is level 5.

This is the largest single upgrade window the record holds, and it is not a Tampa problem. Across the league the same four days bought 39 room-levels. 21 of them went into the four rooms that move a rating. The other 18 went into the two rooms that only carry a number. Not one level went to coaching, medical, marketing or legal, the four tracks that are switched off entirely here.

Travel took the most levels, 9 of them, because a level-zero travel budget is a road penalty and a club can buy it back. Minnesota alone put 2 into travel. Tampa, Vancouver, New Jersey, St. Louis, Washington, Buffalo, Columbus and Philadelphia bought the rest. Equipment moved at Tampa, Pittsburgh, Carolina, Edmonton, Minnesota and Ottawa.

Eighteen levels went into the locker room and scouting and produced no output the model can read. Tampa's +2 in scouting is the largest single jump in the window. Columbus, Dallas and Colorado each bought two unread levels. Nine more clubs bought one each.

Tampa pays, and pays well

The money side of Tampa is the part of this that is easy to read. The payroll is $48.50M against 107 points, which comes to $0.45M a point, the second-best rate in the league. Revenue ran $97.57M, profit $43.72M, and 16191 people bought the $79 seat every home night. On the business page Tampa is doing almost everything right.

That is why the room spending is worth a sentence. A club with $43.72M in profit is not short of cash for rooms. Tampa could have bought travel, the track that moves every attribute on a night away, and it went scouting instead. Scouting now sits at 4, one step under par, and it will not show up in a goal differential or a points total. A general manager may want that for trade evaluation, and the league keeps that valuation behind the door of the club. What he will not get is a number on a page.

The four switched-off rooms never came up in this window, and they have not since the record opened in December. No club has spent a level on coaching, medical, marketing or legal, which is the only honest read of what those rooms do here: nothing that can be shown.

So Tampa's four levels split the way the whole league's 39 did. Three quarters of this window went to rooms that carry a number and return a blank. The live rooms got 21 levels among thirty clubs. Tampa kept its own building below par on every live track it raised, and bought the one jump nobody outside the building can see.
