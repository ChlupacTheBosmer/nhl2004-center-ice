What a Point Costs: Vancouver runs the league's cheapest engine from a building ranked 21st

Emmanuel Yau's club turned $34.94M into 61 points at $0.57M a point, the best rate in the league, from a -16 building

Ruth HalloranBusiness of hockey · The BuildingWednesday, 19 January 2005

Vancouver costs $0.57M per point. No club in this league is cheaper. The Vancouver Canucks payroll sits at $34.94M, second-lowest of 30, and the standing says 61 points in 46 games, second in the Northwest. Edmonton Oilers was the story last week at $0.60M a point from a -18 building. Vancouver does it slightly cheaper from a slightly better room.

The four tracks that move a rating sum to -16 at Vancouver, 21st of 30 clubs. Equipment sits at 2 (-3), workout at 0 (-5), practice at 2 (-2), travel at 0 (-6). A travel level of zero carries a -6 modifier, the same penalty Los Angeles Kings and Phoenix Coyotes and Minnesota Wild carry. The on-the-ice term, averaged over Vancouver's own roster, reads -1.80.

That number does not explain how a club with one of the worst travel budgets in the league is 13-9 on the road. It does not explain how a workout room at zero, which costs 5 points of strength and checking and endurance on every player in the building, produces a team giving up 163 goals. The league keeps the record; it does not explain the result.

What the league does keep is the money. Vancouver took $55.95M in season revenue on 14,816 a night at $78 a ticket. Profit came to $28.80M, second in the league behind New Jersey at $34.02M. Emmanuel Yau runs the club at a prestige standing of 423, 11th in the league's own table. His record reads 25-13-5.

Philadelphia spent $69.32M and got 42 points. Detroit spent $80.89M and got 54. Both are worse per point than Vancouver, and Detroit at least has a practice rink at 5 (+1) and equipment at 1 (-4). Vancouver has neither.

Across the league, the clubs at the bottom of the cost-per-point table are not the ones with the best buildings. Toronto, at -7 live sum, sits at $0.88M a point. Vancouver, at -16, sits at $0.57M. The building is a cost a club carries, and Vancouver carries a heavy one while still turning the cheapest points in the league.

What a point costs is payroll divided by standing. What a building costs is not on the record. The four dead rooms, coaching, medical, marketing and legal, do nothing here, so the whole facility story reduces to equipment, workout, practice and travel, and Vancouver's travel room at zero is the single largest drag on their live sum at -6. The club plays 13-9 away from home anyway.

On the profit line, $28.80M on a $34.94M payroll means the building pays for itself with room to spare, even at 14,816 a night, even at $78. A club spending $80.89M on players and pulling $4.65M in profit, the way Detroit does, is the one running the worst business in this league, and its building is better.
