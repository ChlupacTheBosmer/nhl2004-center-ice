The Road Tax: Colorado plays better in your building than in their own

The Avalanche carry the league's worst practice rink and the second-best travel budget. The tuning file puts a six-point swing in favor of the road, and the records track.

Ruth HalloranBusiness of hockey · The BuildingTuesday, 25 January 2005

Colorado is 15-6 on the road this season, the best winning percentage in the league. At home they are 14-12. Two rooms explain the split. The Colorado Avalanche practice rink sits at level 0, one of three clubs at the floor. The travel budget sits at level 4, second only to Boston Bruins at level 5.

The league's own tuning file applies the practice modifier at home and the travel modifier on the road. Practice level 0 assigns a −6 modifier to all attributes on a home night. Travel level 4 assigns +0 on a road night. The swing between Colorado's building and their opponent's building is six modifiers in the visitors' favor.

The live sum of −14 puts Colorado ninth of 30 on the facilities ledger. The split, not the total, is what sets them apart: a −6 at home and a 0 on the road.

Where the money goes

Kerry Morassutti's payroll stands at $71.56M, the third-highest in the league. Per point the cost is $1.17M, 11th best. Revenue is $71.41M, second behind Toronto. Profit: $22.85M.

The gate covers the payroll. Attendance averages 16781 a night, second in the league, at $80 a ticket. The building brings in money. It gives them no edge at home.

Colorado's 4-round playoff run last spring, which ended in the Stanley Cup Final, came with the same practice rink. The room at the bottom of the tuning table did not cost them in the postseason. Whether it costs them on a Tuesday night in January is what the 14-12 home record measures.

Two clubs, one split

Colorado and Boston Bruins are the only two clubs in the league with travel above level 3. Both run practice rinks at level 0. Both win more on the road than at home.

Boston carries the wider modifier. Travel at level 5 gives +1, a seven-point swing over their home −6. Boston is 9-15 at home, the worst in the league. Colorado's 14-12 at least keeps them above .500 in their own building.

The rest of the league's road records sit behind both. Toronto Maple Leafs at 16-7 carries the most road wins but a .696 winning percentage, two points behind Colorado's .714. Every other club is below .625.

The four rooms that do nothing

Coaching, medical, marketing and legal carry names in the tuning file and no numbers in this build. A coaching room does not touch a player's ceiling. A medical room does not shorten a stay on the injured list. Colorado has logged 20 injury spells and 78 days lost, among the lightest in the league. The tuning file gives no credit to the medical room for that.

The practice rink is the lever Colorado controls, and it sits at the bottom. The upgrade record, open since December 1, shows no move on practice at Colorado. Each level between 0 and 5 on the practice track narrows the gap between a home shift and a road shift. The league does not price the move.
