What a Point Costs: Edmonton runs the league's worst building and its cheapest engine

A $36.81M payroll, a facilities grade that should sink any club, and 59 points anyway. The cost-per-point table says who is getting value and who is getting nothing.

Ruth HalloranBusiness of hockey · The BuildingSaturday, 15 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Edmonton sits 4th in the league at 59 points on a $36.81M payroll. That is $0.62M per point, second-cheapest in the league behind only Vancouver at $0.61M. The Oilers do it with a live facility sum of -18, tied for 5th-worst, and an on-the-ice average of -3.65, tied for dead last with the Islanders. The building is bad. The men in it win games anyway.

The cost-per-point table sorts the league into three groups. At the bottom, clubs paying under $1M for a point: Vancouver Canucks, Edmonton Oilers, Tampa Bay Lightning, Nashville Predators, Minnesota Wild, New Jersey Devils, Mighty Ducks of Anaheim, Toronto Maple Leafs, Buffalo Sabres. At the top, clubs paying over $1.30M: Florida Panthers, New York Rangers, Montreal Canadiens, Detroit Red Wings, St. Louis Blues, Philadelphia Flyers. The spread between the cheapest and the most expensive runs from $0.61M to $1.65M.

What the table cannot sort is whether a club is winning because of its spending or in spite of it. Philadelphia pays $69.32M and has 42 points. That is $1.65M per point, the worst in the league, on the 4th-highest payroll. Detroit pays $80.89M for 50 points, $1.62M each. St. Louis pays $56.63M for 35 points, $1.62M each. These three clubs spend like contenders and produce like the bottom third.

The rooms do not cost what you think

Edmonton's equipment room is level 0, which the tuning file prices at -6 to agility, acceleration, balance and shot accuracy. Their practice rink is also level 0, -6 to all attributes at home. Their workout room is level 1, -4 to strength, checking and endurance. Only travel sits at level 2, a -2 to all attributes on the road. Four of the ten tracks that should matter are switched off in this build: coaching, medical, marketing and legal carry a name and no number. Two more (locker room and scouting) carry numbers that nothing reads. So the live sum of -18 is the whole story of what a facility contributes to a game here, and Edmonton is paying for none of it.

The payroll tells you what a club spends on its players. The live sum tells you what its building does to them on a shift. Edmonton has both at their worst and a 59-point season anyway. The roster is carrying the building.

The profit question

Minnesota Wild is the league's most profitable club at $34.64M, on the lowest payroll ($29.97M) and the second-worst record (38 points). Minnesota has a live sum of -18 and an on-ice grade of -3.41. They are not spending on players and they are not spending on the building, and they are collecting the most money per game of any club that is not pretending to be good. That is a business model. Whether it is a hockey one is a different question the front office may not be asking.

Edmonton's $24.06M profit on a $51.79M revenue line puts them 6th in the league on the books and 4th on the ice. They draw 15005 at $79 a ticket. Vancouver draws 14836 at $78 and clears $28.82M. Both clubs are cheap, both are profitable, and both have facilities that subtract from what their men can do on a shift. The two differences: Vancouver has a scouting room at level 2 and a locker room at level 3 (numbers that nothing reads), and Edmonton has neither. On the ice, Vancouver sits at -1.80 and Edmonton at -3.65.

Gary Roberts told The Tunnel's Marcus Bell after Toronto beat Pittsburgh 2-1 on January 9: "We're doing it the hard way, and I like it." Toronto's live sum is -7, the best in the league, and their payroll is $66.97M. They are doing it the expensive way too. The rest of this table is the argument that you do not have to.
