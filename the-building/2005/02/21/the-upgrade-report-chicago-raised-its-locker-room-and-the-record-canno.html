The Upgrade Report: Chicago raised its locker room, and the record cannot show what moved

Three of ten upgrades since December went to tracks that carry a number and nothing in this pipeline reads it

Ruth HalloranBusiness of hockey · The BuildingMonday, 21 February 2005

Chicago upgraded its locker room between January 26 and January 30, moving the track from level 1 to level 2 in the save's own numbering. The locker room carries a curve in the league's tuning file. Nothing in this pipeline reads it. The record cannot show what that upgrade bought.

Chicago Blackhawks's equipment room sits at level 0, which is the floor. The equipment track moves agility, acceleration, balance and shot accuracy across the club's roster. Chicago left that room untouched and spent its upgrade on a track the file names and this build switches off.

That is one of three such moves in the 10 upgrades the record has held since December 4. Vancouver Canucks and Florida Panthers each raised their scouting room, another track with a number and no reader in this pipeline. Chicago's locker room, Vancouver's scouting, Florida's scouting: three clubs, three dead rooms.

The other seven upgrades went to tracks that actually move a rating. New Jersey Devils raised its workout. So did Montreal Canadiens. Detroit Red Wings raised its practice rink. Toronto Maple Leafs raised its travel budget, then its workout. Washington Capitals and Edmonton Oilers raised their equipment. Those six clubs upgraded rooms the tuning table reads and the record can show.

The room at the bottom

Chicago's live sum, the four tracks that move a rating added together, sits at -17. That is 7th worst in a 30-club league. The equipment room at level 0 contributes a -6 modifier, the single largest penalty any one room can impose.

Jason Hughes runs the club's front office and sits 7th in the league's prestige standing at 508. He defended the locker room move on The Tunnel this week.

"I think the locker room was the right call," he told Jan Kowalczyk on February 16. "We needed it. Morale's where it is for a reason."

Chicago's club morale is 94.1, which is 21st of 30. The record cannot show the locker room's effect on that number, because the pipeline drops the track. The tuning file assigns it a curve on morale. This build ignores it.

Hughes acknowledged the equipment gap in the same interview. "The equipment room, yeah, it's zero," he said. "I know it's zero. We'll get to it when the numbers tell us we can get to it."

What the gate pays

Chicago is 18-30-9, 51 points, 28th in the league. The club carries a 5-game losing streak. Four of those five defeats were by one goal. Since February 10 the losses read: at Vancouver Canucks 2-3, at home to Toronto Maple Leafs 2-3, at home to St. Louis Blues 1-3, at Columbus Blue Jackets 2-3 in overtime, at home to Colorado Avalanche 2-3.

Chicago's revenue is $60.96M against a payroll of $39.27M, and the profit sits at $24.65M. Attendance is 15103 a night at $77 a seat. Per point, the cost is $0.77M, which is 15th of 30. The operation is sound by every number the league files.

The dead rooms are not a secret. Four tracks carry no numbers at all in this build: coaching, legal, marketing, medical. Two more, the locker room and the scouting room, carry curves the file defines and the pipeline drops. Chicago, Vancouver and Florida spent slots on the half-dead pair. Six clubs upgraded rooms that change what a player feels on the ice.

Chicago has the money, the profit and the prestige standing. The equipment room is at zero, and the club has not spent a slot on it.
