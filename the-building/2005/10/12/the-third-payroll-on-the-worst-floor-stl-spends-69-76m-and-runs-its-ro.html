The Third Payroll on the Worst Floor: STL spends $69.76M and runs its rooms at minus sixteen

Colin Skuin's club pays the league's third-highest salary, keeps its men the second-happiest, and plays in one of the four worst facility suites on the sheet

Ruth HalloranBusiness of hockey · The BuildingWednesday, 12 October 2005

St. Louis Blues runs payroll at $69.76M, the third-highest number in the league. Its four live rooms sum to -16, the worst tier among thirty clubs. Colin Skuin sits 27th in prestige (539) and the gap between those two numbers explains the ranking.

The St. Louis Blues carry a practice rink at level zero, the floor. Every attribute that walks onto the ice at the Savvis Center starts 6 points below par. Equipment sits at 1 (-4). Workout sits at 1 (-4). Only travel, raised from 1 to 2 in the May window, saves the club from a worse number. The two upgrades on record since December went to travel (real) and scouting (meaningless here: the scouting room feeds only trade valuation, a number nobody outside a club can see).

St. Louis Blues carries the second-highest club morale in the league, at 96.0. Only Vancouver Canucks runs warmer, at 99.5. The men are content. The rooms around them are not helping.

Four clubs share the -16 live sum. STL is the one paying the third-most for the privilege. Atlanta Thrashers runs $39.32M on payroll, the lowest in the league. Florida spends $57.33M. Phoenix spends $55.41M. All four land in the same hole.

STL took in $27.32M in revenue against $69.76M in payroll and kept $18.89M in profit. The gate works: 14,837 a night at $80 a seat. None of that money went to the practice rink.

What a level-zero rink costs at home

A practice rink at level 0 applies -6 to every attribute at home, halved on the overall. A club at level 2 (-2) gives away 4 points per attribute relative to zero. St. Louis has never raised its practice rink above its floor on the upgrade record. The May 29-31 window, the biggest single facility push on the sheet, raised STL's travel one notch and left the rink alone.

Nine clubs spent that window entirely on rooms that do nothing: PHX, COL, DET, LA, NYI, DAL, CGY, CHI and ATL got only scouting or locker-room upgrades. STL at least got one real thing, barely.

The going concern

Prestige stands at 539. Two Cups sit behind Toronto Maple Leafs and New Jersey Devils, not here. STL's last playoff run went 0 rounds. The record has never listed a St. Louis Cup. Garrett Turmel at New York Rangers carries $92.95M on payroll and prestige 526, the league's lowest, in a building that runs its rooms at -14. At least the New York gap between money spent and money earned is visible in the per-point column: $23.24M a point, the best among clubs with points. STL's per-point sits at $34.88M.

The Blues drew a crowd, sold the seat, made the profit, and kept the men happy. The rooms cost them 6 attributes a night on home ice and nobody has touched the track. That is what $69.76M buys when it goes into contracts instead of concrete.
