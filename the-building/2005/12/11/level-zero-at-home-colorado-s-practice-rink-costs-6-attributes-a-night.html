Level Zero at Home: Colorado's Practice Rink Costs 6 Attributes a Night, and the Club Goes 5-9 Inside It

The Avalanche carry the 4th-highest payroll in the league, two rooms at the floor, and a home record that tracks the penalty

Ruth HalloranBusiness of hockey · The BuildingSunday, 11 December 2005

Colorado Avalanche practises at Level Zero. The practice rink sits at the bottom of the tuning table, which costs every Avalanche player 6 attributes on all measures when he steps on his own ice. Colorado is 5-9 at home.

On the road the penalty disappears. Travel is at Level 4, which is par and reads zero. Colorado goes 7-6 away. The penalty lives in the building.

The numbers are not subtle. The tuning file gives Level 0 on practice a modifier of -6 on all attributes at home. The workout room at Level 0 gives -5 on strength, checking and endurance everywhere. Those two rooms account for 11 of the Avalanche's -14 live sum. The other -3 comes from equipment at Level 2 (-3). Travel costs them nothing because they paid for it.

Kerry Morassutti said the constraint himself on The Tunnel this week. "Workout at zero, practice at zero, yeah, those hurt. I know they hurt. I feel it every day I watch us play at home." Asked whether he could raise both to Level 3, he did not hesitate: "The payroll won't let me. That's the tradeoff and I'm making it with my eyes open."

The money is in four bodies

Colorado's payroll is $66.38M, the 4th-highest in the league behind New York, Toronto and St. Louis. It returns 29 points and a cost of $2.29M per point, the 5th-worst in the league. Morassutti named the problem directly: "Four players account for a huge chunk of it and all four are playing well right now. Forsberg's got 32 in 27, Kariya's got 30 in 27."

The question the record cannot answer is whether that money buys more in a sweater than it would buy in a room. The file says a practice rink at Level 3 moves from -1 to 0 on the modifier, a swing of 5 attributes at home. Colorado's home record would need to move from 5-9 to roughly .500 for the math to look like it pays. That is 4 more wins in 14 home games. Nobody has run that experiment.

What the record does show is the shape of the gap: Colorado is one of only 3 clubs in the league with a better road record than home record after 27 games. The other two are Chicago (1-13 at home) and San Jose (3-11 at home), both carrying a practice rink at Level 0 as well.

Chicago's home disaster is not practice alone. The Blackhawks run Level 0 on equipment, Level 1 on workout, Level 1 on practice and Level 1 on travel. Their -17 live sum is the worst in the league by 3 points. San Jose carries a -6 on practice at home and a -4 on equipment, but its travel is only at Level 2 (-2), so the road penalty partially offsets the home one.

Colorado is the only club in that trio whose penalty is entirely a home problem. The practice rink at zero is the mechanism. Travel at four means the bus costs nothing. The Avalanche pay for their building on a Thursday night at the Pepsi and collect it back on a Saturday in Edmonton.

The room at the top that reads nothing

One thing the record will not let you say: that Colorado's Level 4 locker room has anything to do with the club's league-best 98.2 morale average. The locker room carries a curve in the tuning file. Nothing in this pipeline reads it. Five clubs sit at Level 4 (Colorado, Tampa Bay, Florida, Los Angeles, Vancouver) and they do occupy 5 of the top 6 on the Morale Scale, but the mechanism is not decoded and the desk will not assert one.

What is decoded is the practice rink. It moves a rating. Colorado knows it costs them. They have chosen not to fix it. Morassutti told The Tunnel he'd "love to get both to three tonight" and then named the reason he can't: the payroll. Four bodies have the money.

Colorado is 5-9 at home with a -6 on all attributes in that building. The road will carry them. It already is.
