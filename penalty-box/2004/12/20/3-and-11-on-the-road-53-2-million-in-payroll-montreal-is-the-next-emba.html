3 AND 11 ON THE ROAD, $53.2 MILLION IN PAYROLL: MONTREAL IS THE NEXT EMBARRASSMENT

Six straight losses, 141 goals allowed, a room at 86.9, and one 22-year-old the coach keeps leaning on

Mickey "The Mick" DonnellyColumnist, ex-goalie · The Penalty BoxMonday, 20 December 2004
ShareCopy link

The Montreal Canadiens Canadiens are 9-19-2, on a 6-game losing streak, and 3-11 on the road. They pay $53.20 million for 24 points, which is $2.22 million per point, the second-worst value in hockey. Philadelphia sits alone at the bottom at $2.38 million per point. Everyone else in this league gets more from their money than Montreal gets from its payroll.

The room has checked out. Club morale sits at 86.9, tied with Minnesota Wild and Philadelphia Flyers for the third-lowest average in the league. No individual Canadien cracks the Bureau's lowest 15, but when an entire roster averages 86.9 you get flat starts and blown coverages, a team that stops competing when the third period gets tight.

Komisarek is 22 and carrying everyone else's minutes

Mike Komisarek74 Mike Komisarek, 22, is the only Montreal Canadiens player on the Bureau's Development Index risers list. He sits at +6 form, one of six men in the league at that mark. He's putting up 39 points in 32 games from the blue line. The coach leans on a kid because the men ahead of him aren't showing up.

Richard Zednik77 Richard Zednik leads the club with 50 points in 32 games. He's the only Canadien anywhere near the league's top 30 scorers. One man for a payroll near the top of the cap sheet. Everyone else on the front lines is invisible and the coach keeps rolling them into losses.

The road record is the whole indictment. Three wins, eleven losses. Teams in other buildings see the crest on the sweater, look at the lineup card, and play with confidence because they know what 3-11 means: a team that waits to be scored on, gives up the middle, and doesn't close.

Vancouver Canucks pays $34.94 million and has 42 points, $0.83 million per point. Edmonton Oilers pays $36.81 million and has 41, $0.90 million per point. Those clubs buy wins. Montreal pays $2.22 million per point and buys $77 tickets sold to 15,618 people who watch their team lose in other men's buildings in December.

Revenue reads $49.47 million. Profit reads $21.18 million. The owners are fine. The building fills. The checks clear. The coach doesn't change the lineup, the forwards don't score, and the kid at 22 keeps skating more than he should because nobody else will.

Komisarek is +6 on the Index. The only good news in a $53.20 million season.
