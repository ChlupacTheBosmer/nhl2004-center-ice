98.5 AND LOSING: The Room Is Perfect. The Roster Isn't.

Florida has the highest morale in hockey and 23rd-most points. That's not culture. That's a comfort zone with a $52M price tag.

Mickey "The Mick" DonnellyColumnist, ex-goalie · The Penalty BoxFriday, 24 December 2004
ShareCopy link

The piece

Florida's club morale sits at 98.5 out of 100, the highest of all 30 rooms in this league. The Panthers are 12-17-2. They sit 23rd in points.

I wrote last week about Calgary: four men in the league's morale basement, six straight losses, 31 goals for and 47 against over ten games. A broken room is a broken team. I meant it then. I mean it now.

Florida is the proof of the other half. The room is perfect. The bench is still not good enough.

Joe Nieuwendyk81 sits at 100 morale. He plays 21.6 minutes a night. He grades 76 in the Book. Valeri Bure75 sits at 100 morale. He grades 70 and plays 18.2 minutes. These men are happy. They are also not carrying anybody past 25 wins. When the two guys eating the most minutes are a 76 and a 70, and both are thrilled about it, nobody in that dressing room is going to look sideways and ask for more. That is what 98.5 looks like from the outside: no man is uncomfortable enough to push.

The payroll is $52.17M. The point total is 30. That is $1.74M per point, the 11th-worst value in the league. The building seats 15,216 at $79 a ticket. The front office still books $12.93M in profit off $41.45M in revenue. Nobody in that organization is in financial pain. The coach keeps the room warm. The veterans stay at 100. The 15,216 people paying $79 watch another season of first-period effort, second-period drift, and a third period that comes too late.

Over the last ten games, Florida has scored 32 and allowed 29. They are not getting embarrassed. They are losing 3-2 and 2-1 and calling it a step forward. The Development Index has Vaclav Nedorost77 up 6 on the Index. He has 4 points in 33 games. The Index says he's improving. The scoresheet says he's invisible.

I said the room matters. I still say that. Calgary is my case: a morale average of 87.2 and a 10-20-3 record are the same sentence. But a morale average of 98.5 and a 12-17-2 record are also the same sentence. Calgary needs a coach who can fix a broken group. Florida needs two players who grade 85 and make the 76 and the 70 sweat.

Florida does not need more happiness. They need two upgrades on the blue line and at center who change the standard, not the mood. Until then, they have the nicest room in hockey and the same product they've had all year: competent, pleasant, and 7 games back of a playoff spot.
