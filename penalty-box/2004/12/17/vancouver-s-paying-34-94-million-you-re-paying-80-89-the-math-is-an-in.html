VANCOUVER'S PAYING $34.94 MILLION. YOU'RE PAYING $80.89. THE MATH IS AN INSULT.

The cheapest 40-point team in hockey has the second-highest morale in the league and a pair of forwards who make the rest of the league look like they are buying air.

Mickey "The Mick" DonnellyColumnist, ex-goalie · The Penalty BoxFriday, 17 December 2004
ShareCopy link

Vancouver Canucks has 40 points. Their payroll is $34.94 million. That is $0.87 million per point, the best in the league. The next team on the list is Edmonton at $0.94. After that the numbers start looking like what they are: people paying too much for too little.

Let me put it next to the teams I have been writing about. Detroit: $80.89 million, 36 points, $2.25 million per point. Philadelphia: $69.07 million, 27 points, $2.56 million per point. Vancouver: $34.94 million, 40 points, $0.87 million per point. Vancouver has 4 more points than Detroit and 13 more than Philadelphia, and the payroll is less than half.

Markus Naslund91 is 31. He has 61 points in 31 games, 161 pace, 19.9 minutes a night. Overall grade 91. He is not a rookie. He is not a kid on a hot streak. He is a 31-year-old producing at an elite rate on the second-cheapest payroll in the league.

Todd Bertuzzi95 is 29. 53 points, 140 pace, 21.1 minutes a night. Overall grade 95. That is a man who the Bureau has at the ceiling of what a player can be, playing 21 minutes a night, on a team that costs $34.94 million to field.

The Bureau's Morale Scale has Vancouver at 96.4. That is second in the league, behind only Washington at 96.7. The room is happy. The room is happy and it is 16-9-4. The attendance is 14837, the ticket is $78. People are filling the building for a team that costs less than half what Detroit costs.

I do not know what Vancouver's front office is doing differently. I do not know if it is the coach, the draft, the luck of the draw. What I know is that the rest of the league is paying for air and Vancouver is paying for hockey. $34.94 million buys you 40 points, a 95-graded forward playing 21 minutes a night, and a room with the second-highest morale in the league.

The ticket is $78. The building is 14837 deep. Go look at it. Then go look at what you are paying for in Detroit, in Philadelphia, in a dozen other cities where the payroll is a rounding error on the per-point column and the seats in the blue section are empty.

The math is not close. The math is an insult, and it is aimed at every owner in this league who thinks $70 million buys you a team.
