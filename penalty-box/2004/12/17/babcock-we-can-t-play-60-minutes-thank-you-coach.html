BABCOCK: "WE CAN'T PLAY 60 MINUTES." THANK YOU, COACH.

The head of a 12-12-6 team just told the camera his room cannot sustain a full game. $41.01 million buys you two good periods and a stretch where you're flat.

Mickey "The Mick" DonnellyColumnist, ex-goalie · The Penalty BoxFriday, 17 December 2004
ShareCopy link

Babcock said it on the record. "We're not a team that can play 60 minutes for four nights in a row. We'll get two good periods, we'll get a stretch where you're flat. And the teams in this league, they punish you. Every night."

That is not a post-game non-answer. That is a coach diagnosing his own product on camera and then shrugging.

Mighty Ducks of Anaheim is 12-12-6 in 30 games. 30 points. $41.01 million in payroll. The Bureau's Morale Scale has the room at 91.0, which means the guys are content to be where they are. They are content to be where they are and they cannot finish a hockey game. That is on the man holding the whistle.

He also said "We've got to score more around him" about their goalie, who sits at 10-13. I want you to sit with what that sentence does. It points at the man in the net and says the problem is the team in front of him cannot hold a lead. The goalie is 10-13 because the room in front of him is, by the coach's own words, structurally incapable of playing the third period at the level it needs to.

The room is young. Babcock said so himself. Stajan is 20. Lydman is 20. Mottau is 26 and playing 20 minutes. Sergei Fedorov90 is 34 and has 26 goals, which is a quarter of the team's 97 total. The rest is a group of kids who find their legs for two periods and then go quiet. The coach's answer to that is to point at the goalie.

The attendance is 14515. The ticket is $79. For that ticket you get a team the coach says is broken in the third period. In Detroit you pay $81 for a 40-year-old and a man who plays 9 minutes a night. In Anaheim you pay $79 for a room that the head coach tells you cannot play 60. I do not know which is the bigger con.

"Teams punish you. Every night." He said that too. He said all of it. The question for the Anaheim front office is what they do with a coach who just handed you the diagnosis on a silver platter. You have a man who tells you the product is defective, and you are still paying $41.01 million for it.

The road record is 4-12. The coach said "the details matter more" on the road and "we haven't paid attention to the details enough." The details. As in, he knows what he is failing to do and he is telling you about it on camera. The 9-game losing stretch is over. The 60-minute problem is not.
