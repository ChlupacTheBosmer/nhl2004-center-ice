WHAT $80.89 MILLION BUYS YOU: A 40-YEAR-OLD AND A MAN WHO PLAYS NINE MINUTES

Last week I told you Detroit was the most overpaid team in hockey. Here's the autopsy. The roster isn't just expensive. It's badly built, and the coach is holding the chisel.

Mickey "The Mick" DonnellyColumnist, ex-goalie · The Penalty BoxSaturday, 4 December 2004
ShareCopy link

I'll say it again because nobody in Dearborn listened: Detroit Red Wings is the most overpaid team in hockey. $3.11 million per point. No club in this league gets a worse return on a payroll dollar. You want to know what that money actually bought? I'll tell you. Because the sheet is right there and the numbers don't lie, even if Doug Reimer's little pivot table does.

Your top scorer is Brett Hull84. Forty years old. Twenty-three minutes a night. Forty-two points in twenty-four games. He's good for his age — I'll give him that, because I remember when forty-year-olds hung up the skates and took up broadcasting. But your top scorer is old enough to remember when the fourth line was optional, and you paid $80.89 million to put him on your back and let the rest of the roster limp.

Now here's the part that makes me want to put my mask through a window. Tomas Holmstrom76. Eighty-nine seconds per game of overtime. Eight point nine minutes a night. Morale 74. Salary: $1.30 million. You are paying a second-line winger's money to a fourth-line grinder who can't smell the ice long enough to get his skates wet. And the coach keeps putting him out there. Not because it works — it doesn't. The team scores 89 and gives up 99. They're OUTSCORED by ten goals on eighty-one million dollars of payroll.

Three and eight on the road. THREE. AND EIGHT. These guys can't win a game away from their own building. They've lost five straight. They're 8-9-4 on the season. And somewhere in the front office, a man with a spreadsheet is telling himself this is "transitional."

Let me give you the contrast, because I know Reimer is going to say I cherry-pick. Vancouver Canucks spends $34.94 million. Thirty-four ninety-four. Less than half of Detroit's outlay. They have 33 points and a four-game win streak. Their per-point cost: $1.06 million. You are paying them three times over for a worse product.

Colorado Avalanche spends $71.56 million and has 42 points. Forty-two. They're twenty and four and zero. They score 125 and allow 67. They spend six million dollars LESS than Detroit and they're fourteen points better. Fourteen points. In one month of hockey.

And what does the coach do? He keeps Holmstrom on a line he's not good enough for. He keeps dressing a roster that's outscored. He keeps asking a forty-year-old to carry the offense while the checking centers play ten minutes and collect a million and a half. This isn't bad luck. This isn't "the puck didn't bounce." This is a man who looked at $80 million and decided the right move was a fourth-line winger who doesn't want to be here.

Holmstrom's morale is 74. That's not a player who's locked in. That's a player who knows what he is in this lineup and knows what they're paying him and knows it's a waste of both. And the coach can't see it because he's too busy looking at the spreadsheet that says "positive intangibles."

I was a goalie. I know what it looks like when a locker room doesn't believe. I also know what it looks like when the coach won't make the call. This is the second thing. They're going to keep losing and the only one they'll fire is the wrong one.
