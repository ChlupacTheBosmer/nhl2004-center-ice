THE MOST OVERPAID TEAM IN HOCKEY (DO THE MATH)

Detroit spends $80.89 million to buy 26 points and a five-game skid. Vancouver spends half that and has more wins than Detroit has losses.

Mickey "The Mick" DonnellyColumnist, ex-goalie · The Penalty BoxSaturday, 4 December 2004
ShareCopy link

I'll say it again for the people in the upper deck who just handed over $81 a pop to watch this disaster unfold: the Detroit Red Wings Red Wings are the most overpaid team in hockey. Not the most overpaid player. The most overpaid team. And I want every Wings fan to look at their ticket stub, then look at the standings, and tell me that's not an insult to the game.

Here's the number that should make Detroit management sick to its stomach: $3.11 million per point. That is the highest figure in this league. Highest. Not second-highest, not close. The next-worst value is Atlanta Thrashers at $2.83, and those people are rebuilding from a 5-and-16 season like sensible people. Detroit is supposed to be a contender.

Now put them next to Vancouver Canucks. The Canucks spend $34.94 million. They have 33 points. That's $1.06 million per point. More than double the payroll buys you fewer points. Vancouver's coach is doing his job. Vancouver's room isn't rotting. The whole building has a pulse.

Detroit is 8-9-4 after 24 games with five straight losses. In their last ten, the Wings have scored 32 goals and given up 44. That's not a hockey team. That's a punching bag with a payroll sheet.

And I'll blame the coach before I blame anyone else, because that's where accountability starts. You don't put Tomas Holmstrom68 on the ice for 8.9 minutes a night and expect the building to roll its sleeves up. A 74-overall winger with $1.30 million on his back, morale sitting at 74 — in the league's basement — and he's watching the fourth line outscore him. That's not a player problem. That's a bench problem.

The room is gone in Detroit. I can see it in the morale numbers. The club average is 88.7, which puts them eighth-worst in this league. You cannot win a hockey season in this league with a top-eight worst room. The players look at the coach, the coach looks at the front office, and nobody looks at the ice.

Meanwhile, 15,688 people are showing up each night at $81 a ticket to watch a team that has given up 99 goals in 24 games. The attendance isn't loyalty. It's Stockholm syndrome with a parking pass.

If I ran that building I'd make a change before this skid hits eight games. You don't fire the players — you fire the guy drawing up the power plays and sitting your grinders for six minutes a night while the other team's fourth line is outworking yours. And then you stop pretending that $80.89 million buys something.

It doesn't. Not in this league. Not right now. Not in Detroit.
