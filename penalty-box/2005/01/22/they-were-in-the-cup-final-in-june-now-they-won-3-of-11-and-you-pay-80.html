THEY WERE IN THE CUP FINAL IN JUNE. NOW THEY WON 3 OF 11 AND YOU PAY $80 TO WATCH IT.

$71.56 million. 3 wins in 11. 39 goals against. $22.29 million profit. The Avalanche are selling you a loss and calling it a business model.

Mickey "The Mick" DonnellyColumnist, ex-goalie · The Penalty BoxSaturday, 22 January 2005
ShareCopy link

The piece

The Colorado Avalanche Avalanche finished last June one round shy of the Stanley Cup. Their payroll today is $71.56 million, the third-highest in this league. They have lost 7 of their last 11. They are being outscored 39 to 31 over that stretch. And the building is full at $80 a seat.

This is what accountability looks like from upstairs: $69.98 million in revenue, $22.29 million in profit, and a product that is getting worse every week. The owner doesn't need to fix anything. You bought your ticket.

Three wins in eleven

Colorado went 23 and 9 earlier this season. They are 25-15-3 now. That stretch between 23-9 and today has produced 3 regulation losses, 6 total losses (I'll spare you the overtime math), and a home record that has sagged to 13 and 12. Thirteen and twelve. In front of 16,805 people who paid eighty dollars each to watch their Cup Final team get beat.

Peter Forsberg95 has 87 points. Milan Hejduk89 has 84. That is two top-10 scorers on a roster that cannot close a game. Forsberg told The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk after the ANA loss on January 9 what everyone in that building already knows but nobody upstairs will say: "We are not playing bad hockey. I do not think we are. But we are not winning those games anymore."

He's right about the hockey. He's wrong about the excuse. You are 25-15-3 and $71.56 million and you should be winning those games. That is what $71.56 million buys. Not excuses. Wins.

Vancouver Canucks has 61 points on $34.94 million. Colorado has 59 on $71.56 million. Vancouver makes more profit with half the payroll and fewer people in the building. Do the math yourself. I shouldn't have to.

What the coach owes them

There is no name on the sheet for who sits behind that bench and calls the lines, and Mickey Donnelly has learned that pointing at a title without a name is how you lose a column. So I will point at the results: 13 and 12 at home. 39 goals against in 11 games. A third team that cannot protect a lead. Forsberg said they're "not playing bad hockey." Fine. Then change the matchups. Change the minutes. Change something other than your seat.

The Avalanche host Columbus Blue Jackets tomorrow night. Geoff Sanderson83 has 87 points. The building will be full. The team will probably lose. And the profit line will still say $22.29 million. And nobody upstairs will lose a night's sleep.

That is what $80 a seat buys in Denver. A seat at the loss.
