NOT ONE TOP-30 SCORER, 6 AND 16 ON THE ROAD, $1.65 MILLION PER POINT: PHILADELPHIA IS BUYING LOSSES IN BULK

The Flyers have the 4th-highest payroll in hockey and the worst cost-per-point in the league. The building is full at $78 and nobody upstairs is losing a night's sleep.

Mickey "The Mick" DonnellyColumnist, ex-goalie · The Penalty BoxTuesday, 18 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Philadelphia pays $69.32 million in payroll, the 4th most in this league. The return is 42 points. That comes to $1.65 million per point, the worst figure on any balance sheet in the thirty-club league. The Detroit Red Wings pay more. The Rangers pay more. Colorado pays more. Detroit has 54 points. The Rangers have 57. Colorado has 57. Philadelphia has 42.

And not one man in that sweater cracks the Bureau's top 30 in scoring. Forty-four games in, nobody from Philadelphia Flyers sits among the 30 players with the most points. A top-4 payroll and zero offensive threats the rest of the league tracks. You could dress the third line in street clothes and the opponent's game plan wouldn't change.

The road is a graveyard

Home, they go 13 and 9. Respectable enough. On the road: 6 and 16. In division play, they sit last. One regulation win in seven games, 5 regulation losses, 32 goals for, 32 against. Every division rival has figured out how to beat them outside their building.

The last ten games read LWWLWLWLWL. Five and five. Coin-flip hockey at $69.32 million. The streak column shows L1, and the calendar puts them at home to Tampa Bay Lightning tonight. Tampa is 10 straight, 46 goals for and 22 against over that stretch. Philadelphia will be the 11th victim or it won't, and nobody in the front office will care either way, because the building is full.

The room is fine. That's the problem.

Club morale sits at 91.2, 14th of 30 on the Bureau's scale. The players aren't unhappy. The product isn't close to justifying $78 a seat in front of 14,619 people, but nobody in that locker room is throwing helmets. The disconnect isn't a bad night. It's the whole design: a coach who cannot get a road team to play one style of hockey for 60 minutes, a payroll stacked with players who won't appear on any top-30 list, and a front office that prints $9.02 million in profit and calls it a Tuesday.

Revenue: $53.63 million. Costs: $69.32 million in salaries. And somehow they're profitable, because 14,619 fans at $78 a night plus everything else the building prints covers the gap. You could field the AHL team in Philadelphia and the books would balance by April.

The bill

Here is what a Flyers ticket buys you in 2004-05: $1.65 million per point of production, a 6-16 road record, no player in the league's top 30 scorers, and a last-place divisional standing with 13 games left in the Atlantic. The profit is $9.02 million. The prestige sits at 321. The Cups column is zero.

I've been in buildings where the front office made bad money choices. I've never been in one where the choices were this expensive and the product this thin while the turnstiles kept turning. You are not watching a bad team. You are watching an expensive team that has been asked to be mediocre and is succeeding.
