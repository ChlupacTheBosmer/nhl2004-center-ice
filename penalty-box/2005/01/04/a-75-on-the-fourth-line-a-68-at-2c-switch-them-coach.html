A 75 ON THE FOURTH LINE. A 68 AT 2C. SWITCH THEM, COACH.

Pittsburgh buries its best center on checking duty and plays a 68 at second-line wing. The room knows. The goals against say so.

Mickey "The Mick" DonnellyColumnist, ex-goalie · The Penalty BoxTuesday, 4 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Brian Holzinger76 grades 75 in the Bureau's Book. He plays the fourth line and the second penalty-kill unit. Kris Beech70 grades 68. He wears the second-line center, the power-play, and the fourth-line wing all in the same shift rotation. The Pittsburgh Penguins coaching staff keeps doing this, and the product matches.

[[team:Pittsburgh]] has allowed 171 goals in 38 games. St. Louis Blues has also allowed 171 in 38. Two teams, tied for the worst defensive club in the league. In the last ten games, Pittsburgh has been outscored 28 to 43. You do not fix a goal differential that bad with a fourth-line center killing penalties while a 68 takes the second-draw faceoffs.

The Development Index shows the rot is spreading. Billy Tibbetts65 has fallen to 65, two points below his base. He doesn't appear in the games-played column. When the Index drops you and the minutes disappear at the same time, you don't come back. You go somewhere else, or you go home. The man knows it. The room knows it. Pittsburgh's team morale is 87.1, the third-lowest in the league, and the only clubs below them are Ottawa at 85.8 and Calgary at 86.8, both of which at least have the excuse of a six-game losing streak.

Mario Lemieux88 is 39 years old and the entire reason [[team:Pittsburgh]] scores hockey goals. 66 points in 32 games. A pace of 169 over 82. He plays 19.9 minutes a night, the lowest of any top-ten scorer, and at 39 you can argue that is mercy. You can also argue the coach is asking a 39-year-old to carry an offense because he won't trust a 75 on the second line.

The math on the door: $79 a seat, 14,097 in the building, $43.35M payroll, $1.31M per point, $15.09M profit. The owners are fine. The owners are always fine. The man buying the $79 ticket is watching a fourth-line platoon and a 39-year-old trying to drag a franchise to 33 points.

Here is the fix. You put Holzinger on the second line. You play Beech on the fourth. You see who has the legs, who has the head, who has the grade. You tell the room that 75 gets more than 68. That is how you raise a morale from 87.1. Not with speeches. With a line card that respects the grade.

The last ten reads as bad as the goal differential says it does. Two wins, eight losses, and a coach still running a 68 through the dots while a 75 kills penalties. I don't care what the system is. I don't care about the matchups. A 75 gets the top six. A 68 gets the bottom. You are the coach. You make the switch.
