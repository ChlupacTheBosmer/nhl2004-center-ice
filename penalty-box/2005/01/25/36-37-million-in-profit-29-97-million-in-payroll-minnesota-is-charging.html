$36.37 MILLION IN PROFIT, $29.97 MILLION IN PAYROLL: MINNESOTA IS CHARGING YOU $80 TO WATCH A BUSINESS PLAN

The cheapest roster in hockey is printing the most money in hockey. You are the customer.

Mickey "The Mick" DonnellyColumnist, ex-goalie · The Penalty BoxTuesday, 25 January 2005
ShareCopy link

The piece

The Minnesota Wild Wild made $36.37 million in profit on a payroll of $29.97 million. That is the highest profit in hockey. It is also the lowest payroll in hockey. Sixteen wins, twenty-five losses, three overtime losses, 45 points, and a line item that says the owner walked away richer than any man in the building.

You paid $80 a seat to watch that. The building held 13,522 of you per night. You watched 158 goals for, 183 against, and a team that lost 25 games in regulation. The profit didn't come from winning. It came from spending less on players than any other club in the league and charging you more.

The next-cheapest payroll belongs to the Atlanta Thrashers Thrashers at $36.79 million. Atlanta has 44 points. Minnesota has 45. Both buildings are half-empty on a good night. Both owners are rich.

The Wild's cost per point is $0.67 million, which looks efficient on a spreadsheet until you realize it means the roster is too thin to fill its gaps. Pierre-Marc Bouchard77 is 20 years old, has 21 points in 49 games, and sits +6 on the Bureau's Development Index. He is the best thing in that building and he is 21 points in 49 games. That is not a core. That is a lottery-ticket line.

Willie Mitchell72 has 60 penalty minutes in 49 games, 1.22 per game. The team that can't score is also spending time in the box, which means the penalty kill is eating the legs of a roster already too short to carry a full sixty minutes.

Home record: 11 and 16. Away record: 10 and 12. The building doesn't matter. They are equally bad everywhere. The crowd comes, pays $80, watches a team that has lost 25 in regulation, and goes home. The owner counts $61.78 million in revenue against a $29.97 million bill and calls it a business model.

The prestige points say 586, third in the league behind Nashville and New Jersey. The history is there. The building has seen better. The men on the ice are not built to see it again this year, and the men in the front office have made sure they don't have to spend enough to change that.

Doug Reimer will tell you the cost-per-point is excellent. Doug Reimer can put it in his spreadsheet and frame it. I played in rooms where a man making $0.67 million per point was a man who couldn't clear the puck out of his own zone, and the room smelled like mediocrity by February. You cannot build on a profit margin. You build on a roster. Minnesota has neither.
