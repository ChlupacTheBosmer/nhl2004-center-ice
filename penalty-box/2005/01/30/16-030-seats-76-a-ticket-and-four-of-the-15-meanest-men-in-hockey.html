16,030 SEATS, $76 A TICKET, AND FOUR OF THE 15 MEANEST MEN IN HOCKEY

Buffalo has the cheapest ticket in the league and four players in the Bureau's bottom-15 morale. The room is broken and the building is full anyway.

Mickey "The Mick" DonnellyColumnist, ex-goalie · The Penalty BoxSunday, 30 January 2005
ShareCopy link

Buffalo has the cheapest ticket in hockey, one of the best crowds in the league, and four of the fifteen most miserable players in the sport.

The Buffalo Sabres have 16,030 people through the turnstiles at $76 a night. That is the lowest ticket price in the league and the fifth-highest attendance. The room is paying for a product it hates. And the Bureau's Morale Scale backs it up: four Buffalo players sit in the league's bottom 15, all at 74, the second-lowest score on the published list.

Martin Biron88 is at 74. He is 88 overall, the starting goaltender, making $2.10M with zero years left on his contract. Andrew Brunette74 is at 74. He is 74 overall, on $1.10M, and playing 8.6 minutes a night. Eric Boulton66 is at 74. Mike Leclerc74 is at 74. Buffalo has four men in that list. No other club has more than three.

The team is 18 and 21 and 5. Fifty-one points. The last ten games read: L, L, L, W, L, W, L, OT, L, O, T, L, L. Two regulation wins in ten. Nobody on this roster cracked the top 30 in scoring. The room averages 88.4 on the Morale Scale, fifth-lowest in the league.

And the building is full.

$44.16M payroll. $0.87M per point. $53.47M in revenue. $20.21M in profit. The cheapest ticket in hockey, 16,030 seats, and the front office clears $20.21M a season watching its own players hate coming to work. Nobody in that building is losing a night's sleep.

Steve Begin68 is down three on the Development Index. The coach keeps him in the lineup. Biron is a free agent after June. He plays 55.7 minutes a night. His morale is 74 and his contract has zero years left. He will leave. You can see it from here.

Buffalo is at San Jose Sharks tonight, first meeting of the season. San Jose is 17 and 24 and 7, 10 and 13 at home. Buffalo's last road win is somewhere in the middle of that 2 and 7 and 1 stretch. This is the floor. This is the night a coach either shows his men the next shift matters or he shows them he has already gone home.

I say Buffalo loses.
