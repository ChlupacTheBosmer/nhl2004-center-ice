12 AND 15 AT HOME: THE LEAGUE'S BEST SCORER PLAYS FOR THE HAPPIEST ROOM THAT CAN'T WIN IN FRONT OF YOU

Jaromir Jagr leads hockey with 102 points. Washington wins more on the road than at home, and the ticket is $78.

Mickey "The Mick" DonnellyColumnist, ex-goalie · The Penalty BoxMonday, 7 February 2005
ShareCopy link

Jaromir Jagr84 has 102 points, more than anyone in the league. Washington Capitals win more games on the road than in their own building. You pay $78 to watch the second part.

The Washington Capitals are 12 and 15 at home and 18 and 10 away. Jagr is 32 years old, plays 22.1 minutes a night, and is the only Capital among the Bureau's top 30 scorers. The team has 204 goals in 55 games. The scoring is real. The home wins are not.

The Bureau's Morale Scale has Washington at 94.9, tied for second-best in the league. The profit is $15.71 million on a $57.88 million payroll. The cost per point is $0.86 million. Nobody in that front office is losing sleep. The attendance sits at 16,049 and the ticket is $78. The only person losing sleep is the one who drives home after another Tuesday loss at the arena.

ROAD WINS DON'T PAY YOUR TICKET

Eighteen wins on the road. Twelve at home. It is not a talent gap. Jagr gets the same 22.1 minutes in a building where 16,049 people can see him as he does in one where nobody knows his name. Whoever lines this team up for home games is making decisions that do not survive the first 20 minutes, and that is the coach's problem before it is anyone else's.

New York Islanders come to town tonight. The Islanders own the season series, 2 wins to 1, outscoring Washington 11 to 9 across three meetings. New York's road record is 11 and 12, so this is a matchup of two clubs that lose on the road and cannot fix it at home either. One of them leaves the building tonight still unable to figure out how to win in front of its own fans.

Jagr is putting up a 152-point pace at 32. He is not finished. The 12 and 15 says the men around him can't find a way to win where it should be easiest, and you are paying to watch them try every Tuesday and Thursday.
