95 PIM. 78 PIM. 16 WINS: LOS ANGELES IS THE PENALTY BOX WITH A GOALTENDER

Sean Avery leads the league in minutes. Ian Laperriere is fourth. Zigmund Palffy scores 92 points for a team that will not let him win.

Mickey "The Mick" DonnellyColumnist, ex-goalie · The Penalty BoxSunday, 13 February 2005
ShareCopy link

Sean Avery71 sits 1.70 minutes a night in the penalty box. 56 games in, 95 total. That leads the league. Ian Laperriere69 sits 1.39 a night, 78 total. That is fourth. They play for the same club. That club has won 16 hockey games.

Los Angeles Kings are 16 and 26 and 6. They play Calgary Flames tonight at home and the most likely outcome is another night watching two men serve penalties while Zigmund Palffy81 does something with a puck that nobody else can copy.

The numbers that prove nothing works

You cannot lose because you dress physical players. The Detroit Red Wings dress Darren McCarty75 at 83 PIM and Kirk Maltby81 at 69. Two top-nine penalized men. They are 23 and 21 and 5. The Vancouver Canucks dress Todd Bertuzzi93 at 71 and Scott Ferguson67 at 66. They are 29 and 17 and 5.

You lose when you have physical players and no plan around them. The road record is 10 and 19. When the home crowd is not screaming at the referee after Avery pushes a man into the boards, Los Angeles Kings get beaten. Ten wins in 29 chances on the road.

The one man who could change it

Mike Cammalleri77 is up six on the Bureau's Development Index. He has 38 points in 35 games at age 22. He is the second riser on this roster (the first being Jared Aulin69, also at +6). The coach has a 22-year-old producing 1.09 points a game and 56 games of a roster built for the box.

The payroll is $48.73 million. That buys 54 points, $0.90 million per point. The profit is $15.98 million. The attendance is 13,663, sixth-worst in the league, at $78 a ticket. The last ten games: four wins, five losses, one in overtime. The last two: losses.

The room is patient and that is the problem

Espen Knutsen72 and his Columbus club are the saddest room in hockey at 85.3 morale. Los Angeles sits at 92.1, middle of the pack. Nobody is crying in that locker room. They are comfortable losing. The owner banks $15.98 million, the coach keeps the third line, Avery keeps his ice time, Laperriere keeps the job description, and Palffy keeps putting up 92 points in 22.8 minutes a night for a team that has lost nine of eleven on the road since late December.

Boris Van Ryswyk of Florida Panthers told The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk this week: "Fifteen thousand people are buying a ticket at seventy-nine dollars and watching a team lose at home nine and eighteen. That bothers me more than the profit." Florida fills fifteen thousand seats. Los Angeles fills 13,663. The building is emptier and nobody in the front office flinches.

You are paying $78 to watch a penalty box that happens to skate. Avery leads the league in minutes. Laperriere leads it from the other side of the bench. And the wins never come.
