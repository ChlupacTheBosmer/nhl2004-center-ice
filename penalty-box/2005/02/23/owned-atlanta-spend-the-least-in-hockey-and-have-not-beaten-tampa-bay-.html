OWNED: ATLANTA SPEND THE LEAST IN HOCKEY AND HAVE NOT BEATEN TAMPA BAY ONCE

Kovalchuk has 98 points. Heatley has 91. The payroll is $36.79 million, the lowest in the league. The owner banked $23.15 million. Tampa Bay beat them four times.

Mickey "The Mick" DonnellyColumnist, ex-goalie · The Penalty BoxWednesday, 23 February 2005
ShareCopy link

Ilya Kovalchuk86 has 98 points in 59 games. He is 21. The Bureau's Development Index has him at +5, climbing off a base of 89 toward a 92 that puts him with the men who carry franchises. Dany Heatley85 has 91 points in 56 games at 24. Those two men have produced 189 points between them for the cheapest roster in professional hockey.

The Atlanta Thrashers Thrashers spend $36.79 million. That is the lowest payroll in the league. Lower than Edmonton Oilers, which is $36.81M. Lower than Minnesota Wild, which is $37.91M. The men below them on the scoring list all play for clubs that write checks. Kovalchuk's own front office writes a check that would fit in a coat pocket.

The owner banked $23.15 million in profit. The attendance is 14,193. The ticket is $78. The $36.79 million buys garbage around those two men, and the season series proves it: not a single win over Tampa Bay.

Four games against Tampa Bay Lightning this year. Four losses. 52 goals for, 66 against in the division. Tampa Bay pays $45.79 million and sits 20 points ahead in the standings. Washington Capitals pays $57.88 million and has 77 points. Atlanta has 60 points in a division where the bottom is a stone wall and the top is a building they cannot afford.

The Development Index is the part that makes this worse. Kovalchuk is rising. He is 21. In June he turns 22, which means this is not his ceiling, it is his floor. The Bureau has him at 92 overall today with the form term telling you there is more coming. Heatley reads 90 and he is already a top-10 scorer at 24. The window is open. The owner has looked at that window and decided the $23.15 million matters more than walking through it.

Atlanta is 22-28-2. They just lost one. They sit 4th in the Southeast, 14 points out with 23 games to play. Their 60 points ties them with Philadelphia Flyers, New York Islanders, and Phoenix Coyotes. Three clubs with more money and more depth than a team with a 92 and a 90 in their top two.

The hot stove crowd loves to print the per-point number: $0.61 million. Efficient, they call it. Good business. Here is what I call it: a man who owns two future Hall of Famers, spends less than the last-place team, and calls it fiscal responsibility while 14,193 people pay $78 a night to watch them lose the same opponent four times.

The Development Index does not forecast forever. Kovalchuk goes up. Then he stops going up. Then he goes down. Heatley is 24 now. At 27, if nothing changes around him, 91 points starts to feel like a fluke instead of a floor. The roster around them costs what it costs. The owner will spend what he spends. The math keeps working for him.

That is the whole thing. The cheapest room in hockey has the best young duo in the league and an owner who would rather bank another $23 million than chase a playoff spot. The man with the money has already decided next season is worth more than this one.
