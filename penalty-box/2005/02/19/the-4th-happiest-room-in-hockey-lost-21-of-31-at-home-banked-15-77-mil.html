THE 4TH HAPPIEST ROOM IN HOCKEY LOST 21 OF 31 AT HOME, BANKED $15.77 MILLION, AND 14996 PEOPLE KEEP SHOWING UP

Florida's locker room is content. Their home record is a disaster. The owner's profit is $15.77 million. The math says nobody in that building has any reason to change a thing.

Mickey "The Mick" DonnellyColumnist, ex-goalie · The Penalty BoxSaturday, 19 February 2005
ShareCopy link

I want to tell you about a room that is happy. Not satisfied. Not optimistic. Happy. The Florida Panthers locker room sits at 95.9 club morale, 4th in this league, above Tampa Bay, above New Jersey, above every team except the three that actually matter in the standings. They are happy. They have won 19 games.

The record at home: 10 and 21. Second-worst in the league. Only Chicago is worse at home, and Chicago has an excuse nobody in Sunrise gets to use. Florida's record in front of 14996 people who paid $79 each is a joke and the men in the sweater know it and the man who owns the team knows it and the room, somehow, is fine.

Here is the business. Revenue: $60.07 million. Payroll: $51.07 million. Profit: $15.77 million. The owner is $15.77 million ahead with a team that scores 174 goals and allows 182 in 58 games. He has no incentive to spend a dollar more. He has no incentive to trade for a scorer. He has no incentive to fire anyone. The ledger is green and the locker room is smiling and the record is 19 and 29 and 8.

Their head coach, Boris Van Ryswyk, put it plainly when he sat down with The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk on February 6: "Fifteen thousand people are buying a ticket at seventy-nine dollars and watching a team lose at home nine and eighteen. That bothers me more than the profit." He's right that it bothers you. It bothers me. But he was describing the problem the way a man describes weather. He was not describing a fire.

Nine and eighteen it was then. It is 10 and 21 now. Three weeks later the wound got deeper. They are not close to fixing it. They are not angry about it.

And that is what gets me. I have been in rooms that lost and I have been in rooms that won, and the ones that lost, the ones I remember, were the ones where a man broke a stick over his knee at 9 p.m. on a Thursday because his teammate took a soft hit instead of a hard one. The 95.9 means nobody is breaking anything. The 95.9 means Igor Ulanov67 is at 100 morale on a team that is last in the Southeast division and nobody finds that strange.

I have looked at the scorers. There is no Florida name in the top 30. Not one. You are paying $79 to watch a team that cannot put a man on the league's scoring sheet. Vaclav Nedorost83 has 17 points in 58 games. That is the closest thing to a centerpiece, and he is 77 overall and 22 years old, meaning the future is also not on the ice right now.

The last time Florida made the playoffs, they won a round. That was the whole thing. Zero Cups. 324 prestige points, 27th in this league. They have been this franchise for exactly as long as it has taken to build a $15.77 million habit of losing contentedly.

Boris told The Tunnel the team is "the healthiest team in the league." He said that with pride. The healthiest team in the league is last in its division. That is not a fact about injuries. That is an indictment of everything else, and the man in the room does not hear it.

14996 people will buy the ticket again. The profit will clear again. The room will be happy again. And the record will say 19 and 29 and something, and nobody will be mad, because being mad is what a room does when it is losing money or losing a job, and Florida is doing neither.
