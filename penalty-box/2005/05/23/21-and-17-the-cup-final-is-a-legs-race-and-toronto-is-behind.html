21 AND 17: THE CUP FINAL IS A LEGS RACE AND TORONTO IS BEHIND

Dallas has played four fewer playoff games, Turco has a 1.48, and Modano is 34 years old and leading this series in points

Mickey "The Mick" DonnellyColumnist, ex-goalie · The Penalty BoxMonday, 23 May 2005
ShareCopy link

21 games. That's what Toronto Maple Leafs have played since April 8. Dallas Stars have played 17. Four games. A Game 7 against Pittsburgh Penguins. A Game 7 against Washington Capitals. Four games of hockey that Dallas didn't have to play, and here we are, tied 1-1 in the Stanley Cup Final with Game 3 in Toronto tomorrow.

Toronto went 33-8 at home this season. I'm not going to pretend that doesn't matter. It matters. But 33-8 was a regular season, where you play Tuesday and Friday and you don't need to survive three overtime periods against Pittsburgh at 1:12 a.m. and then turn around seven days later and do it again against Washington. That's 4 games in the legs that Marty Turco95 and Mike Modano88 and every man on that Dallas bench simply didn't pay.

The goaltender with the lower number

Roberto Luongo97: 13-8, 1.89, .943, 2 shutouts. Marty Turco: 13-4, 1.48, .917. You want to talk save percentage? Luongo stops a higher share of shots. Fine. But a 1.48 means your team wins on one goal, means your defensemen aren't constantly bailing you out because you've already given them a cushion. Luongo's .943 tells me his team is under more pressure, over more games. Twenty-one games of it. The GAA tells me the game is over when Dallas scores once.

I stopped rubber in this league. I played 64 games in a season. I know what 21 games of April-May hockey does to a goaltender's eyes in the third period of Game 6, Game 7. Turco has sat on the bench on nights his team won in five. He has 17 games in the tank. Luongo has 21 and a 1.89 that only got worse as the rounds went deeper.

The 34-year-old with the freshest legs

Modano has 20 points in 17 playoff games. He's 34 years old. He has 4 fewer games of wear than Mats Sundin92 and Gary Roberts85 and every Toronto forward who grinded through two Game 7s. At 34, that differential is everything. It's the difference between a third-period rush and a third-period limp.

Brenden Morrow86 has 18 points. Sergei Zubov84 has 15. They lost Bill Guerin after 8 games, and they found a way to win Game 2 without him. They were shut out in Game 1 with him on the bench. The depth is there. I said last week it wasn't. The record disagreed.

Game 3 in the 33-8 building

Toronto has the home ice. They have the $109 ticket, the $121.34M in revenue, the biggest profit line in hockey. They have Luongo's .943 and Sundin's 16 points in 21 games and a crowd that will make every Dallas break-in howl. I respect all of it.

But 4 fewer games is not a small number in May, and a 1.48 GAA means you win close games that go the other way for tired teams. I'm putting my call on Dallas tonight.
