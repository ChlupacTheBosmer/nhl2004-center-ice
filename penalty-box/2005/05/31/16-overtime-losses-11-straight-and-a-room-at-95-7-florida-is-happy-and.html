16 OVERTIME LOSSES, 11 STRAIGHT, AND A ROOM AT 95.7: FLORIDA IS HAPPY AND LOST

Boris Van Ryswyk told The Tunnel his team couldn't close. The Morale Scale says nobody in that building noticed.

Mickey "The Mick" DonnellyColumnist, ex-goalie · The Penalty BoxTuesday, 31 May 2005
ShareCopy link

The piece

Florida Panthers lost 16 games in overtime this season, the most in the league. They closed the year on an 11-game losing streak, finished 25-36-16 for 76 points, went 15-26 at home, and their room sits at 95.7 on the Bureau's Morale Scale. That is the second-happiest room in hockey, behind only the team hoisting the Cup.

That number should make you sick.

"We were right there eleven times in a row and we couldn't put the thing away," their general manager Boris Van Ryswyk told The Tunnel's Marcus Bell on May 29. "Losing in overtime means you got to the promised land and you couldn't cash the check."

Eleven times. He is describing the L11 from the standings, the streak that turned a competitive season into an embarrassment, and his voice has no fire in it. He knows the number. He knows the record. Nobody in that building has said a single angry word about it.

A happy room is a good sign when you win. Toronto Maple Leafs is at 98.1 and leads the Stanley Cup Final 4-2. Tampa Bay Lightning is at 96.7 and won 107 points. The link between morale and standing is obvious. Florida's number says a room can be content while the product on the ice is 236 goals for, 255 against, and a 15-26 mark in front of 14,971 paying customers at $79 a ticket. Content is not the same as fine. A room that never gets mad at a 16-overtime-loss season is a room that will not fix itself.

Valeri Bure70 is the clearest case. He played 80 games, scored 71 points, and his overall reads 72, five below his base on the Development Index. He is 30 years old. That is not a crash, that is a long slide and nobody pulled the cord. Meanwhile Vaclav Nedorost84, 23, sits at 84 overall, up 12 on the Index, with 25 points in 69 games. One man rising, one man falling, and the average morale in that building is 95.7.

The payroll is $53.89M. The profit line reads $13.59M. They are not bleeding money. They are spending $0.71M per point while the cheapest winning teams in this league pay $0.42M or less. The building is solvent, the ticket is reasonable, the room is content, and the team finished fourth in the Southeast Division with nothing to play for after April.

Van Ryswyk told The Tunnel what he needs: "Somebody who makes the other team's life miserable in the last ten minutes." He knows what this team lacks. He said it into a microphone with the draft 8 days away. The question is whether he can find that man, or whether next May he will stand at another podium and describe another year where his players were right there and could not put the thing away.

The room is fine. I have never seen a room be more of a problem.
