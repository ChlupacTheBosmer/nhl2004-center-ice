2-0. THEN 0-4. GAME 7 IN THE 33-8 BUILDING.

Washington had the series in a vice and dropped four straight. Jagr is 33, Kolzig has a 1.69, and none of it may survive a Game 7 in Toronto.

Mickey "The Mick" DonnellyColumnist, ex-goalie · The Penalty BoxTuesday, 17 May 2005
ShareCopy link

The piece

Washington led 2-0 on the road. They lost four in a row. Tomorrow they play Game 7 in Toronto, the building that went 33-8 this season.

I told you on 5/11 I owed these men a column when they were up 2-1. I was writing too early. They went out and lost Game 3 in overtime, Game 4 by two, Game 5 by three in Toronto's building, and Game 6 by one at home. Four losses. Three of them by a single goal. The room is still 96.8 on the morale scale, second only to Toronto's 98.6. They are not broken. They just cannot close.

What keeps them breathing is the goaltending. Olaf Kolzig88 is 9-3 with a 1.69 GAA and .926 in 12 starts. Josh Harding83 is 2-0 with a 1.00 and .952 in his two turns. Those 14 decisions are the only ones Washington has made this spring and the two men have been absurd. A 1.69 in a Game 7 gives you a chance against anyone, anywhere. I said it from the crease and I'll say it from the column: a hot goaltender is the only true equalizer in hockey.

And the 33-year-old. Jaromir Jagr90 has 5 goals and 16 assists in 14 games, 21 points, 22.1 minutes a night. He's out here against Roberto Luongo95, who is 11-7 with a 2.09 and .939. Luongo is good. Kolzig has been better. That's the argument for Washington winning tomorrow.

The problem is the building. Toronto went 6-3-1 against Washington this season and outscored them 32-24. Toronto Maple Leafs have won the Cup and they are home. 17,713 people paying $101 a seat showed up all season. They will show up tomorrow and they will be loud and they will be mean and the Capitals will be the seventh team to play a Game 7 in this building this spring.

Dallas leads Anaheim 4-1 in the other conference final and that one is over or nearly so. This is the series. The 133-point team against the 104-point team. $60.04M in payroll against $55.92M. A 29-12 road record against a 33-8 home record. A 33-year-old with 21 points in 14 games against a goaltender with a 1.69 who has to be perfect for 60 minutes in the most expensive building in hockey.

I don't know who wins. I know that a 2-0 lead is not a lead until the series is over, and that the Capitals learned that the hard way four times in eight nights. The goaltending gets them to the third period. What happens after that is Jagr's problem and it always is.
