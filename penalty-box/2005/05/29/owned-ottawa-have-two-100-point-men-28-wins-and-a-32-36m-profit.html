OWNED: OTTAWA HAVE TWO 100-POINT MEN, 28 WINS AND A $32.36M PROFIT

The two men who carried the offense are 32 and 24. The team won 28 games. The building turned a profit on the cheapest roster in the division.

Mickey "The Mick" DonnellyColumnist, ex-goalie · The Penalty BoxSunday, 29 May 2005
ShareCopy link

Daniel Alfredsson89 scored 108 points. Martin Havlat88 scored 103. The Ottawa Senators won 28 games. Those three sentences are the whole column and I want somebody in that building to explain them to me.

The roster cost $42.80M, 24th of 30 clubs. The profit came in at $32.36M, 7th best in the league. The attendance held at 14,731 on $80 tickets. The people show up, the money flows, and two men who scored 108 and 103 sit in a locker room that the Bureau's Morale Scale has at 88.4, sixth-worst in a 30-team league.

Alfredsson is 32 years old. He played 81 games at 21.5 minutes a night and carried a 90 overall on a team that finished 28-39-9. Havlat is 24. He played 82 games at 20.5 minutes a night with an 88 overall and watched his club finish fourth in the Northeast behind Boston, Buffalo and themselves in goals-for.

The team scored 254 goals. It gave up 279. That is not a team with a goaltending problem or a luck problem or a coaching problem. That is a team that bought two men who score and nobody who plays the rest of hockey. The Development Index shows zero Ottawa risers and zero Ottawa fallers. Nobody on that roster is going anywhere. Nobody is getting better. Nobody is getting worse. They are 28 wins and a chequebook and a room at 88.4 and the rest of the season hasn't happened yet.

The cheapness is the point

A lot of people will tell you a $42.80M payroll means Ottawa is a poor club that can't compete. That is wrong. They made $32.36M in profit. They have one of the cheapest payrolls in the entire league and one of the fattest profit lines. The money is there. The owners don't want it spent. The GM keeps the books clean and the wins stay low and the gate stays full because 14,731 people in Ottawa will watch 28 wins and come back next year for another 28 wins and the room will stay at 88 and Alfredsson will be 33 and Havlat will be 25 and the points will drop.

That is what a roster looks like when the men in charge have decided that a profit line is a better trophy than a playoff berth. The ticket is $80. The building is full. The scoreboard says 28-39-9 and nobody in a seat is going to explain why Alfredsson is shooting for 30 goals again next year on a team that can't get a third-line winger past 20.

I've said it before and I'll keep saying it: a room at 88.4 means those men know what they are. They know they are the team with two 100-point scorers and a minus-25 goal differential. They know they are profitable. They know they are not good enough and nobody upstairs is going to fix it because fixing it costs money and the money makes the owners happy.

Alfredsson turned 32 in a Senators sweater. He will turn 33 in October. The draft is 10 days away and the Senators pick somewhere in the teens and they will take another young man who will put up 40 points on this roster and call it development. And the profit line will move up again.
