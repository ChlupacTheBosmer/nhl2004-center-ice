YOUR 80 PLAYS WING ON THE 3RD LINE. OF COURSE NOBODY WANTS TO BE IN THAT ROOM.

Columbus is tied worst on the Morale Scale at 90.3, carries a $62.03M payroll, and its coach parks Tyler Wright on the right side of the third line while a man rated 76 centers the second.

Mickey "The Mick" DonnellyColumnist, ex-goalie · The Penalty BoxWednesday, 12 October 2005
ShareCopy link

Columbus Blue Jackets sits at 90.3 on the Bureau's Morale Scale. Dead last in the league, tied with Atlanta Thrashers. Eight days into the season.

The lineup card tells you why.

Tyler Wright76 is a center rated 80. He skates right wing on the third line. Yanic Perreault74, rated 76, centers the second line. Jim Montgomery, rated 65, skates fourth line wing and kills penalties on the first unit. The Bureau flags it: a third-liner rated 15 points above the man on the second line who plays his position.

That is not a system. That is a man behind the bench who has not looked at his own depth chart since training camp ended.

The room knows. Mike Peluso63 carries a 73 overall and has dropped 4 ticks on the Development Index. Josh Holden68, rated 72, centers the fourth line while Joe Juneau71, also a 73, gets the third line down the middle. There is no logic a player can see, and a player who cannot see it will not play happy.

The money is there

Columbus carries a $62.03M payroll. The building draws 14,146 per game at $83 a ticket. Last season the club cleared $22.90M in profit. The owner can afford to put his 80 on the first line. The coach simply will not.

The goaltending is fine. Pascal Leclaire89 arrived on the Development Index at +9, jumping from a base of 83 to 91. He is not the problem. The team scored 1 goal in its opening game against Chicago Blackhawks, a 2-1 loss. One goal in one game. A 90.3 room is not going to find the second.

You do not win by hiding your best man. You cannot keep a locker room from drifting when your top center sees his name on the wrong wing on the wrong line. That is how a team gets to 90.3. Not one bad line change, not one bad trade. A hundred small decisions that tell every man in the building the coach does not trust them where they belong.

Wright is 27 years old, rated 80, paid for center ice. Put him there. Or explain to 14,146 people paying $83 why you are not.
