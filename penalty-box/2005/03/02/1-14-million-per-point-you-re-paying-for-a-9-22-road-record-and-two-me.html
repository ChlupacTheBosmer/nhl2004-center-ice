$1.14 MILLION PER POINT: YOU'RE PAYING FOR A 9-22 ROAD RECORD AND TWO MEN WHO HATE EVERY SHIFT

Philadelphia has the worst cost-per-point in hockey. The room is at 88.2. The owner banked $6.09 million and nobody is getting fired.

Mickey "The Mick" DonnellyColumnist, ex-goalie · The Penalty BoxWednesday, 2 March 2005
ShareCopy link

Philadelphia pays $1.14 million per point. Worst in the league. Not close to being close. Detroit sits at $1.11 million with 73 points on the board, and at least the Red Wings won 22 at home. The Flyers have 61 points from a $69.34 million payroll, the fourth most spent in this league, and the return is a 21-28-9 record, a 9-22 road mark, and a five-game losing streak that has the building at 14,659 wondering why they're dropping $78 a head.

The two men at the bottom of the room

Donald Brashear79 is at 74 morale. He plays 10.4 minutes a night. He collects $2.10 million. Cliff Ronning80 is at 74 morale. He plays 9.4 minutes a night. He collects $2.77 million. Two veterans, two unhappy rooms, 10.4 minutes and 9.4 minutes a night from a pair of checkers whose best years are a decade behind them. The Flyers' club morale sits at 88.2, eighth-lowest in the league, and when your fourth-line grinders are that miserable at that price, the locker room is not a place where a comeback lives.

Simon Gagne86 does 86 points in 63 games at age 25. He plays 21.9 minutes. He is the only reason this club is not dead. The roster around him is a graveyard of expensive veterans and guys the Bureau has pegged as fallers. The development index has no Philadelphia name in the top risers. Nobody is getting better.

The owner is fine

Revenue: $66.77 million. Profit: $6.09 million. The building fills to 14,659 because the prestige is 345 and people in that city still show up for a team that wins nine games on the road in a season. The GM goes home at night, looks at the profit line, and decides nothing needs fixing. The practice rink stays where it is. The workout room stays where it is. The 9-22 stays where it is.

I have written this column 30 times this season from 30 different rinks and the math is the same everywhere: as long as the profit number is positive, as long as the seats are warm, nobody in the front office has a reason to change a single thing. Philadelphia is the proof. $69.34 million in, $6.09 million out, 21 wins, worst per-point in hockey, and the phone does not ring in the summer.

The trade deadline is 17 days away. What the calendar has as the trade deadline will pass and the Flyers will be the same club they are tonight. Gagne will carry them another year. Brashear will sit in that dressing room at 74 morale and collect his $2.10 million. Ronning will do the same with his $2.77 million. And the 9-22 will become the 9-25, and the owner will bank another summer, and you will pay $78 to watch it.
