HE SAID IT HIMSELF: THE PRACTICE RINK IS AT ZERO AND THE HOME RECORD IS 3-17

The GM of San Jose told The Tunnel he built a Level 5 equipment room nobody can train in. The building is the second-emptiest in hockey and $1.68M buys one point.

Mickey "The Mick" DonnellyColumnist, ex-goalie · The Penalty BoxSunday, 15 January 2006
ShareCopy link

Jules Tong, on The Tunnel's Jan Kowalczyk on January 5, said the quiet part with both feet: "Our practice rink is at zero. Our workout room is at one. I'm paying for a front end that the guys can't actually train in." He also said the equipment room is "the best in this league. Level 5." They bought the stuff fans photograph. They skipped the stuff that makes men play hockey at 9 p.m. on a Tuesday.

The result is on the standings. San Jose Sharks is 3-17 at home. Fifteen losses in 20 tries at their own building. The second-emptiest house in this league at 13,151 a night, $80 a seat, and the ones who show up watch their team lose seven out of ten times in front of them.

They are paying $63.99M for 38 points. That is $1.68M per point, the worst rate in this league. The next-worst is NYR at $1.50M, and the Rangers are at least in the hunt with 62 points. San Jose is buying the most expensive nothing in hockey.

The GM's own words put the coach in the frame

Tong said on The Tunnel: "I walk into a practice facility that is the worst in this league and I look at the man's face and I know what he's thinking." He did not name the coach, but he did not need to. The roster is what the GM says it is. The 3-17 home record is what the coach produces. Both men own this.

The Morale Scale has San Jose at 89.4, tied sixth-worst. Their last ten reads WLWWLLLOTLLL. Four wins, six losses, one overtime loss. They won one on the 14th, a 3-2 road win at Phoenix Coyotes, which is the kind of night that makes a 3-17 home team feel briefly alive.

Tomorrow they host New York Islanders at home. The Islanders are 22-14-5 with 53 points, a road record of 8-10, and their own set of problems. This is the softest home date on the schedule for weeks. If a team that carries a 3-17 record cannot find one at home against the Islanders, then the zero on the practice rink is the cause, not the excuse.

Tong said the equipment room took time and money pulled from somewhere else. "Somewhere else was practice," he said. He called it the honest answer. I call it a receipt. The men cannot skate where they should, they cannot lift where they should, and the guys in the stands who paid $80 on a Tuesday to watch a 3-17 team get outworked in their own building deserve more than a Level 5 display case they never see.

Pittsburgh pays $0.85M per point and has a 40-year-old center still outscoring first lines. San Jose pays double that and has 38 points in a division where Colorado Avalanche has 55. The front office built the wrong things. The GM admitted it himself, on camera, to a man holding a microphone. The only question left is whether the coach can squeeze 2 points out of the Islanders tomorrow or whether the zero stays zero all season.
